### The front.

**1. The Swarm Front** (hook item, one of the three strongest; death 281)
- Facts:
  - *Eciton burchellii* raids as a swarm: a front up to about 15 m wide, made of up to about 200,000 workers, advancing over the forest floor and up into trees.
    The raid runs up to about 100-200 m from the nest. Sources: Franks 1982/1989 (Barro Colorado Island), Rettenmeyer 1963, Schneirla 1971 *Army Ants: A Study in Social Organization*.
  - One colony can catch tens of thousands of prey items a day. The common lead is ~30,000 (Franks 1982 or Franks & Fletcher 1983); use it only if the source says it.
  - The workers are almost blind. Each one has a single tiny eye facet or none. They follow chemical trails and touch, not sight (Gotwald 1995 *Army Ants: The Biology of Social Predation*; verify).
- Analogy: a front as wide as a tennis court is long (check: about 24 m, so pick a sourced object that matches about 15 m, for example a city bus and a half), moving at walking pace for an ant
  (give the source's speed in m per hour).
- **Cold-open fact (first sentence):** one army ant colony can catch around 30,000 animals in a single day, almost blind. Use whichever number item 1's source gives.
- **Twist by 0:45 (≤145 words):** the swarm isn't hunting anything in particular. It's a carpet, and it takes whatever doesn't get out of the way.
  The whole forest floor runs. A grown person can just walk off.
- Doug beat:
  - Doug is asleep in a hammock strung low across the raid path. "Doug did not agree to this." (here)
  - The narrator: "A grown person can simply walk away. Doug is not awake." The carpet climbs the hammock ropes. Cut to black. Death 281, flat.
  - The hammock is empty except for the cap. The ants carry the cap off along the trail (cargo beat). Field guide: NO.
  - **Cartoon only:** no ants on skin in close-up, no bites drawn, nothing left behind but the cap. Never a skeleton.
  - Re-hook: "And the ants aren't even the strangest thing in this crowd."

**2. The Followers** (Doug SURVIVES; "Doug is fine.")
- Facts:
  - More than 300 species of animals live with or follow *E. burchellii*. Rettenmeyer et al. 2011, *Insectes Sociaux*, "The largest animal association centered on one species".
  - Professional ant-following birds (antbirds, woodcreepers) catch the insects fleeing the swarm, not the ants (Willis & Oniki 1978, *Annual Review of Ecology and Systematics*).
  - Parasitic flies hover over the front to lay eggs on fleeing crickets and cockroaches. Some butterflies follow the birds to feed on their droppings (Ray & Andrews 1980, *Science*; verify).
- Analogy: a raid with its own crowd of hangers-on, like gulls behind a fishing boat. Use a sourced count for a typical bird flock at a swarm, if one exists.
- Twist: a whole travelling economy rides on this raid, and the birds are the ones eating.
- Doug beat: Doug joins the birds on a branch above the front as an "ant-follower". "hi friend?" (the only one this episode, to an antbird). A butterfly lands on him and leaves. Doug is fine. Field guide: "MAYBE".
  - Keep the dropping-feeding butterflies to one dry line. No close-up.

**3. The Menu** (costume item; death 282)
- Facts:
  - What *E. burchellii* actually eats: mostly other arthropods, above all the brood of other social insects (ants, wasps), plus spiders, scorpions, katydids, cockroaches. Small vertebrates are rare.
    Sources: Franks 1982; Rettenmeyer 1963; Powell & Franks 2006 (prey composition; verify).
  - Prey is cut into pieces small enough to carry, and the column carries it back to the nest. Say it plainly in one line, with no injury description.
- **Costume gag:** army ants don't hunt people, "which is exactly why Doug is dressed as a katydid". Say this once. Doug plays it straight, with the red cap on top of the costume.
- Analogy: one raid day's catch vs a sourced weight of something everyday (only if the source gives a biomass figure; otherwise cut the analogy).
- Twist: a katydid's best defence is to look like a leaf and keep perfectly still, and against a blind swarm that's the worst plan there is.
- Doug beat: Doug the katydid freezes, perfectly leaf-like. The carpet goes over him. The costume goes flat (X eyes). Death 282. The costume comes off for good.

**4. The Soldiers** (Doug SURVIVES with the 022 ankle bandage; "Doug is fine.")
- Facts:
  - *E. burchellii* soldiers have huge sickle-shaped jaws and guard the flanks of the column. They don't carry prey (Franks 1985; Powell & Franks 2006; verify).
  - Their jaws are hooked so that once they close they're hard to pull out. Say it as a fact about the jaw shape, with no wound description.
  - Workers also sting (*Eciton* workers have a functional sting; verify).
  - **Do not use the "stitches/sutures" history.** It is medical-adjacent and needs a wound to explain.
- Analogy: jaw length vs body length, compared with a person carrying two ice-hockey sticks on their head (check the proportions).
- Twist: the scariest ants in the swarm don't hunt at all. They're bouncers.
- Doug beat: Doug pokes a soldier with a stick. It clamps the stick. Doug walks away limping with the white ankle bandage (never red). "Doug is fine." Re-hook into "The trail."

### The trail.

**5. The Road Crew** (death 283)
- Facts:
  - Workers lie down in holes in the trail as living "pothole plugs", sized to the hole, so the traffic runs faster (Powell & Franks 2007, *Animal Behaviour*, "How a few help all").
  - They link bodies into living bridges across gaps and move them to shorten the route (Reid et al. 2015, *PNAS*).
- Analogy: a motorway where the potholes are filled by road workers lying in them, and the traffic drives over them all day.
- Twist: the colony uses its own bodies as the road.
- Doug beat: Doug lies down across a gap to help: "Doug volunteered." The column starts using him as a bridge, and doesn't stop. Death 283, played as a deadpan time-lapse (sun up, sun down), then X eyes. The narrator: "Doug became infrastructure."

**6. The Death Spiral** (midpoint, a strong item; death 284)
- Facts:
  - Army ants that lose the trail can start following each other in a circle (a "circular mill") and keep going until they die of exhaustion.
    Sources: Schneirla 1944, *American Museum Novitates* (*Labidus praedator*); Beebe 1921 for the famous ~360 m circle (verify it's an army ant and the figure; cut it if unsourced).
  - Couzin & Franks 2003 (*Proc. R. Soc. B*) model the same trail-following rules that build the efficient highways.
- Analogy: everyone following the car in front of them round a roundabout forever.
- Twist: the same simple rule that makes the colony unstoppable also kills it when the trail loops.
- Doug beat: Doug finds the circle and falls in line. Lap counter in the corner. Death 284, cartoon dizzy spiral eyes, then X eyes, still walking. Re-hook: "The mill is what happens when they get lost. When they move house on purpose, it's worse."

**7. The Night Move** (death 285)
- Facts:
  - The colony has two phases. In the nomadic phase (~2 weeks) it moves to a new nest almost every night. In the statary phase (~3 weeks) it stays put while the queen lays eggs.
    The cycle runs on the brood (Schneirla 1971; Teles da Silva 1977, or Franks & Fletcher 1983 for the timings).
  - On the move, the whole colony streams along one trail carrying larvae, which takes hours (verify the duration).
- Analogy: a city of several hundred thousand moving house every night for two weeks, carrying the children.
- Twist: they do it in the dark. The route goes through anything that's in the way.
- Doug beat: Doug pitches a tent. It's on the route. Cut to the morning: the tent is still standing and the zip is open. Death 285 shown only as the empty tent and the field-guide NO.
  The DISTANCE TO QUEEN sign has quietly moved. Re-hook: "So Doug went to see where they were going."

### The nest.

**8. The Living Nest** (death 286)
- Facts:
  - The bivouac: the nest is made of the workers themselves, hundreds of thousands of ants linked by their legs and hanging from a log or branch.
    A colony has about 300,000-700,000 workers (verify the range: Franks 1985; Schneirla 1971).
  - The bivouac holds its own temperature, warmer and steadier than the forest around it (Franks 1989, *Physiological Entomology*; Jackson 1957; verify the figures).
  - The queen and the brood are inside it.
- Analogy: a building of half a million people that is the people. A sourced comparison of the colony's head count to a city (for example about the population of a mid-size city; check it).
- Twist: there is no nest. The ants are the nest, and it's warm in there.
- Doug beat: Doug finds the cap stuck on the outside of the bivouac (cargo payoff), reaches for it and climbs in after it. "It's warm." Death 286, cut to black on the bivouac's surface rippling.
  The cap pops out and lands at his ghost's feet.

**9. The Queen** (death 287)
- Facts:
  - The *E. burchellii* queen is wingless and much bigger than any worker. She lays on the order of 100,000-300,000 eggs over a few days in each statary phase (Schneirla 1971; verify the figure).
  - When a colony splits, the daughter queens compete. The workers choose a queen, and a queen they don't choose is abandoned and dies (Franks & Hölldobler 1987, *Biological Journal of the Linnean Society*).
- Analogy: eggs per day compared with something everyday that's counted per day (sourced).
- Twist: the most powerful animal in the colony doesn't get a vote, because the workers decide who stays queen.
- Doug beat: Doug arrives with the cap on (0 M on the sign) and bows. The workers pick the other queen. Doug is "left behind with the losers". Death 287, flat, quiet.
  The narrator: "That was the biggest army ant colony in this forest. It is not the biggest one."

### The bigger nest.

**10. The Driver Ants** (boss, the most extreme item; Doug SURVIVES; narrator quietly disappointed)
- Facts:
  - African driver ants (*Dorylus*, the siafu) can have colonies of about 20 million workers (*D. wilverthi*; Raignier & van Boven 1955; Gotwald 1995. Verify the figure and the species).
  - *Dorylus* queens are the largest ants known, about 5 cm (verify the species and size; Gotwald 1995 or Kronauer 2020 *Army Ants: Nature's Ultimate Social Hunters*).
  - Their raids take small animals that can't get away, and farmers have reported them clearing a field or a hut of pests (verify; drop it if it's anecdote only).
- Analogy: a colony of 20 million is more than the population of a sourced city (for example, compare with a metro-area figure from a census source).
- Twist (and the payoff of the title): even the biggest swarm on Earth catches only what can't run away.
- Doug beat: the narrator sets Doug in the path of the 20-million swarm, in the hammock again (callback to item 1). This time Doug is awake. He looks down, gets up and walks away.
  The narrator: "For the first time in this episode, Doug moved." Then: "Doug survived." (pause) "The narrator would like that noted." The counter stays at 287.
- Closing: field guide sticky note "JUST WALK AWAY". The cap is back on Doug. Outro ≤30 words, no subscribe pitch.

## 7. Risks
- **Gore (keep it cartoon):** army ants are known for "stripping things to the bone", and we don't show or say that. No skeletons, no carcasses, no ants on skin in close-up, no bite or sting marks,
  no prey shown being cut up (item 3 says it in one plain line, with no detail). Deaths are cut to black, X eyes, flat costume, empty hammock or tent with the cap.
- **Kid appeal (HIGH for this topic):** the topic's search results are full of "1000 ants vs X" simulations aimed at young viewers.
  - The ants are never cute: no faces, no smiles, no googly eyes, no marching-band or "ant army" military-cartoon look.
  - Ants are drawn as dark red-brown, crude and numerous. The soldiers' jaws read as tools, not characters.
  - Jokes are adult-coded and deadpan ("Doug became infrastructure", "left behind with the losers"). No nursery tone, no sound-effect gags.
  - Packaging: no "vs", no numbers-of-ants battle framing.
- **Honesty:** the episode must say clearly, once, that a healthy adult can walk away from a raid, and the title is built on that.
  - No claims of army ants killing people. No "flesh-eating" or "eat everything" language. No folklore (the 1904 Congo or Leiningen-style stories) presented as fact.
  - Driver-ant livestock or human stories are out unless a peer-reviewed or agency source exists, and even then they are cut to keep the episode clean.
- **No medical or safety advice:** "just walk away" is the narrator's joke about Doug, not guidance. No sting treatment, no allergy content, no suture history.
- **Facts that need strong sources:** the ~30,000 prey a day (cold open), raid width and length, colony size (300K-700K), the 20-million *Dorylus* figure, the size of the largest queen,
  the 300+ associates (Rettenmeyer et al. 2011), the circular-mill figures (Schneirla 1944, Beebe 1921), eggs per cycle, and the queen-choice result (Franks & Hölldobler 1987).
- **Pronunciation (editor):** *Eciton* "EH-sih-ton", *burchellii* "bur-CHEL-ee-eye", *Dorylus* "DOR-ih-lus", siafu "see-AH-foo". Add any that Chirp gets wrong to `voice.pronunciations`.
