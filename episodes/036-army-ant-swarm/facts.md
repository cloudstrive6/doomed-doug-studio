How each source was checked:
- **Fetched** means I read the passage on the page (WebFetch, curl, or an archived copy).
- **Search** means I confirmed the claim only from search-result text quoting the page.
- **H&W** = Hölldobler, B. & Wilson, E. O. (1990) *The Ants*, chapter 16 "The army ants", reproduced on AntWiki:
  https://www.antwiki.org/wiki/The_Ants_Chapter_16 (live page returns 403 to bots; read in full from the Wayback copy
  https://web.archive.org/web/2025id_/https://www.antwiki.org/wiki/The_Ants_Chapter_16). Fetched. It quotes and summarises Schneirla, Rettenmeyer, Franks, Raignier & van Boven and Gotwald, so it stands in for several primary sources the brief named.

**FLAGGED (screener decision needed):**
1. **Cold open "three thousand animals an hour".** Science News (Couzin and Franks raid study): "During its 10-hour workday, an ant colony flows across the forest floor catching some 3,000 invertebrates each hour." Popular-science source reporting the researchers. Primary source now added (screener-supplied, abstract confirmed): Couzin & Franks 2003, see the Cold open table. The brief's ~30,000 a day is NOT used: no source states that daily total (30,000 would be my own arithmetic, so it is not spoken).
2. **"Less than twenty seconds" to walk twenty meters.** Uses the US MUTCD design walking speed of 3.5 ft/s (about 1.07 m/s), as quoted by the NYSAMPO signal-timing fact sheet (Search only). 20 m / 1.07 m/s = about 19 s. A conservative speed; typical adult pace is faster.
3. **Wembley 90,000.** Wembley Stadium's own 2013 history page (Search only) and Wikipedia.
4. **Greater New York about twenty million.** Census-derived figures for the New York-Newark-Jersey City metropolitan area, 2020: about 20.0 to 20.1 million (FRED/Census, citypopulation.de; Search only). Script says "about as many ... as people in the whole of greater New York" against "more than twenty million" ants.
5. **City bus "twelve meters".** Standard rigid-bus maximum length in the EU (Directive 96/53/EC, 12.00 m). Not fetched; widely used reference. Only used as "wider than a city bus is long" against 15 m or more.

## Brief checks (corrections and cuts)
- **~30,000 prey a day:** not found as a stated figure. Replaced with the sourced 3,000 an hour.
- **Raid "up to about 15 m wide, up to 200,000 workers, 100-200 m":** kept as 15 m or more (Schneirla via H&W), up to 200,000 (Science News, Bristol), up to 200 m (ADW). Note H&W's "100 to 200 m every day" is how far a *colony* travels per move, not raid length; not used.
- **Katydid twist (keeping still is the worst plan):** contradicted by Schneirla (a motionless insect has some chance). Twist rewritten; Doug dies by moving (thumbs up). Costume changed to grasshopper (sourced prey item).
- **Soldier jaws "hard to pull out", ice-hockey-stick proportions:** not found; cut. Stitches history not used (per brief).
- **Beebe ~360 m:** confirmed as 1,200 ft (about 366 m) from the primary text; Beebe calls them Eciton. Schneirla 1944 (*Labidus praedator*) not opened; the death-by-exhaustion line uses Delsuc 2003 citing Schneirla.
- **Emigration duration:** the H&W timing (queen 8-10 pm, done by midnight) used instead of an "hours" figure. Wikipedia's "six to eight hours" not used.
- **Colony size 300,000-700,000:** H&W gives 150,000-700,000; NatGeo "half a million". Both used where they fit.
- **Bivouac temperature figures:** none opened; only the qualitative Baudier 2019 abstract is used.
- **Eggs 100,000-300,000:** confirmed (H&W).
- **Queen choice (Franks & Hölldobler 1987):** paper not opened; the sealing-off account in H&W (Schneirla) carries the item.
- **Dorylus 20 million:** confirmed as up to 22 million for *D. wilverthi*. Queen size: 39-50 mm or more for Dorylus generally (H&W); script says "up to about five centimeters".
- **Farmer pest-clearing reports, livestock and human stories:** cut. H&W reports Savage 1847 and Du Chaillu anecdotes; not used (folklore/anecdote, and the brief bans them).
