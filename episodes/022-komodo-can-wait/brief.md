### Seconds.

**1. The Ambush** (THE WAIT: 0 s; hook item; death 179; "Doug did not agree to this.")
- Facts:
  - Komodos are ambush hunters: they lie still beside game trails and strike deer and pigs from cover (Auffenberg 1981, *The
    Behavioral Ecology of the Komodo Monitor*; Smithsonian National Zoo).
  - They can sprint in short bursts, about 20 km/h (verify; Smithsonian or San Diego Zoo), but like most lizards they tire fast.
  - Range: only a few islands in Indonesia (Komodo, Rinca, Flores and small neighbours; Komodo National Park). Verify the island list.
- Analogy idea: the sprint speed against a sourced everyday speed (a city cyclist, a school-zone speed limit).
- Twist (must land by 0:45): **it can chase you. It almost never has to, because it started waiting before you arrived.**
- Doug beat: Doug strolls down a trail reading the field guide KOMODO page ("FRIEND?" empty). A log beside the trail opens one eye.
  Dust cloud. Death 179. Kicker: the dragon covered about two metres. It had been waiting there all morning (only if "all morning" is
  supported; otherwise "for hours").

**2. The Teeth** (THE WAIT: 1 s; Doug survives; "Doug is fine."; the one 010 callback)
- Facts:
  - About 60 serrated, curved teeth, replaced throughout life (verify the count; Auffenberg or a zoo source).
  - 2024: the cutting edges and tips are coated in iron, which shows as an orange stripe (LeBlanc et al. 2024, *Nature Ecology &
    Evolution*; verify).
  - Its bite force is weak for its size; it cuts by pulling back with strong neck muscles (Fry et al. 2009, *PNAS*; this part of the
    paper was not used in 010; verify the comparison and state it without a wound description).
- Analogy idea: the tooth edge against a sourced everyday object (a steak-knife edge, a saw). Describe the tooth, never the cut.
- Twist: **the bite is weak and it doesn't matter. It is not built to hold you. It is built to let you go.**
- Doug beat: a tiny nip on the ankle (a small bandage, no red, the thumbnail moment). Doug walks off whistling. "Doug is fine." The
  010 callback: "The last time Doug met this lizard, he brought mouthwash. This time he brought a bandage." The narrator starts THE WAIT
  clock. Counter stays at 179.

### Hours.

**3. The Tongue** (THE WAIT: minutes to hours; death 180)
- Facts:
  - The forked tongue picks up scent particles and delivers them to the vomeronasal (Jacobson's) organ; the two tips help it tell
    direction (Schwenk 1994, *Science*, forked-tongue hypothesis; verify).
  - Commonly cited: it can detect carrion from several kilometres away (the "up to 9.5 km" figure needs a primary source; if only
    secondary, give the lower verified figure or cut).
- Analogy idea: the detection distance against a sourced everyday distance (walking time across a city centre).
- Twist: **Doug left. His smell didn't.**
- Doug beat: Doug hides behind a rock, very still, very pleased. A forked tongue appears round the side. Death 180. Cap on the rock.

**4. The Follow** (THE WAIT: hours; death 181)
- Facts:
  - After a bite, prey that escapes is often followed at walking pace until it weakens (Auffenberg 1981; verify how the papers phrase
    it; one clause on the mechanism is allowed as "the bite does the rest", no 010 venom detail).
  - Komodos can cover long distances on foot in a day (a sourced daily-movement figure from telemetry, e.g. Ciofi et al. or Jessop et
    al.; cut if unsourced).
- Analogy idea: the daily distance against a sourced commute.
- Twist: **the longest part of the hunt is a walk.**
- Doug beat: Doug jogs, checks over his shoulder, nothing. He sits down for a sandwich. The dragon arrives at walking speed, unhurried.
  Death 181.

**5. The Swim** (THE WAIT: hours; death 182)
- Facts:
  - Komodos swim and have been recorded crossing between islands (verify; Smithsonian / Auffenberg / Komodo National Park).
  - Separate island populations are genetically distinct (Ciofi et al. or Jessop et al.; verify; cut if not needed).
- Analogy idea: the channel width between two islands against a sourced everyday distance.
- Twist: **leaving the island is not leaving the dragon.**
- Doug beat: Doug paddles to the next island on an inflatable ring, plants a tiny flag. A head surfaces behind him. Death 182. The cap
  floats on the ring.

### Days.

**6. The Crowd** (THE WAIT: days; death 183)
- Facts:
  - Other dragons gather at a kill by scent; larger dragons feed first and smaller ones wait (Auffenberg 1981; verify the hierarchy and
    any count of dragons at one carcass).
  - Most fights are ritual: rearing up and wrestling, mostly between males (verify).
- Analogy idea: the number of dragons at one meal against a sourced everyday group (a football team).
- Twist: **one bite invites everyone.** (Don't reuse 010's "open invitation" hornet line; write a new one.)
- Doug beat: Doug arrives to "help". Several heads turn. Cut away. Death 183. No carcass on screen; the "kill" is off-frame.

**7. The Meal** (THE WAIT: days; Doug survives; "Doug is fine. The picnic is not.")
- Facts:
  - It can eat a very large share of its body weight in one meal (commonly "up to 80%"; verify with a zoo or primary source).
  - Loosely hinged jaws and an expandable stomach let it swallow large pieces fast. Auffenberg's often-quoted record of a dragon
    finishing a pig in about 17 minutes (verify the weights and the time; cut if secondary only).
- Analogy idea: the meal as a share of a person's body weight, with a sourced everyday object for scale (a dishwasher).
- Twist: **it doesn't need to hunt often, because it eats like it might never eat again.**
- Doug beat: Doug offers a full picnic as a peace treaty (`picnic_blanket.json`). The dragon eats the basket, the blanket and the thermos.
  Doug survives. No body content anywhere. Counter stays at 183.

**8. The Buffalo** (THE WAIT: days to weeks; death 184)
- Facts:
  - Water buffalo were introduced to the islands by people (verify when).
  - A bitten buffalo can take days or longer to die, and the dragons wait nearby (Auffenberg; Bull et al. 2010, *PLoS ONE*. Use only the
    waiting and timing. **No bacteria theory**, it is 010's and the paper argues against it anyway).
  - An adult buffalo is many times the dragon's weight (sourced weights).
- Analogy idea: buffalo weight against a sourced everyday weight (a small car).
- Twist: **the dragon picks prey ten times its size (verify ratio), because it never has to win the fight, only the wait.**
- Doug beat: Doug stands next to a buffalo for moral support. The dragon looks at both. It waits. A calendar flips. Death 184 (cut to black
  and the cap on the grass, no body). One costume beat is allowed here only if the writer finds it funnier (Doug in a buffalo costume);
  not required, Komodos do take people.

### Weeks.

**9. The Fast** (THE WAIT: weeks; Doug survives; narrator quietly disappointed)
- Facts:
  - Slow metabolism: a large dragon can survive on a dozen or so meals a year (Smithsonian National Zoo; verify wording).
  - After a big meal, it basks to digest (verify).
- Analogy idea: 12 meals a year against a person's roughly 1,000 meals a year (arithmetic from sourced figures only).
- Twist: **an animal that needs a dozen meals a year can afford to wait for any one of them.**
- Doug beat: Doug walks past a bloated, basking dragon, waves ("hi friend?"). It does nothing. Doug survives. The narrator notes that it
  is full, not friendly. Don't reuse "had the death drawn already" or "sharpened the pencils".

### Years.

**10. The Nursery** (THE WAIT: years; death 185)
- Facts:
  - Hatchlings spend their first years mostly in trees, which is linked to avoiding adult dragons (Auffenberg 1981; Imansyah et al.;
    verify).
  - Adults do eat young dragons (cannibalism share of diet; verify the figure, cut if not primary).
- Analogy idea: the years in trees against a sourced everyday span (primary school).
- Twist: **the first thing a Komodo learns is that adults wait at the bottom of the tree.**
- Doug beat: Doug climbs a tree to escape an adult below. A juvenile is already up there, hard-eyed (not cute, no big eyes). Death 185.
  Cap on a branch.

**11. The Virgin Birth** (THE WAIT: years; Doug survives; "Doug was not required.")
- Facts:
  - Female Komodos in zoos with no male laid eggs that hatched (parthenogenesis; Watts et al. 2006, *Nature*; Chester Zoo and London Zoo).
  - Because of how Komodo sex chromosomes work, these offspring were all male (same paper; verify the wording).
- Analogy idea: none needed; one sourced number (eggs, hatchlings) only.
- Twist: **it doesn't need to chase prey, and it doesn't strictly need a partner either.** Clinical tone, no innuendo beyond "not
  required" (adult-coded, not crude).
- Doug beat: Doug arrives with flowers at a nest mound. Eggs hatch without him. "Doug was not required." Counter stays at 185. Captive
  animals here are a research fact (zoos named), not a captivity theme.

**12. The Last Giant** (the boss; THE WAIT: about 4,000,000 years; death 186)
- Facts:
  - Fossils show Komodo-dragon relatives in Australia about 4 million years ago, and the species is older than once thought; it is not
    an island-dwarfing giant but a survivor of large Australasian monitors (Hocknull et al. 2009, *PLoS ONE*; verify the dates and the
    claim's wording).
  - Its relatives and neighbours included the much larger *Varanus priscus* (Megalania) in Australia and pygmy *Stegodon* on Flores
    (verify; one line each, no "it ate pygmy elephants" unless sourced as a hypothesis).
  - Today: listed Endangered by the IUCN in 2021, with rising sea levels named as a threat to its islands (IUCN Red List; verify the
    mature-adult estimate and the threat wording). One sober sentence, no moral.
- Analogy idea: 4 million years against a sourced span (the age of the earliest human-relative fossils, or "about 1,000 times
  as long as written history", arithmetic from sourced figures).
- Twist: **it outwaited every giant lizard that ever lived. The one thing it cannot wait out is the sea rising around its islands.**
  Attributed to the IUCN assessment. Don't claim more than the source.
- Doug beat: Doug decides to win by out-waiting it. He sits on a rock with THE WAIT clock and a folding chair. The dragon lies down
  opposite. Day and night cycles. Doug yawns. He nods off. Cut to the empty chair and the cap. Death 186.
- **Closing image:** the tide edging up the beach, the dragon lying still, the cap on the folding chair. THE WAIT clock keeps running.
  The field guide KOMODO page: FRIEND? gets "NO". Kicker (one line): it waited out the giants; it will wait for the next Doug.
- **Outro:** 30 words or fewer, final counter (186) and one dry line. No recap, no subscribe pitch.

## 7. Risks
- **Gore (HIGH for this topic):** predation is the subject and Komodos are messy eaters in real footage. **No blood, no wounds, no
  carcasses, no feeding on screen, no gastric pellet, no real attack photos.** Teeth are described, cuts never are. The bite on the
  thumbnail is a bandage. Doug's deaths are dust clouds, cut-aways and the cap. The buffalo is never shown dying.
- **Human incidents (MEDIUM):** one sober line only (item 1 or 4): attacks on people are rare but real, with the IUCN or park authority
  as the source. No names, no injuries, no children, no joke on that line. 010 already used the 2009 figure; don't repeat it.
- **Kid-appeal risk (MEDIUM):** "dragon" pulls fantasy and kids' searches. Countermeasures: dry adult narrator; dragons drawn heavy and
  menacing; juveniles never cute (no big eyes, no "baby" wording, say "hatchling" or "young dragon"); no fantasy dragon imagery, fire or
  wings; metadata never targets kids. The virgin-birth item stays clinical.
- **Safety advice (HIGH):** no "what to do if a Komodo approaches", no "you can outrun it", no "they're harmless if you stay calm". The
  video says they can sprint (item 1). Description line: "Entertainment and education only. Do not approach wild animals." (titler).
- **Over-claiming (HIGH):** the "without chasing" frame must stay true: say "rarely" or "doesn't need to", never "can't". The 9.5 km
  smell figure, the 80% meal, the 17-minute pig, the 12-meals-a-year line and the cannibalism share are all widely repeated online and
  must be traced to Auffenberg, a peer-reviewed paper or an accredited zoo; otherwise cut.
- **Facts that need strong sources (cut if unsourced):**
