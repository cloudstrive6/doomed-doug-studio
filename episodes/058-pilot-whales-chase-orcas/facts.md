# 058 Pilot whales chase orcas: facts (draft 2)

Every spoken claim maps to a source below. "Fetched" = page or PDF text read on 2026-11-25. "Cited" = known through a fetched secondary source that quotes it.

## Brief corrections (read first)
1. **Item 6 is reversed in the brief.** The 2021 Iceland case is a **female killer whale (Sædís) seen with a newborn long-finned pilot whale calf**, not pilot whales with a killer whale calf. No source for the brief's version was found; the script uses the real event (Mrusczok et al. 2023). The twist still works: Dalhousie notes that if killer whales take pilot whale calves, pilot whales would have a motive to chase them.
2. **"A Third Their Weight" is not supported by NOAA figures.** NOAA maxima: killer whale up to 11 (US) tons, about 10 tonnes; long-finned pilot whale adults 2,900 to 5,000 lb, about 1.3 to 2.3 tonnes. That is roughly a quarter by maximum weight. The script says "up to about ten tonnes" vs "about two and a quarter" and makes no ratio claim. Recommend the titler use a different scale phrase or none.
3. **"Fjords":** the Iceland interactions were recorded at the Westman Islands (an archipelago, south) and Breiðafjörður (a broad bay, west). The script says "the bay", not "fjords". Avoid "out of fjords" in packaging.
4. **Curé 2012 does not report how close whales got, or calling rates.** Script uses the start distance (mean 2.4 km) and 4 of 5 only.
5. **Weller 1996 full text not reachable;** item 5 uses new Ecuador detail (GREMM) and the one-line 055 callback (group of twelve, Gulf of Mexico, already sourced in 055 facts).
