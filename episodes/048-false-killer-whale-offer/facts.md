## 1. The Sperm Whales
- Not orcas; name from the skull: Baird 2018 EMM, "The genus Pseudorca, and thus the common name, came from similarity in skull morphology to that of killer whales ...
  rather than any resemblance in external morphology." (Fetched)
- Male up to six meters, about one and a third tonnes: NOAA, males "20 feet", adults "up to about 3,000 pounds" (Fetched); Baird 2018 EMM longest recorded male 5.96 m (Fetched).
- Female sperm whale about twelve meters, around thirteen tonnes: NOAA sperm whale page "40 feet (females)", "15 tons (females)" (Fetched). 40 ft = 12.2 m.
- City bus analogy: a standard transit bus is 35-40 ft, about 11-12 m. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transit_bus (Search); 40-ft class = 12.2 m, National Academies https://nap.nationalacademies.org/read/21947/chapter/8 (Search).
- Published 1996, "attack" in the title: Palacios & Mate 1996 above.
- Three options (predation, food competition, kleptoparasitism/"food robbery"); "no evidence that any sperm whales were actually killed": Baird, R.W. (2025). "False killer whales are
  dolphin predators? Not so fast..." *Whalewatcher* 45(1): 63-65, American Cetacean Society. https://cascadiaresearch.org/files/Baird_2025_Pseudorca-dolphin-predators_Whalewatcher.pdf (Fetched)
- Harassing sperm whales and eating squid the sperm whales regurgitated: Baird 2018 EMM, "documented harassing sperm whales, most likely as a form of kleptoparasitism, feeding on
  squid that the sperm whales regurgitated." (Fetched). Script: "They were after its lunch" is the narrator's summary of kleptoparasitism.

  study revealed that all 61 individuals in the area were linked in a single social network"; "These partnerships were found to span more than 5 years and up to 650 km." (Fetched). Abstract: 34.2% of
  bottlenose resighted with false killer whales over 1,832 days (about 5 years) (Search).
- "The two species even hunt side by side": abstract, foraging observed in 39.5% of mixed-species encounters (Search, zendy/Zaeschmar abstract). Baird 2018 EMM: long-term associations with
  bottlenose dolphins documented (Fetched).
- Cocos Island (Isla del Coco), Costa Rica, false killer whales aggressively chasing bottlenose dolphins: Acevedo-Gutiérrez et al. 1997, *Marine Mammal Science* 13: 307-314, as summarised in Baird 2025
  Whalewatcher (Fetched). Paper PDF: https://faculty.aceveda.wwu.edu/PDFs/Acevedo%20papers/Acevedo%2097%20Pcra%20resightings.pdf (Search).
- 1970s tuna purse-seine observers reporting attacks on dolphins released from nets: Perryman & Foster 1980, NMFS Admin. Report LJ-80-05, as described in Baird 2025 Whalewatcher and Baird 2018 EMM (both Fetched).
- No solid evidence they eat other dolphins: Baird 2025 Whalewatcher, "no convincing evidence has emerged"; "the available evidence is insufficient to confirm they were doing it to kill and consume" (Fetched).

## 3. The Swordfish

  false killer whales ... taken in July 2024 off Lāna'i ... Cluster 4"; photo by Mark Mohler/Cascadia) (Fetched).
- Ping-pong table 2.74 m long: ITTF table dimensions via Western Australia sports dimensions guide https://dlgsc.wa.gov.au/sport-and-recreation/sports-dimensions-guide/table-tennis (Search).
- Ten species of pelagic fish documented as prey of the insular population, including yellowfin tuna, albacore, skipjack, swordfish, mahimahi, wahoo (ono): Baird 2009 MMC review, section 4.F (Fetched).
- 40 species of fish and 22 cephalopods worldwide: Baird 2025 Whalewatcher, citing Zaeschmar & Baird in press (Fetched). Script: "twenty-two kinds of squid and octopus" (cephalopods).
- Dives deeper than 1,200 m from satellite tags: Shaff, Kratofil & Baird (2022), "Beneath the surface: diving behavior of false killer whales from two populations ...", poster, Society for Marine Mammalogy;
  table max dive depths 1,271.8 m (PcTag026) and 1,263.5 m (PcTag032), main Hawaiian Islands stock. https://cascadiaresearch.org/files/Shaff_SMMPoster2022_JuneFinal.pdf (Fetched). Note: conference poster, not peer-reviewed.
- Prey caught at depth brought to the surface: Baird 2009 MMC review, "frequently share their prey, including prey caught at depth and brought to the surface" (Fetched).

## 4. The Line
- Most frequently bycaught whale/dolphin in Hawaii's longline fishery: Hawai'i DLNR Division of Aquatic Resources, "the most frequently bycaught species in the Hawai'i longline fishery"
  https://dlnr.hawaii.gov/dar/10-16-23-hawaiis-false-killer-whales/ (Fetched); Hana Hou 2011, "the most frequent cetacean bycatch in Hawai'i's longline fishery" (Fetched).
- Deep-set main line 25-45 nautical miles (46-83 km), "about forty-five to eighty kilometers"; weighted branch lines each ending in a single baited hook: NOAA MMPA List of Fisheries, Hawaii deep-set longline
  https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/national/marine-mammal-protection/hawaii-deep-set-longline-fishery-mmpa-list-fisheries (Fetched).
- Longer than a marathon (42.195 km): standard marathon distance (World Athletics). Writer's comparison.
- About three thousand hooks per set: Hawaii Seafood Council fact sheet, 2,500-3,000 circle hooks for deep-set tuna gear https://hawaii-seafood.org/wp-content/uploads/2015/08/Module-4-–-Fact-Sheet-Hawaii-Longline-Fishery.pdf (Search);
  NOAA Hawaii deep-set longline 2025 status report, 4,551,729 hooks in 1,511 sets, about 3,000 per set (writer's division)
  https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/resource/data/hawaii-deep-set-longline-fishery-quarterly-and-annual-status-report-2025 (Search).
- Take bait and hooked fish off lines = depredation; can lead to hooking, entanglement, serious injury or death: NOAA species page (Fetched).
- About 139 in the main Hawaiian Islands insular population, 2022: Cascadia page, "approximately 139 individuals (in 2022)" (Fetched); NOAA species page "139" / "approximately 140" (Fetched).
- Endangered since 2012: NOAA species page, final rule November 2012, effective 28 December 2012 (Fetched).
- Honesty line ("the danger mostly runs the other way"): supported by the above plus Baird 2018 EMM, bycatch of the Hawaii pelagic population exceeds potential biological removal (Fetched).
- Tuna heads on hooks are staging only (the narration says "every tuna he pulled up was only a head" as Doug's cartoon beat; the sourced claim is that whales take fish off lines).

## 5. The Share
- One catches a big fish, others converge and share: Baird 2018 EMM, "if one individual captures a large fish others will usually converge and they will share prey" (Fetched); NOAA species page (Fetched).
- Passed whale to whale, sometimes before anyone takes a bite: Hana Hou 2011, "When they catch a fish they pass it around—without taking a bite—and return it to the original whale" (Fetched). **MAGAZINE**;
  script softens to "sometimes before anyone takes a bite".
- Fish caught deep carried up to be divided: Baird 2009 MMC review (Fetched).
- Baird thinks it is about trust and reaffirming bonds: Hana Hou 2011, Baird quote "I think they do this as a way of showing trust ... reaffirming their social bonds" (Fetched). **MAGAZINE (interview)**
- "Hunting as a team": Baird 2018 EMM, "They are cooperative hunters" (Fetched). The orca comparison is framed as a comparison only.
- Sharing with people in boats and in the water, in Hawaii and elsewhere: Baird 2009 MMC review; Baird 2018 EMM; Cascadia page (all Fetched).

## 6. The Pod
- Same individual resighted up to 20.1 years after first documented; repeated associations up to 15 years: Baird, R.W. et al. (2008). "Site fidelity and association patterns in a deep-water dolphin:
  false killer whales in the Hawaiian Archipelago." *Marine Mammal Science* 24: 591-612, abstract (Search) https://cascadiaresearch.org/files/Baird-et-al-Pseudorca.pdf
- A handful of social clusters (four): Cascadia page (Fetched). Cluster of 40 to 50 animals: NOAA species page (Fetched).
- Females to early sixties, males aged up to 58: NOAA species page, oldest estimated ages 63 (female) and 58 (male) (Fetched); Baird 2018 EMM (Fetched).
- Six or seven years between calves: Baird 2018 EMM, calving interval "between 6 and 7 years" (Fetched).
- Menopause in early forties, then living into early-to-mid sixties: Baird 2018 EMM, "females may live into their early to mid-60s, they go through menopause in their early 40s" (Fetched).
- "Roughly another twenty years": early 40s to early 60s. Writer's arithmetic.

## 7. The Beach (sober card)

  recorded involving an estimated 835 individuals" (Fetched). Date varies in press (9 or 10 October); script uses the MMC review's 10 October.
- Stranded groups larger than groups seen at sea: Baird 2009 MMC review, "group sizes from strandings were significantly larger (Ferreira 2008)" (Fetched).
- "Roughly four kilometers" nose to tail: 835 x about 4.5-6 m adult length (NOAA 16-20 ft) = 3.8-5.0 km. Writer's calculation.
- Described 1846 by Owen from a subfossil skull from the Lincolnshire Fens near Stamford: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/False_killer_whale citing Owen 1846, *A History of British Fossil Mammals and Birds*,
  pp. 516-520 (Fetched); Mammal Diversity Database https://www.mammaldiversity.org/taxon/1006425 (Search); Plazi treatment https://tb.plazi.org/GgServer/html/0D4F3DFBF44AB0BC21C18959D03CA373 (Search).

  Slijper monograph https://repository.naturalis.nl/pub/318156/ZM1939021003.pdf (Search) and https://biodiversitylibrary.org/bibliography/155045 (Search). Script avoids the unsourced "thought extinct" claim
