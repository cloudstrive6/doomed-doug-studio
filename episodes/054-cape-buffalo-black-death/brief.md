## 7. Risks
- **Gore.** Horns and charges are the subject. No goring, no wounds, no blood, no tossed bodies, no trampling drawn in detail; deaths cut to black or
  X eyes. Oxpecker blood-feeding is said once, plainly, with nothing drawn on the hide. No injured lion or calf drawn.
- **Hunting and advertiser risk.** Doug never carries a gun; no trophy-hunting framing, no wounded-animal stories, no named real hunters who died
  (real deaths are not played for laughs). The Black Death nickname is attributed and the "revenge" reputation gets the honesty line.
- **Safety advice.** No "what to do if a buffalo charges", no tree-climbing tips, no safari rules. Item 2's tree is a gag, not advice.
- **Kid appeal.** Safari animals and a calf can read as a children's nature show: keep the deadpan adult-coded, no cute calf, no nursery tone, the
  Battle at Kruger told flat.
- **Repetitive content.** Nothing restages 034 bison items or drawings; 052 lions get one callback line.
- **Overclaims to avoid:** "most dangerous animal in Africa", "kills 200 people a year", "buffalo hold grudges/take revenge" as fact, bulletproof
  boss claims, charge speed without a primary source, Battle at Kruger view count without a source.
- **Strong sources needed:** Sinclair 1977 / Prins 1996 (social structure, voting) and Conradt & Roper 2003 (*Nature*); a SANParks or equivalent
  source for weight, boss and dagga boys; Weeks 2000 (*Behavioral Ecology*) for oxpeckers; WOAH or a veterinary review for SAT FMD and Corridor
  disease, and a government or peer-reviewed source for the cordon-fence length; Funston, Mills & Biggs 2001 (or equivalent) for lions hunting
  buffalo; a reputable news source for the Battle at Kruger; a published herd-size figure for the dry-season aggregations. Never a listicle or a
  viral clip alone for a number.
