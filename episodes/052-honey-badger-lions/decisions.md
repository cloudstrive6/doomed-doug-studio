### A · "Every Reason Lions Think Twice About a 9 kg Honey Badger" + TOO MUCH WORK: **REVISE (closest to approval)**
Would I click: almost. The lion's closed-eye "can't be bothered" face plus TOO MUCH WORK is the right verdict joke and pairs cleanly with
the title (title gives the reasons, image gives the lion's answer). But at feed size it reads as "lion and a skunk", with no tension.
1. **The raised paw reads as a wave or a high five, not "no thanks".** Turn the paw so the palm faces the badger (a flat "stop" or "talk to
   the paw"), or swap to the lion leaning back with the paw half-raised over its own chest. The brief's "leaning back" is missing: the lion
   sits bolt upright. Tilt the torso away from the badger.
2. **Close the dead gap.** There is about 150 px of empty orange between paw and badger and the whole upper right is text-only. Move the
   badger left and scale it up (roughly 1.3x) so its nose sits under the lion's paw. The size gap still reads with the lion this large.
3. **The badger must read as a honey badger at 168 px.** Right now it is a grey-and-black box on legs with no visible face. Give it a clear
   eye and a slightly open, teeth-showing mouth (cartoon, as in B) and a defiant head-up angle, so it is "standing its ground", not grazing.
4. **Doug is invisible at feed size** (a red dot). Scale him up to about 0.6 and put his head and cap fully above the badger's back
   line, eyes on the lion. Keep him behind the badger.
5. Optional: a faint horizon or a single acacia silhouette top right under the text so the flat orange reads as savanna, not a wall.

### B · "Every Reason Six Lions Lost Interest in One Honey Badger" + IT CHARGED: **REVISE (strongest idea, weakest execution)**
Would I click: yes on the concept (the charging badger is the best action image of the three and the title has a number in it), but the
picture does not deliver the "six lions" the title promises, and that is the beat that earns the click.
1. **The six lions are not countable.** The four lionesses overlap the two males so only about three heads show and it reads as three
   lions with too many legs. Space all six so **six heads are clearly visible**, either in a loose staggered line or two rows.
2. **The lions are too small and too far back** (about 12% of frame height; specks at feed size). Raise the horizon and bring the pride
   forward so they are at least a third of the frame height. Cut the empty top half: the sky band is ~45% of the image and holds only text.
3. **Give the lions a reaction.** They stand in profile like a parade. The brief asks for them "looking at each other": turn two or three heads
   toward each other or toward the camera, one stepping back. That glance is the joke and the title's "lost interest".
4. **Doug's red cap disappears into the orange-red sky.** Either put Doug lower against the brown ground with the cap clearly on brown,
   or move the sky to a yellow-orange (as A) so the red cap and white-outlined text both pop.
5. Keep the badger exactly as charged: teeth, speed lines, low body. The mouth interior stays dark cartoon red, no drool and no darker.

### C · "Every Reason Lions Think Twice Before Killing a Honey Badger" + IT BITES BACK: **REVISE (the concept is not drawn)**
Would I click: not as rendered. The badger floats in mid-air in front of the lion, with nothing holding it, so the image reads as a
layout error rather than "it turns round inside its own skin". The concept in `thumbnail_brief` is not on the canvas.
1. **Show the grip.** The lion's mouth (or a paw) must visibly hold the badger by the loose scruff at the white pinched bump behind its
   shoulders. No teeth into skin, no wound. A closed jaw on the fold is enough. Lower and angle the lion head so the contact point is
   unmistakable.
2. **The badger's legs must dangle**, not stand on an invisible floor. Use or request a hanging pose (legs loose, body sagging from the
   scruff) with the head twisted back toward the lion's face. That twist is the whole fact.
3. **Doug's HOW TO HOLD A BADGER card is missing.** Add it (Doug holding the card, panicking), or drop it and say so; but the card is the
   adult-coded joke that makes this variant more than "animal angry".
4. Make the "turned in its skin" read without the faint grey C-marks: add one or two bold fold lines at the neck where the skin twists.
5. Blue sky is fine for contrast against the other two variants; keep it.
