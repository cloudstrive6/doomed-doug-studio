# Facts: 039 Every Reason Chimpanzees Kill Their Own Relatives, Including Doug (draft 2)

Every factual claim in script.md is listed here with its source. Checked 2026-11-08 (the 11-06 run was cut off before this file was written; every row below was re-checked in this pass, and the script was corrected where a check failed: see "Corrections made in this pass").

How each source was checked:
- **Fetched** means I read the passage in the paper or page (WebFetch, Europe PMC full text, or the PDF text).
- **Search** means I confirmed the claim only from search-result text quoting the page (page blocked or behind a wall).
- **Arithmetic** means a conversion or comparison I worked out from sourced numbers. The working is shown.

The screener should re-open the **Search** rows and the FLAGS first.

## FLAGS for the screener
1. **Ngogo split counts (item 9).** The UT Austin release (EurekAlert) says the team observed or inferred with high confidence "seven attacks on adult males and 17 on infants" (2018-2024); Reuters and Ground News report these as seven adult males and seventeen infants killed. Other outlets differ (NPR "at least six other adults and 17 infants"; Daily Texan "at least six adult males and 14 infants"; Discover Wildlife "20 adults and more than 25 infants"). The script uses the release/Reuters figures. I could not open the Science paper itself.
2. **"Seventeen of the smallest" (item 9)** = the seventeen infants, per brief section 7 (infants referred to in one plain clause, never described).
3. **Patrol duration "about two hours" (item 6).** Mitani & Watts 2005 give mean patrol time as a garbled PDF string ("2:13 ± 1:01 h, N = 72", read as about 2 h 13 min) and "up to 6 h". Fetched, but the extraction is garbled; screener please confirm the mean in the PDF.
4. **Divergence dates (FAMILY TREE sign).** Ranges vary by method. I used the commonly cited ranges and only "about"/"more than". The gorilla figure (8-10) sits between Scally 2012 (~10 Ma) and Langergraber 2012 (at least 8 Ma).
5. **Congo depth 220 m** rests on Guinness World Records citing a 2008 USGS/AMNH survey; I did not find the USGS document itself.
6. **Phone analogy (item 8)** uses secondary aggregator data (SellCell quoting Assurant): average device age at trade-in 3.64 years (2024) and 3.84 years (2025), so five years is "longer than most people keep a phone".
7. **Bonobo-river link** is contested (Takemoto et al. 2015 argue the river is much older), so the script says "probably".
