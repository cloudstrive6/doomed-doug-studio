## The Underwater Crop Circles
- Circles first found off Amami-Oshima in 1995 by an underwater photographer; builder found in 2011; named as a new species
  in 2014: Saiyu Travel wildlife notes https://www.saiyu.co.jp/en/blog/wildlife_japan/tag/torquigener-albomaculosus/
  (search snippet); Matsuura 2014, *Ichthyological Research*, https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10228-014-0428-5.
- Diameter about 2 m; depths 10-30 m at Amami-Oshima; radially aligned peaks and valleys; built by males as spawning nests:
  Kawase et al. 2022, *Scientific Data*, "3D model of the geometric nest structure", https://www.nature.com/articles/s41597-022-01466-4
  and https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9253097/ (search snippet).
- Fish up to about 12 cm long (maximum length; Kawase 2013 gives about 10 cm typical; script says "up to about twelve centimeters"); species name not spoken (TTS), kept here and for the description; 7-9 days to build; female lays eggs in the center: Kawase, Okata & Ito 2013, *Scientific Reports* 3:2106,
  https://www.nature.com/articles/srep02106 (redirect; search snippet) and PMC copy https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3696902.
- Males dig valleys "at various angles" by fin movements; the structure "played an important role in female mate choice";
  it gathers fine sand particles into the nest; "males never reuse the nest, always constructing a new circular structure":
  Kawase et al. 2013 abstract (search snippet of nature.com/srep02106).
- "Roughly the length of a phone": a typical smartphone is about 15 cm (common knowledge). "About as wide as a bed is long":
  a standard bed is about 1.9-2 m long (common knowledge).
- "Perfectly symmetrical": Kawase et al. describe "geometric circular structures"; ScienceBlog summary "perfectly symmetrical
  circles" https://scienceblog.com/m-for-years-divers-off-the-coast-of-japan-kept-finding-mysterious-perfectly-symmetrical-circles-carved-into-the-seafloor-sand-and-nobody-could-explain-them-it-took-until-2011-for-res/ (search snippet).

## The Lost City
- "Most deep-sea vents are black chimneys pouring out water heated by magma": UW news contrasts Lost City with typical volcanic
  black-smoker vents (verified); NOAA Ocean Exploration "Black Smoker", https://oceanexplorer.noaa.gov/multimedia/black-smoker/.
- Found by chance on 4 December 2000 on the Atlantis Massif, Mid-Atlantic Ridge: University of Washington news 2003,
  https://www.washington.edu/news/2003/04/17/science-comes-to-lost-city/ (verified: "found serendipitously on December 4, 2000");
  Kelley et al. 2001, *Nature*.
- Depth about 750 m: Wikipedia "Lost City Hydrothermal Field" (750-900 m, citing Kelley et al. 2005), https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lost_City_Hydrothermal_Field
  (search snippet); "more than 700 meters" (ScienceAlert/NOAA framing).
- Structures "nearly 100 percent carbonate, the same material as limestone"; white, cream or gray; Poseidon about 180 feet
  (about 55-60 m): UW news (verified). Wikipedia: Poseidon "60 m-tall" (search snippet). "As tall as a twenty-story building":

- Not driven by volcanic heat: seawater reacts with mantle rock (olivine to serpentine), producing heat, hydrogen and methane;
  microbes consume methane and hydrogen: UW news (verified). "Turning one mineral into another" = olivine to serpentine.
- Active for about 120,000 years: Ludwig et al. 2011, "U-Th systematics and 230Th ages of carbonate chimneys at the Lost City
  Hydrothermal Field", *Geochimica et Cosmochimica Acta* 75(7):1869-1888, doi:10.1016/j.gca.2011.01.008,
  https://www.researchgate.net/publication/223806107_U-Th_systematics_and_230Th_ages_of_carbonate_chimneys_at_the_Lost_City_Hydrothermal_Field
  (search snippet: deposit ages range from 17 ± 6 years to 120 ± 13 kyr; field "may be >120 ky"). Draft 2 correction: Kelley et al.
  2005 reported about 30,000 years and is no longer cited for this figure. The age belongs to the field's carbonate deposits, not to
  Poseidon itself, so the kicker now reads "The Lost City has been venting for a hundred and twenty thousand years. Poseidon lasted
  about four seconds with Doug on it." "Well over a thousand centuries" = 120,000 years (arithmetic).
- Poseidon height: UW gives about 180 ft (about 55 m, "18 stories"); Wikipedia gives 60 m. Script keeps "about sixty meters ... as
  tall as a twenty-story building" (screener advisory noted; within rounding of both sources).
- Origin-of-life framing: UW news, "Kelley and colleagues speculate that early Earth life may have originated in such
  environments" (verified). Script: "Its discoverers think the first life on Earth may have started in a place a lot like this."

## The Bloop
- Summer 1997; consistent with icequakes from large icebergs cracking and fracturing; detected on multiple sensors at a range
  of over 5,000 km; likely origin between Bransfield Strait (Antarctic Peninsula) and the Ross Sea, or Cape Adare: NOAA PMEL,
  https://www.pmel.noaa.gov/acoustics/sounds/bloop.html (verified).
- Rises rapidly in frequency over about a minute; several times louder than the blue whale, the loudest recorded animal;
  NOAA's Christopher Fox first suggested ice, then a year later thought it probably animal in origin: Wikipedia "Bloop",
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloop (search snippet). Script: "for a while even some of the researchers thought it was
  probably made by an animal."
- "Farther than New York is from Los Angeles": about 3,940 km straight line, https://www.travelmath.com/distance/from/Los+Angeles,+CA/to/New+York,+NY
  (search snippet). 5,000 > 3,940.
- SOFAR (deep sound) channel: layer of minimum sound speed; traps low-frequency sound and carries it thousands of kilometers;
  close to 1,000 m deep in the tropics: NOAA Ocean Service, https://oceanservice.noaa.gov/facts/sofar.html (search snippet).
- Upsweep: present when PMEL began recording in August 1991; seasonal, peaks in spring and fall; source roughly 54°S 140°W
  "near the location of inferred volcanic seismicity"; "the origin of the sound is unresolved": NOAA PMEL,
  https://www.pmel.noaa.gov/acoustics/sounds/upsweep.html (verified). "More than thirty years later": 1991 to 2026.
- Lovecraft line: cut (no reliable source located; brief said cut if unsourced).

## The Jacuzzi of Despair
- Brine pool about 3,300 ft (about 1,000 m) down in the Gulf of Mexico; about 100 ft (about 30 m) in circumference and about
  12 ft (about 4 m) deep; four or five times saltier than surrounding seawater; Erik Cordes, Temple University, published in
  *Oceanography*; kills benthic crabs, amphipods and occasional fish: DSBSoc reprint of Discovery News (May 2016),
  https://dsbsoc.org/new-research-2/deep-sea-brine-lake-dubbed-jacuzzi-of-despair-discovery-news/ (verified).
- Discovered about 2014, team returned a year later; about 65 °F against 39 °F surrounding water; hydrogen sulfide and methane;
  creatures get "pickled and get preserved"; "warmth of the lake draws unsuspecting marine life such as crabs that look for
  food": Tech Times, 2016-11-02, https://www.techtimes.com/articles/184535/20161102/jacuzzi-of-despair-underwater-lake-of-death-beneath-gulf-of-mexico-kills-creatures-that-swim-there.htm (verified).
  Inhabitat, https://inhabitat.com/the-deep-sea-lake-that-kills-whoever-wanders-in/ (search snippet): discovered 2014 aboard E/V
  *Nautilus*, scientists believe the warmth may have attracted the creatures found dead just inside the rim; surrounded by
  a wall of salt deposits, bacteria and giant mussels that feed on hydrogen sulfide and methane.
- 65 °F = 18.3 °C; 39 °F = 3.9 °C (arithmetic): "about eighteen degrees Celsius ... about four".
- No oxygen; creatures die on contact: Discovery reporting via search snippet ("absence of oxygen kills sea creatures as soon as

- "Came back the following year with a robot": Tech Times names the ROV *Hercules* (verified).

## The Champagne Vent
- Champagne site discovered in 2004 (March-April 2004, NOAA Submarine Ring of Fire) at 1,604 m on NW Eifuku volcano, Mariana
  Arc; droplets mainly liquid CO2 with H2S; droplets "sticky and adhered to the ROV like clumps of grapes"; film on droplets
  assumed to be CO2 hydrate, which forms whenever liquid CO2 contacts water under these conditions; vent fluid 103 °C; pH 3.4 to 4.8:
  Lupton et al. 2006, *G-cubed*, NOAA PMEL summary https://www.pmel.noaa.gov/pubs/outstand/lupt2843/lupt2843.shtml (verified).
- Name and "champagne" look: NOAA Ocean Explorer, https://oceanexplorer.noaa.gov/explorations/04fire/logs/april10/media/eifuku_champagne_qt160.html;
  ScienceDaily 2005 https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2005/01/050104114942.htm (search snippets).
- Droplets cold, below 4 °C: Lupton et al. 2006 abstract via ADS https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2006GGG.....7.8007L/abstract
  (search snippet). Script: "in water barely above freezing".
- Pressure about 160 times the surface: NOAA, pressure rises about one atmosphere per 10 m,
  https://oceanservice.noaa.gov/facts/pressure.html (search snippet). 1,600 m / 10 = 160 atm (arithmetic).
- "Two grown men standing on every fingernail": 160 atm ≈ 165 kg per square centimeter (1 atm ≈ 1.03 kg/cm²); a fingernail is
  roughly 1 cm²; two adults ≈ 160 kg (arithmetic, common knowledge).
- Okinawa Trough CO2 lake: natural liquid CO2 lake in sediments at the Yonaguni Knoll IV hydrothermal field, 1,380 m; capped by
  CO2 hydrate; microbial community in the sediments above: Inagaki et al. 2006, *PNAS* 103(38):14164,
  https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2006PNAS..10314164I and commentary https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.0606709103 (search snippets).
- "A lake of something that should be a gas": CO2 is a gas at surface conditions (common knowledge).

## The Scaly-Foot Snail
- Discovered 2001 at the Kairei vent field, Indian Ocean; iron sulfide ("fool's gold") on sclerites and shell; only known animal
  using iron as a significant component of its skeleton; Kairei about 2,780 m: Wikipedia "Scaly-foot gastropod",
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scaly-foot_gastropod; Animal Diversity Web https://animaldiversity.org/accounts/Chrysomallon_squamiferum/
  (search snippets). Warén et al. 2003, *Science*: iron sulfide sclerites (greigite and pyrite).
- Shell average about 32 mm, up to 45.5 mm: Animal Diversity Web (search snippet). "A little bigger than a quarter": US quarter
  is 24.26 mm (common knowledge).
- Kairei black smoker fluids about 360 °C (max 365 °C): Wikipedia "Kairei vent field", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kairei_vent_field;
  Gallant & Von Damm 2006, *G-cubed*, https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1029/2005GC001067 (search snippets).
  "More than three times the boiling point of water at the surface": 360 > 3 x 100 °C (arithmetic).
- First species listed as endangered because of deep-sea mining, IUCN Red List, July 2019; three known vent fields; "only known
  homes ... under mining exploration licences"; even exploratory mining could damage vents or smother snails with sediment:
  Australian Museum blog, https://australian.museum/blog/amri-news/first-animal-officially-endangered-by-deep-sea-mining/ (verified).
- Two of the three vents under exploration licences; up to 2,900 m: Atlas Obscura, https://www.atlasobscura.com/articles/deep-sea-scaly-foot-snail-endangered
  (search snippet); Sigwart et al. 2019, *Nature Ecology & Evolution*. Stated neutrally; no advocacy.

## The Asphalt Volcano
- Chapopote Knoll, southern Gulf of Mexico, discovered 2003, at about 3,000 m; lava-like flows of solidified asphalt cover more than
  1 km² of the rim of a dissected salt dome; chemosynthetic tubeworms and bivalves colonize the seafloor near the asphalt; oil seeps,
  gas hydrate deposits: MacDonald et al. 2004, *Science* 304:999, https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.1097154 and PubMed
  https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15143278/ (search snippets).
- Flows resemble a'a or pahoehoe basaltic lava; marine asphalt and basalt lava compared directly: *Oceanography*, "Slow Volcanoes:
  The Intriguing Similarities Between Marine Asphalt and Basalt Lavas", https://tos.org/oceanography/article/slow-volcanoes-the-intriguing-similarities-between-marine-asphalt-and-basal (search snippet).
- Tubeworms (Escarpia) in fissures in or protruding from below the asphalt: Frontiers in Marine Science 2025,
  https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/marine-science/articles/10.3389/fmars.2025.1534328/full (search snippet). "Clams and other
  shellfish" = bivalves (MacDonald 2004).
- Asphalt lightly to heavily biodegraded; petroleum hydrocarbons serve as substrates for the chemosynthetic communities; lipids
  indicating hydrocarbon-degrading bacteria in asphalt samples; biological methanogenesis contributes much of the methane:
  Schubotz et al. 2011, *Geochimica et Cosmochimica Acta* 75:4377 (MARUM), https://publications.marum.de/1081/ and
  https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2011GeCoA..75.4377S/abstract (search snippets).
- "About a hundred and forty soccer fields": a standard pitch is 105 x 68 m = 7,140 m²; 1,000,000 / 7,140 ≈ 140 (arithmetic).

## Dark Oxygen
- "Nearly all the oxygen made on Earth comes from living things using sunlight": Northwestern, the finding challenges "the
  assumption that only photosynthetic organisms generate Earth's oxygen", https://news.northwestern.edu/stories/2024/july/deep-ocean-floor-produces-its-own-dark-oxygen (verified).
- Clarion-Clipperton Zone, about 4,000 m (13,000 ft); nodules from tiny particles to "an average potato in size"; contain cobalt,
  nickel, copper, lithium and manganese, battery materials; up to 0.95 V on single nodules: Northwestern (verified).
- CCZ is 4.5 million km² between Hawaii and Mexico, "as wide as the continental United States"; potato-size nodules: Pew,
  https://www.pew.org/en/research-and-analysis/fact-sheets/2017/12/the-clarion-clipperton-zone (search snippet).
- Sweetman et al., "Evidence of dark oxygen production at the abyssal seafloor", *Nature Geoscience* 17:737-739, published
  22 July 2024; oxygen rising in benthic chambers; electrolysis hypothesis: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41561-024-01480-8;
  Wikipedia "Dark oxygen production", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dark_oxygen_production (verified).
- Mining target: Northwestern (verified); Pew (search snippet).
- Rebuttals: The Metals Company rebuttal, https://metals.co/tmc-statement-on-claims-of-dark-oxygen-production/; Downes et al. 2025,

  https://www.science.org/content/article/claim-seafloor-dark-oxygen-faces-doubts; phys.org 2025-03 "a deep-sea discovery that
  has split scientists", https://phys.org/news/2025-03-dark-oxygen-deep-sea-discovery.html (search snippets; Wikipedia verified).
- Original team says the mechanism is unclear (2025 AGU abstract): Wikipedia (verified). Script: "The original team has since

## The Deepest Ecosystem Ever Found
- Fendouzhe dives 8 July to 17 August 2024 in the Kuril-Kamchatka Trench and western Aleutian Trench; 23 dives; communities
  dominated by siboglinid tubeworms and bivalves along 2,500 km, from 5,800 m to 9,533 m; deepest and most extensive
  chemosynthesis-based communities known; methane produced microbially from deposited organic matter, carried with hydrogen
  sulfide along faults; such communities might be more widespread than anticipated: Du et al. 2025, "Flourishing chemosynthetic
  life at the greatest depths of hadal trenches", *Nature*, published 30 July 2025, https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-09317-z
  (redirect; search snippets); Astrobiology.com, https://astrobiology.com/2025/08/chemosynthetic-life-discovered-in-extreme-depths-of-hadal-trenches.html (verified);
  NBC News, https://www.nbcnews.com/science/science-news/deep-sea-creatures-discovery-hadal-trench-submersible-rcna222158 (search snippet: dates, 23 dives).
- "Thousands of them": ScienceBlog/PetaPixel summaries, "tube worms and clams, thousands of them" (search snippet).
