### Harmless.

**1. The Boom** (hook item; Doug survives)
- Facts:
  - Southern cassowaries make very low-frequency booming calls, down to about 23 Hz, at or below the bottom edge of human hearing
    (Mack & Jones 2003, *The Auk*; verify the lowest frequency).
  - Low sounds travel well through dense rainforest, which is the usual explanation for why a forest bird would use them (same source;
    verify the wording).
  - They make the sound with the neck inflated or the body vibrating (verify the mechanism; cut it if it isn't sourced).
- Analogy idea: 23 Hz against a sourced everyday sound. A large subwoofer or the lowest pipe-organ notes might work. Verify it.
- Twist (by 0:45): **the forest's largest bird may be calling to Doug right now, and he can't hear it. He can only feel it.**
- Doug beat: Doug checks his pocket because he thinks his phone is buzzing. It's a cassowary three trees away. It walks off. "Doug is
  fine." The counter stays at 147.
- Shorts note: "cassowary sound/call/noise" is the top autocomplete. This is the hook Short ("This Bird's Call Is Too Low to Hear").

**2. The Helmet** (the casque; Doug survives, narrator quietly disappointed)
- Facts:
  - The casque is a tall crest of keratin over a spongy, bony core (verify).
  - For a long time people said it was used to push through undergrowth or to fight. Those ideas are not well supported (Naish & Perron
    2016, *Historical Biology*, review; verify).
  - Eastick et al. 2019 (*Scientific Reports*) used thermal imaging to show the casque **releases heat** in hot weather and holds it in
    the cold, a thermal window (verify).
- Analogy idea: casque height against a sourced object (a smartphone stood on end, a cereal box). Verify the size.
- Twist: **it isn't armour. It's a radiator.**
- Doug beat: Doug knocks on it ("knock knock"). Nothing. He offers his cap for shade, then thinks better of it. It does not need a hat.
  Survival. The narrator says one dry line about how much paper he wasted on a death drawing (don't reuse "had the death drawn already",
  which 010, 011 and 013 used).

**3. The Plum** (death 148; "Doug did not agree to this.")
- Facts:
  - Cassowaries swallow large fruit whole and spread the seeds of many rainforest plants. Some fruit is too big for any other animal to
    swallow (Westcott et al. 2005, or a Queensland Government or Wet Tropics Management Authority source; verify the species count).
  - Seeds pass through in hours and come out in a pile of fertiliser. One flat line only, no bathroom jokes.
  - The **cassowary plum** (*Cerbera floribunda*) is reported to be toxic to many animals, but cassowaries eat it (verify with a
    botanical or government source. If it can't be sourced, swap in another sourced cassowary fruit, or cut the toxin and keep the
    gardener angle).
- Analogy idea: the biggest fruit it swallows whole against an everyday object (a tennis ball, a mango). Verify it.
- Twist: **the bird is the rainforest's gardener. It can eat the plum. Doug cannot.**
- Doug beat: the narrator hands Doug a plum ("Doug did not agree to this.", the first danger). Doug copies the bird. Green face, X eyes,
  ghost Doug. Death 148. Nothing about symptoms.

### Dangerous.

**4. The Road** (Doug survives; the twist is that the danger is Doug)
- Facts:
  - The southern cassowary is listed as **Endangered** in Australia (EPBC Act; verify).
  - Car strikes and dog attacks are among the leading known causes of death (Queensland Department of Environment; verify).
  - There are road signs warning drivers about cassowaries in north Queensland (sourced).
- Analogy idea: the remaining population in Australia, set against a sourced everyday number (a school, a small stadium crowd), only if
  there is a good estimate. If not, skip the number.
- Twist: **for one item, the dangerous animal on this road is Doug.**
- Doug beat: Doug drives `small_car.json` past a cassowary-crossing sign, brakes in time and waves. The bird looks at him for a long
  time. Survival. The counter stays at 148. Played straight with one dry line, no lecture and no moral (style bible 3.5).

**5. The Dad** (death 149)
- Facts:
  - The **male** incubates the eggs for about 50 days, eating little or nothing (verify the duration and the fasting claim).
  - He then raises the striped chicks alone for about 9 months (verify).
  - The female leaves and may mate with other males (polyandry; verify).
  - The eggs are large and green (verify the colour and size).
- Analogy idea: one egg against a sourced everyday object (a can of soup, an avocado). Verify the weight.
- Twist: **the angriest parent in the forest is the dad.**
- Doug beat: Doug sees "small striped birds" and crouches ("hi friends?"). The dad arrives. Dust cloud. Death 149. The chicks stay small
  and in the background, drawn at the detailed tier, never cute (see 7).

**6. The Swim** (death 150)
- Facts:
  - Cassowaries are strong swimmers. They cross rivers and have been seen swimming between islands or in the sea (verify; cut the sea if
    it isn't sourced).
- Analogy idea: a river width against a sourced distance (a swimming pool length). Verify it.
- Twist: **Doug's escape plan was a river. The bird's commute is a river.**
- Doug beat: Doug jumps into the river, relieved. A casque glides past like a shark fin. Death 150, said flat ("Doug has died. Again.").
  The cap floats downstream.

**7. The Sprint** (death 151)
- Facts:
  - Top speed is about 50 km/h through forest (verify the figure and the source).
  - The legs are very long and powerful for its body (verify the leg proportions if they're used).
  - The standing jump gets one clause only ("and, as Doug found out last time, it jumps").
- Analogy idea: 50 km/h is a sourced everyday speed (city speed limits, a racehorse is faster, a sprinter is slower). Verify it.
- Twist: **it is fastest in the place Doug is slowest: thick forest.** A short fragment burst.
- Doug beat: Doug runs and looks back. Nothing is there. He relaxes. It is in front of him. Death 151.

### Deadly.

**8. The First Pet** (death 152)
- Facts:
  - Douglass et al. 2021 (*PNAS*): eggshells from rock shelters in New Guinea, about 18,000 to 6,000 years old. Many eggs were collected
    late in incubation, which suggests that people **hatched and raised** cassowary chicks (verify the wording and the dates).
  - That would make it one of the earliest known cases of people managing a bird, thousands of years before chickens (verify; attribute
    it to the authors).
  - Cassowaries are still raised and traded in New Guinea today (verify).
- Analogy idea: 18,000 years against a sourced comparison (older than farming, older than the pyramids by about 13,000 years). Verify it.
- Twist: **people may have been raising the most dangerous bird alive for 18,000 years. Doug has a weekend.**
- Doug beat: Doug raises one ("hi friend?"). Cut to a shot captioned "LATER", where it is taller than him. Death 152. The cap sits on the
  empty nest.

**9. The Toe** (death 153)
- Facts:
  - Each foot has three toes. The long dagger-like claw is on the **inner toe**, digit II (verify; 011 used the 12 cm length, so use a
    new analogy or skip the number).
  - Birds are living dinosaurs, and raptor dinosaurs such as *Deinonychus* carried their big sickle claw on **the same toe**, digit II
    (verify both sides. If the "same toe" claim can't be sourced cleanly, cut the comparison and keep the toe facts).
  - Cassowaries belong to the ratites (paleognaths), alongside emus, ostriches, kiwis and the extinct moa (verify).
- Analogy idea: claw shape against a kitchen object (a sourced length; not the ballpoint pen 011 used).
- Twist: **it's the same toe.** Say it once, flat.
- Doug beat: Doug holds a museum-cast-style raptor claw up to the bird's foot for comparison ("same?"). The bird agrees, briefly. Death 153.
  Off-screen. No contact shown.

**10. The Kick** (the boss; death 154)
- Facts:
  - Guinness and many zoological sources call it the world's most dangerous bird. Attribute this (verify which source says what).
  - Only two human deaths are well documented. In 1926 in Queensland, people had attacked the bird first. In 2019 in Florida, the bird
    was kept by the man it killed (verify both; **no names**, no ages, no description of injuries).
  - Attacks are rare, and the bird is mostly shy (verify).
  - The kick is a forward strike with the feet (verify the mechanics; cut anything that isn't sourced).
- Analogy idea: the bird's weight (female heavier; 011 used about 58 kg, so use a different comparison object if it's repeated). Or the
  height of the kick against a door handle. Verify it.
- Twist (the episode's thesis): **the most dangerous bird alive has killed two people in about a century, and both times the person had
  come to the bird: once to attack it, once to keep it.** (verify both circumstances; if 2019 doesn't support "keep it", say only "both
  times, the person was the one who got close"). Then Doug walks up to it.
- Doug beat: Doug opens the field guide, crosses out "NO", writes "FRIEND?" and steps forward. Cut to the forest floor. Death 154.
- **Closing image:** a quiet forest at dusk. The cap is on the ground. A cassowary bends down, looks at it, swallows it whole like fruit,
  and walks off (callback to item 3). Next shot: a seedling with a tiny red cap sprouts from a pile in the forest (cartoon only; the art
  director makes it clean). Kicker: the cap has been planted. The art director may cut the seedling if it reads as a bathroom joke; the
  fallback is the cap left on the forest floor by the footprints.

Deaths: 7 (148-154). Survivals: The Boom, The Helmet, The Road. 10 items. **Option for the writer:** if the draft runs under 2,900
words, do not add a chick item. Add **"The Quills"** between items 4 and 5 instead: the tiny wings carry 5-7 bare, spiny feather shafts
whose function is unclear (verify). Doug teaches it to fly, it doesn't, survival. Do not add a death; the boss stays 154.

## 7. Risks
- **Kid-appeal risk (MEDIUM-HIGH):** "cassowary" search shows Planet Zoo builds and a kids' ranger series (signals 10-09).
  Countermeasures:
  - Dry adult narrator. The jokes are about Doug's choices and the narrator's indifference.
  - **Chicks only at The Dad and The First Pet**, small and drawn at the detailed tier, never big-eyed or cute, and never on the
    thumbnail.
  - No songs, no funny bird voices, no slapstick sound effects, no "dinosaur" kid framing. The raptor comparison stays scientific and is
    said once.
  - One flat line about droppings at item 3, and no bathroom humour anywhere else (the closing seedling must read as "planted", not as a
    poop joke).
  - Metadata never targets kids.
- **Safety advice (HIGH):** no "what to do if a cassowary charges", no "don't run", no "put a backpack between you", no "never feed
  them" written as a tip.
  - Behaviour is stated as science, never as instructions. Doug's choices are always wrong and never framed as advice.
  - Description line: "Entertainment and education only. Do not approach wild animals." (the titler owns it).
  - The Road item states conservation facts plainly. No preaching and no driving advice.
- **Gore (MEDIUM):** the claw invites injury talk. **The claw never touches Doug on screen.** Deaths cut away to a dust cloud. Nothing
  describes what the claw does to a body. No real attack photos. Animal photos only with a verified licence.
