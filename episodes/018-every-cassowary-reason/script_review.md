## 1. Facts

### Verified OK (re-opened or independently checked)
- Mack & Jones 2003 abstract: 32 Hz southern (one wild recording, Crater Mountain), 23 Hz dwarf (captive). Lowest piano key 27.5 Hz, hearing about 20 Hz.
- Eastick et al. 2019: 20 adult southern cassowaries, 5-36 °C, the casque "acts as a thermal radiator".
- Westcott et al. 2005: 238 species, mean retention 309 min, mean dispersal 239 m, about 4% over 1,000 m.
- Saber & Hassanin 2014 abstract: "only four long, slightly curved quills"; "the second digit bore a long curved claw".
- CSIRO PC060175: 110 deaths, 1986-2004, vehicles 55%, dogs 18%.
- EPBC status is still Endangered (Mongabay May 2026, Australian Geographic 2025). There was no change in 2025-26.
- Guinness: 2 m, 60 kg, 12 cm claw, the 1926 hunter fell while fleeing. SciAm (Naish): digit II claw, digit I absent, chest-height jump, "naturally wary", the 1926 victim struck the bird first.
- AP/ABC30 2019: the owner was raising the birds (wildlife officials); "when he fell, he was attacked" (fire rescue).
- Penn State / Sci.News 2021: 1,019 fragments, 18,000-6,000 years, a large majority harvested late, unburned late-stage shells, still traded, dwarf about 20 kg, "earliest indication".
- Bingil Bay, 31 Oct 2023, DES release 13 Nov 2023, turtle/shark-fin confusion, officials unsure how long or why.
- The default built-up-area speed limit in Queensland is 50 km/h. SDZWA: wing claw, skin colour changes with mood.

### FAIL items
- **F1. Opera House number is out of date.** Line 127: "seats two thousand six hundred and seventy-nine people". 2,679 was the
  capacity before the 2020-22 renovation. The official venue page now says "up to 2664 people in the round or up to 2102 facing the
  stage" (sydneyoperahouse.com/visit/our-venues/concert-hall). Both facts.md sources are pre-renovation. The joke still works with the
  new figure.
- **F2. "Sixty-kilogram bird" at Bingil Bay is contradicted by the script's own source.** Line 223: "A sixty-kilogram bird walking out
  of the surf". The DES release and the coverage call it a **sub-adult/juvenile**. The 60 kg figure is for large adults, so the
  sentence states something false about this bird.
- **F3. "Unlike an emu" is a claim the source itself doubts.** Line 343: "Unlike an emu, a cassowary isn't limited to kicking
  backwards." Naish (SciAm) writes that emus "**reputedly** can only kick backwards" and says he doubts it. The script states it as fact.
- **F4. Item 1's twist mixes up the two species.** Lines 19-31: the southern cassowary's call went to 32 Hz. That is *above* the
  lowest piano key and well inside human hearing. Only the dwarf cassowary (a captive bird) reached 23 Hz. Line 31 then says "a bird
  up to two meters tall could be calling ... and you might not hear a thing. You would only feel it." The two-metre bird is the
  southern one, which the numbers say you *can* hear. Mack's quote is "can be heard **or** felt". As written, the script's own
  numbers contradict the hook's twist.
- **F5. "Earliest people in New Guinea" is wrong.** Line 277: "Some of the earliest people in New Guinea seem to have done the
  opposite." People have lived in New Guinea, including the highlands, for about 49,000-50,000 years (Ivane Valley, *Science* 2010).
  The eggshells are 18,000-6,000 years old, so these were not the earliest people.
- **F6. Hedge dropped on the 18,000-year claim.** Line 301: "The people of New Guinea had eighteen thousand years of practice."
  The study only says people *may* have raised chicks, and the oldest shells are 18,000 years old. It is not 18,000 years of
  continuous practice. Line 295 hedges correctly with "may have been"; line 301 drops the hedge.
- **F7. The casque review is overstated, and part of it is unsourced.** Line 57: "One major review found the casque far too light and
  fragile to be any kind of weapon". The Naish & Perron abstract (Southampton eprint, Academia mirror) says the bony core is
  **fragile** and that a percussive origin is **unlikely**. I could not find "light" or "any kind of weapon" in any source. facts.md
  admits this line is "search" only and Eastick et al. don't repeat it. This breaks the brief's rule: cut it, don't hedge it.
- **F8. Double feathers "may be unique to cassowaries" is probably contradicted.** Line 155. The only source is SDZWA, which itself
  cites a personal communication. Several sources (City of Wanneroo collection, Freiburg museum, the Clemet zoo resource) say
  **emus** have the same equal-length double feather. Australian viewers will spot this in the comments.
- facts.md only (fix it, but it doesn't block): the Wet Tropics "more than 70 species" quote is on **wettropics.gov.au/cassowaries**,
  not /seed-dispersal. The seed-dispersal page says "over 150 different fruits". Fix the URL. The spoken line is attributed, so it
  can stay.
- facts.md only: line 19 says "In two thousand three, researchers recorded". 2003 is the *publication* year. No source gives the date
  of the recordings.

## Required fixes (numbered)
1. **Line 127** "seats two thousand six hundred and seventy-nine people": change it to the current official figure, "seats up to two
   thousand six hundred and sixty-four people", and update the label to "CONCERT HALL: 2,664" and the facts.md source to the official
   venue page.
2. **Line 223** "A sixty-kilogram bird walking out of the surf": remove the weight, for example "A young cassowary walking out of the
   surf at a campground was unusual enough ...".
3. **Line 343** "Unlike an emu, a cassowary isn't limited to kicking backwards. It can kick forward and downward": cut the emu clause.
   Start with "A cassowary can kick forward and downward, ...".
4. **Lines 19-31 (The Boom twist):** make the numbers support the twist. Either (a) say the deepest calls recorded, from the dwarf
   cassowary, dropped below the lowest piano key toward the edge of hearing, and change line 31 to "you might feel it as much as hear
   it", or (b) keep the felt-not-heard line but tie it to Mack's quote ("heard or felt"), not to the southern bird's 32 Hz. Don't
   claim a two-metre southern cassowary's call is inaudible. Optional: line 19 becomes "A study published in two thousand three ...".
5. **Line 277** "Some of the earliest people in New Guinea": change it to "People in New Guinea, at least eighteen thousand years
   ago, seem to have done the opposite."
6. **Line 301** "The people of New Guinea had eighteen thousand years of practice.": restore the hedge or reframe it, for example
   "The people of New Guinea may have had thousands of years of practice." This line is also the item kicker (see fix 9).
7. **Line 57** "found the casque far too light and fragile to be any kind of weapon": change it to the sourced wording, "found its bony
   core fragile and judged the weapon idea unlikely, but it still couldn't settle ...", and update facts.md with the abstract source
   (Academia/Southampton).
8. **Line 155** "which may be unique to cassowaries": cut the clause. Optionally say "a trait it shares only with its cousin, the
   emu", but only with a better source than the ones above.
9. **Rule 4, kicker length 8-25 words:** The First Pet ends on "Doug skipped the practice." (4 words) and The Toe ends on "Some of them
   just took up fruit." (7 words). Make each final sentence at least 8 words, for example "Some of them just gave up hunting and
   took up fruit." Also, The Quills' Doug beat is 33 words (limit 30); trim it.
10. **Rule 7, no transitions:** line 119, "So far, the cassowary has been the danger in every item. For this one, we are going to look
    at it from the bird's side of the road.", talks about the list itself. Replace it with a frame line that doesn't mention the list,
    for example "On the roads of far north Queensland, the danger runs the other way."
11. **Rule 8, scale analogy:** The First Pet has no everyday-object analogy. The chicken is a time comparison, not a scale one. Add
    one, for example the dwarf cassowary's twenty kilograms against a sourced everyday weight, with the source in facts.md.
12. **Rule 10, at most one joke per 60 s:** The Plum has 4-5 joke lines in about 70 s ("no table manners whatsoever", "free
    fertiliser", "decided it looked easy", "stayed behind as a lesson"). The Sprint has 4 ("catching a bus", "exactly the legal
    limit", "decent shoes", "a route Doug didn't know existed"). The Swim has 4. Cut each of these items to its best 1-2 lines. I'd
    suggest cutting "no table manners whatsoever" and making "free fertiliser" plain ("in a pile of fertiliser"). In The Swim, cut
    "and all of that can swim" or "which is more than most swimmers manage".
13. facts.md: change the Wet Tropics URL to https://www.wettropics.gov.au/cassowaries, and record the 2003 publication-versus-recording
    wording from fix 4.

Recommended, not required: lengthen the short items toward the brief's 270-310 band, using the words freed by fix 12, and put more
mechanism into The Plum and The Boom. Vary three of the "Here's ..." twist markers.
