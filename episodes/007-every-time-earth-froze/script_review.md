**FAIL items**

- **F1. Chicago ice thickness (item 6).**
  > "Doug plants a flag on the ice, three kilometers above where Chicago will be"

  The 3 km figure is the Laurentide's *maximum*, near Hudson Bay, and not the thickness at its southern margin. Illinois DNR puts the ice in northern Illinois at about 2,000 ft (about 600 m). **Change to** "Doug plants a flag on the ice above where Chicago will be". Alternatively write "more than half a kilometer above", but only if you add the Illinois DNR source to facts.md.

- **F2. How fast Antarctica froze (item 7 kicker).**
  > "Antarctica took millions of years to freeze, and it has not thawed out since."

  CO₂ fell over millions of years, but the ice sheet itself formed in under about 400,000 years, in two quick steps (Coxall et al. 2005, *Nature*; EOT reviews). The brief's research note says "formed quickly in geologic terms". As written, the kicker reverses the science. **Change** the kicker so that the slow part is the CO₂ and the fast part is the ice, then add the Coxall source to facts.md. For example: "The air took millions of years to cool. The ice moved in fast, and it never left."

- **F3. Kopp 2005 misattributed to the Huronian (item 11).**
  > "A two thousand and five study by researchers at Caltech argued that the oxygen makers could have tipped Earth into a snowball in as little as a million years."

  The line sits in the Huronian paragraph, so it reads as evidence for *this* freeze. Kopp et al. explicitly say the Huronian glaciations **predate** the snowball they model, which is the Makganyene, about 2.3-2.2 Ga, in South Africa. facts.md itself concedes this. **Change** the line so it names a later freeze, for example "...could have tipped Earth into a full snowball, recorded in rocks in South Africa, in as little as a million years". If not, cut the sentence.

- **F4. Storegga run-up misquoted (item 3).**
  > "In Shetland and Scotland, the water ran up at least five meters, and possibly several meters more."

  Walker et al. 2020 say "run-up heights (**up to** 5 m)" for the mainland coast. They add that the observed figures are minimum estimates and the true heights "may actually have been metres higher". The script turns "up to" into "at least". **Change to** "On the Scottish coast, the wave left sand up to five meters above the sea, and the real height may have been several meters more." That wording matches the source. Do not add a Shetland figure unless you source it.

**Advisory (not blocking)**
- Item 9: "which is like glaciers reaching the sea at Singapore". Singapore is at about 1°N, but the claim is "within about ten degrees". A place near 10° latitude would be accurate, for example Costa Rica or southern Thailand.
- Item 11: "The first great freeze" is fine because of "great", but the Pongola glaciation (about 2.9 Ga, regional) is older. Don't let the titler or the description say "first ice age".
