  NPS (verified): "In June, heavy snow smothered the ground in Albany, New York and Dennysville, Maine" https://www.nps.gov/articles/000/1816-the-year-without-summer.htm
- Kitchen chair seat about 45-46 cm high: common knowledge (standard seat height about 45 cm).
- Caused by the Tambora eruption more than a year earlier (April 1815); Tambora in Indonesia, on Sumbawa.
  NOAA NESDIS (verified: April 5-10, 1815; VEI 7): https://www.nesdis.noaa.gov/news/day-history-mount-tambora-explosively-erupts-1815 ;
  UCAR SciEd (verified: "On April 5, 1815, Mount Tambora... started to rumble"; "the largest volcanic explosion in recorded
  history"): https://scied.ucar.edu/learning-zone/how-climate-works/mount-tambora-and-year-without-summer ;
  Sumbawa: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Tambora
- "Far side of the planet": Sumbawa (about 8°S, 118°E) and Vermont (about 44°N, 72°W) are separated by roughly 170° of
  longitude; common geography.
- About 100 km³ of material (range 100-150): Britannica infographic (search snippet: "24 cubic miles (100 cubic km)")
  https://www.britannica.com/story/the-eruption-of-mount-tambora-animated-infographic-spotlight ; NOAA "approximately 31
  cubic miles of ash" (verified, link above).

- Sulfur aerosols in the stratosphere formed a haze that blocked sunlight; ash fell back:
  NPS (verified: "sent lighter particles into the stratosphere, forming an aerosol cloud that blocked sunlight");
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1815_eruption_of_Mount_Tambora (search snippet, 60 megatons of sulfur).

## 2. Frost Fairs
- The Thames froze over 24 times between the early 1600s and early 1800s; Little Ice Age temperatures about 0.5°C lower;
  1683-84 fair lasted two months with bowling; printing shops offering personalised keepsakes; old London Bridge had 19
  closely spaced arches that slowed water and could be blocked by ice; replaced in 1831 by a five-arch bridge; dredging and
  embankments then deepened and sped the river: London Museum (verified)
  https://www.londonmuseum.org.uk/collections/london-stories/frost-fairs-festivities-frozen-river-thames/
- Printed name cards for a small fee (1684, Croom, sixpence; name, date, "printed on the Thames"):
  https://www.londonmuseum.org.uk/collections/v/object-141216/souvenir-frost-fair-keepsake/ (search snippet);
  https://artuk.org/discover/stories/frost-fairs-and-the-frozen-thames (search snippet, John Evelyn account).
- 1814 was the last fair, between Blackfriars Bridge and London Bridge, about four days; elephant led across near
  Blackfriars; printing presses on the ice; ended when the wind changed and snow became rain:
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Thames_frost_fairs (search snippet: began 1 February, lasted four days, elephant);
  https://thehistorypress.co.uk/article/the-last-thames-frost-fair/ (search snippet: presses, certificates with names,
  "By the fifth day... the wind had changed direction, snow became rain"). London Museum gives "January 1814"; the script
  says only "eighteen fourteen".
- Blackfriars to London Bridge about 1 km, about ten football pitches: map distance about 1.1 km along the river; a
  pitch is about 100-110 m. Common knowledge/arithmetic.

## 3. Doggerland
- Sea level about 120 m lower at the Last Glacial Maximum; Doggerland joined Britain to Europe; shrank to islands, then an
  island: Hill et al. (arXiv 1707.05593) https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.05593 (search snippet: "LGM lowstand of roughly 120 m");
  USGS fact sheet https://pubs.usgs.gov/fs/fs2-00/ (search snippet: about 120 m lower 20,000 years ago);
  University of Bradford (verified: land bridge; "people once actually lived on what is now the bottom of the North Sea";
  shrinking to archipelago and island) https://www.bradford.ac.uk/news/archive/2025/final-days-of-doggerland-interactive-map-and-animation.php
- 120 m about a forty-story building: arithmetic, about 3 m per storey.
- Storegga Slide about 8,150 years ago, 3,200 km³ of sediment, tsunami; run-up up to 5 m in Shetland and Scotland and
  possibly metres higher; Doggerland reduced to a sandbank by then; final inundation by sea-level rise, not the tsunami:
  Walker et al. 2020, *Antiquity* (verified)
  https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/antiquity/article/great-wave-the-storegga-tsunami-and-the-end-of-doggerland/CB2E132445086D868BF508041CC1B827
- Landslide "roughly the size of Scotland": University of Bradford (verified, link above).
- Mesolithic people on these coasts (one plain line): Bradford (verified) and Walker et al. 2020.

## 4. The Younger Dryas
- Warming began about 14,500 years ago; Northern Hemisphere suddenly returned to near-glacial conditions; named after
  *Dryas octopetala*, a flower that grows in cold conditions and became common in Europe; end about 11,500 years ago was
  abrupt, Greenland +10°C in a decade (Alley 2000); European varved lake sediments show the same shifts (Brauer 2008);
  leading cause: Laurentide meltwater rerouted from the Mississippi toward the St. Lawrence/North Atlantic, slowing the
  thermohaline circulation; Antarctica warmed during the Younger Dryas (bipolar see-saw): NOAA NCEI Paleo Perspective (verified,
  PDF text extracted) https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/sites/default/files/2021-11/3%20The%20Younger%20Dryas%20-FINAL%20NOV%20(1).pdf
- Duration about 1,200 years; onset about 12,900 years ago (dial): Britannica https://www.britannica.com/science/Younger-Dryas-climate-interval
  (search snippet, 403 on fetch); Alley 2000, *QSR* https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0277379199000621
- Dryas octopetala flowers are white with about eight petals: common botanical knowledge (the species name means "eight petals").
- Fresh water is less dense than salt water: common knowledge.
- Fridge (about 4°C) to a chilly kitchen (about 14°C) = 10°C: common knowledge/arithmetic.
- "Before turning twenty": a decade-scale change fits inside one childhood; arithmetic on the NOAA figure.

## 5. The Missoula Floods
- 18,000-15,000 years ago; ice dam; water rose 2,000 ft (610 m) behind the dam; lake held as much as 600 cubic miles,
  about Lake Ontario and Lake Erie combined; emptied in about two days; 65 mph (105 km/h); ten times the flow of all the
  world's rivers today; dam repeatedly formed and burst, dozens of floods over thousands of years; Bretz initially
  rejected, later validated: NPS Ice Age Floods National Geologic Trail (verified) https://nps.gov/iafl/learn/index.htm
- Ice lobe from the Cordilleran ice sheet (out of Canada) blocking the Clark Fork in northern Idaho: NPS (search snippet,
  "an ice dam blocked the Clark Fork River in northern Idaho"; "Cordilleran ice sheet continued moving south")
  https://www.nps.gov/iceagefloods/d.htm
- Channeled Scablands, soil stripped to bare rock, dry canyons: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Channeled_Scablands ; NPS above.
- 610 m deeper than CN Tower (553 m) is tall: common knowledge.
- Bretz proposed the flood in 1923; dismissed for decades; Penrose Medal (highest award of the Geological Society of
  America) in 1979; "All my enemies are dead" (basis of the "most of his critics were dead" kicker):
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J_Harlen_Bretz (search snippet); https://www.famousscientists.org/j-harlen-bretz/ (search snippet).

## 6. The Last Glacial Maximum
- About 20,000 years ago; Laurentide covered most of Canada; land still rebounding near Hudson Bay/James Bay about
  10 mm a year, about 1 m per century: NASA Earth Observatory (verified)
  https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/contours-of-the-james-bay-lowlands/
- Laurentide maximum about 3 km thick, reached the northeastern US: National Geographic Education (verified)
  https://education.nationalgeographic.org/resource/ice-sheet/
- Lake Michigan lobe extended into northeastern Illinois at the maximum: npj Climate and Atmospheric Science 2024
  https://www.nature.com/articles/s41612-024-00760-9 (search snippet); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Chicago
- Sea level about 120 m lower: USGS fact sheet (above).
- Weight of the Laurentide depressed the land; rebound continues today; oldest beaches farthest inland formed about
  7,500 years ago when relative sea level in Hudson Bay was about 130 m above present: NASA Earth Observatory (verified)
  https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/rebounding-in-hudson-bay-147405
- Nine Eiffel Towers: 3,000 m / 330 m = 9.1. Kitchen counter about 90 cm, "about a meter": common knowledge.

## 7. Antarctica Freezes
- About 53 million years ago, palm trees on the coast, beech and conifers inland, summer highs near 25°C:
  Smithsonian Magazine (verified) https://www.smithsonianmag.com/smart-news/ancient-climate-change-meant-antarctica-was-once-covered-with-palm-trees-12098835/
- Southern beech (*Nothofagus*) in Antarctica's fossil record: British Antarctic Survey (verified)
  https://www.bas.ac.uk/data/our-data/fossils-from-the-antarctic/
- About 40% CO2 decrease over three million years before glaciation; ice sheet formed about 34 Ma: Yale study via phys.org
  (verified) https://phys.org/news/2011-12-plunge-co2-antarctica.html
- Eocene-Oligocene boundary about 34 Ma; prime suspect gradual CO2 reduction plus an orbital trigger making summers cold
  enough for ice to remain; "ice-house at the poles that has continued to the present day": Amgueddfa Cymru, Museum Wales
  (verified) https://museum.wales/blog/1036/When-Antarctica-went-into-the-deep-freeze/
- CO2 threshold and the gateway debate: DeConto & Pollard 2003, *Nature* 421:245; UNH/ScienceDaily 2014 "Antarctic ice
  sheet is result of carbon dioxide decrease, not continental breakup" https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2014/07/140730141020.htm
  (search snippet); Smithsonian (above) names the Antarctic Circumpolar Current as a major reason it stays cold.
- Ice grew and shrank since but the continent stayed glaciated: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Antarctic_Ice_Sheet
  (search snippet: "the Antarctic has apparently been continuously glaciated ever since the EOT"; "likely diminished in the Miocene").
- Average thickness about 2 km, thickest about 4.8 km: National Geographic Education (verified, above).
- Five Empire State Buildings: 2,000 m / 381 m (roof) = 5.2. Common knowledge.

## 8. The Karoo Ice Age
- Late Paleozoic Ice Age (formerly Karoo Ice Age, named for glacial deposits in the Karoo, South Africa); longest-lived
  icehouse of the Phanerozoic (last ~540 Myr); glacial deposits on South America, southern Africa, India, Antarctica and
  Australia; roughly 360/330-260/255 Ma:
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Late_Paleozoic_icehouse ; Montañez & Poulsen 2013, *Annual Review of Earth and Planetary
  Sciences* 41:629 https://www.researchgate.net/publication/234148971_The_Late_Paleozoic_Ice_Age_An_Evolving_Paradigm
  (search snippet); Pangea B paper (Rutgers) https://eps.rutgers.edu/images/Pangea_B_and_the_Late_Paleozoic_Ice_Age.pdf (search snippet).
- Gondwana over the South Pole; land gathering into Pangaea: Wikipedia LPIA (above); common geology.
- Equatorial coal swamp forests; high oxygen (estimates up to about 35%, "nearly 40%" in some); Meganeura wingspan about
  70 cm, about 300 Ma, equatorial forests: The Conversation (verified)
  https://theconversation.com/are-the-paleozoic-eras-giant-dragonflies-still-among-us-102384 ; Clapham & Karr / PMC
  hawker-predator paper https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6092361/ . Script says "may have held far more oxygen".
- Front door about 80-90 cm wide: common knowledge.
- Most coal deposited in the Carboniferous (359-299 Ma) from plant debris; drawdown of CO2; brought Earth close to global
  glaciation about 300 Ma: Feulner 2017, *PNAS* 114:11333 https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29073052 (search snippet);
  PIK press release https://www.pik-potsdam.de/en/news/latest-news/formation-of-coal-almost-turned-our-planet-into-a-snowball
- Organic carbon burial rates highest 330-260 Myr: Montañez 2016, *PNAS* https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1600236113 (search snippet).
- Coal that powered the Industrial Revolution formed then: Feulner 2017 (search snippet: "The coal driving the Industrial
  Revolution was deposited during the Carboniferous").
- Mountain uplift and weathering also lowered CO2: Goddéris et al. 2017, *Nature Geoscience*
  https://www.nature.com/articles/ngeo2931 (search snippet).
- Trees take carbon from CO2 to build wood: common knowledge (photosynthesis).
- Callback "He has frozen on Gondwana before": episode 003 (series bible). No "fungi couldn't digest wood" claim used.

## 9. The Marinoan Snowball
- About 650/645-635 Ma (dial "640"); ended about 635 Ma: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snowball_Earth (search snippet);
  PNAS 2025 "Four-million-year Marinoan snowball" https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2418281122 (search snippet).
- Before the first big animals: Ediacaran biota appear after about 575 Ma; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ediacaran_biota
- Glacial sediments deposited within about 10 degrees of the equator (paleomagnetism); Kirschvink coined "Snowball Earth"
  in 1992: Kirschvink 1992 https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Late-Proterozoic-low-latitude-global-glaciation:-Kirschvink/20ec3d2322e63638d56ccb73ab50255c5ffd55e4 ;
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snowball_Earth (search snippets).
- Singapore at about 1°N: common geography.
- Ice-albedo feedback; Snowball vs Slushball debate; weathering shuts down under ice: Hoffman & Schrag 2002, *Terra Nova*
  14:129 https://www.researchgate.net/publication/227536827 ; Scientific American, Hoffman & Schrag "Snowball Earth"
  https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/snowball-earth/ (search snippets); Dartmouth DUJS
  https://sites.dartmouth.edu/dujs/2010/05/30/oceans-of-ice-the-snowball-earth-theory-of-global-glaciation/
- Ended when volcanic outgassing raised CO2 to about 350 times the modern level; extreme greenhouse; cap carbonates
  precipitated on top of the glacial deposits worldwide: Hoffman et al. 1998, *Science* 281:1342
  https://courses.seas.harvard.edu/climate/eli/Courses/EPS281r/Sources/Snowball/more/Hoffman-et-al-1998.pdf (search snippet of abstract).
- Built up over millions of years: Hoffman & Schrag 2002 (above).

## 10. The Sturtian Snowball
- About 717 to 660 Ma, about 57 million years: Rooney et al. 2015, *Geology* 43:459 (search snippet via
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sturtian_glaciation ); Harvard SEAS (verified: "roughly 717 to 660 million years ago",
  lasted "56 million years"; weathering of Franklin Large Igneous Province basalt drew down CO2; oscillation between
  snowball and hothouse intervals) https://seas.harvard.edu/news/new-explanation-snowball-earth
  The script says "about fifty-seven million years" (717 - 660) and "may not have been one long freeze".
- Franklin LIP is in northern Canada: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franklin_Large_Igneous_Province
- Dinosaurs died out 66 Ma: Natural History Museum https://www.nhm.ac.uk/discover/what-killed-the-dinosaurs.html (common knowledge).
- Average global temperatures of minus 50°C on Snowball Earth; possible refuges: open ocean patches, deep-sea hydrothermal
  vents, under ice sheets, meltwater ponds on equatorial ice where dark dust enhanced melting; 2025 study found eukaryotic
  life in every Antarctic meltwater pond sampled: MIT EAPS (verified)
  https://eaps.mit.edu/news-impact/when-earth-iced-over-early-life-may-have-sheltered-in-meltwater-ponds ;
  ScienceDaily June 2025 https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2025/06/250619090850.htm (microalgae in ponds).
- Home freezer about minus 18°C, so minus 50 is about 30 degrees colder (32): common knowledge/arithmetic.

## 11. The First Snowball
- 2.4 billion years ago is more than half of Earth's 4.54-billion-year history (2.4/4.54 = 53%):
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Age_of_Earth (4.54 ± 0.05 Ga); USGS https://pubs.usgs.gov/gip/geotime/geotime.html
- One-day analogy: (4.54 - 2.4) / 4.54 x 24 h = 11.3 h, "a little after eleven in the morning". Arithmetic.
- No plants, no animals, little free oxygen before the GOE: common knowledge; Wikipedia Huronian (below).
- Methane a strong greenhouse gas; oxygen from cyanobacteria (oxygenic photosynthesis, splitting water) built up in the
  Great Oxidation Event and oxidised methane into CO2 and water, weaker greenhouse gases; cooling triggered the Huronian
  glaciations: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huronian_glaciation (verified); Kopp et al. 2005, *PNAS* 102:11131
  https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.0504878102 (search snippet: "the evolution of cyanobacteria destroyed a methane
  greenhouse and thereby directly and rapidly triggered a planetary-scale glaciation"; "on timescales as short as 1 million
  years"; authors at Caltech incl. Kirschvink).
- Huronian glaciations about 2.4 to 2.1 Ga, about 300 million years, at least three ice ages; Gowganda Formation near Lake
  Huron, Ontario; debate whether global (low-latitude palaeomagnetic evidence vs discontinuous diamictites indicating
  temperate spells); GOE devastating for anaerobic organisms: Wikipedia Huronian glaciation (verified, above).
- GOE and Paleoproterozoic snowball ordering: Warke et al. 2020, *PNAS* https://www.pnas.org/doi/abs/10.1073/pnas.2003090117 (search result title).
- Chloroplasts of algae and plants descend from a cyanobacterial endosymbiont: Ponce-Toledo / PMC review "Are Cyanobacteria
  an Ancestor of Chloroplasts..." https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8227023/ (search snippet: "Chloroplasts of plants
  and algae are currently believed to originate from a cyanobacterial endosymbiont").
