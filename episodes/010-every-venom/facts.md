# Facts: 010 How Every Deadly Venom Would Kill Doug

Every factual claim in script.md is listed here with its source. Fiction and framing are not claims: Doug, his deaths,
the field guide, the cap, speech bubbles, the costume, the mouthwash, the fly swatter, the UV torch and boot, the grey paint
and "NOT A MOUSE" sign, "twenty-five minutes later" at the Irukandji (a Doug beat), the narrator's asides ("which tells you
who the weapon is really meant for", "most predators take the hint", "the narrator had already written the death",
"has earned every bit of that reputation", "closes the distance by itself"). Everyday comparisons are marked "common
knowledge" and checked by arithmetic.

Checked 2026-10-09 (draft 1; updated for draft 2 after the screener review). "Verified" = I read the claim on the page (WebFetch). "Search snippet" = the page blocked
WebFetch (403/cookie wall) and the claim was confirmed through search-result text quoting that page; the screener should
re-open these if possible.

**Medical-advice check:** the script has no first aid, treatment steps, "what to do" lines or dose guidance. Antivenom
appears only as history (funnel-web 1981, mamba "before antivenom existed") or as a flat fact ("there is no antivenom",
octopus). The blue-ringed octopus "enough venom to paralyze ten adults" is a potency estimate, not guidance.

## Flagged items from the brief (status)
- **Platypus morphine:** supported by the Australian Platypus Conservancy ("not relieved by standard analgesics such as
  morphine") and by the 1992 MJA case (Fenner, Williamson & Myers, "Platypus envenomation: a painful learning experience",
  Med J Aust 1992, PMID 1454022), confirmed via search snippets. Draft 2 softens the line to "morphine gave him little relief" (screener advisory: one secondary account says repeated
  doses eventually made the pain tolerable). No dose numbers are spoken.
- **Hornet deaths in Japan:** the 191 figure is "wasps and bees" (the ministry category), not hornet-only. The script says
  "wasp and bee stings". The allergy line is attributed to "an earlier Japanese review" (1979-1998 data).
- **Hornet alarm pheromone:** the 2003 Nature paper shows the alarm pheromone is in the venom. The "swatting can release it"
  line is from nippon.com. The 1995 marking-pheromone paper is NOT used (that pheromone comes from a different gland).
- **Komodo human deaths:** "four people killed in the thirty-five years up to 2009" (NBC/AP, May 2009). No names, ages or
  details. Not all Komodo deaths are proven to be venom-driven; the script never says the venom killed those four.
- **Komodo debate:** the brief's Weinstein 2012 lead was not found. The script uses Goldstein et al. 2013 (16 captive dragons,
  no virulent bacteria) and their caveat that oral microbes are not fully ruled out.
- **Indian red scorpion "most dangerous":** worded as "described as one of the deadliest scorpions in the world" (Frontiers
  review abstract). The "most lethal" superlative was not used.
- **Saw-scaled viper:** "probably bites and kills more people than any other species of snake" is the 1970s Warrell et al.
  wording (hedged "probably" kept). Attributed to "a team of snakebite doctors" studying patients in Nigeria.
- **Black mamba "almost always fatal" before antivenom:** National Geographic states it; Wikipedia notes the figure rests on
  old, limited data. The script keeps the hedge "was considered". Speed: NatGeo's 12.5 mph = about 20 km/h, "in short
  bursts" per Live Science/NatGeo. No horse myth.
- **Funnel-web 15 minutes:** attributed to a museum arachnologist (Dr Robert Raven, Queensland Museum, quoted by Australian
  Geographic). No victim age. Placed "before" the antivenom (all deaths predate 1981).
- **Inland taipan "no confirmed deaths":** Guinness World Records ("no confirmed deaths on record") plus the Australian Museum
  ("only a handful of people have ever been bitten... all survived"). The "one bite kills 100 men" myth is not used.
- **Irukandji "impending doom":** ALiEM (emergency-medicine education site) and Australian Geographic (quoting researcher
  Lisa-Ann Gershwin). StatPearls (NBK562264) also lists it per search snippet. The two 2002 deaths: Fenner & Hadok, MJA 2002.
- **Box jellyfish "most venomous":** attributed to NOAA ("the most venomous marine animal"). Time to death "within a few
  minutes": NOAA. 63 deaths since 1884: Fenner & Williamson 1996, MJA.
- **Stonefish, cone snail item, man o' war, lancehead, jewel wasp:** not in the script. The cone snail appears only as the
  allowed one-line 001 callback.
