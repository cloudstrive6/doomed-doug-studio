## 2. Escalation axis and items
**Axis: era, run backwards. 125,000 years ago to about 4.4 billion years ago.** Every stop is older than the last. Each band takes away
one more thing the hippo needs: first other hippos, then grass, then plants, then air. Most extreme last.

**Episode mechanic.** The time machine (`time_machine.json`) carries Doug and one adult common hippo. The first trip makes the
running joke: "The hippo does not fit in the time machine. Nobody has explained how it gets in. It gets in." After that, don't explain it.
At each stop the narrator gives the hippo a verdict:
- **The verdict rule (important for honesty):** a verdict is the narrator's reasoned call from sourced facts (the predator's size or bite
  compared with an adult hippo's, the food or the air). It is always worded as a judgement ("probably", "on size alone", "the hippo would
  likely..."), never as a study's result. The script must say once, early: "Nobody has run this experiment. These are educated guesses
  from the fossils."
- **Episode-only device, proposed: the HIPPO SURVIVED tally** beside the death counter, a crude chalkboard "HIPPO SURVIVED: n / 12"
  with a tick or a cross per era. It only moves on survivals. The art director may cut it if the screen is busy; the death counter
  always stays.
- **The hippo's results:** survives 1, 2, 4, 5, 8 and 9 (6 / 12). Loses 3, 6, 7, 10, 11 and 12. **A hippo loss is never shown as a
  death.** Show a cut to black, a stamp, or the time machine door shutting fast with hippo ears visible. No X-eyed hippo, no carcass, no
  predator eating it. Doug keeps the cartoon deaths, and the hippo is played as a deadpan heavyweight.

Spoken section headers (two-word style):
- **"Hippo country."** (opener, items 1-2)
- **"No hippos."** (before item 3, about 15%)
- **"No grass."** (before item 7, about 55%)
- **"No plants."** (before item 10, about 78%)

### Hippo country.

**1. Ice Age London** (about 125,000 years ago, the last interglacial; hook item; death 187; hippo SURVIVES)
- Facts:
  - Hippo bones were dug up under Trafalgar Square (excavations in the late 1950s) along with lions, straight-tusked elephants and
    rhinos, from the last interglacial (Natural History Museum London; verify the dates and the species list).
  - Hippos ranged as far north as northern England then (Kirkdale Cave, Yorkshire: a hyena den with hippo bones, Buckland 1822; verify).
  - The lions were cave lions (*Panthera spelaea*) or a close relative. Use NHM's wording. One clause, no Sparta.
- Analogy idea: today Trafalgar Square has four bronze lion statues. 125,000 years ago, the lions were real.
- Twist (must land by 0:45): **the first stop isn't Africa. It's London, and the hippo is already there.**
- Doug beat: Doug steps out with a tourist map and the hippo, ready to explain where they are. A herd of hippos is already in the river.
  A real lion steps out of the reeds where a statue will one day stand. Death 187. Cap on the riverbank. Hippo verdict: survives ("It was
  home.").

**2. The Giant Croc of Turkana** (about 2-4 million years ago, Kenya; Doug dies; death 188; hippo SURVIVES)
- Facts:
  - *Crocodylus thorbjarnarsoni*, the largest true crocodile known, perhaps up to about 7.5 m (Brochu & Storrs 2012, *Journal of
    Vertebrate Paleontology*; verify the length estimate and the age range).
  - It lived alongside early humans' relatives, and the paper suggested hominins could have been prey (verify the exact wording).
  - Hippos were common in the same lake basin (hippopotamid fossils at Turkana; verify the genus).
- Analogy idea: 7.5 m against a sourced everyday length (a city bus is about 12 m, so more than half a bus).
- Twist: **the giant crocodile's menu probably included our ancestors. The hippo was not the one in danger.**
- Doug beat: Doug paddles the hippo across the lake like a raft, waving at the early humans on the bank ("cousins?"). One log is not a
  log. 011 banned the log-croc gag as a killer. **Here the croc must be a visible 7 m head surfacing**, not a log reveal. Death 188.
  Hippo verdict: survives (adult hippos sized out of most crocodile attacks; one hedge word, source needed).

### No hippos.

**3. Purussaurus** (about 8 million years ago, South America; death 189; hippo LOSES)
- Facts:
  - *Purussaurus brasiliensis*, a giant caiman, estimated 10-12.5 m and about 8.4 tonnes (Aureliano et al. 2015, *PLoS ONE*; verify).
  - Its estimated bite force is about 69,000 newtons, among the highest of any animal (same paper; it is an estimate, so say so).
  - South America never had hippos. Say in one line that this is a thought experiment.
- Analogy idea: 8.4 tonnes against one sourced everyday weight (for example a loaded garbage truck). The hippo is a fraction of it;

- Twist: **for the first time, the hippo is the small one.**
- Doug beat: Doug hands the hippo a "BIGGEST ANIMAL HERE" badge. Purussaurus surfaces. Cut to black. Doug tries to take the badge back.
  Death 189. The badge floats next to the cap. Hippo verdict: probably not.

**4. The Walking Whale** (about 48-50 million years ago, Pakistan; death 190; hippo SURVIVES; the one 013 callback)
- Facts:
  - *Ambulocetus natans*, the "walking swimming whale", a crocodile-like ambush predator in coastal waters, about 3 m (Thewissen et al.
    1994, *Science*; verify).
  - Whales and hippos share a common ancestor, and hippos are the whales' closest living relatives (Gatesy; Boisserie et al. 2005,
    *PNAS*; verify the current wording).
  - Earlier relatives such as *Pakicetus* were land animals (Thewissen et al. 2001, *Nature*; one line).
- Analogy idea: none needed. The family-tree card does the work. One sourced number (length).
- Twist: **the hippo goes back to meet its relatives, and the relatives are walking into the river to eat things.**
- Doug beat: the 013 callback line (new wording; something like "Last time, Doug learned that the hippo's closest relatives are
  whales. Today he is meeting one, and it has legs."). Doug holds up a family photo ("cousin?") to an Ambulocetus in the shallows.
  Death 190. Hippo verdict: survives, because a 3 m whale cousin can't take on a hippo (on size alone).

**5. Titanoboa** (about 58-60 million years ago, Colombia; Doug SURVIVES; "Doug is fine."; hippo SURVIVES)
- Facts:
  - *Titanoboa cerrejonensis*, about 13 m and around a tonne, from the Cerrejón coal mine (Head et al. 2009, *Nature*; verify).
  - The same paper used its size to estimate tropical temperatures, about 30-34 °C (verify; say "estimated").
  - Its diet was probably mostly fish, judging by the Cerrejón fauna and its skull (verify the source; if unsupported, say "may have").
  - Six million years after the dinosaurs, a hippo would be one of the largest mammals alive by a huge margin (verify with a source on
    Paleocene mammal sizes, or cut).
- Analogy idea: 13 m against a sourced everyday length (longer than a city bus).
- Twist: **the largest snake that ever lived probably ate fish. The hippo and Doug are not fish.**
- Doug beat: Doug, frozen, as thirteen metres of snake slide past him and the hippo and take a fish. "Doug is fine." The narrator
  tells us how long the snake was again, slowly. No death; the counter stays at 190.

**7. The Morrison** (about 150 million years ago, Late Jurassic, western USA; Doug SURVIVES; narrator quietly disappointed; hippo

**8. The Gorgon** (about 255-252 million years ago, Late Permian; death 192; hippo SURVIVES)
- Facts:
  - *Inostrancevia*, the largest gorgonopsian, about 3.5 m long with saber teeth over 12 cm (verify). It was recently found in South
    Africa as well as Russia (Kammerer et al. 2023, *Current Biology*; verify).
  - Gorgonopsians were synapsids, the group that later led to mammals, so in a sense they are the hippo's distant relatives too (verify

  - Its estimated mass is far below an adult hippo's (verify a published mass estimate; if none, compare only the length and say so).

**9. Hyneria** (about 365 million years ago, Late Devonian, Red Hill, Pennsylvania; death 193; hippo SURVIVES)

    Sciences / Daeschler).
  - The same site has one of the earliest four-limbed animals, *Hynerpeton* (Daeschler et al. 1994, *Science*; verify).
  - The first forests had appeared by then (*Archaeopteris*; verify). There was still no grass, flowers or seeds as the hippo knows them.
- Analogy idea: the fish's length against a sourced everyday length (a small car).
- Twist: **the hippo's own lineage is crawling out of the river, and the thing in the river is waiting for it.** (The first

### No plants.

**10. The Bare Land** (about 500-510 million years ago, Cambrian; Doug SURVIVES; "Doug is fine."; hippo LOSES)
- Facts:
  - No plants on land. The oldest land-plant spores are from about 470 million years ago, in the Ordovician (verify).
  - The land had microbial crusts on the ground. The first animal trails on land are tidal-flat tracks such as *Climactichnites*
    (slug-like) and *Protichnites* (arthropods) (MacNaughton et al. 2002, *Geology*; Getty & Hagadorn; verify the ages).
  - Nothing on land could hurt Doug.
- Analogy idea: the gap between these tracks and the first land plants (tens of millions of years) against a sourced span, e.g.
  longer than the time since the dinosaurs died (arithmetic from sourced figures).
- Twist: **nothing on land can kill the hippo, and nothing on land can feed it either.**
- Doug beat: Doug befriends the slug that leaves *Climactichnites* tracks ("hi friend?"). It is the only other thing on the beach.
  "Doug is fine." The narrator is disappointed. The hippo stares at bare rock, then walks back into the time machine on its own.
  Hippo verdict: no (it would starve; say it, don't show it). Counter stays at 193.

### No air.

**11. The Archean** (about 3 billion years ago; death 194; hippo LOSES)
- Facts:
  - The air had almost no oxygen, under a millionth of today's level, from sulfur-isotope evidence (Farquhar et al. 2000, *Science*;
    Pavlov & Kasting 2002; verify the wording). Don't call it the Great Oxidation Event (007).
  - Stromatolites, built by microbial mats, are among the oldest evidence of life (verify an age, e.g. the 3.48-billion-year-old
    Dresser Formation; `stromatolites.json` exists).
  - Some of these microbes would later fill the air with oxygen (one line, no GOE detail).
- Analogy idea: the oxygen level against today's 21% (the arithmetic is sourced).
- Twist: **the only things alive are the microbes that will one day make the air the hippo needs, and they are about a billion years
  from finished.** (Verify the timing wording.)
- Doug beat: Doug steps out with a snorkel for the hippo and one for himself. The narrator explains it is not that kind of problem.
  Death 194 (Doug holds his breath with cheeks puffed, then floats up as a ghost). The hippo's ears go back into the time machine.
  Hippo verdict: no.

**12. The Hadean** (the boss; about 4.4 billion years ago; death 195; hippo LOSES)
- Facts:
  - Zircon crystals from the Jack Hills, Western Australia, about 4.4 billion years old, are the oldest known pieces of Earth. Their
    chemistry suggests liquid water at the surface very early (Wilde et al. 2001, *Nature*; Valley et al. 2014, *Nature Geoscience*;
    verify).
  - The Moon formed from a giant impact roughly 4.5 billion years ago and was much closer to Earth than now (verify; say "roughly").
  - Heavy impacts continued. The "Late Heavy Bombardment" is debated, so say "debated" or leave it out.
  - No free oxygen, no life known for certain, no continents as we know them (verify each).
- Analogy idea: the zircon crystal against a sourced everyday object (a grain of sand); 4.4 billion years against a sourced span
  (about a third of the age of the universe; arithmetic from sourced figures).
- Twist: **the hippo finally finds the one thing it needs, water, and there is nothing else on the planet at all.**
- Doug beat: Doug, in a heat-shimmer, plants a tiny flag on black rock under a huge Moon. The hippo stands in a steaming lake looking
  satisfied for one second. A meteor streak. Cut to white. Death 195. Hippo verdict: no.
- **Closing image:** back in the present, the Trafalgar Square fountain. The hippo stands in it, between the bronze lions, with the cap
  on its head. The HIPPO SURVIVED tally reads 6 / 12. Kicker (one line, roughly): "The hippo survived six eras. Doug survived three. The
  cap survived all twelve."
- **Outro:** 30 words or fewer, final counter (195) and one dry line. No recap, no subscribe pitch.

## 7. Risks
- **Kid-appeal (HIGH, the main risk of this episode):** hippo, T. rex, a time machine and "would X beat Y" is exactly the shape of
  kids' dinosaur content, and "prehistoric animals" autocomplete has toy and kids results. Countermeasures:
  - the dry adult narrator, with adult-coded jokes ("Doug has rarely been anyone's first choice")
  - the hippo drawn heavy and menacing, never round-eyed or smiling
  - no "dino", no toy or game references, no "who would win" playground framing in the title or the script
  - real paper citations on screen as evidence cards
  - metadata never targets kids (titler: no kids' tags; made_for_kids false).
- **Gore (MEDIUM):** predators and a hippo as prey. No blood, no wounds, no feeding, no carcass, no hippo death shown at all (see the
  verdict rule). Doug's deaths are cut to black, the cap and the ghost float. Bite forces are numbers, never injuries.
- **Over-claiming (HIGH):** every verdict is a judgement, not a finding (see 2). Hedge every size and bite estimate as "estimated". The
  Purussaurus thought experiment must say South America had no hippos. Hominins as croc prey must use the paper's own hedge. The
  Titanoboa diet uses "probably" or "may have". Oxygen levels use the source's bound, not a made-up figure.
- **Facts that need strong sources (cut if unsourced):**
  - Ice Age London: the Trafalgar Square species list and date, Kirkdale.
  - Turkana: *C. thorbjarnarsoni* size and age, the hominin-prey suggestion, Turkana hippopotamids.
  - Purussaurus: Aureliano 2015 length, mass and bite.
  - Walking Whale: Thewissen 1994 and 2001, the hippo-whale relationship.
  - Titanoboa: Head 2009 length, mass and temperature; its diet; Paleocene mammal sizes.
  - Grass: Prasad 2005, when grasslands spread.
  - T. rex: Gignac & Erickson 2017 bite; Hutchinson 2011 mass.
  - The Morrison: the flora; *Allosaurus* size; a sauropod footprint size.
  - The Gorgon: *Inostrancevia* size, teeth and mass; Kammerer 2023.
  - Hyneria: size; *Hynerpeton*; *Archaeopteris*.
  - The Bare Land: the earliest land-plant spores; *Climactichnites* / *Protichnites* ages.
