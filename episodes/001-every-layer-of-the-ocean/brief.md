## 2. Escalation axis: depth (surface to Challenger Deep)
There are 12 items, with spoken zone headers ("Sunlight zone.", "Twilight zone.", "Midnight zone.", "The abyss.",
"Hadal zone."). The background gets darker at each header (art bible ramp). The deepest item is last.
Depths are typical ranges that the writer must confirm; if a source contradicts the order, reorder rather than bend the fact.

### Sunlight zone (0 to 200 m)
**1. Portuguese Man o' War** (surface, the hook item)
- Research: it is a siphonophore, a colony of specialised zooids rather than a single animal. Tentacles average about
  10 m and can reach about 30 m. Detached tentacles and stranded animals can still sting. It sails on a gas-filled float.
- Scale analogy: tentacles longer than a city bus, hanging under something the size of a sandwich bag.
- Doug beat: Doug waves at "the floating purple bag" and tries to pop it. Death 1. "Doug did not agree to this" lands here.
- Twist beat (by 0:45): "it isn't one animal".

**2. Geography Cone Snail** (shallow Indo-Pacific reefs)
- Research: its harpoon-like modified tooth; the venom cocktail; Conus geographus releasing weaponised insulin to slow
  fish (Safavi-Hemami et al. 2015, PNAS); documented human fatalities (a count with a source, or cut it).
- Scale analogy: it is the size of a TV remote and has a nickname about how long you have left (only if sourced).
- Doug beat: Doug picks up the pretty shell to take home as a souvenir. Death 2.
- **No first-aid or "what to do" content.**

**3. Bobbit Worm** (reef sand, shallow)
- Research: Eunice aphroditois hides buried in sediment with only its antennae showing; its snap strike can cut fish;
  it can reach up to about 3 m long; the 2009 case of one found living inside an aquarium in Newquay, UK (source it).
- Scale analogy: a worm longer than a sofa, living in the floor.
- Doug beat: near-miss. It takes the cap, and Doug has to go in after it (the cap survives, as always).

### Twilight zone (200 to 1,000 m)
**4. Humboldt Squid**
- Research: the "red devil" nickname; fast colour flashing (chromatophores); it hunts in groups and moves up at night
  (diel vertical migration); size about 1.5 m and up to about 50 kg. Aggression toward divers is disputed, so only say
  what a source says.
- Scale analogy: a squid as heavy as a fully packed suitcase that also changes colour like a broken traffic light.
- Doug beat: Doug turns on a flashlight. Everything in the area notices. Death 3.

**5. Giant Squid**
- Research: the largest eyes in the animal kingdom (about 27 cm, a figure to confirm); first filmed alive in its deep
  habitat in 2012 off the Ogasawara Islands at about 630 m; sperm whales as its predator (sucker scars on whales).
- Scale analogy: eyes the size of a basketball.
- Doug beat: no death. It stares at Doug for a long time and leaves. Doug is more upset by that than by dying.

**6. Barreleye Fish**
- Research: Macropinna microstoma; a transparent fluid-filled head shield; tubular eyes that rotate upward and forward
  (Robison & Reisenbichler 2008, MBARI); the green "eyes" on its front are actually its nostrils.
- Scale analogy: a fish with a head like a see-through phone case.
- Doug beat: Doug and the barreleye stare at each other through its forehead. Nobody wins.

### Midnight zone (1,000 to 4,000 m)
**7. Vampire Squid** (a deliberate false-alarm item at the midpoint zone shift)
- Research: the "vampire squid from hell" name (Vampyroteuthis infernalis); it lives in the oxygen minimum zone; it eats
  marine snow, not prey (Hoving & Robison 2012); it defends itself with a cloud of glowing mucus instead of ink.
- Scale analogy: the scariest name in the ocean on something the size of a football.
- Doug beat: Doug braces for death and instead gets glowing snot thrown at him. No death. "Doug is fine." (He is sticky.)
- Confirm its depth range. If it sits mainly above 1,000 m, keep it as the last twilight item and move the
  "Midnight zone." header to item 8.

**8. Anglerfish** (deep-sea ceratioids)
- Research: the bioluminescent lure is powered by symbiotic bacteria; in some species the male fuses permanently to the
  female and shares her bloodstream (sexual parasitism, Pietsch 2005). Tell it clinically, with no sex jokes.
- Scale analogy: a lure glowing like a phone screen in a dark bedroom.
- Doug beat: **befriend-the-thing gag.** Doug swims toward the "nice light". Death 4.

**9. Black Swallower**
- Research: Chiasmodon niger has an expandable stomach and swallows prey larger than itself; a 2007 specimen found
  floating dead off Grand Cayman had a much longer snake mackerel inside it. Keep the decomposition-gas explanation to
  one cartoon-level sentence.
- Scale analogy: like swallowing a surfboard whole when you are the size of a banana.
- Doug beat: Doug assumes the small fish is harmless. Swallowed whole. Death 5. Only the cap floats up.

### The abyss (4,000 to 6,000 m)
**10. Zombie Worms**
- Research: Osedax on whale falls; they have no mouth or gut and use root-like tissue plus bacteria to get nutrients
  from bone; the microscopic males live inside the female (Rouse et al. 2004, Science, found at about 2,900 m in
  Monterey Canyon). **Confirm the deepest Osedax or whale-fall record.** If none is below 4,000 m, place this under
  "Midnight zone." and open "The abyss." with the next item instead.
- Scale analogy: a whale skeleton the length of a school bus, carpeted with worms like a pink shag rug.
- Doug beat: Doug lies down on the whale for a rest in 2 to 4 °C water (confirm the temperature) and becomes a
  cartoon ice-cube Doug. Death 7. The worms consider him and go back to the whale. The bones on screen are only ever the
  whale's, never Doug's. This death keeps the title honest, because every layer has a death.

### Hadal zone (6,000 to 11,000 m)
**11. Hadal Snailfish**
- Research: the deepest fish ever filmed was a Pseudoliparis at about 8,336 m in the Izu-Ogasawara Trench (2022, announced
  2023 by UWA/Minderoo). Its gelatinous body works without a swim bladder. There is a predicted physiological depth limit
  for fish around 8,200 to 8,400 m (the TMAO hypothesis, Yancey et al. 2014).
- Scale analogy: pressure like a small car parked on your thumbnail (check the number).
- Twist: the scariest thing at 8 km is a cute pink tadpole-looking fish, and it is thriving.
- Doug beat: Doug without a sub gets a cartoon pancake flatten (flat outline plus X eyes; no injuries). Death 6.

**12. Challenger Deep** (the boss)
- Research: the deepest known point, about 10,900 m (cite the survey used; published figures vary around
  10,902 to 10,935 m); pressure over 1,000 times surface pressure; the Trieste dive in 1960, Cameron's 2012 solo dive,
  and Vescovo's 2019 dives; a plastic bag recorded at about 10,898 m (Chiba et al. 2018, Marine Policy); amphipods
  from the trenches carrying high PCB levels (Jamieson et al. 2017, Nature Ecology & Evolution).
- Scale analogy: Everest dropped in, with its peak still about 2 km underwater.
- Twist: Doug finally reaches the bottom of the ocean in a sub, alive, and the first thing he finds is our trash.
- Kicker idea: humans got here first, and so did a plastic bag. Doug survives the boss, which is the one time the narrator is disappointed.

## 4. Running gags (this is the pilot, so establish them)
- "Doug did not agree to this." Use it once, in the cold open or at the first danger.
- **Death counter**: an on-screen "DOUG DEATHS: N" that starts at **0** in this episode (the channel total starts here).
  Plan 7 deaths, at least one per zone (the writer may choose 6 to 9). It ticks on each death.

- Doug befriending the thing that kills him (anglerfish, man o' war).

- **Outro (30 words or fewer):** the final death count on screen plus one CTA asking viewers to comment where Doug
  should go next. No recap and no "thanks for watching".

## 7. Risks
- **Gore:** cartoon only. Deaths are X eyes, a ghost Doug, a floating cap and a pancake flatten. Never describe stings,
  venom effects, drowning, decomposition or pressure injuries in bodily detail. The black swallower and Osedax
  are the highest-risk items, so keep each to one clinical sentence.
- **Medical/safety advice:** none. No sting treatment (vinegar and so on), no "what to do if". Venom facts stay descriptive.
- **Kid-appeal:** the pilot sets the channel's tone. Deadpan, adult-coded humour, no cutesy voice and no nursery
  words. The anglerfish mating fact is told as biology, with no innuendo (advertiser-friendly).
- **Facts that need strong sources** (cut the claim if it can't be sourced): man o' war tentacle lengths; cone snail
  human deaths; bobbit worm maximum length; Humboldt squid aggression toward humans (often exaggerated); giant squid eye
  size; vampire squid depth range; the deepest Osedax record (it decides the zone placement); snailfish record depth and date;
  Challenger Deep depth and the source used; the plastic bag and PCB studies.
- **Title honesty:** the title promises a Doug death in every layer. Each of the 5 zones (sunlight, twilight, midnight,
  abyss, hadal) must contain at least one on-screen death. The boss survival is fine because the hadal death is the snailfish.
  Check this at script approval.
