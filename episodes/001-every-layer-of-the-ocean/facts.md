# Facts: 001 Every Layer of the Ocean

Every factual claim in script.md is listed here with its source. Derived numbers show the calculation. Doug's deaths and
reactions are fiction and need no source. Everyday-object sizes in the analogies (a city bus about 12 m, a sofa about 2 m,
a basketball about 24 cm, a banana about 18 to 20 cm) are rough common-knowledge comparisons.

## Zones (headers)
- Sunlight 0 to 200 m, twilight 200 to 1,000 m, midnight 1,000 to 4,000 m, no sunlight below 1,000 m; hadal 6,000 m and deeper.
  https://www.whoi.edu/ocean-learning-hub/ocean-topics/how-the-ocean-works/ocean-zones/ ;
  https://oceanexplorer.noaa.gov/ocean-fact/light-distributed/
- Abyss 4,000 to 6,000 m. https://oceana.org/blog/animals-of-the-ocean-depths/ (WHOI gives 3,000 to 6,500 m; both include the 4,204 m Osedax record)
- Hadal zone is mostly ocean trenches. https://www.whoi.edu/ocean-learning-hub/ocean-topics/how-the-ocean-works/ocean-zones/

## 1. Portuguese Man o' War
- Gas-filled float, up to about six inches (15 cm) above the waterline; blue, violet or pink. https://oceanservice.noaa.gov/facts/portuguese-man-o-war.html
- No propulsion; moved by winds and currents; legions of 1,000 or more. Same NOAA page.
- Tentacles average 10 m, can reach 30 m. Same NOAA page.
- Stinging nematocysts: capsules with coiled, barbed tubes delivering venom. Same NOAA page.
- A siphonophore: a colony of genetically identical zooids (clones), specialised parts for floating, prey capture, feeding and reproduction. Same NOAA page; https://www.nationalgeographic.com/animals/invertebrates/facts/portuguese-man-of-war
- "None could survive on its own": zooids are not capable of independent survival. https://www.nationalgeographic.com/animals/invertebrates/facts/portuguese-man-of-war
- Stranded animals can still sting weeks after washing ashore. https://oceanservice.noaa.gov/facts/portuguese-man-o-war.html
- Analogy: 30 m is longer than two 12 m city buses.

## 2. Geography Cone Snail
- Conus geographus; shallow tropical Indo-Pacific reefs, sand pockets and coral rubble, 0 to 200 m. https://www.aquariumofpacific.org/onlinelearningcenter/species/geographic_cone_snail ; https://animaldiversity.org/accounts/Conus_geographus/
- Shell 7 to 15 cm (TV remote analogy). Same two sources.
- Hollow, modified radular tooth used as a harpoon, housed in the proboscis. https://animaldiversity.org/accounts/Conus_geographus/
- Stretchy mouth-like part aimed "like a gun barrel" at fish. https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2015/01/150119154316.htm
- Releases venom insulin into the water; fish-hunting species use a fish-type insulin; causes hypoglycaemic shock, fish become disoriented and stop moving (Safavi-Hemami et al. 2015, PNAS). https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2015/01/150119154316.htm ; https://www.nationalgeographic.com/science/article/150119-cone-snail-venom-insulin-fish-animal-ocean-science
- "Nirvana cabal" toxin nickname; false mouth thrown over stupefied fish like a cape. https://www.nationalgeographic.com/science/article/150119-cone-snail-venom-insulin-fish-animal-ocean-science
- Very few animals weaponise insulin (researcher: none known except possibly humans; found in C. geographus and C. tulipa). Same NatGeo page; https://elifesciences.org/articles/41574
- Hundreds of cone snail species (about 500 known in older counts). https://www.nationalgeographic.com/animals/invertebrates/facts/geography-cone
- C. geographus accounts for almost all known human cone snail fatalities (Kohn 2016). https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27285461/

## 3. Bobbit Worm
- Eunice aphroditois; buried in sediment, five antennae act as tripwires, scissor-like jaws, strike can slice prey in two. https://www.sciencefocus.com/nature/what-is-a-bobbit-worm
- Drags prey into the burrow; ambush predator in shallow warm waters. https://link.springer.com/10.1038/s41598-020-79311-0 (Pan et al. 2021, Scientific Reports)
- Up to about 3 m long. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eunice_aphroditois ; https://animals.howstuffworks.com/marine-life/bobbit-worm.htm
- Arrive in aquariums hidden in live rock. https://animals.howstuffworks.com/marine-life/bobbit-worm.htm
- "Barry", 1.2 m, Blue Reef Aquarium, Newquay, Cornwall; fish injured or disappearing, coral sliced; found when the display was dismantled. https://www.sciencefocus.com/nature/what-is-a-bobbit-worm ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eunice_aphroditois (year differs between sources, 2008 vs 2009, so the script gives none)

## 4. Humboldt Squid
- "Diablo rojo" / red devil, from fishermen in Baja California; flashes red and white. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humboldt_squid
- Up to 1.5 m mantle length, up to 50 kg. https://animaldiversity.org/accounts/Dosidicus_gigas/ ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humboldt_squid
- Diel vertical migration toward the surface dusk to dawn, shown by electronic tagging. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humboldt_squid
- Chromatophores; whole-body red and white flashing at 2 to 4 Hz, likely intraspecific signalling. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humboldt_squid
- Brad Seibel (University of Rhode Island) among hundreds of squid: dive light made them flee; "surprised at how timid they were". https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2009/07/090723113516.htm
- Markaida: 533 stomachs, cannibalism in 26 percent, more common in larger squid. https://www.nwf.org/Magazines/National-Wildlife/2005/The-Curious-Case-of-the-Cannibal-Squid ; https://www.scielo.org.mx/scielo.php?script=sci_arttext&pid=S0188-88972014000100006
- Analogy: 50 kg vs a typical 23 kg airline checked-bag limit.

## 5. Giant Squid
- First filmed alive in its habitat July 2012, near the Ogasawara Islands, about 1,000 km south of Tokyo; team led by Tsunemi Kubodera (National Museum of Nature and Science); near-infrared lights; 1 m squid as bait; about 100 dives; seen at 630 m, followed to about 900 m; animal about 3 m. https://www.voanews.com/a/first_images_of_giant_squid_filmed_in_deep_ocean_reuters/1580116.html
- Eye about 27 cm with a 9 cm pupil; swordfish eye (9 cm) would fit in the pupil; largest eyes in the animal kingdom; used to detect sperm whales via bioluminescence they disturb (Nilsson et al. 2012, Current Biology). https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22425154/ ; https://www.nationalgeographic.com/animals/article/120315-giant-squid-eyes-basketball-whales-nilsson-science
- Sperm whales eat giant squid and bear sucker scars. https://ocean.si.edu/ocean-life/invertebrates/giant-squid-sucker-marks ; https://www.amnh.org/exhibitions/permanent/ocean-life/sperm-whale-and-giant-squid

## 6. Barreleye Fish
- Macropinna microstoma; 600 to 800 m, North Pacific; up to 15 cm; spots above the mouth are nostrils (nares); eyes are green orbs inside the head. https://www.mbari.org/animal/barreleye-fish/
- Transparent fluid-filled shield; tubular eyes with green lenses; described 1939; eyes thought fixed (tunnel vision); rotate upward and forward (Robison and Reisenbichler, MBARI, using ROVs); nets destroyed the shield. https://www.mbari.org/news/researchers-solve-mystery-of-deep-sea-fish-with-tubular-eyes-and-transparent-head/
- May take food from siphonophore tentacles. https://www.mbari.org/animal/barreleye-fish/ ; same MBARI news page
- Man o' war is a siphonophore. https://oceanservice.noaa.gov/facts/portuguese-man-o-war.html

## 7. Vampire Squid
- Vampyroteuthis infernalis, "vampire squid from hell"; neither vampire nor squid; last surviving member of an ancient cephalopod group; about 30 cm; 600 to 900 m in the oxygen minimum zone; dark reddish brown; turns webbed arms inside out; releases bioluminescent fluid. https://www.mbari.org/animal/vampire-squid/
- Name translation. https://www.montereybayaquarium.org/animals/animals-a-to-z/vampire-squid
- Low oxygen keeps most predators out. https://www.montereybayaquarium.org/animals/animals-a-to-z/vampire-squid
- Eats marine snow with two sticky filaments, forms mucus-bound food balls (Hoving and Robison 2012, Proc. R. Soc. B). https://www.researchgate.net/publication/231215191_Vampire_squid_Detritivores_in_the_oxygen_minimum_zone ; https://www.mbari.org/animal/vampire-squid/
- Cloud of glowing sticky mucus instead of ink. https://www.aquariumofpacific.org/onlinelearningcenter/species/vampire_squid
- Placement: 600 to 900 m sits in the twilight zone, so the "Midnight zone." header moved to the anglerfish (as the brief instructs).

## 8. Anglerfish
- Deep-sea (ceratioid) anglerfishes; bioluminescent esca lit by symbiotic bacteria. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ceratioidei ; https://oceanbites.org/how-the-anglerfish-gets-its-light/
- Only females have the lure; males a fraction of female size; in some species males fuse, bloodstreams connect, eyes lost, nutrition from female, male provides sperm; only deep-sea species. https://www.nhm.ac.uk/discover/bizarre-love-life-of-the-anglerfish.html
- Sexual parasitism helps where males and females rarely meet; immune tolerance studied like transplant rejection (Pietsch; Swann et al. 2020). https://www.futurity.org/deep-sea-anglerfishes-sexual-parasitism-2414982-2/ ; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8780861/
- Deep-sea ceratioids live mainly in the bathypelagic (midnight) zone. https://content.ucpress.edu/chapters/10866.ch01.pdf

## 9. Black Swallower
- Chiasmodon niger; about 25 cm; tropical and subtropical deep water; distensible stomach stretches until transparent; prey more than twice its length and 10 times its mass. https://www.australiangeographic.com.au/blogs/creatura-with-bec-crew/2013/11/black-swallower/
- 2007, Grand Cayman: 19 cm swallower found dead with an 86 cm snake mackerel inside (more than four times its length); too large to digest, decomposition gas floated it to the surface. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_swallower ; https://www.australiangeographic.com.au/blogs/creatura-with-bec-crew/2013/11/black-swallower/
- Analogy: twice body length for a 1.8 m person is about 3.6 m, canoe length.

## 10. Zombie Worms
- Whale falls feed deep-sea communities. https://oceanbites.org/the-backbone-of-an-ecosystem-bone-eating-zombie-worms-control-biodiversity-at-deep-sea-whale-falls/
- Osedax described 2004 (Rouse, Goffredi and Vrijenhoek, Science) from whale bones in Monterey Bay/Canyon; females consume bone via ramifying roots; dwarf males live in female tubes. https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.1098650 ; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15286372/
- No mouth, no gut; root bacteria digest bone proteins and lipids; dozens of males inside, not eating. https://www.mbari.org/news/a-motley-collection-of-boneworms/
- Secrete acid to dissolve bone (Tresguerres et al. 2013). https://scripps.ucsd.edu/news/bizarre-bone-worms-emit-acid-feast-whale-skeletons ; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23760644/
- Males microscopic. https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.1098650 (dwarf, paedomorphic males)
- Deepest Osedax record: whale carcass at 4,204 m, São Paulo Ridge, South Atlantic off Brazil (Fujiwara et al. 2019). https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6333729/ — confirms placement in the abyss.
- Abyssal water about 2 °C (NOAA measured 2.2 °C at 5,000 m). https://oceanexplorer.noaa.gov/ocean-fact/temp-vary/ ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abyssal_zone
- Pink worms: Osedax rubiplumus ("red plume"). https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.1098650

## 11. Hadal Snailfish
- Pressure distorts proteins; fish accumulate TMAO (trimethylamine N-oxide) with depth; osmotic limit extrapolated to about 8,200 m (Yancey et al. 2014, PNAS). https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1322003111 ; https://phys.org/news/2014-03-marine-fish-dont-deeper-blue.html
- Predicted limit 8,200 to 8,400 m; Pseudoliparis juvenile filmed at 8,336 m, Izu-Ogasawara Trench, baited camera, deepest fish recorded; no swim bladder, gelatinous body, flexible skeleton. https://www.nhm.ac.uk/discover/news/2023/april/deepest-ever-fish-filmed-depth-8336-metres.html
- Filmed 15 August 2022 (announced April 2023, UWA and Tokyo University of Marine Science and Technology). https://www.guinnessworldrecords.com/news/2023/4/snailfish-sink-to-new-lows-to-extend-their-record-as-the-worlds-deepest-fish-743278 ; https://www.npr.org/2023/04/05/1168051498/deepest-fish-japan-photograph
- Pressure: +1 atm per 10.06 m (NOAA) → 8,336 / 10.06 ≈ 829 atm, i.e. "more than eight hundred times". https://oceanservice.noaa.gov/facts/pressure.html
- Car analogy: 829 atm × 1.033 kg/cm² ≈ 856 kg per cm²; a thumbnail is roughly 1 to 1.5 cm², so about 0.9 to 1.3 tonnes, a small car.
- Pale, tadpole-like look: visual description from the published footage. https://www.cnn.com/2023/04/03/asia/deepest-fish-filmed-off-japan-scn-intl-hnk/index.html

## 12. Challenger Deep
- Deepest known point, southern end of the Mariana Trench, western Pacific; 10,935 m ±6 m from 2020 submersible transects (Greenaway et al. 2021). https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/33477 ; https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0967063721001813
- Everest 8,848.86 m (2020 survey) → 10,935 − 8,849 ≈ 2,086 m, "about two kilometers underwater". https://www.britannica.com/place/Mount-Everest ; https://kathmandupost.com/national/2020/12/08/it-s-official-mount-everest-is-8-848-86-metres-tall
- Pressure over 1,000 times sea level (108.6 MPa). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Challenger_Deep ; also 10,935 / 10.06 ≈ 1,087 atm via https://oceanservice.noaa.gov/facts/pressure.html
- Trieste, 23 January 1960, Jacques Piccard and Don Walsh; descent nearly five hours; Plexiglas window cracked; about twenty minutes on the bottom. https://geology.com/records/bathyscaphe-trieste.shtml ; https://www.britannica.com/place/Challenger-Deep
- Nobody returned until James Cameron's solo dive in Deepsea Challenger, 26 March 2012 (1960 → 2012 = 52 years). https://www.britannica.com/topic/Deepsea-Challenger
- Victor Vescovo, Limiting Factor, four dives to the bottom of Challenger Deep between 28 April and 5 May 2019. https://www.hydro-international.com/content/news/victor-vescovo-makes-deepest-submarine-dive-in-history ; https://tritonsubs.com/five-deeps-expedition-conquers-challenger-deep-with-multiple-historic-dives/
- Plastic bag at 10,898 m in the Mariana Trench, found in JAMSTEC's Deep-sea Debris Database (Chiba et al. 2018, Marine Policy); more than 33 percent of debris macro-plastic, 89 percent of that single-use. https://www.euronews.com/2018/05/11/we-ve-found-the-world-s-deepest-plastic-bag-say-scientists ; https://www.ecowatch.com/plastic-bag-mariana-trench-2567907997.html
- 2017 (Jamieson et al., Nature Ecology & Evolution): amphipods from the Mariana and Kermadec trenches, 7,000 to over 10,000 m; every sample contaminated with POPs incl. PCBs; highest Mariana PCB levels 50 times those of crabs from paddy fields fed by the Liaohe River, one of China's most polluted. https://www.ncl.ac.uk/press/articles/archive/2017/02/marianatrenchpollution/ ; https://theconversation.com/how-we-discovered-pollution-poisoned-crustaceans-in-the-mariana-trench-72900
- PCBs produced from the 1930s to the 1970s. https://www.ncl.ac.uk/press/articles/archive/2017/02/marianatrenchpollution/
- "Eleven kilometers": 10,935 m rounded.
