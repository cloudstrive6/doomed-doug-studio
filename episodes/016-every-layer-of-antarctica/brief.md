## 2. Escalation axis and items
**Axis: the thickness of ice above Doug (depth below the ice surface).** The whole episode runs down one rig: a hot-water drill borehole,
which is how the real projects (WISSARD, SALSA, Ellsworth, ITGC) reach under the ice. Doug is lowered down it on a rope, past a growing stack
of ice. The rig "moves" between items: one cut per item to a small map of Antarctica with a red dot.

On-screen, episode-specific meter beside the death counter: **"ICE ABOVE DOUG: N m"**, with an ice-cube icon. It is monotonic (only grows),
like 015's DEPTH meter and 013's DISTANCE TO HIPPO. The art director owns it. Spoken section headers (never used before):
- **"Thin ice."** (opener, under 1 km)
- **"Thick ice."** (before item 7, about 1 to 3.5 km)
- **"The bottom."** (before item 10)

Background ramp: pale blue-white, then glacier blue, cobalt, deep navy, ending in black with one faint red tone at the boss. The art director
defines it, and it must stay distinct from the ocean ramp (more white and cyan, visible ice texture).

**Order rule:** every depth needs a source. Each value below is the brief's working number. **The writer verifies each one.** If a verified
value breaks the order, swap items; never fudge the meter. If Denman's ice column (item 11) turns out *thinner* than Vostok's, swap 10 and 11
and make Vostok the boss. Never describe injuries. Deaths are cartoon only: X eyes, ghost Doug, flat Doug, a Doug-shaped ice block, the

### Thin ice.

**1. Blood Falls** (Taylor Glacier, McMurdo Dry Valleys; meter 0 m at the outlet; the hook item; death 131)
- Facts:
  - A blood-red flow out of the face of a white glacier (verify the cliff height).
  - The colour is iron in a salty brine that oxidises (rusts) when it meets air.
  - The brine has been sealed under the glacier for about 1.5-2 million years (Mikucki et al. 2009, *Science*; verify).
  - Microbes live in it with no light and no oxygen, "breathing" iron and sulfate (same paper; attribute).
  - Airborne radar/EM surveys mapped a brine network running under the glacier (Mikucki et al. 2015, *Nature Communications*; verify depth
    and extent). If a sourced source depth exists, the meter may show it for the twist shot only.
- Analogy idea: the brine is several times saltier than seawater (verify the factor); compare with a sourced everyday solution.
- Twist (by 0:45): **it isn't blood. It's water nobody has touched for over a million years, and it rusts the moment it sees air. Something is
  living in it.**
- Doug beat: Doug poses for a selfie in front of the red waterfall. Death 131: a block of the glacier front lets go (cartoon, flat Doug under an
  ice block). The first sticker, "BLOOD FALLS", lands on the suitcase. "Doug did not agree to this." lands in the opener (see 3).

**2. Erebus Ice Caves** (Mount Erebus, Ross Island; meter tens of metres, verify; Doug survives)
- Facts:
  - Erebus is Earth's southernmost active volcano (verify) with a long-lived lava lake.
  - Volcanic gas melts caves and towers (fumarolic ice towers) into the ice on its flanks.
  - Inside it is warm, around 25 °C in places (verify), and pitch dark.
  - DNA studies found microbes, and possibly traces of animals and plants, living in the caves with no sunlight
    (Tebo et al. 2015 / Fraser et al. 2018, *Polar Biology*; verify and attribute "possibly").
  - Optional one-liner: Erebus releases tiny gold crystals in its gas (verify source; skip if weak).
- Analogy idea: "warm as a sourced room temperature" inside a cave cut into ice on a volcano.
- Twist: **the warmest room in Antarctica is inside the ice, on a volcano, and something has already moved in.**
- Doug beat: Doug takes his jacket off and relaxes. **"Doug is fine."** Counter stays 131. The narrator, flatly, does not trust it. No death and
  no medical detail (do not explain volcanic gas symptoms; at most "the air in volcanic caves can be the dangerous part" without detail).

**3. Thwaites' Hidden Cavity** (the "Doomsday Glacier", West Antarctica; meter about 590 m, verify; death 132)
- Facts:
  - Thwaites is nicknamed the "Doomsday Glacier" (attribute the nickname as a media nickname).
  - In 2019, NASA radar found a cavity under it about two-thirds the area of Manhattan and almost 300 m tall (Milillo et al. 2019,
    *Science Advances*; verify).
  - In 2020 the ITGC team drilled through the ice near the grounding line. The Icefin robot (Schmidt et al. 2023, *Nature*) found warm
    water carving terraces and crevasses into the ice from below (verify the ice thickness at the borehole).
  - Thwaites could raise sea level by about 65 cm if it fully melted (verify, attribute).
- Analogy idea: a hole the height of a sourced skyscraper, hidden under the ice.
- Twist: **the glacier is being eaten from underneath, where nobody can see it.**
- Doug beat: Doug explores the cavity with a torch. Death 132 is cartoon: the ceiling creaks, a chunk drops, flat Doug, cap floats in the
  water. Sticker "THWAITES". **No sermon:** state the sea-level number once, flat, and move on. No climate moralising.

**4. The Fish Under the Ice** (Whillans grounding zone, Ross Ice Shelf; meter about 740 m, verify; death 133)
- Facts:
  - In 2015, WISSARD lowered a camera through about 740 m of ice into a gap of water only about 10 m deep, about 850 km from the open
    ocean (Nature news, January 2015; verify all three numbers).
  - They expected nothing. Fish and amphipods swam up to the camera.
  - Nobody knows exactly what feeds them that far from sunlight (attribute).
- Analogy idea: 740 m of ice is about two and a half Eiffel Towers (verify 330 m) stacked above a puddle the height of a sourced object.
- Twist: **scientists drilled to the edge of the world and found something already waiting at the camera.**
- Doug beat: Doug waves at a fish ("hi friend?"). The ice shelf rises and falls with the tide (verify that grounding-zone ice flexes with
  tides). Death 133: the 10 m gap gets smaller (cartoon, flat Doug). Sticker "ROSS ICE SHELF".

**5. Lake Whillans** (meter about 800 m, verify; Doug survives)
- Facts:
  - In 2013 WISSARD reached a lake under about 800 m of ice and sampled it cleanly (Christner et al. 2014, *Nature*).
  - Every sample held life: thousands of bacteria per millilitre (verify about 130,000 cells/mL) and nearly 4,000 types (verify
    about 3,931).
  - Many of them get energy from rock and ammonium, with no sunlight at all (attribute).
- Analogy idea: cells per millilitre against a sourced everyday comparison (a drop of pond water) if one exists; otherwise the lake's size
  as a sourced object.
- Twist: **the first lake ever sampled under the ice wasn't empty. Nothing down here is empty.**
- Doug beat: Doug brings a sandwich and a fishing rod. The microbes eat rock and want nothing from him. **"Doug is fine."** Counter stays
  133. The narrator notes, flatly, that nothing at the bottom of a lake has ever ignored Doug before.

**6. The Sponges Under the Ice** (Filchner-Ronne Ice Shelf; meter about 872 m, verify; death 134)
- Facts:
  - In 2021 (Griffiths et al., *Frontiers in Marine Science*) a borehole through about 900 m of ice, about 260 km from open water, hit a
    boulder with sponges and other stationary animals on it (verify numbers).
  - The animals don't move and live by filtering food, which shouldn't reach that far (attribute the open question).
  - Water about −2 °C (verify).
- Analogy idea: 260 km from food is about a sourced drive (a city pair).
- Twist: **they hit a rock by accident, and it was alive.**
- Doug beat: Doug decides to wait by the sponges for food to drift by, like them. Death 134 (Doug-shaped ice block, cap on top). Sticker
  "FILCHNER-RONNE".

### Thick ice.

**7. Mercer Subglacial Lake** (meter about 1,087 m, verify; death 135)
- Facts:
  - In late 2018 / early 2019 the SALSA team drilled through about 1,087 m of ice (verify).
  - They found dead remains of crustaceans and a tardigrade. These animals don't live there; they came from the ocean or from ancient
    lakes kilometres away, thousands of years ago (Nature news 2019; Venturelli et al. 2023, *AGU Advances*; verify ages and distances).
- Analogy idea: tardigrade size against a sourced object (a grain of salt).
- Twist: **the animals in this lake are not alive, and nobody knows exactly how they got there.** Say it flat; no descriptions of remains.
- Doug beat: Doug takes notes on a clipboard. The rope goes slack. Death 135 (X eyes on the lake floor; the clipboard drifts). Sticker
  "MERCER LAKE". Show the crustaceans only as tiny cartoon shells, never as bodies.

**8. Volcanoes Under the Ice** (West Antarctic Rift; meter about 2,000 m, verify; death 136)
- Facts:
  - A 2017 study (van Wyk de Vries et al., *Geological Society Special Publication*) identified 138 volcanoes under the West Antarctic
    ice sheet, 91 of them new, some taller than 3 km (verify).
  - Melting ice could make some more active, as the weight comes off (attribute as a hypothesis; verify source, e.g. Coonin et al. 2025).
  - A subglacial eruption melts ice into hidden floods (verify a sourced Antarctic example; otherwise state generally).
- Analogy idea: a buried volcano the height of a sourced mountain.
- Twist: **the largest volcanic region on Earth might be the one nobody can see** (only if sourced; otherwise "one of the largest").
- Doug beat: Doug finds a warm patch on the bed and lies down. Death 136: a meltwater flood (cartoon wave). Sticker "WEST ANTARCTIC RIFT".

**9. Lake Ellsworth** (meter about 3,000 m, verify; death 137)
- Facts:
  - In 2012 a British team tried to drill through about 3 km of ice to reach this lake.
  - They abandoned the attempt after a technical failure (a link between the boreholes could not be made; verify the exact cause).
  - A hot-water borehole refreezes once the heat stops (verify the timescale from the project).
- Analogy idea: 3 km of ice is about 7 Empire State Buildings stacked (verify 443 m).
- Twist: **the lake has been sealed for maybe hundreds of thousands of years (verify), and the hole to it closed behind them.**
- Doug beat: Doug is halfway down when the drill crew packs up. Death 137: the borehole closes around him (Doug-shaped ice column, cap on
  top). No real people are hurt or blamed. Sticker "LAKE ELLSWORTH".

### The bottom.

**10. Lake Vostok** (meter about 3,750 m, verify; death 138)
- Facts:
  - Under about 3.7-4 km of ice; about 250 km long, roughly the size of Lake Ontario (verify).
  - Sealed for possibly up to 15 million years (attribute: estimates vary).
  - Oxygen concentrations far higher than ordinary lakes (verify the "50 times" claim before use).
  - In February 2012 Russian drillers reached it, and lake water rushed up the borehole and froze (verify).
  - The borehole used kerosene-based drilling fluid, which raised contamination worries (attribute).
  - Accretion-ice DNA claims (Shtarkman et al. 2013) are disputed. Say that if used.
- Analogy idea: a lake the size of a sourced US state/lake under ice taller than a sourced landmark.
- Twist: **for millions of years nothing touched it, and the first thing it did when we arrived was come up the hole.**
- Doug beat: Doug knocks on the ice ("hello?"). Death 138: the lake surges up the borehole and Doug freezes in the column, mid-wave (cartoon,
  fine because 012's amber death was a different variant; keep it a frozen ice pillar with the cap on top, not a "preserved" body). Sticker
  "VOSTOK".

**11. The Deepest Point on Land** (Denman Glacier trough, East Antarctica; the boss; meter = verified ice thickness there; death 139)
- Facts:
  - BedMachine Antarctica (Morlighem et al. 2019, *Nature Geoscience*) found an ice-filled canyon under Denman Glacier reaching more than
    3,500 m below sea level, the deepest point on land (UCI; Guinness World Records).
  - About 2,000 m deeper than earlier estimates. The depth comes from radar plus ice-flow mass conservation, not a direct sounding; say so
    in one line.
  - Length/width sources disagree (20 km × 100 km in UCI coverage vs a 150 km valley in a Springer Nature piece); use only the verified
    figure.
  - Denman's grounding line retreated about 5 km between 1996 and 2018, and the bed slopes downward inland (Brancato et al. 2020,
    *Geophysical Research Letters*; verify).
- Analogy idea: 3,500 m below sea level is deeper than a sourced comparison (e.g. the Dead Sea shore at about −430 m, eight times over;
  verify).
- Twist (the thesis line): **the bottom of every continent is down here, under the ice, and it slopes the wrong way.** Doug isn't at the end
  of the world. He's at the bottom of it.
- Doug beat: Doug plants a small flag ("DOUG WAS HERE"). The glacier is moving. Death 139: the ice slides and carries Doug and the flag off
  frame (cartoon). Sticker "DENMAN".
- **Closing image:** back at the surface, the borehole is a tiny dark dot in white. The rope comes up with only the cap on the end, then the
  full suitcase (9 stickers) beside it. The meter shows the final verified depth. Kicker about the cap (e.g. it is the only thing that has
  been to the bottom of every continent and come back). "The suitcase still has room." is allowed as the outro line.

Deaths: 9 (131-139). Survivals: Erebus Ice Caves (2) and Lake Whillans (5). 11 items. **Option for the writer:** if under 2,900 words, extend
items 1, 10 and 11 or add one survival item between 8 and 9: **The Gamburtsev Mountains** (an Alps-sized range buried under ice, mapped
2008-09, verify). Place it only if its verified ice depth fits the order. Do not add a death; the boss stays 139.
