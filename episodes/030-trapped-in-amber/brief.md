## 2. Escalation axis and items
**Axis: size. How big the trapped animal was, from a tick to a dinosaur.**
- Spoken bands are "Bugs." / "Bigger." / "Backbones." / "Dinosaur." (the boss only).
- Every number below is a lead. The writer verifies each one in facts.md. **If a fact can't be sourced, cut it; don't hedge it.**
- **Size order rule:** the order follows the sourced size of the whole animal, stated as "about" where the paper only estimates it.
  Items 5-7 are the most likely to swap after sourcing; the writer may reorder them and notes it in the script header.
  Item 1 and the boss are fixed.
- **Time machine** (the standard `prehistoric` vehicle) at every item. Doug steps out into the forest moments before the resin closes.

### Episode device: the specimen tray (optional; the art director decides)
- A crude museum tray in the corner. One small amber bead drops into its slot after each item, so it fills up left to right.
- It is not a meter and not a tape measure, so nothing is reused from 025. The closing pays it off: the last slot gets the cap (see the boss).

### The rule that makes the deaths work: the catch is small, the neighbours are not
- Doug is never trapped in amber. 012 used that once and it is not a precedent.

### Bugs.

**1. The Tick** (death 241)
- Facts:
  - A hard tick nymph was found in about 99-million-year-old amber from Myanmar, **still gripping a dinosaur feather** (Peñalver et al. 2017, *Nature Communications*,
    "Ticks parasitised feathered dinosaurs as revealed by Cretaceous amber assemblages"; *Cornupalpatum burmanicum*).
  - In the same study, a new tick, *Deinocroton draculi* ("Dracula's terrible tick"), was found **swollen with blood**, about 8 times its unfed size (verify the figure).
  - **The twist, the Jurassic Park myth:** you can't get dinosaur DNA from it. DNA has a measured half-life of about 521 years, and readable DNA is gone
    long before a million years (Allentoft et al. 2012, *Proc. R. Soc. B*, moa bones; verify the exact figures). Name the film once, in narration only.
  - Whose feather it was isn't known. The paper only says a feathered dinosaur, possibly a bird. Word it that way.
- Analogy: the tick against a sesame seed or a grain of rice (source the size).
- Doug beat:
  - Doug steps out of the time machine holding a tiny "DNA KIT" and a test tube labelled "DINO".
  - The kit doesn't work, and the feather's owner arrives (a silhouette of a big feathered shape over Doug). Death 241, cut to black. The cap is left on the forest floor.
  - "Doug did not agree to this." (once, here).

**2. The Web** (Doug SURVIVES; "Doug is fine."; narrator quietly disappointed)
- Facts:
  - A spider attacking a parasitic wasp caught in its web, in Myanmar amber of about 100 Myr. It is called the oldest fossil evidence of a spider attacking prey
    in its web (Poinar & Buckley 2012, *Historical Biology*; verify the title and the strand count). Note any second spider in the same piece only if the paper states it.
  - The twist: the bite never landed. Both animals were frozen a fraction of a second before it, and they have stayed that way ever since.
- Analogy: the whole scene would fit on a fingernail (verify the size).
- Doug beat: Doug is tangled in a single strand of silk. It doesn't hold him, and he walks off. "Doug is fine." The narrator is quietly disappointed.

**3. The Hell Ant** (death 242; costume gag, once)
- Facts:
  - Hell ants (haidomyrmecines) had jaws that swung **upward**, unlike any living insect, and many had a horn-like head plate.
  - One was preserved holding its prey, an extinct cockroach relative (*Ceratomyrmex ellenbergeri* with a *Caputoraptor elegans* nymph; Barden et al. 2020, *Current Biology*).
  - Optional, verify it or cut it: *Linguamyrmex vladi*, the "vampire" hell ant, whose horn may have been reinforced with metal (Barden et al. 2017). Only use "may".
  - The lineage is extinct and has no living descendants (verify the wording).
- Analogy: the ant's size against something everyday (source it).
- Twist: the creepiest predator on the list is the size of a grain of rice, and it hunted with a jaw that worked like a bear trap turned upside down.
- Doug beat: the costume gag, played straight. Doug wears a cockroach-relative costume with the cap on top ("which is exactly why Doug is dressed as an
  extinct cockroach"). He kneels to meet the ant, "hi friend?". Death 242: the costume goes flat. Cartoon only.

### Bigger.

**4. The Ammonite** (death 243)
- Facts:
  - An ammonite, a **sea animal**, was found inside tree resin in Myanmar amber of about 99 Myr (Yu et al. 2019, *PNAS*). The shell is about 33 mm (verify).
  - The same piece holds sea snails, an isopod, mites and insects, so land and sea animals are mixed (verify the list).
  - The shell was probably empty, and the resin flowed over it on or near a beach (say "probably"; use the paper's interpretation).
- Analogy: shell size against a coin (source it).
- Twist: a sea creature trapped in a tree. The forest grew right up to the sea, and the resin dripped onto the beach.
- Doug beat: Doug sunbathes under a resin-dripping tree with a beach towel. The Cretaceous tide comes in. Death 243 (a wave, cut to black). The cap floats.

### Backbones.

**5. The Snake** (death 244)
- Facts:
  - It is the first fossil baby snake: *Xiaophis myanmarensis* (Xing et al. 2018, *Science Advances*). The body fragment is about 47.5 mm, with about 97 vertebrae, and
    **the head is missing** (verify it).
  - Another piece holds the shed skin of a larger snake, so snakes lived in that forest.
  - It is the first evidence of snakes in a Cretaceous forest (word it to the paper).
- Analogy: the fragment against a paperclip or a finger (source it).
- Twist: a newborn snake, without its head, 99 million years old, and its parent was in the same forest.
- Doug beat: Doug looks for the snake with a flashlight and says "hi friend?". Something bigger is behind him: the shed skin is huge. Death 244, cut to black.
  No snake attack is shown.

**6. The Wrong Dinosaur** (Doug SURVIVES; "Doug is fine.")
- Facts:
  - In 2020, a tiny skull in Myanmar amber, *Oculudentavis khaungraae*, was published in *Nature* as **the smallest dinosaur ever**, about hummingbird-sized (Xing et al. 2020).
  - The paper was **retracted** the same year.
  - A second specimen showed it was a **lizard** (Bolet et al. 2021, *Current Biology*, *Oculudentavis naga*).
  - Verify the retraction date and the skull length (about 14 mm).
- Analogy: the skull against a fingernail.
- Twist: the "smallest dinosaur in history" was a lizard, and scientists only knew because the amber caught a second one.
- Doug beat: Doug arrives with a tiny saddle labelled "SMALLEST DINOSAUR", but the lizard ignores him. "Doug is fine."
  - **The one 019 callback:** the narrator notes that this is not the first time scientists drew an animal wrong. Use one sentence and new words.

**7. The Hatchling** (death 245)
- Facts:
  - A baby enantiornithine bird, an extinct toothed-bird group, is preserved with skin, feathers and claws (Xing et al. 2017, *Gondwana Research*; nicknamed "Belone";
    verify the nickname and size).
  - It probably died within days or weeks of hatching (word it to the paper).
  - Optional: in 2016, a separate piece held two tiny wings (Xing et al. 2016, *Nature Communications*).
  - These birds hatched with flight feathers already grown (hedge it to the source).
- Analogy: the hatchling against something everyday (source the size).
- Twist: a bird with **teeth and clawed wings**, from a family that died out with the dinosaurs.
- Doug beat: Doug finds the nest, "hi friend?". The parent bird lands behind him. Death 245, cut to black.
- **Keep it adult-coded:** no cute chick close-ups and no baby animal in danger on screen. The hatchling is only ever drawn as the fossil.

### Dinosaur.

**8. The Tail** (the boss; death 246)
- Facts:
  - The first **dinosaur** body part ever found in amber: a 3.65 cm section of a juvenile coelurosaur's tail, with 8 vertebrae, bone, soft tissue and feathers
    (Xing et al. 2016, *Current Biology*; nicknamed "Eva"). Verify every figure.
  - The paper says the whole animal was about sparrow-sized (verify).
  - Iron traces were detected in the tissue, which may be a leftover of the blood (word it to the paper: "traces consistent with").
  - **It was found at an amber market** in Myitkyina, Myanmar, where it had been cut and polished, almost certainly for jewellery (verify).
- **Honesty line, required:**
  - Much of this amber comes from mines in a conflict zone in northern Myanmar.
  - In 2020, the Society of Vertebrate Paleontology asked journals to stop publishing on Myanmar amber obtained after 2017; other journals and the 2021 coup
    tightened this further (verify the dates and the wording against SVP's letter and Sokol 2019, *Science*, "Troubled treasure").
  - Use 1-2 flat sentences, no joke and no Doug on that sentence.
- Analogy: the tail section against something everyday (a thumb or a matchbox; source the length).
- **Twist (the payoff of the promise):** after a whole video of things that lived around dinosaurs, this is the dinosaur. It was in a jewellery shop.
- Doug beat:
  - Doug finally meets the owner: a sparrow-sized, feathered, menacing juvenile. "hi friend?"
  - Then the parent arrives, much bigger. Death 246, played flat.
- **Closing image:** a slow drop of resin rolls down a trunk and closes over the **cap**, never Doug. Cut to the specimen tray: the last slot holds the cap, in amber, under a museum spotlight.
  - Kicker, which the writer words: the cap is now a specimen. Doug is not.
- **Outro:** 30 words or fewer. Give the counter (246) and one dry line, re-hooked to the end slate. No recap and no subscribe pitch.

**Writer options:**
- **Draft under 1,950 words:** add **"The Malaria Mosquito"** to "Bugs." (the oldest malaria parasite, *Plasmodium dominicana*, in a mosquito in Dominican amber,
  Poinar 2005, *Systematic Parasitology*; verify). It is the real version of the film's mosquito. Doug survives, with one 002 callback at most.
- **Draft over 2,350 words:** cut item 6's 2016-wings line first, then item 3's *Linguamyrmex* option.

**Totals:** 6 deaths (241-246). Doug survives The Web and The Wrong Dinosaur. 8 items, or 9 with the mosquito.

## 7. Risks
- **Kid appeal (HIGH):** dinosaurs, baby birds and a famous film all pull in kids' content. Countermeasures:
  - the deadpan adult narrator and adult-coded jokes
  - the conflict-amber honesty line, which is adult framing
  - no cute dinosaur design: the boss dinosaur is a menacing, feathered small predator, and the hatchling is only ever drawn as a fossil
  - no "dino" baby-talk anywhere in the narration (the "DINO" test-tube label is the only use); the film's name only in narration
  - made_for_kids false, and no kids' tags
- **Over-claiming (HIGH):**
  - "dinosaur DNA": state plainly that it can't be recovered
  - "blood" in the tick and the tail: only as the papers word it
  - Oculudentavis: say it was retracted, without implying misconduct; the retraction note's own wording only
  - "the only dinosaur in amber"
  - hatchling age and flight feathers
  - hell-ant metal ("may")
- **Ethics and policy (MEDIUM):** Myanmar amber gets one sober, sourced line and no political commentary beyond the sources. Never tell viewers to buy amber, and don't name sellers.
- **Gore (LOW):** no inside-the-body views. Nothing is shown being eaten. The deaths are cut to black.
- **Repetition (MEDIUM):** no Doug-in-amber death (012). No tape measure (025). Not the Spinosnack frame. No 230-Myr Dolomites droplet item (012), only the one-sentence callback.
- **Facts that need strong sources (cut if unsourced):** Peñalver 2017, Allentoft 2012, Poinar & Buckley 2012, Barden 2017 and 2020, Yu 2019, Xing 2016 (both papers), Xing 2017, Xing 2018,
  the Xing 2020 retraction notice, Bolet 2021, the SVP 2020 letter, and Sokol 2019. Use Poinar 2005 only if the mosquito is used.
