## 1. Facts

I re-checked 30+ claims, including every claim facts.md marked "search snippet" that I could reach. Arithmetic re-run: Puerto Triunfo to the
Okavango is 11,009 km, 1,609/105 = 15.3 pitches, 500/365 = 1.37, 2 x 1,546 = 3,092 kg.

**Verified (re-opened the source):** Escobar's 4 hippos, 1 male and 3 females (SciAm/Nature). Escobar died in 1993, hippos "too dangerous to move" (Smithsonian).
169 official count as of late 2023 (Smithsonian). 181-215 census with boats, drones and footprints (Nature, 2 June 2023). Invasive
declaration, environment ministry, 25 March 2022, Resolución 346 (search snippet now confirmed by the ministry and El Heraldo).
1,400 by 2040 (Smithsonian). Honk carries at least a mile (SDZ). Yawning contests at about 7 years (SDZ). 150 vs 45 degree gape (SDZ).
50 cm canines, about twice a female's (SDZ). Skin 5-6 cm on back and rump, no sweat glands, "dries like lacquer" (SDZ). Male average 1,546 kg (SDZ).
Charge at over 30 km/h (SDZ). 3-5 km paths and 40-60 km drought moves (SDZ). Groups of 10-15, ranging 2-50 and up to 150 (SDZ). Bulls hold 50-100 m and the daytime range is 200 m (SDZ).
Underwater 5 minutes, average 104 s (SDZ). Cannot swim or float, sleep-surfacing reflex, dung flicked with the tail, "one of Africa's most
dangerous animals" (NatGeo). 500 a year, "one of the deadliest large mammals", "neglected by scientists" (Leeds). Two pigments, red
and orange, named by a Japanese team, UV shielding, the red one inhibits P. aeruginosa and K. pneumoniae (Nature news 2004). Colourless,
then red, then brown (Hashimoto et al. 2007, Pure Appl. Chem.). 169 strides from 32 hippos, Flamingo Land in North Yorkshire, almost exclusively
trot (Hutchinson and Pringle 2024 PeerJ, read via PMC11227274). Coughlin and Fish 2009: 2 hippos analysed frame by frame, gallop-like gait with
extended unsupported intervals, microgravity analogy (abstract).

**Failures and required changes:**

- **F1. Contradicted: "it has no interest in meat whatsoever" (The Mouth).** Dudley et al. 2016, *Mammal Review* 46(3):191-203, documents
  carnivory and scavenging across the hippo's range, including impala, zebra, wildebeest, elephant carcasses and other hippos. The line has
  no source in facts.md either. "It does not hunt, it does not stalk" is also unsourced.
- **F2. Not supported by the primary paper: "In the Royal Veterinary College study, hippos mostly sprinted when one of them was chasing off
  another, so the fastest thing a hippo does is usually aimed at a rival." (Bull Territory).** The only source is earth.com, which says the
  opposite direction ("when chased by another of their kind"). The PeerJ paper says the fastest hippos were "avoiding lions or rhinoceroses
  or aggressive behaviour toward each other", and toward humans and vehicles. It does not say "mostly", and it does not say the run is aimed at a rival.
- **F3. Over-stated: "In the study, that happened for about fifteen percent of the time they spent running, for a little under a third of a
  second each time." (The Charge).** The PeerJ paper gives no aerial-phase percentage or duration. It calls the phases "brief" and infers them from
  timing in about 12 internet videos. The RVC release (search snippet) says "sometimes for as much as 15 per cent of the stride cycle". The 0.3 s
  figure appears only in secondary coverage. This is the snippet-only claim facts.md flagged for re-checking. It doesn't hold as written.
- **F4. Unverified: "even at weaker strengths than on the hippo itself" (The Red Sweat).** facts.md's only support is a search snippet of the
  Saikawa 2004 abstract. Nature blocks the paper, and I could not reproduce the snippet. Nature news 2004 and the 2007 IUPAC paper give no concentration.
- **F5. Unsourced: "and adults keep using it for the rest of their lives" (The Yawn).** SDZ only says males *begin* yawning contests at
  adolescence (7 years). facts.md has no entry for this.

**Advisory (not blocking):**
- "the first database of hippos across the whole of Africa": Leeds says the database currently covers southern Africa only and will expand.
  "is building Africa's first hippo database" is the safe wording.
- facts.md says the Castelblanco-Martínez 2021 abstract has no headline number. That paper is the usual origin of the "about 1,400 by 2040"
  projection. The Smithsonian citation is fine as it is. This is for the record only.
- "the hippos were judged too dangerous to catch": the source says "move". Changing it to "move" costs nothing.
