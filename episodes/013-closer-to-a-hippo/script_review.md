## 1. Facts

**Re-opened sourcing (as you asked):**
- **Hipposudoric and norhipposudoric acid: VERIFIED.** Nature news 2004 (re-opened) says the team was Kimiko Hashimoto's at Kyoto Pharmaceutical University, the
  pigments are "one red and one orange", they are named hipposudoric acid and norhipposudoric acid, they shield from UV, and the red one restricts the growth of
  *P. aeruginosa* and *K. pneumoniae*. The red colour holds "for several hours". I reproduced the Saikawa et al. 2004 abstract (Nature 429:363) wording this
  round: colourless, then red within minutes, then brown; "non-benzenoid aromatic compounds that are unexpectedly acidic"; may act as sunscreens.
  Science News (Milius) independently says the pigments are "highly acidic compounds containing carbon rings". So "unusual, unexpectedly acidic aromatic
  compounds" holds. Nature.com and IUPAC both return 403, so this is still from abstract and search text, but two independent sources now agree.
  Advisory: add the Science News URL to facts.md as a second source.
- **15 percent of the stride: VERIFIED as now worded.** The PeerJ paper (re-opened) gives no percentage; it says "brief aerial phases". The figure is the RVC
  team's own, from its 2 July 2024 release. That release is still a 403 for me, but ScienceFocus (re-opened) says "as much as 15 per cent of their stride cycle", and CNN/KTVZ
  and New Atlas, citing the RVC statement, say "the fastest ... around 15% of each stride". The script's "For the fastest ones, that airborne
  moment took up as much as fifteen percent of each stride" matches this, and the script never attributes the figure to the paper. The 0.3 s figure stays out: it is
  inconsistent with the paper's top stride frequency of 3.2 Hz, which gives a cycle of about 0.31 s.

**Also re-checked this round:** RVC study details (169 strides, 32 hippos, North Yorkshire, veterinary purpose); Thévenet 2022 via ScienceDaily (the
stronger response to strangers, the vocal reply, approach and dung spraying); Mongabay Kafue 2025 (capsizing, danger by boat, a 2025 expedition along the
length of the river); the Zambezi canoe capsize (Wild Horizons via Tampa Bay 28: "a hippo emerged underneath the canoe"); Coughlin and Fish 0.47 m/s
(the facts.md BioOne quote); Leeds wording; and the arithmetic (3,092 kg, 1.37 a day, 15.3 pitches).

**New failures:**
- **F6. Over-stated: "Until recently, scientific descriptions of how hippos move on land were limited and frequently contradictory." (The Charge)**
  The PeerJ abstract says "limited and **sometimes** contradictory". facts.md quotes "sometimes" correctly, but the script says "frequently".
- **F7. Unsupported: "That river is considered one of the last great hippo strongholds in Africa." (The Canoe)** Mongabay says "one of the last
  strongholds of the species in Southern Africa", and its headline says "one of Africa's last strongholds". "Great" is not in either.

**Advisory:**
- "louder replies, a closer approach" (The Honk): ScienceDaily says "responding vocally, approaching" with greater "overall intensity". "Louder" is not
  stated. "more replies" or "stronger replies" is safer.
- The facts.md Night Walk row 57 still lists a line that draft 2 no longer has. This is housekeeping only.
