## 2. Escalation axis and items
**Axis: distance between Doug and the nearest hippo**, from about 11,000 km to zero. Each item is a real hippo fact that becomes relevant at
that distance. The distance is shown on screen as an episode-specific meter (**"DISTANCE TO HIPPO"**, a ruler icon) beside the death counter,
monotonic (it only shrinks), like 009's NIGHT CLOCK. The art director owns it. Spoken section headers (2 words, must not repeat earlier bands):
**"Far away."** (opener), **"On land."** (before item 3), **"In the water."** (before item 7, about 60%). Background ramp: Colombian green
pasture, then dusk savanna gold, night blue, riverbank mud, murky river green-brown, ending in a dark pink-red interior (the mouth; flat
colour only, no anatomy). Art director defines the ramp.

Every number needs a source; cut what can't be sourced. Every Doug death must be one hippos have really caused in people (species level).
**Never describe injuries.** Deaths are cartoon only: dust cloud, X eyes, ghost Doug, floating cap.

### Far away.

**1. Cocaine Hippos** (about 11,000 km from Africa; hook item; Doug survives)
- Facts: Pablo Escobar imported a few hippos (verify: 1 male + 3 females, late 1980s) to Hacienda Nápoles; after his death in 1993 they were left
  behind and spread along the Magdalena River; the population estimate (verify: about 169 in 2022, Instituto Humboldt / Colombian Ministry of
  Environment) and the projection from Castelblanco-Martínez et al. 2021, *Biological Conservation* (verify the number and year); Colombia declared
  them an invasive species in 2022 (sourced). Optional: the 2023 relocation plan (verify status; cut if unresolved).
- Analogy idea: from 4 to about 169 is like a sourced everyday comparison (one family car to a full bus convoy; writer finds a cleaner one).
- Twist (by 0:45): **Doug went as far from Africa as he could, and the hippos got there first.** No natural predator there, and they keep growing.
- Doug beat: Doug in a sunhat on a Colombian riverbank, relieved; a pod surfaces behind him. Doug survives. "Doug is fine." Counter stays 107.
- Wording rule: Escobar is mentioned **once**, flatly, as the importer. No drugs shown, no drug jokes, the word "cocaine" spoken once as the
  item name only. Never "cocaine" on the thumbnail.

**2. The Honk** (about 1 km; Doug survives)
- Facts: hippo "wheeze honks" carry over long distances (sourced distance, verify); Thévenet et al. 2022, *Current Biology*: hippos in Maputo
  Special Reserve reacted differently to calls of familiar neighbours vs strangers, responding to strangers more strongly, including by
  **dung-spraying** (verify every detail); hippos spray dung with a spinning tail to mark territory (sourced).
- Analogy idea: a honk audible about as far as a sourced everyday sound (a car horn, a church bell).
- Twist: **they know their neighbours by voice. Doug is not a neighbour.**
- Doug beat: Doug plays a recorded hippo call from a speaker ("hi friend?"). Reply: a dung spray (cartoon brown scribble cloud, no detail). Doug
  survives, brown. "Doug is fine." (with less conviction). Counter stays 107. Gross-but-safe, keep it to one shot.

### On land.

**3. The Night Walk** (a few km, on land; death 108)
- Facts: hippos spend the day in water and leave it at night to graze (sourced); they eat about 35-40 kg of grass a night (verify range) and walk
  several km (verify; sources vary from about 3 to 10 km); they use the same paths repeatedly (sourced).
- Analogy idea: a night's grass weighs about as much as a sourced object (a large bag of dog food, a full suitcase; objects only, never a person).
- Twist: **the animal walking through camp in the dark is a herbivore. That does not help.**
- Doug beat: Doug pitches a tent on a well-worn path ("nice flat spot"). Night shot: tent, two eyes. Death 108. "Doug did not agree to this."
  (first danger; the narrator notes Doug chose the campsite).
- Risk: don't phrase "most attacks happen when you're between a hippo and water" as a tip; state behaviour, never an instruction.

**4. The Charge** (about 50 m; death 109)
- Facts: top speed on land about 30 km/h (sourced; give the range honestly); Hutchinson et al. 2023 (*PeerJ*, Royal Veterinary College, verify)
  found hippos at speed **get all four feet off the ground** briefly, despite a trot-like gait; adults weigh about 1,300-3,200 kg (verify range,
  males heavier).
- Analogy idea: about 30 km/h is a sourced everyday speed (a bike in town, a sprinter's top speed); a male weighs about as much as a sourced object
  (a small car, two small cars).
- Twist: **it doesn't really run. It trots. It still briefly flies.** One-line fragment burst.
- Doug beat: Doug thinks he can outrun "something shaped like a sofa". Death 109. Dust cloud.

**5. The Red Sweat** (about 10 m, close enough to see it; Doug survives, narrator disappointed)
- Facts: hippo skin secretes a red-orange fluid, not blood and not sweat (sourced); Saikawa et al. 2004, *Nature*: two pigments, hipposudoric and
  norhipposudoric acid, absorb UV and inhibit bacteria (verify wording); hippo skin is thin-surfaced and dries out quickly out of water (sourced).
- Analogy idea: the skin is thick (verify, about 5-6 cm on the flanks) like a sourced object (a phone book, a paperback stack).
- Twist: **it looks like it's bleeding. It's wearing sunscreen.** Doug, meanwhile, is not.
- Doug beat: Doug offers a plaster (bandage) to a "bleeding" hippo; it ignores him and walks to the river. Doug gets sunburnt.
  Don't use "the narrator had the death drawn already" (used in 010/011); the narrator is quietly disappointed in one flat line instead. Counter stays 109.
  Keep the red fluid orange-red and clearly not blood in the drawing (art director: use orange, never the blood red).

**6. The Yawn** (about 5 m; death 110)
- Facts: the "yawn" is a threat display (sourced); the jaw opens up to about 150 degrees (verify; cut the number if unsourced); lower canines
  can reach about 50 cm in length including the root (verify) and are kept sharp by grinding against the upper teeth (sourced).
- Analogy idea: a lower canine is about as long as a sourced object (a ruler is 30 cm; a baseball bat is too long; check).
- Twist: **it is not tired. It is telling Doug how big its mouth is.**
- Doug beat: Doug yawns back, politely. Death 110 (off-screen, dust cloud). No teeth shown in contact with Doug ever.

### In the water.

**7. The Shallows** (about 2 m, the water's edge; death 111)
- Facts: adult hippos can't swim or float; they walk or bound along the bottom (Coughlin & Fish 2009, *Journal of Mammalogy*, verify); dense
  bones help them sink (sourced); they can hold their breath about 5 minutes (verify range).
- Analogy idea: 5 minutes is a sourced everyday duration (a song, boiling an egg).
- Twist: **hippos can't swim. Doug can. Doug assumed that settled it.**
- Doug beat: Doug wades in feeling safe ("they can't swim"). Underwater shot: a hippo bounding along the bottom toward him. Death 111.

**8. The Canoe** (about 1 m, directly over it; death 112)
- Facts: hippos capsize boats and attack canoes in rivers such as the Zambezi and Okavango (reputable source, no named victims); bulls hold
  stretches of river as territory (sourced); hippos surface to breathe even while asleep, without waking (verify; cut if unsourced).
- Analogy idea: a territory length as a sourced everyday distance (a few football pitches; verify the figure).
- Twist: **from a canoe, a hippo is a rock until it isn't.** (Do not echo 011's log gag: the hippo is a rock that has ears.)
- Doug beat: Doug in a canoe, paddling over "rocks". One rock has ears. Death 112. The cap floats in the canoe, upright.

**9. Bull Territory** (about 0.5 m, between two bulls; death 113)
- Facts: rival bulls fight with their canines (sourced, general, no injury detail); bulls' scarred hides (one flat line only); groups ("pods"/
  "bloats") of about 10-30, up to 100+ (verify).
- Analogy idea: a group of about 30 hippos weighs about as much as a sourced object (a loaded freight car, a blue whale is too heavy; check).
- Twist: **the fight was never about Doug. He was just standing in it.**
- Doug beat: Doug tries to mediate ("hi friends?", plural). Death 113. Dust cloud, two hippos, no contact shown.

**10. The Mouth** (0 m; the boss; death 114)
- Facts: hippos are herbivores (sourced); the figure "hippos kill about 500 people a year" is widely quoted but **hard to source** (attribute
  honestly: say it's the commonly cited estimate and that reliable data are scarce; if a reviewed estimate exists, use that instead); hippos are
  still widely counted among Africa's deadliest large animals (attribute).
- Analogy idea: the open gape is about as tall as a sourced object (a front door, `front_door.json` if it fits; check).
- Twist: **it doesn't want to eat Doug. It never did. It's a vegetarian. It killed him anyway.** That is the episode's thesis line.
- Doug beat: Doug stands in front of the open mouth like a doorway, holding out a lettuce. Cut to black (no closing shown). **Death 114.**
- **Closing image:** the river at dusk; the cap drifts past a hippo's ears on the water's surface; the DISTANCE TO HIPPO meter reads 0 m.
  Kicker about the cap (e.g. the cap is the only thing the hippo let leave). Doug's field guide page "HIPPO: FRIEND? NO" may appear for one beat.

Deaths: 7 (108-114). Survivals: Cocaine Hippos, The Honk, The Red Sweat. 10 items. **Option for the writer:** if the draft runs under 2,900
words, do **not** add a calf item (baby animal, kid appeal); add instead **"The Weight"** between 4 and 5 (a survival: Doug is very
nearly stepped on; hippos' foot pressure/mass, sourced) or extend item 1 with the projection data. Do not add a death; the boss stays 114.

## 7. Risks
- **Kid-appeal risk (HIGH):** "hippo" autocomplete is polluted with kids' content ("hippopotamus song", "Hippo Happo", "hippo fart", signals
  10-09). Countermeasures: dry adult narrator; jokes about Doug's assumptions, bad campsites and the narrator's indifference; **no fart or poop
  jokes beyond the one factual dung-spray beat, no baby hippos, no zoo celebrity hippos (Moo Deng, Fiona), no songs, no "funny animal" voices,
  no slapstick sound effects.** The hippo is drawn at the detailed cartoon tier and looks dangerous. Metadata never targets kids.
- **Advertiser-friendliness (MEDIUM):** the cocaine-hippo item. Name Escobar once, flatly, as the importer. No drug imagery, no drug jokes, no
  "cocaine" in title, thumbnail or tags. The word appears as the item/chapter name only (the standard name of the story).
- **Safety advice (HIGH):** no "what to do if a hippo charges", no "never get between a hippo and the water", no "climb a tree". State behaviour as
  science, never as instructions. Doug's choices are always wrong and never framed as tips. Description line: "Entertainment and education
  only. Do not approach wild animals." (titler owns it).
- **Gore (MEDIUM):** canines and bull fights invite injury talk. **No teeth ever touch Doug on screen; one clinical line per item at most, about
  size or behaviour.** Red sweat drawn orange-red, explicitly "not blood" in narration. No real attack photos; animal photos only with a verified licence.
- **Human deaths:** no named victims, no individual stories, no children. The "500 a year" figure only with honest attribution (see item 10).
- **Assets:** there is no hippo in `assets/library/`. The director must request at least: hippo (standing, land), hippo yawn/open mouth, hippo
  ears-and-eyes at the waterline ("rock with ears"), hippo underwater bounding; plus tent, canoe, speaker, lettuce, plaster. Reuse the existing
  river/croc backgrounds only as scenery.
- **Facts that need strong sources (cut if unsourced):** Escobar import count/year, 2022 population estimate, invasive declaration, the 2021
  projection; Thévenet 2022 neighbour/stranger + dung spraying; honk carrying distance; nightly grass intake and walking distance; top speed,
  Hutchinson 2023 airborne finding, adult mass range; Saikawa 2004 pigments, skin thickness; jaw gape angle, canine length; Coughlin & Fish 2009
  bottom-walking, breath-hold, surfacing while asleep; boat attacks (general), territory size, pod size; herbivory and the "500 a year" claim.
- **Myths to avoid:** "hippos are the deadliest land animal / most dangerous animal in Africa" stated as fact (attribute or cut); "hippo milk is
  pink" (false; do not use); "hippos sweat blood" (only as the misconception); unsourced bite-force numbers (the often-quoted 1,800 psi is
  poorly sourced; cut); "hippos are closely related to pigs" (they're closest to whales and dolphins; only use if sourced, optional fun fact).
- **Duplication:** don't reuse OctoLab's, KPassionate's or Mr. Science's wording or item order. Our twists (the hippos got there first, Doug is
  not a neighbour, the herbivore in camp, it briefly flies, it's wearing sunscreen, it's telling you its mouth size, Doug can swim and that
  settled nothing, a rock with ears, he was standing in it, a vegetarian killed him anyway) carry the video.
