## 2. Escalation axis: age, youngest to oldest
Declared axis for the script header: **"Age: time since death, from about 28,000 years to about 506 million years. The
older it is, the less of it should have survived."** This is a depth gradient in time. The last item is the oldest,
and it still has its brain.

Two spoken section headers:
- **"Frozen."** before item 1 (ice-age permafrost; pale blue and white backgrounds)
- **"Stone."** before item 6 (the zone shift at about 42%; rock greys and browns, getting darker, ending in near-black
  Cambrian sea)

**Recurring visual (for the director):** each item opens on the find as it looks today (drawn, plus a small real photo as
evidence where licensing allows). Then the time machine's dial shows the date ("28,000 YEARS AGO") and Doug steps out into
that animal's last day. The dial numbers grow every item, which makes the axis visible.

Each item follows style bible 3.3: Name, Frame, Mechanism, Twist, Kicker, Doug beat. Ages and causes of death are stated
**only as the source gives them**, with one honest hedge word if the source hedges ("probably", "likely").

### Frozen.
**1. Sparta the Cave Lion Cub** (the hook item, about 28,000 years)
- Research: found in 2018 in Yakutia, Siberia, near the Semyuelyakh River, about 15 m from another cub, Boris, found in
  2017; Sparta is about 28,000 years old and Boris about 43,000 (Chernova et al. 2021, *Quaternary*); fur, whiskers and
  skin intact, one of the best-preserved ice-age animals ever found; about 1-2 months old; cause of death probably a
  mudslide or a fall into a permafrost crack (as the paper says).
- Scale analogy: the cub is about the size of a house cat (sourced length or weight).
- Twist (must land by 0:45, about 145 words): they look like littermates found side by side, but they were born about
  15,000 years apart.
- Doug beat: Doug steps out of the time machine and kneels to pet the cub ("hi friend?"). The cub's mother was not
  consulted. Death 37 off-frame (big shadow, cut to the lone cap). Doug dies, **never the cub**.

**2. Blue Babe** (Doug survives)
- Research: a steppe bison found in 1979 by gold miners near Fairbanks, Alaska; about 36,000 years old; coated in blue
  vivianite (hence the name); claw and tooth marks plus a lion tooth fragment in the hide, which shows a lion killed it
  (Guthrie, *Frozen Fauna of the Mammoth Steppe*, 1990; University of Alaska Museum of the North); in 1984 the researchers
  cooked part of the neck into a stew and ate it at a dinner (sourced: Guthrie's account or the UA Museum).
- Scale analogy: the bison's weight against a small car, or its horns against something everyday (sourced).
- Twist: the creepiest part isn't the lion; it's that scientists **ate** it, and lived.
- Doug beat: the narrator sets the dial for the lion attack, but Doug lands in 1984 at the dinner instead and gets a bowl. He
  survives ("nice"). The narrator is quietly disappointed. Counter stays at 37. **No gross-out**: a deadpan line about the
  taste is fine only if it is sourced; no descriptions of the meat.
- Callback: the lion that killed Blue Babe is a relative of item 1's mother. One line at most.

**3. Lyuba the Baby Mammoth** (about 42,000 years)
- Research: found in 2007 by a reindeer herder on the Yamal Peninsula; about one month old; the most complete mammoth ever
  found (sourced); she probably died by inhaling mud after getting stuck at a river bank (Fisher et al. 2012, *Journal of
  Paleontology*, or the Field Museum / National Geographic); the soaked body was naturally "pickled" by lactic acid from
  microbes (sourced).
- Scale analogy: she is about the size of a large dog (sourced weight/height).
- Twist: the thing that killed her is the thing that preserved her: the same mud.
- Doug beat: Doug tries to help her out of the mud and sinks instead. Death 38: only the cap is left on the mud surface.
  **One clinical line about the mud in her trunk, no more**, and nothing about Doug's breathing.

**4. The Batagaika Foal** (about 42,000 years)
- Research: a foal of an extinct Lena horse found in 2018 in the Batagaika crater, Yakutia; about 41,000-42,000 years
  old; about two weeks old; reported liquid blood in the heart vessels and urine in the bladder (North-Eastern Federal
  University / Russian-Korean team statements of 2019, via Reuters, CNN or similar; **cut or soften "liquid blood" if no
  strong source exists**); the cloning attempt with cells from the foal (sourced, reported as failed or no viable cells);
  the Batagaika crater itself: a permafrost slump about 1 km wide that grows every year, called the "gateway to the
  underworld" by locals (sourced, e.g. NASA Earth Observatory / Murton et al.).
- Scale analogy: the crater's width against a stack of football fields or a city's landmark (sourced width).
- Twist: 42,000 years later, it still had liquid blood in it. (If unsourced, use "scientists tried to clone it" instead.)
- **Fallback item** if the foal facts are too thin: Yuka the mammoth (about 39,000 years; Yamagata et al. 2019,
  *Scientific Reports*: nuclei from its cells showed signs of biological activity when put into mouse eggs).
- Doug beat: Doug steps out at the crater rim to admire the view, and the rim does what it has been doing for decades.
  Death 39. The cap rolls down the slope after him.

**5. Zhùr the Wolf Pup** (Doug survives; the soft beat)
- Research: a mummified grey wolf pup about 57,000 years old, found in 2016 by a gold miner in the Yukon, on Tr'ondëk
  Hwëch'in territory; the name means "wolf" in the Hän language (sourced); about 7 weeks old; likely died when her den
  collapsed; her diet was mostly fish, probably salmon (Meachen et al. 2020, *Current Biology*).
- Scale analogy: the pup is about the size and weight of a loaf of bread or a small bag of flour (sourced weight).
- Twist: ice-age wolves on the mammoth steppe, and this one lived on salmon.
- Doug beat: **the narrator breaks pattern once**: Doug is told to stay by the time machine for this one, and for once he
  does. No death, no joke about the pup. The counter stays at 39. The narration names the First Nation with respect and
  the deadpan resumes at the next header.

### Stone.
**6. The Messel Turtles** (the zone shift, about 47 million years)
- Research: Messel Pit, Germany (UNESCO World Heritage); nine pairs of the turtle *Allaeochelys crassesculpta* fossilised
  while mating; they probably sank into toxic deep water in the lake while coupled (Joyce et al. 2012, *Biology Letters*);
  the lake's deep water was poisonous, maybe from volcanic gas (sourced hypothesis).
- Scale analogy: each turtle is about the size of a dinner plate (sourced length).
- Twist: the only fossils of vertebrates that died **in the act**, nine times over.
- Doug beat: Doug wades into the lake at a very private moment, apologises, and walks deeper. The lake doesn't care.
  Death 40. The humour stays adult-coded and dry (embarrassment, not innuendo). **No reference to Lake Nyos (004)**: that was a
  real mass tragedy.

**7. The Fighting Dinosaurs** (about 71-75 million years)
- Research: a *Velociraptor mongoliensis* and a *Protoceratops andrewsi* locked together, found in 1971 by a
  Polish-Mongolian expedition in the Gobi (Djadokhta Formation, Tugrikin Shiree); the raptor's sickle claw at the other's
  throat while the Protoceratops bites its arm; both were probably buried by a collapsing dune or sandstorm (AMNH,
  Mongolian national treasure status; sourced).
- Scale analogy: a real *Velociraptor* was about the size of a turkey, not the film version (sourced).
- Twist: neither won. Something bigger buried them both mid-fight.
- Doug beat: Doug steps between them with both arms up ("guys"). The dune decides for everyone. Death 41. **The fight is
  described in one line; no wound detail.**

**8. Borealopelta** (about 110 million years)
- Research: a nodosaur found in 2011 by Shawn Funk at the Suncor Millennium Mine, Alberta; Royal Tyrrell Museum; armour
  and skin preserved in 3D, so it looks like a statue (Brown et al. 2017, *Current Biology*); reddish-brown pigment
  suggests countershading, which means even a tank needed camouflage; its stomach shows its last meal, mostly ferns
  (Brown et al. 2020, *Royal Society Open Science*); it was washed out to sea, drifted, and sank on its back
  ("bloat and float", sourced).
- Scale analogy: its weight (about 1,300 kg, sourced) against a small car.
- Twist: something out there hunted an armoured, 1,300 kg tank, and it had to hide.
- Doug beat: Doug rides the floating carcass out to sea like a raft, thumbs up. It flips. Doug is underneath. Death 42.
  **"Bloat" is the only body word**: no smell, gas or rot details.

**9. The Death March** (about 150 million years)
- Research: a horseshoe crab, *Mesolimulus walchi*, found in the Solnhofen limestone, Germany, dead at the end of its own
  trackway of about 9.7 m (Lomax & Racay 2012, *Ichnos*); the lagoon floor was hypersaline and low in oxygen (sourced); the
  longest trackway of its kind with the trackmaker at the end.
- Scale analogy: the trackway is about as long as a city bus (sourced length).
- Twist: you can read its last minutes step by step, as the tracks get more erratic toward the end.
- Doug beat: Doug follows the trail across the lagoon floor, curious. His own footprints end next to the crab's. Death 43.
  The cap sits at the end of two trails. Strong image, no joke needed beyond the narrator's kicker.

**10. The Ichthyosaur With Its Skin On** (about 180 million years)
- Research: a *Stenopterygius* from Holzmaden, Germany, with preserved skin, blubber and pigment cells; smooth, scaleless
  skin, countershading and blubber, which suggests it was warm-blooded (Lindgren et al. 2018, *Nature*); ichthyosaurs ate
  belemnites, squid-like animals whose hooklets are found in their stomachs (sourced).
- Scale analogy: the reptile looks like a dolphin but isn't related; its eye size against a dinner plate if the species
  supports it (sourced; otherwise its length against a canoe).
- Twist: it has blubber like a whale, 180 million years before whales existed in that form (state it only as the source does).
- Doug beat: **costume gag** callback (once in the episode): Doug in the squid costume (`squid_costume.json`), cap on top,
  "to get close". The narration explains: it ate squid-like animals, which is exactly why Doug is dressed as one. Death 44:
  the costume goes flat. No hooklets in Doug.

**11. The Gogo Fish Heart** (about 380 million years)
- Research: Gogo Formation, Western Australia, a Devonian reef; an arthrodire (armoured fish) with the oldest known heart, in
  3D, plus liver, stomach and intestine (Trinajstic et al. 2022, *Science*); the heart sits further forward than in sharks,
  which tells us how jawed animals' bodies were reorganised (sourced framing).
- Scale analogy: the age, 380 million years, is older than trees with seeds or than the first animals with four legs walking
  on land (pick one, sourced).
- Twist: the oldest heart ever found, still in its chest, still in 3D.
- Doug beat: Doug befriends a bigger arthrodire at the reef ("hi friend?"). Death 45 (off-frame crunch, cap floats). **No
  organs shown on Doug, and no heart jokes about Doug.** One line may call back the Devonian reef from 003 ("Doug has been
  to this reef before. It went badly then, too.").

**12. Stanleycaris** (the boss, about 506 million years, the longest item)
- Research: a small radiodont from the Burgess Shale, British Columbia; 84 specimens, many with the **brain and nerves**
  preserved; a large **third eye** in the middle of its head, plus two stalked eyes (Moysiuk & Caron 2022, *Current
  Biology*; Royal Ontario Museum); Burgess Shale animals were buried alive by underwater mudflows, which is why their soft
  parts survived (sourced, Parks Canada / ROM).
- Scale analogy: the animal is about the length of a pencil or a finger (sourced max length); half a billion years
  compared with something like "if the age of the Earth were a day..." only with sourced arithmetic.
- Twist: older than almost every animal on this list by hundreds of millions of years, and it is the one that still has its
  **brain**.
- Doug beat: the dial reads "506,000,000 YEARS AGO". Doug steps onto the Cambrian seafloor, meets the three-eyed thing
  and stares back. The mudflow arrives. Death 46.
- Final image (**not** a fossil cap: 003 already did that): back in the present, the time machine door opens on its own;
  the cap is lying on the floor inside. It came back alone. Kicker: one dry line about the cap. Then the outro.

**Death plan:** the counter is on screen from the first shot at **36** and ends at **46** (10 deaths; survivals at item 2
Blue Babe and item 5 Zhùr). By zone: Frozen 3, Stone 7.

## 8. Risks
- **Gore:** medium-low, but this topic sits near the line, because the frozen mummies are real carcasses. **Real photos
  are small, evidence-only, museum or press shots**: no close-ups of exposed tissue, no blood sampling of the foal, no
  necropsy images. All deaths are cartoon (X eyes, flat pose, ghost Doug, the lone cap). Wound details are banned:
  the raptor fight, the lion attack on Blue Babe and the stew are one line each.
- **Baby animals and kid appeal:** the cub, mammoth, foal and wolf pup attract kids and invite a sad or cute tone. **The
  baby animals never die on screen and are never the joke**; only Doug dies. No cute voices or nursery words. The jokes stay
  adult-coded (the 1984 stew dinner, the private moment at Messel, the dune "deciding").
- **Messel turtles:** mating is stated as science in one plain clause. No innuendo, no puns, nothing that risks the
  advertiser-friendly rating.
- **Indigenous naming:** Zhùr was named by the Tr'ondëk Hwëch'in. Name them correctly, with a source, and make no jokes in that item.
- **Avoid on purpose:** Myanmar (Burmese) amber specimens such as the "dinosaur tail in amber" (conflict-mineral ethics);
  human remains of any kind (bog bodies, Ötzi, mummies): off-lane and near our gore line; the "Lake Nyos" callback.
- **Viral myths to avoid or source:** "liquid blood" (item 4; cut if weak); "scientists will clone a mammoth soon" (no
  forecasts); "the turtles died because of the mating" (the paper says they sank into toxic water); "the fighting dinosaurs
  were killed by a sandstorm" (state it as "probably").
- **Facts that need strong sources (cut the claim if it can't be sourced):** Sparta and Boris ages and the cause of death;
  the Blue Babe age, the lion evidence and the 1984 stew; Lyuba's age, the mud and the lactic acid; the Batagaika foal's age,
  the blood and cloning claims, and the crater width; Zhùr's age, diet and naming; the Messel turtle count and the toxic
  water mechanism; the fighting dinosaurs' year of discovery and burial hypothesis; Borealopelta's weight, pigment, last meal
  and bloat-and-float; the trackway length and the lagoon conditions; the ichthyosaur skin, blubber and warm-blood claim;
  the Gogo heart's age and position; Stanleycaris' age, specimen count, brain and third eye. Prefer primary papers,
  museum press releases (ROM, Royal Tyrrell, AMNH, Field Museum, UA Museum of the North, Senckenberg) and Parks Canada;
  avoid listicles and the Siberian Times as a sole source.
- **Repetition and overlaps:** no mass extinctions (003). Item 11 shares the Devonian reef with 003, so one callback line
  is allowed and nothing more. This episode absorbs nothing else from the backlog; #27 "Drawn Wrong" and #17 "Glad Are
  Extinct" stay separate, and no item here may be reused there.
- **Description:** fill at most 4,800 characters, with at least one primary or institutional source per item.
