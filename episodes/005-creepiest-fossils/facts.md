Every factual claim in script.md is listed here with its source. Doug, his deaths, the time machine, the squid costume,
the narrator's asides and the jokes ("Doug did not make it to lunch", "with vegetables", "a very reasonable guess, which
in paleontology is high praise", "the referee was a sand dune", "any millennium now") are fiction or framing. Everyday
comparisons that rest on common knowledge are marked "common knowledge" and checked by arithmetic.

Checked 2026-10-01; draft 2 revisions noted inline. "Verified" = I read the quoted text on the page (WebFetch or curl). "Search snippet" = the page
returned 403/451 to WebFetch and the figure was confirmed through search-result text quoting that page; the screener
should re-open these if possible.

## Sparta the Cave Lion Cub
- Stockholm University news, 2021-08-06: https://www.su.se/english/news/articles/2021-08-06-sparta---the-28000-year-old-cave-lion (verified)
  - Found by mammoth tusk hunters on the banks of the Semyuelyakh River, Siberia; found 15 meters apart.
  - Sparta about 28,000 years old; Boris 43,448 years old by radiocarbon dating. 43,448 − 28,000 ≈ 15,000: "roughly fifteen thousand years apart".
  - Both one or two months old. Sparta "probably the best preserved Ice Age animal ever found", whiskers preserved;
    Boris "a bit more damaged".
  - "no signs of them being killed by a predator"; "maybe they died in a mudslide, or fell into a crack in the permafrost."
  - Initially thought to be siblings: CBS News, https://www.cbsnews.com/news/ice-age-cave-lion-cubs-discovered-russia/ (search snippet).
- Years found (2017 and 2018), female and male: Chernova et al., "First Description of the Fur of Two Cubs of Fossil Cave
  Lion... Found in Yakutia in 2017 and 2018", doi:10.1134/S0012496620030011, abstract via Europe PMC (verified):
  "finds of 2017-2018, Semyuelyakh River, Yakutia".
- Size: Chernova et al. 2021, *Quaternary* 4(3):24, https://www.mdpi.com/2571-550X/4/3/24 (search snippet):
  body length counting head 515 mm, tail 123 mm, weight after defrosting 0.8 kg. Script: "not counting her tail, about
  half a meter long"; "less than a one-liter bottle of water" (one liter of water = 1 kg, common knowledge; 0.8 < 1).

## Blue Babe
- University of Alaska Museum of the North, "Blue Babe": https://www.uaf.edu/museum/press/spotlight/blue-babe/ (verified)
  - "36,000 year-old mummified Alaska steppe bison"; gold miners discovered it in 1979 using hydraulic equipment to melt
    frozen earth; on display in the Gallery of Alaska.
  - Blue from vivianite "that formed on the skin when the body reacted with the minerals in the silt during its long burial."
  - Named after Babe the Blue Ox, Paul Bunyan's companion.
  - "Claw marks on the rear of the carcass and tooth punctures in the skin indicate that the bison was killed by an Ice
    Age American lion."
- Lion tooth fragment in the hide: IFLScience, https://www.iflscience.com/it-had-a-strong-pleistocene-aroma-in-1984-scientists-ate-a-50000-year-old-bison-in-a-stew-80973 (verified: "tooth fragments found embedded in Blue Babe's neck").
- Near Fairbanks: Yukon Beringia Interpretive Centre, https://beringia.com/exhibit/ice-age-animals/steppe-bison (verified).
- Steppe bison similar body size to modern bison, much larger horns: same Beringia page (verified).
- Modern bison bulls weigh up to 2,000 pounds (900 kg): NPS Yellowstone, https://www.nps.gov/yell/learn/nature/bison.htm (search snippet).
  Small car about 900 to 1,300 kg: common knowledge.
- American lion as a "cousin" of the cave lion (F1, draft 2): Barnett et al. 2009, "Phylogeography of lions (*Panthera leo*
  ssp.) reveals three distinct taxa and a late Pleistocene reduction in genetic diversity", *Molecular Ecology* 18(8):1668-1677,
  doi:10.1111/j.1365-294X.2009.04134.x (the screener's note said *Current Biology*; the paper is in *Molecular Ecology*).
  Abstract via Europe PMC, PMID 19302360 (verified): mtDNA from 52 lions gives three clusters, modern lions, cave lions
  "extending from Europe across Beringia", and American lions as "a separate population"; "The American lion appears to
  have become genetically isolated around 340 000 years ago". Two closely related lineages that split from a shared
  population: "cousin". Also summarised in the 2024 review "20th anniversary of the history of genetic research on cave
  lions", https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2950475924000133 (403 to WebFetch; listed in search results).
- Frame "a piece of the suspect's tooth still stuck in the victim": the lion tooth fragment in the hide (IFLScience, below).
- The 1984 stew: IFLScience (verified), quoting R. Dale Guthrie's account: dinner at Guthrie's Alaska home in 1984 (April 6);
  neck meat diced and simmered with stock and vegetables; "well aged but still a little tough", "a strong Pleistocene
  aroma"; "none of us suffered any ill effects from the meal." Also NPR, https://www.npr.org/2022/08/26/1119353933/bison-blue-babe-preserved-eaten (search snippet).
  Note: later dating suggested about 50,000 years; the script uses the museum's 36,000, attributed to the museum.

## Lyuba the Baby Mammoth
- National Geographic, "Ice Baby": https://www.nationalgeographic.com/magazine/article/mammoths (verified): found by Yuri
  Khudi, Nenets reindeer herder, Yamal Peninsula, May 2007; named Lyuba after Khudi's wife; about a month old; sediment in
  respiratory passages; "pickled" by lactic acid after death.
- Archaeology magazine, Top 10 Discoveries of 2007: https://archive.archaeology.org/0801/topten/lyuba.html (verified):
  "about 110 pounds" (50 kg), "the size of a large dog", "the most complete mammoth carcass ever found". Script: "often
  called the best-preserved mammoth ever found".
- Australian Museum blog: https://australian.museum/blog/museullaneous/the-story-of-baby-lyuba/ (verified): about 42,000
  years old; lactic acid produced by bacteria after death "essentially 'pickling' her soft tissues".
- Script says "a medical scan" (TTS fix T1); the source scans were CT scans.
- University of Michigan (Fisher et al. 2012): https://news.umich.edu/short-lives-violent-deaths-two-ct-scanned-siberian-mammoth-calves-yield-trove-of-insights/
  (search snippet): CT scans showed "a solid mass of fine-grained sediment blocking the air passages in the middle of
  Lyuba's trunk"; both calves died after inhaling mud and suffocating. Script: "near a river" (found near the Yuribei River).
- Weight "about forty-five kilograms" (A2, draft 2): Field Museum-era coverage gives about 92 pounds (42 kg), Daily Herald
  2010 below (search snippet: "45 inches long and weighed about 92 pounds"); Archaeology magazine gives about 110 pounds
  (50 kg). "About forty-five" sits between both.
- Organs intact, mother's milk in stomach, first display in the United States at the Field Museum in 2010: Daily Herald,
  https://www.dailyherald.com/20100303/news/42000-year-old-baby-mammoth-on-display-at-the-field-museum/ (search snippet).
- Sauerkraut is made by lactic acid fermentation: common knowledge.

## The Batagaika Foal
- Crater: USGS EROS, https://eros.usgs.gov/media-gallery/image-of-the-week/batagaika-crater-and-the-megaslump (verified):
  a thaw "megaslump" in permafrost, cliffs up to 86 meters, "expanding by more than 10 meters per year".
- Width 990 meters and the nickname "gateway to the underworld": Live Science 2024,
  https://www.livescience.com/planet-earth/arctic/siberias-gateway-to-the-underworld-is-growing-a-staggering-amount-each-year (verified via curl:
  "a 3,250-foot-wide (990 meters) depression"). Football field incl. end zones = 109.7 m; 990 / 109.7 ≈ 9.
- Foal: VICE, https://www.vice.com/en/article/scientists-discover-42000-year-old-liquid-blood-in-preserved-foal/ (verified via curl):
  "only a few weeks old", found August 2018 in the Batagai depression; Lena horse, extinct, native to Yakutia; skin,
  hair, muscle and organs well preserved; liquid blood samples "taken from the animal's well-preserved heart tissue";
  team from North-Eastern Federal University in Yakutsk and South Korea's Sooam Biotech; "after two months of trying and
  20 attempts" they had not managed to grow cells. Script says "more than twenty attempts" (A1): Smithsonian,
  https://www.smithsonianmag.com/smart-news/scientists-extracted-liquid-blood-42000-year-old-foal-found-siberian-permafrost-180971979/
  (search snippet: "more than 20 unsuccessful attempts" to grow viable cells; NEFU with Sooam Biotech).
- "The unsettling part came the next year": foal found August 2018, blood and urine reported April 2019 (VICE, CNN below).
- 42,000 years old, liquid blood and urine from the bladder, reported April 2019, called the oldest liquid blood:
  CNN, https://www.cnn.com/2019/04/18/europe/prehistoric-foal-liquid-blood-urine-trnd-scli-scn-intl (search snippet);
  Earth.com, https://www.earth.com/news/42000-horse-liquid-blood-urine/ (search snippet: "the oldest liquid blood on
  record, say scientists"). The script attributes the blood claim to the team ("reported", "described it as"), because
  it is a university statement rather than a peer-reviewed result.
- "Probably stuck in mud": Live Science, https://www.livescience.com/65268-oldest-liquid-blood-siberian-foal.html (search
  snippet: "likely by drowning in mud").

## Zhùr the Wolf Pup
- Meachen et al. 2020, *Current Biology* 30(24): R1467-R1468, doi:10.1016/j.cub.2020.11.011, abstract via Europe PMC
  (verified): July 2016, Klondike goldfields near Dawson City, Yukon; recovered during hydraulic thawing; named Zhùr by
  the Tr'ondëk Hwëch'in, "meaning 'wolf' in the Hän language"; most complete wolf mummy known; about 57,000 years old;
  died in her den during a collapse; ate aquatic resources.
- Sci.News summary of the paper: https://www.sci.news/paleontology/zhur-wolf-pup-09179.html (verified via curl): 41.7 cm
  snout to base of tail, 670 g; "died instantaneously by den collapse"; "particularly salmon"; Klondike River still
  has Chinook salmon.
- ScienceDaily (Des Moines University release): https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2020/12/201221160503.htm (verified):
  about 7 weeks old; found by a gold miner; "basically 100% intact".
- Found on Tr'ondëk Hwëch'in territory: Government of Yukon, https://yukon.ca/en/news/life-zhur-mummified-ice-age-wolf-pup-klondike (search snippet).
- Loaf of bread about 600-800 g: common knowledge.
- "Ice-age wolves are supposed to hunt big animals on the frozen grasslands": ScienceDaily release above (verified) contrasts
  her salmon diet with the land animals expected of Ice Age wolves.

## The Messel Turtles
- Joyce et al. 2012, "Caught in the act: the first record of copulating fossil vertebrates", *Biology Letters* 8(5):846,
  https://royalsocietypublishing.org/rsbl/article-abstract/8/5/846/49286/ (search snippet of abstract): pairs of
  *Allaeochelys crassesculpta* from Messel, the first fossil vertebrates that perished during copulation; turtles began
  in habitable surface water and died when their skin absorbed poisons while sinking into toxic layers.
- National Geographic: https://www.nationalgeographic.com/science/article/120621-fossils-turtles-mating-joyce-biology-letters-science (verified):
  nine suspected pairs recovered over the last 30 years; 47 million years old; males about 20 percent smaller; sank about
  30 feet (about ten meters); carbon dioxide from volcanic upwellings; Joyce: "There are no other vertebrate fossils to be
  found like this" and "a very, very reasonable guess."
- "In twenty twelve, paleontologists described nine pairs of fossil turtles from one German lake, and every pair died in the
  middle of mating" (draft 2 frame): Joyce et al. 2012 *Biology Letters* (published 2012) and National Geographic above
  (nine pairs, Messel, died while mating). Draft 3 (A4): hedged to "nine suspected pairs" and "every pair seems to have
  died" (NatGeo: nine suspected pairs, not all in contact), and the twist date-stamped "as of that study, no other... were
  known" (Joyce quote, 2012). Kicker "sank as a pair": same sources (P1 fix, no innuendo).
- About 20 cm long: CBS News, https://www.cbsnews.com/news/coitus-interruptus-ancient-turtle-sex-fossilized/ (search snippet).
  Dinner plate about 25-28 cm: common knowledge.
- Messel Pit near Frankfurt, former oil shale quarry, UNESCO World Heritage Site, ancient lake: UNESCO,
  https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/720 (Messel Pit Fossil Site).

## Borealopelta
- National Geographic 2017: https://www.nationalgeographic.com/science/article/nodosaur-dinosaur-fossil-study-borealopelta-coloration-science (verified):
  about 110 million years ago, Alberta; Shawn Funk, heavy-equipment operator, 21 March 2011, oil sands mine; front half
  preserved in 3D; Mark Mitchell spent more than 7,000 hours; red-brown pigment on back, absent from underside, read as
  countershading camouflage that makes it harder for predators to spot; carcass "back-first" on the sea floor.
- 5.5 m, 1,300 kg, ate almost exclusively ferns: CBC, https://www.cbc.ca/news/science/nodosaur-borealopelta-stomach-1.5600224 (search snippet).
- Last meal ferns plus charcoal, from a recently burned area: Brown et al. 2020, *Royal Society Open Science*; Live
  Science, https://www.livescience.com/nodosaur-dinosaur-ate-ferns.html (search snippet).
- Bloat and float: National Geographic graphic, https://www.nationalgeographic.com/magazine/graphics/making-of-a-dinosaur-fossil-nodosaur-illustrations (search snippet):
  belly-up carcass floated down river, kept afloat by gases, drifted out to sea, sank back-first.
- "Best-preserved armored dinosaur ever found": National Geographic 2017 (Brown, Royal Tyrrell Museum) and CBC,
  https://www.cbc.ca/documentaries/the-nature-of-things/face-to-face-with-a-perfectly-preserved-dinosaur-that-looks-like-it-was-alive-yesterday-1.7590095 (search snippet).
- Frame (draft 2) "Stand in front of it at the Royal Tyrrell Museum in Alberta ... you are looking at a dinosaur's face": on
  display at the Royal Tyrrell Museum, head and front half preserved in 3D with skin and armour (National Geographic 2017,
  verified; CBC documentary above, "looks like it was alive yesterday").
- Named after Mark Mitchell (*markmitchelli*): National Geographic / Inverse.
- Small car about 1,300 kg: common knowledge. Deer-type countershading not used.

## The Death March
- Lomax & Racay 2012, "A Long Mortichnial Trackway of *Mesolimulus walchi* from the Upper Jurassic Solnhofen
  Lithographic Limestone near Wintershof, Germany", *Ichnos* 19(3): https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/10420940.2012.702704
- Sci.News: https://www.sci.news/paleontology/article00554.html (verified): over 9.7 m, beginning and end, crab preserved at
  the end; about 150 million years ago; Bavaria; juvenile, 12.7 cm total length (script: "about thirteen centimeters");
  probably flung into the lagoon by a storm; predation ruled out for lack of evidence; footprints, telson drag marks and

- Lagoon very salty with an anoxic bottom; tracks become deeper and more erratic; the anoxia that killed it preserved it:
  Deposits magazine (Lomax), https://depositsmag.com/2020/10/31/the-worlds-longest-death-track-the-last-footsteps-of-an-ancient-horseshoe-crab/ (search snippet).
- "Longest complete death track ever found" (now in the frame, draft 2) and the crab at the end: Sci.News.
- City bus about 12 m: common knowledge.

## The Ichthyosaur's Skin
- Lindgren et al. 2018, *Nature* 564: 359-365. Phys.org: https://phys.org/news/2018-12-soft-tissue-jurassic-ichthyosaur-warm-blooded.html (verified):
  *Stenopterygius* MH 432, Holzmaden, Germany; about 180 million years; preserved part 85 cm, "roughly half of the original
  length"; smooth, scaleless skin; cell layers visible; pigment cells; countershading for camouflage; material
  "chemically consistent with vertebrate blubber", "the first direct, chemical evidence for warm-bloodedness in an
  ichthyosaur"; resembles dolphins but "not at all closely related".
- Countershading as in modern marine predators such as orcas: Sci.News, https://www.sci.news/paleontology/early-jurassic-ichthyosaur-warm-blooded-blubber-camouflage-06703.html (search snippet).
- Belemnite hooklets in *Stenopterygius* stomach contents from Holzmaden: Dick et al. 2016, *Palaeontology*,
  https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/pala.12232 (search snippet).
- 85 cm × 2 = 170 cm, about an adult's height: arithmetic.
- Blubber is the fat layer whales use to stay warm: common knowledge.

## The Gogo Fish Heart
- Trinajstic et al. 2022, *Science*, doi:10.1126/science.abf3289. Phys.org: https://phys.org/news/2022-09-million-year-old-heart-illuminates-evolutionary-history.html (verified):
  380 million years; Gogo Formation, Kimberley, Western Australia, originally a large reef; 3D heart with stomach,
  intestine, liver in an arthrodire; S-shaped, two chambers, smaller on top; hearts "in their mouths and under their
  gills" like sharks; scanned with neutron beams and synchrotron X-rays; larger leap between jawless and jawed vertebrates.
- Curtin University: https://www.curtin.edu.au/news/piece-by-piece-the-gogo-fossils-and-their-tale-of-evolution/ (verified):
  "Australia's first great barrier reef"; fish died and sank into oxygen-depleted deeper water and limestone nodules
  formed; arthrodires ranged from bottom feeders to "six-metre apex predators"; heart "250 million years" older than the
  previously documented vertebrate heart.
- Draft 3 (F3 fix): the "small bottom feeders to six-meter top predators" range is for arthrodires as a whole order
  (includes giants such as *Dunkleosteus*, which did not live at Gogo), not for the Gogo fish, which were mostly small
  (many under half a meter, e.g. *Incisoscutum* about 25 cm). Script now says "As a group, arthrodires ranged from...".
- Arthrodires: armored placoderm fish with plated head and thorax, Devonian: ScienceAlert, https://www.sciencealert.com/this-380-million-year-old-fossil-contains-the-oldest-heart-weve-ever-seen (search snippet).
- Family car about 4.5 m: common knowledge.
- Callback to 003's Devonian reef: series lore.
- Kicker (draft 2) "The Gogo reef is famous for keeping everything that sinks into it": framing of the Curtin source above
  (dead fish sank into oxygen-poor water and were sealed in nodules, preserving soft tissue).

## Stanleycaris
- Royal Ontario Museum release: https://www.rom.on.ca/news-releases/500-million-year-old-fossilized-brains-stanleycaris-prompt-rethink-evolution-insects (verified):
  506 million years; 268 specimens, mostly collected in the 1980s-90s; 84 with brain and nerve tissue; brain of two
  segments (protocerebrum, deutocerebrum) connected to the eyes and frontal claws; two stalked eyes plus a large central
  eye, never seen in radiodonts before; "no more than 20 centimetres long"; circular mouth lined with teeth; frontal
  claws with spines; Burgess Shale, Yoho National Park, British Columbia; Joseph Moysiuk; "as if we were looking at an
  animal that died yesterday". Rethink of the evolution of insects and spiders (radiodonts are early arthropod relatives).
- F2 (draft 2), volunteer find separated from the later study: ABC News, https://www.abc.net.au/news/science/2022-07-09/brain-three-eyed-marine-predator-500-million-year-old-fossils/101217790
  (verified via WebFetch): "Mr Moysiuk first stumbled across the unstudied fossils in a collection at the Royal Ontario
  Museum in Toronto while he was working as an undergraduate volunteer"; he "decided to study them for his PhD at the
  University of Toronto"; "lead author and PhD candidate". ROM release above: Moysiuk, University of Toronto PhD
  candidate, studied the 268 specimens. Script: "until a student volunteer, Joseph Moysiuk, came across them. Years later,
  for his doctorate, he studied all two hundred and sixty-eight." 
- Burial by mudflows; low oxygen and fine sediment slowed decay of soft parts: Smithsonian, https://www.smithsonianmag.com/arts-culture/evolution-world-tour-burgess-shale-british-columbia-canada-7698618/
  and https://naturalhistory.si.edu/research/paleobiology/collections-overview/burgess-shale (search snippets).
- New pencil about 19 cm: common knowledge.
- Calendar analogy (Earth 4.54 billion years old, USGS / common knowledge): 28,000 / 4.54e9 × 525,600 min ≈ 3.2 min;
  57,000 → 6.6 min ("the last seven minutes before midnight"). 506e6 / 4.54e9 × 365 ≈ 40.7 days before 31 December,
  i.e. about 21 November ("late November").
- "Everything on this list so far kept something it should have lost": summary of items 1-11.
