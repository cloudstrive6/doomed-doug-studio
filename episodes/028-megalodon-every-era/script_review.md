### FAIL items
**F1. The Nursery leaves out a contradiction from its own source.** "A newer look at the same kind of evidence puts it closer to four."
- That newer study is Shimada 2025, the same abstract the cold open relies on. It says the 3.6-3.9 m birth size makes "less likely the need for nursery areas".
- The item still presents the nursery as unchallenged and ends "For the megalodon, this is home."
- **Fix:** after "puts it closer to four.", add one clause such as: "and that same study thinks pups that big may not have needed a nursery at all."
- Then hedge the kicker slightly, for example "this was probably home." Add the quote to facts.md. (This would also make a better twist than the current one; see H2.)

**F2. The boss kicker is false by the episode's own facts.** "It lost the one it actually lived in."
- Megalodon also lived in The Nursery era (about 10 Ma), where it won, and in The Rival era (9-10 Ma).
- **Fix:** reword along the lines of "It lost the last one it actually lived in," or "It won at home ten million years ago, and lost the only era that was real."
- Keep the result clearly different from the structure of 023's closing.

**F3. The Trench's 1959 rebuttal lines rest on a fossil-dealer blog.**
- "The growth rate he used was far too fast, so the ages came out far too young."
- "The teeth had also been found alongside fossils of other sharks known only from millions of years ago."
- facts.md marks both "Verified" from buriedtreasurefossils.com and Southern Fried Science. I opened Southern Fried Science: it does **not** mention Tschernezky, the growth rate or the associated sharks. It only says that manganese crusts "grow slowly" and "take millions of years to form". The other corroborating pages are a cryptid fandom wiki and a Harvard maths homework PDF.
- **Fix:** keep the 1959 sentence and cite the *Nature* DOI for it. Reword the rebuttal to what a reliable page supports, for example: "Manganese crusts on the deep sea floor grow far more slowly than that. They take millions of years to form." (Southern Fried Science)
- Either cut the "alongside fossils of other sharks" sentence or source it to peer-reviewed or museum literature.
- Correct facts.md row 164 so it doesn't mark the blog as verified.

**F4. "The Pisco Formation, are famous for fossil whales" has no supporting source.**
- The cited Wikipedia Livyatan page doesn't say it. It says "well-preserved assemblage of marine vertebrates".
- **Fix (facts.md only):** cite Bianucci et al. 2021, PLoS ONE, "Taphonomy of marine vertebrates of the Pisco Formation... an outstanding Fossil-Lagerstätte", https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8282071/ . It describes rich whale and dolphin assemblages.
