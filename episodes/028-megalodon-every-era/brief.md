- Facts:

  - The first skull was found in a quarry near Maastricht in the 1700s. French troops took it to Paris in 1795, and it is still in

  - The same sea had *Cretoxyrhina*, a big lamniform shark (optional, one line; verify the size).

**3. The Giant Ichthyosaur** (about 202 million years ago, end of the Triassic, Somerset, England; Doug SURVIVES; narrator quietly

- Analogy idea: up to 25 m against a sourced everyday length (two buses end to end, or about a blue whale; pick one and source it).

### Wrong water.

**6. The Hothouse** (about 56 million years ago, the PETM, the tropics; death 230; MEG LOSES)
- Facts:
  - At the Paleocene-Eocene Thermal Maximum, tropical sea-surface temperatures may have passed 35-36 °C (Frieling et al. 2017,
    *Science Advances*; verify the figure and the location).
  - Parts of the ocean lost oxygen (verify with a PETM deoxygenation source).
  - Megalodon was partly warm-bodied: clumped isotopes in its teeth suggest a body about 7 °C warmer than the water around it
    (Griffiths et al. 2023, *PNAS*; verify the figure).
  - **Make the verdict carefully:** warm water plus a warm body plus low oxygen is the narrator's reasoning, not a study's result.
- Analogy idea: 36 °C sea water is close to hot-tub temperature (source a typical hot-tub range).
- Twist: **the megalodon's own advantage, a warm body, is the problem in water this hot.**
- Doug beat:
  - Doug brings a rubber duck and a towel, as if it's a spa. Death 230, cap floating with the duck.
  - Megalodon verdict: probably not.

**7. The Ice Age** (about 20,000 years ago, the Last Glacial Maximum; death 231; MEG LOSES)
- Facts:
  - Sea level was about 120 m lower than today, so much of the warm, shallow coastal shelf was dry land (verify a source such as
    Clark et al. 2009).
  - Baleen whales only became giants in about the last 4.5 million years, probably linked to ice-age upwelling that concentrated food in
    cold seas (Slater et al. 2017, *Proceedings B*; verify).
  - Megalodon's fossils are mostly from warm and temperate waters (verify with Pimiento et al. 2016 or similar, or cut).
- Analogy idea: 120 m against a sourced height (taller than the Statue of Liberty with its base).
- Twist: **the food got bigger than ever, and it moved to water too cold to follow.** Hedge the "too cold" part as reasoning.
- Doug beat:
  - The cage sits on a beach that should be under water. Doug walks to the sea. Death 231 (cold; cartoon ice-block Doug, never the
    007 time-machine ice-block ending).
  - Megalodon verdict: probably not.

**9. The Trench** (today, the abyssal Pacific; death 232; MEG LOSES; the myth item)

- Analogy idea: about 4 km of depth against a sourced height (several Burj Khalifas).
- Twist: **the deep-sea teeth that started the "still alive" rumour are exactly what proves it isn't.** The writer words this to match

### The real one.

**10. The Last Megalodon** (the boss; about 3.6 million years ago, Pliocene; death 233; MEG LOSES, for certain)
- Facts:
  - A 2019 re-dating of the fossil record found no reliable megalodon fossils younger than about 3.6 million years. That pushed the
    extinction a million years earlier than the old estimate of about 2.6 million (Boessenecker et al. 2019, *PeerJ*; verify).
  - In the Pliocene, about a third of marine megafauna genera disappeared, and the loss was linked to shrinking coastal habitat as
    sea levels swung (Pimiento et al. 2017, *Nature Ecology & Evolution*; verify the percentage).
  - A warm body means a big appetite (Griffiths 2023, one clause, already sourced at item 6).
  - **The one 009 callback:** early great whites were eating at nearly the same level (McCormack et al. 2022, *Nature Communications*).
    Competition is a possible factor, not a proven one. Say so.
- Analogy idea: 3.6 million years against a sourced span (older than the oldest known stone tools; source both dates).
- Twist: **every other verdict was a guess. This one is in the rocks. The greatest predator of its time died out in an ordinary
  ocean, with no asteroid and no supervolcano.**
- Doug beat:
  - Doug's cage is lowered beside the last megalodon. A small, early great white swims past. The megalodon turns and swims slowly into
    the dark. Doug waves ("bye friend?"). Death 233 (the cage cable snaps, played flat).
  - Red X on the MEG chalkboard. 4 / 10.
- **Closing image:** a modern beach. Doug, alive, digs a megalodon tooth out of the sand. He puts the cap on the tooth. Kicker, roughly:

- **Outro:** 30 words or fewer. Final counter (233) and one dry line. No recap and no subscribe pitch.
