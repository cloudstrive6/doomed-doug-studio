### Episode device: the CASE FILE
A crude MS Paint manila folder in the corner, next to the death counter. One line per item, handwritten: the fossil's catalogue name, then
**"CAUSE: …"** with the reading (FALL?, TRAP, ROCKFALL?, EAGLE, LEOPARD, CROCODILE, LIVER?, HYENA). **A "?" stays on every contested cause**;
it is never rubbed out to look certain. At the boss the narrator adds "DOUG, 2026. CAUSE: HE WENT." (adjust only if the writer finds a dryer line).
Episode-only; the art director may propose it for future "fossil evidence" episodes. Not a tray, not slots, no specimen beads (030).

### Items (in order)
**Bad luck.**
1. **Lucy's Fall** (AL 288-1, *Australopithecus afarensis*, Ethiopia, about 3.18 Ma)
   - Facts: Kappelman et al. 2016 (*Nature*) read compressive fractures in the right upper arm, and others, as perimortem, consistent with a fall from a tall
     tree and **arms thrown out to break it**; she probably climbed to sleep or forage. **Contested:** Johanson and White said the cracks are post-mortem
     (common in fossils). The script says both in one sentence each. She is about 1.1 m tall (source a height).
   - Scale analogy: the fall height the paper estimates, in "a two-storey house" terms (only the number the paper gives).
   - Doug beat: Doug climbs the same kind of tree to say hello. The branch decides. **Death 254.** The cap stays on the branch.
   - Opening hook fact (first sentence): she may have died falling out of a tree (rule 2 of the retention update).
2. **The Sinkhole** (Malapa, *Australopithecus sediba*, South Africa, about 1.98 Ma)
   - Facts: two skeletons (MH1, a juvenile male; MH2, an adult female) found together in a cave deposit (Berger et al. 2010, *Science*;
     Dirks et al. 2010, *Science*); the leading reading is that they **fell into a deep cave shaft or death trap**, possibly while looking for water, and
     were washed into the chamber; the bodies were buried fast enough that the skeletons stayed partly articulated. Found in 2008 when **a 9-year-old,
     Matthew Berger, picked up a rock with a collarbone in it** (well documented; one sentence, no joke about him).
   - Scale analogy: the shaft depth in everyday terms only if a source gives one; otherwise compare to a well.
   - Doug beat: "Doug did not agree to this." (the episode's one use, here, his first deliberate stop). Doug follows two silhouettes into the dark. **Death 255.**
   - Keep the two victims as silhouettes; never draw a juvenile falling.
3. **The Cave Ceiling** (Shanidar Cave, Neanderthals, Iraqi Kurdistan, about 60-70 ka; writer confirms the dates)
   - Facts: Shanidar 1 lived for years with a withered arm, a damaged eye socket and a bad leg, so his group must have looked after him (Trinkaus; Solecki).
     Ralph Solecki proposed that he and others there were **killed by rocks falling from the cave roof**; the twist is that he survived everything else.
     Pomeroy et al. 2020 (*Antiquity*, Shanidar Z) for the modern re-excavation. **The rockfall reading is a hypothesis: say "Solecki thought".**
   - Scale analogy: rock size only if sourced; otherwise "rocks the size of a fridge" is **not** allowed. Use what the dig report says.
   - Doug beat: Doug brings a hard hat. Hard hats are rated for something smaller. **Death 256.** The cap sits on the hard hat.

**Hunted.**
4. **The Eagle** (the Taung skull, *Australopithecus africanus*, South Africa, about 2.8 Ma)
   - Facts: Berger and Clarke 1995 (*J. Human Evolution*) and Berger 2006 (*Am. J. Physical Anthropology*): **talon punctures and raking marks in the eye
     sockets** match the damage crowned eagles leave on monkey skulls today; the cave deposit has the eagle's other prey too. First described by
     Raymond Dart in 1925 (the first African hominin fossil).
   - Scale analogy: a crowned eagle's weight against what it carries (sourced kg only), in grocery-bag terms.
   - Doug beat: **survival.** The eagle hunts things a fraction of Doug's size, so it takes the cap and leaves Doug. "Doug is fine. The cap is not."
     The cap comes back down two beats later (cap-survives gag). The narrator is quietly disappointed.
   - **Sensitivity (HIGH):** this victim was about three years old. Show the skull, the marks and the eagle on a branch. **No juvenile ever drawn
     being carried. No joke about the victim.** The joke is only on Doug and the cap.
5. **The Leopard** (SK 54, *Paranthropus robustus* juvenile skull cap, Swartkrans, South Africa, about 1.8-1.5 Ma)
   - Facts: two round holes in the back of the skull; C. K. Brain (1970, *Nature*; *The Hunters or the Hunted?*, 1981) showed they fit **the lower canines
     of a fossil leopard jaw from the same cave** (SK 349). Leopards cache prey in trees; the bones dropped into the cave shaft below. This flipped
     "man the hunter": our relatives were prey.
   - Scale analogy: the gap between the holes in "two fingers apart" terms (sourced mm only).
   - Doug beat: **the costume gag (once):** the leopard hunts animals our relatives' size, "which is why Doug is dressed as a *Paranthropus*" (adult-sized
     costume, red cap on top, played straight). The leopard looks, then looks up at its tree. **Death 257** (cut to the tree; cap hanging from a branch).
6. **The Crocodile** (OH 8 foot and OH 35 leg, *Homo habilis*, Olduvai Gorge, Tanzania, about 1.8 Ma)
   - Facts: Njau and Blumenschine 2012 (*J. Human Evolution*): **crocodile bite marks on the foot and ankle bones**, plus mammal carnivore marks.
     The Olduvai crocodile is *Crocodylus anthropophagus* (Brochu et al. 2010, *PLoS ONE*), and **the name means "human-eating"** (the twist).
   - Scale analogy: the crocodile's estimated length (sourced) against a car.
   - Doug beat: Doug wades in for a drink at the old lake edge with one foot. That was enough. **Death 258.** One callback to 027 at most:
     "The last crocodile only followed him." Cap floats.

**Slowly.**
7. **The Bad Liver** (KNM-ER 1808, *Homo erectus*, Koobi Fora, Kenya, about 1.6 Ma)
   - Facts: Walker, Zimmerman and Leakey 1982 (*Nature*): a layer of abnormal bone over the skeleton, read as **hypervitaminosis A**, possibly from eating
     **a carnivore's liver**; Skinner 1991 proposed bee brood instead; other diagnoses exist. The bone growth means she **lived for weeks or months**
     with it, which is why it is in "Slowly.", and someone may have kept her alive (Walker's reading; hedge).
     **Honesty:** say plainly that the cause is debated. No medical advice, no symptom list beyond the bone itself.
   - Scale analogy: how much vitamin A is in a small portion of carnivore liver vs a safe daily amount, only with a strong source; otherwise drop the number and keep the image.
   - Doug beat: Doug is offered the liver of the thing that tried to eat him in item 5. He eats it, out of spite. "Doug is fine." A calendar flips, weeks.
     **Death 259**, flat.

**Stored.**
8. **The Giant Hyena** (Zhoukoudian Locality 1, "Peking Man", *Homo erectus*, China, roughly 0.78-0.4 Ma; writer confirms dates)
   - Facts: Boaz et al. 2004 (*J. Human Evolution*): about two thirds of the *H. erectus* specimens show **carnivore tooth marks and breakage**, consistent
     with **the giant hyena *Pachycrocuta brevirostris*** using the cave as a den and bringing the bodies in; the older reading was cannibalism.
     *Pachycrocuta* was about lion-sized (source the kg). The cave yielded dozens of individuals (source the count; "about forty" only if sourced).
     **Kicker: the original fossils were lost in 1941 and have never been found** (casts survive). One sentence, no war jokes, no blame.
   - Scale analogy: the hyena next to a lion, or next to Doug.
   - Doug beat: Doug finds the den, says "hi friend?" to a hyena cub, the adult comes home. Cut to black. **Death 260, played flat** (round number: no milestone bit).
   - Closing image: the CASE FILE gets its last line. The cap is the one thing the hyenas leave. Outro (≤30 words): counter update plus one comment prompt.
     Suggested: "The cap has gone missing in that cave too. Unlike the fossils, it came home." (writer may improve; must stay dry, not about the 1941 loss itself.)

**Optional 9th item (only with strong sources; otherwise 8):** Little Foot (StW 573, Sterkfontein, about 3.67 Ma; Clarke; Granger et al. 2015),
fell down a shaft, slot it as item 2 or 3 in "Bad luck." and cut The Cave Ceiling if Shanidar's rockfall can't be sourced beyond Solecki's hypothesis.
**Do not** add a sabre-tooth item unless a peer-reviewed paper documents sabre-tooth damage on a hominin bone; Dmanisi carnivore claims need a primary source.

## 7. Risks
- **Gore (MEDIUM):** bite marks, punctures and fractures are the evidence. Show them **on the fossil only** (clean drawn bone, red arrows, red "?"), never on Doug,
  never wounds in flesh. Doug's deaths are cartoon (X eyes, cut to black, ghost Doug). No blood beyond a cartoon splash, and none at all at The Eagle.
- **Real individuals and juveniles (HIGH):** Taung, SK 54, MH1 and maybe others were young. **No juvenile is ever drawn dying, falling or being carried.**
  No joke lands on a victim; jokes land on Doug, the cap or the narrator. Matthew Berger (2008 finder) is named once, plainly.
- **Human remains and photos:** fossil photos only with a verified licence or public-domain status (art rule 9). Prefer our own drawings of the bones.
- **Kid-appeal (LOW-MEDIUM):** "Lucy" and eagles could drift cute. Keep the narrator dry and adult-coded; no "cavemen", no grunting, no cartoon ape faces.
- **Contested science (HIGH, the main fact risk):** Lucy's fall, Shanidar's rockfall and 1808's diagnosis are debated. Each gets "may", "one study", a named
  author, and the CASE FILE "?" stays. Malapa's death-trap reading, Taung's eagle, SK 54's leopard, OH 8's crocodile and Zhoukoudian's hyenas are
  stronger but still interpretations: attribute them. **Facts that need strong sources:** every date (Ma/ka), every size (eagle, leopard canine spacing,
  *Pachycrocuta* kg, crocodile length), the Zhoukoudian individual count and the two-thirds figure, the 1941 loss, Lucy's height and fall height.
- **Superlatives:** "the most famous human ancestor ever found" needs a source that calls Lucy that (Smithsonian, Natural History Museum or similar);
  otherwise the writer uses "one of the most famous". Same for "lion-sized".
- **Policy:** no human-on-human violence, no religion or "evolution debate" framing, no claims about modern populations or ethnic groups (the
  Zhoukoudian and Shanidar items are about the fossils, not modern China or Iraq).
