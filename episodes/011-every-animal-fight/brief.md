## 2. Escalation axis and items
**Axis: how badly an unarmed adult loses (severity), driven by measurable size, weapons and documented attacks.** The order runs from the
animals most people think they could beat to the largest land animal alive. The final item is the heaviest, and Doug's **100th death**.

**The spine of the episode is a real poll.** In 2021 YouGov asked British adults (and, in a separate poll, Americans) which animals they
could beat in an unarmed fight (goose, house cat, kangaroo, wolf, chimpanzee, gorilla, crocodile, grizzly bear, elephant and others). Where an
item was in the poll, its **frame line** gives the share of people who think they'd win; the **twist** is what the science says. The writer must
cite the exact YouGov article(s) and use only the numbers they print. Don't trust the numbers I remember: they are leads. If an item wasn't
in the poll, it gets a normal frame.

Spoken section headers (2 words; must not repeat 004's or 010's): **"Doug wins."** (folded into the opener, before item 1), **"Doug loses."**
(before item 3, about 17%), **"No contest."** (before item 7, about 58%, the midpoint zone shift). Background ramp: bright park green and pond
blue (wins), to dusty outback orange, forest dusk and rainforest green (loses), to grey jungle, muddy river brown, Arctic white-blue and a dark
red savanna sunset (no contest). The art director defines the ramp.

Each item lists 2-4 facts to research. Every number needs a source; cut what you can't source. "Doug loses" is shown as a cartoon outcome
(dust cloud, X eyes, ghost Doug, the floating cap). **Never describe the injury, the bite, or what the claw does to a body.**

### Doug wins.

**1. Goose** (the hook item; Doug wins on a technicality and survives)
- Facts: the YouGov share of Britons who think they'd beat a goose (verify, I recall about 6 in 10); geese defend nests by hissing, charging and
  striking with the bony bend of the wing (sourced, e.g. a wildlife agency or ornithology source); Canada geese weigh about 3-6.5 kg (sourced
  range); *optional, only if well sourced:* many goose-related injuries come from people falling while running away.
- Analogy idea: the goose's weight against a sourced everyday object (a bag of flour, a bowling ball), against a full-grown man.
- Twist (must land by 0:45, about 145 words; aim for 25-35 s): **the goose isn't trying to win. It's trying to make you leave, and it nearly
  always gets what it wants.** Or, if sourced, the fleeing-injury fact.
- Doug beat: Doug "wins": the goose walks off with his cap. Doug is fine. Doug is on the ground. The cap is recovered in the next shot (cap-survives gag).
  Counter stays at 93. This is item 1's near-death.

**2. House Cat** (Doug wins, survives)
- Facts: the YouGov share who think they'd beat a house cat (verify; one of the highest); a domestic cat weighs about 4-5 kg (sourced); a
  Mayo Clinic study of cat bites to the hand found that about **1 in 3 patients were hospitalised** (Babovic et al. 2014, *J Hand Surg Am*,
  verify; state it as one flat statistic, **no description of the infection, no treatment advice**); cats' reaction speed (only if a reputable
  source gives a number; otherwise cut).
- Analogy idea: a cat weighs about the same as a sourced object (a house brick or two, a large bag of sugar).
- Twist: **Doug won the fight. The hospital statistics suggest the cat may still win the war.** One line, then move on.
- Doug beat: the cat loses interest and leaves. Doug declares victory with a bandaged hand. "Doug is fine." Counter stays at 93.
  Adult-coded: the joke is Doug's pride, never the cat's cuteness.

### Doug loses.

**3. Red Kangaroo**
- Facts: the YouGov share who think they'd beat a kangaroo (verify); large males stand about 1.8 m or more and weigh up to about 85-90 kg
  (sourced); males fight by **balancing on their tail and kicking with both hind feet** (sourced); the tail works as a **fifth leg** when they move
  slowly (O'Connor et al. 2014, *Biology Letters*, verify); a fatal attack in Western Australia in 2022 was reported as the first in Australia
  since 1936 (sourced; **no victim name, no age, no details**).
- Analogy idea: a big male weighs about the same as a sourced object (a fridge-freezer, a grown man) and stands taller than a sourced object
  (a standard door, `front_door.json`).
- Twist: **it fights standing on its tail**, so both feet are free. Doug brought two fists. The kangaroo brought five limbs.
- Doug beat: Doug takes a boxing stance (boxing gloves = new gear, art director decides). Death 94. First death of the episode lands by about 2:40.

**4. Grey Wolf**
- Facts: the YouGov share who think they'd beat a wolf (verify); adults weigh about 30-80 kg depending on region (sourced); wolves hunt and fight
  as a **pack** (sourced); attacks on people are **rare** and most recorded cases involved rabies or wolves used to people (Linnell et al., NINA
  reports 2002 and 2021, verify the exact counts, which include a small number of fatal attacks).
- Analogy idea: a big wolf weighs about the same as a sourced object (a large suitcase full, a sack of cement) or is longer than a sofa.
- Twist: **the poll asked about one wolf. Wolves don't come as one.** State honestly and flatly that real attacks are rare.
- Doug beat: Doug squares up to one wolf (`grey_wolf.json`). Pan out: there are more. Death 95.

**5. Cassowary**
- Facts: one of the heaviest living birds, females up to about 60-70 kg and about 1.5-1.8 m tall (sourced); each foot has a **dagger-like inner
  claw** of about 10-12 cm (sourced); a 1999/2003 study of 221 cassowary attacks found that **most involved people who had fed them**
  (Kofron 1999/2003, verify); recorded human deaths are very few (1926 in Queensland, 2019 in Florida, sourced; **no names, no details**).
- Analogy idea: the inner claw is about the length of a sourced object (a teaspoon, a pen), and the bird is as tall as a sourced object (a fridge).
- Twist: **most attacks start with a snack.** The bird learned that people mean food, and gets angry when the food stops.
- Doug beat: Doug arrives with a bag of birdseed and the "hi friend?" bubble. Death 96. The cap lands on the seed bag.

**6. Chimpanzee**
- Facts: the YouGov share who think they'd beat a chimpanzee (verify); the popular claim that chimps are "5 to 8 times stronger" than people is a
  **myth**: a 2017 study measured chimp muscle at about **1.35 times** a human's power output, mostly from more fast-twitch fibre (O'Neill et al.
  2017, *PNAS*, verify); adult males weigh about 40-60 kg (sourced); chimpanzees have killed people in the wild and in captivity (sourced count or
  general statement from a reputable source; **no individual cases, no children, no description**).
- Analogy idea: a male chimp weighs about the same as a sourced object (a large dog, two bags of cement) and is shorter than Doug.
- Twist: **the myth was wrong, and it doesn't help Doug.** Only 1.35 times stronger, plus longer arms, bigger canines, and a lifetime of practice.
- Doug beat: Doug reads the 2017 study, relaxes, and challenges it to arm-wrestle. Death 97. Keep it off-screen (a dust cloud), no struggle shown.

### No contest.

**7. Gorilla** (the planned survival; the narrator is quietly disappointed)
- Facts: the YouGov share who think they'd beat a gorilla (verify; under 10%); adult male silverbacks weigh about 140-200 kg (sourced range);
  gorillas mostly **bluff-charge** rather than fight (sourced); chest-beating signals the gorilla's body size and can carry about 1 km (Wright et al.
  2021, *Scientific Reports*, verify); **no confirmed human deaths from wild gorillas** (sourced; if no reliable source says this, reframe as "attacks
  on people are very rare" and still keep Doug alive).
- Analogy idea: a silverback weighs about as much as a sourced object (two to three grown men, a vending machine is too heavy; check).
- Twist: **the chest beat is a size announcement.** The gorilla tells Doug exactly how big it is, from a kilometre away, so that nobody has to fight.
- Doug beat: Doug beats his own chest back. The gorilla looks at him and leaves. "Doug is fine." The narrator had the death drawn already. The counter
  stays at 97. Field guide page: "MAYBE".

**8. Saltwater Crocodile**
- Facts: the largest living reptile, males up to about 6 m (sourced); in a 2012 study it had the **strongest bite force ever measured in a living
  animal**, about 16,400 N (about 3,700 lbf) (Erickson et al. 2012, *PLoS ONE*, verify); it ambushes from water (sourced); crocodiles kill
  hundreds of people a year worldwide (CrocBITE or a reviewed source, with year and species scope; if the figure is for all crocodilians, say so).
- Analogy idea: the bite force is about the weight of a sourced object (a small car, `small_car.json`); the length is longer than a sourced
  object (a car, a canoe).
- Twist: **the strongest bite on Earth comes from something lying perfectly still.** It doesn't need to fight. It needs Doug to come close to the water.
  **Do not mention the "weak jaw-opening muscles, hold its mouth shut" fact**: it reads as a survival tip.
- Doug beat: Doug looks for the crocodile on the riverbank. The log next to him is the crocodile (`saltwater_crocodile.json`). Death 98. The cap
  floats on brown water.

**9. Polar Bear**
- Facts: the largest land carnivore, adult males about 350-700 kg (sourced); a study of 73 polar bear attacks from 1870 to 2014 counted 20 human
  deaths, and found that most attacking bears were **thin, hungry adult males** (Wilder et al. 2017, *Wildlife Society Bulletin*, verify);
  it is considered one of the few predators that may hunt a person as prey (attribute to the same or a reputable source).
- Analogy idea: a big male weighs about the same as a sourced object (a grand piano, a small car) and stands taller than a sourced object
  (a basketball hoop is too tall; a front door, check) on its hind legs.
- Twist: **the dangerous one is the hungry one.** It isn't defending anything. Optional line, if the poll supports it: "Only a few percent of people
  think they could beat a grizzly. Nobody asked about this one."
- Doug beat: Doug arrives on the ice with a fish as a peace offering. The bear is not interested in the fish (`polar_bear.json`). Death 99. The
  narrator notes, flatly, that the next one is a round number.

**10. Elephant** (African bush elephant; the boss, Doug's 100th death)
- Facts: the largest living land animal, adult males up to about 6 tonnes and about 3-4 m at the shoulder (sourced); the YouGov share who think
  they'd beat an elephant (verify; the poll joke is that it isn't zero); elephants in Amboseli could tell **from human voices alone** which people
  were a threat to them (Maasai men vs. Kamba men, women and boys) (McComb et al. 2014, *PNAS*, verify); elephants kill hundreds of people a
  year (for India, the Ministry of Environment's figures given to Parliament, about 500 a year; verify and give the year range; **no individual
  cases, no details**).
- Analogy idea: an elephant weighs about the same as a sourced object (four small cars, a school bus is too heavy; check), and is taller than a sourced
  object (a double-decker bus is too tall; a ceiling, check).
- Twist: **it can tell which humans are dangerous just by listening.** It listened to Doug and decided he was harmless. It was right. It doesn't matter.
- Doug beat: the shortest fight of the episode. Doug walks up, says "hi friend?". The elephant has already decided. Cartoon flattening only
  (Doug as a flat outline with X eyes, the classic cartoon gag), no impact shown. **Death 100.** The counter ticks to 100 and nothing celebrates.
- **Closing image:** Doug's field guide lies open in the red dust. Every page reads "NO" except goose, house cat ("WIN?") and gorilla ("MAYBE").
  The cap sits on top. Kicker about the cap: it is the only thing on this list with a winning record.

Deaths: 7 (94 to 100). Survivals: Goose, House Cat (wins), Gorilla (no fight). 10 items.
**Option for the writer:** if the draft comes in under 2,900 words, add **Honey Badger** as a survival (Doug loses, nobody dies; no recorded human

## 7. Risks
- **Kid-appeal risk (HIGHEST so far):** a goose, a house cat, a kangaroo, boxing gloves and a poll about fighting animals is exactly the kind of
  topic kids' channels make. Countermeasures: the narrator is dry and adult; jokes are about poll overconfidence, Doug's pride, bad science myths
  and the narrator's indifference, **never about cuteness, never slapstick sound effects, never a "funny animal" voice**. No baby animals, no
  pets in danger, no children in any fact or example. The creatures stay at the detailed cartoon tier and look dangerous. The thumbnail must
  read as nature horror, not a zoo poster.
- **Safety advice (HIGH):** **no "how to win" or "how to survive" content.** No "play dead", no "fight back", no "hold its mouth shut", no "make
  yourself big", no bear spray, no "run zigzag". Doug's choices are always wrong and are never framed as tips. The poll is about overconfidence;
  the video never suggests a person could or should fight any animal. Add one line to the description: "Entertainment and education only. Do
  not approach wild animals." (the titler owns the description).
- **Gore (medium):** fights invite injury description. **One clinical line per item at most**, and only about size, force or behaviour. Deaths are
  cartoon only: dust cloud, X eyes, ghost Doug, floating cap, the flat-outline gag at the elephant. No blood, no claws in bodies, no bites shown
  landing. No real photos of attacks or injuries. Real photos of the animals are fine with a verified licence.
- **Animal cruelty optics:** Doug never hurts an animal. He loses or is ignored. Doug "winning" at the goose and the cat means the animal leaves.
- **Human deaths:** counts with a source and a year range. No named victims, no ages, no stories. No children.
- **Facts that need strong sources (cut if unsourced):** every YouGov percentage (UK and US articles, 2021); goose wing strike and weight; the
  Mayo cat-bite statistic; red kangaroo size, tail-kick and fifth-leg study, the 2022 death and "first since 1936"; wolf attack counts (NINA);
  cassowary claw length, Kofron's 221 attacks and the feeding finding, the 1926 and 2019 deaths; chimp 1.35x (O'Neill 2017) and the myth;
  gorilla weight, bluff charges, chest-beat study, and "no confirmed deaths"; saltwater crocodile bite force (Erickson 2012) and annual death
  figures with scope; polar bear Wilder 2017 numbers; elephant mass, McComb 2014 voice study and India death figures. Every size is "about" or a range.
- **Myths to avoid:** "chimps are 5-8x stronger" (only as the debunked myth), "a kangaroo can disembowel a person" (gore and weakly sourced),
  "hippos kill 500 people a year" (not in this episode anyway), "the cassowary is the world's most dangerous bird" (attribute or cut), "a goose
  can break your arm" (cut unless sourced), "honey badgers are the most fearless animal" (cut unless sourced).
- **Duplication:** don't reuse Fossil's wording, item list or ranking. Our twists (the goose wants you gone, the cat wins later, five limbs, wolves
  don't come as one, attacks start with a snack, the myth was wrong, the chest beat is a size announcement, the strongest bite is lying still, the
  hungry one, it can tell from your voice) carry the video.
