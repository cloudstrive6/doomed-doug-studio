# Facts: 011 Every Animal Doug Could Beat in a Fight, Until He Dies

Every factual claim in script.md is listed here with its source. Fiction and framing are not claims: Doug, his deaths, the
field guide, the cap, speech bubbles, boxing gloves, the birdseed, the fish, the log, the "four full seconds", the
narrator's asides ("which suggests the British have met more geese", "not a word anyone had used about chimps before",
"did the arithmetic", "Doug has spent most of his life practicing nothing in particular", "they had heard enough humans to
know which ones mattered", "the birds did not change on their own; they were taught" as a reading of Kofron's finding).
Everyday comparisons are marked "common knowledge" and checked by arithmetic.

Checked 2026-10-09 (draft 1); updated for draft 2 per script_review.md (source swaps, three corrected claims, new beats marked "draft 2"). "Verified" = I read the claim on the page (WebFetch). "Search snippet" = the page blocked
WebFetch (403 / captcha / binary PDF) and the claim was confirmed through search-result text quoting that page; the
screener should re-open these if possible.

**Safety-advice check:** no "how to win / survive" content anywhere. Cut on purpose: goose "back away slowly / don't run"
advice, the cassowary "don't feed" advice (the feeding finding is stated as a study result only), crocodile "in-water
position" risk factor and jaw-holding, polar bear group-size finding, bear spray, playing dead. The crocodile line "one of the
two biggest factors in who survived was how much bigger the crocodile was than the person" is a study result, not an action;
the second factor is deliberately not named.
**Gore check:** no injury descriptions. The cassowary claw "can cut open an abdomen" (Artis) and the 1926 case details
are NOT used. **Children check:** chimpanzee victims in Uganda were mostly young children, the 1926 cassowary victim was
16, and some Australian croc victims were children: none of this is spoken; the script gives only flat counts or general
statements with no ages or names.

## Flagged items from the brief (status)
- **YouGov numbers:** only figures printed in the two YouGov articles are used. Kangaroo, wolf and chimpanzee have NO
  overall topline in the articles; the script uses the men-only figures the US article prints ("one in six American men"
  for wolves and kangaroos = 16-17%; "more than one in five American men" for chimps = 22%). Crocodile and polar bear: no
  number used. The UK article gives no wolf/kangaroo/chimp/crocodile numbers. Fox13's 71% goose figure is the men-only
  figure and is NOT used.
- **"No confirmed human deaths from wild gorillas":** not sourceable (Ian Redmond, quoted by BBC Earth, says rare
  fatalities have happened). Reframed per the brief to "very rare and usually defensive"; Doug survives anyway.
- **Kangaroo "both feet":** sourced to Dr Mark Eldridge (Australian Museum) in Australian Geographic.
- **Cassowary:** "one of the few birds known to have killed a person" (Smithsonian); the brief's "1999/2003": the 221 /
  150 / 75% numbers are Kofron 1999 (J. Zool.); Kofron 2003 is a different case-history paper (not used).
- **Cassowary deaths:** "only two are well documented" = 1926 Queensland and 2019 Florida. Scientific American notes an
  unverified third report (a zookeeper) that its author could not confirm; hence "well documented".
- **Crocodile death figures:** no reliable global annual figure exists (sources disagree; Gitnux/CrocBITE totals are
  untraceable). Script uses the Australian peer-reviewed dataset (Fukuda et al. 2015) with its scope stated.
- **Elephant deaths:** the India Rajya Sabha figures (471-628 a year, 2019-2023) are for Asian elephants, so they are NOT
  used for an African bush elephant item. Script uses the 2025 Zimbabwe study (African elephant + Nile crocodile).
- **Myths avoided:** chimps "5-8x" appears only on screen as a crossed-out myth; narration says "many times stronger",
  which ScienceDaily attributes to popular accounts. No "disembowel", no "world's most dangerous bird", no "goose breaks
  arms", no "most fearless animal".
- **Honey Badger:** cut in draft 2 (screener fix S1) so the first death lands at about 3:44. Its claims, sources and the
  IFAW "thick skin" swap are no longer needed and were removed from this file.
