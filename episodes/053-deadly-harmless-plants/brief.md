## 7. Risks
- **Safety and medical advice (highest risk on this topic).**
  - No foraging guidance, no "how to tell X from Y" identification keys, no first aid, no treatments or antidotes, no "what to do if".
  - No doses, no "N berries/leaves can kill", no lethal amounts, no preparation methods (teas, extracts, smoke). The screener fails any of these.
  - No children's poisoning cases or statistics. No pets ("poisonous plants for cats" is a big autocomplete; stay out of it).
  - Mad honey: never mention modern sale, recreational or aphrodisiac use.
  - Excluded plants: castor bean (ricin) and rosary pea (abrin), because they are weapon-adjacent toxins; *Cerbera odollam* and every self-harm
    association (including the gympie-gympie anecdotes).
- **Gore and body horror.** Skin reactions are the subject. Doug only goes flat cartoon pink. There are no blisters, rashes, burns, seizures or vomiting drawn
  or described in detail. Seizures are said once ("violent seizures") and never shown; deaths cut to black or X eyes.
- **Kid appeal.** Flowers and berries can read as a children's nature show. Keep the deadpan adult-coded, keep the drawings botanical and plain,
  no smiling plants, no nursery tone, no "kids, don't eat berries" framing (that is advice and kid-coded).
- **Honest title.** Every item must "look harmless" in a sourced way (a common garden or roadside plant, a vegetable look-alike, honey, milk, a
  beach tree). The boss honesty line ("deaths are rare") stays.
- **Overclaims to avoid:** "the deadliest plant on Earth" (unless attributed), "kills on touch" for anything, "one berry kills", "the rain will
  kill you", the oleander skewer story as fact, Nancy Hanks Lincoln's cause of death as certain, yew ages as exact.
- **Strong sources needed:** a USDA/ARS or extension source for water hemlock; Schep et al. 2009 (*Clinical Toxicology*); a toxicology source for
  taxine and oleandrin; Jansen et al. 2012 (*Cardiovascular Toxicology*) and Xenophon's *Anabasis*; a Lincoln historian or NPS for milk sickness;
  NHS or a government invasive-species page for hogweed; Gilding et al. 2020 (*Science Advances*) for the gympietides; Guinness World Records,
  UF/IFAS and Strickland (*BMJ*) for manchineel. Never a listicle or a viral clip alone for a number or a symptom.
