## 7. Risks
- **Kid appeal (highest risk).** Octopuses, and the dumbo octopus in particular, are a kids' favourite (the backlog lowered Demand for kids pollution).
  Keep it adult-coded and deadpan: no cute faces, no big sparkly eyes, no smiling octopus, no Disney references (never mention the elephant), no
  nursery voices. The dumbo octopus is drawn as a pale deep-sea animal with fins, not a mascot. The thumbnail must read as eerie, not cute.
- **Title delivery.** "Most Alien" is a framing superlative, not a measurable claim. The script must earn it with sourced strangeness (arms with most of the
  neurons, colour change while colour-blind, 53 months without eating, 20,000 at a vent, 6,957 m). Do not say octopuses are literally from space; the
  2018 "octopus DNA came from comets" paper is **not** to be used, even as a joke.
- **Overclaims to avoid:** "octopuses lead hunts" and "the mimic chooses its disguise" must be stated only as strongly as the papers state them. The brooder
  "never seen eating" is not "never ate".
- **Gore:** cartoon only. No ink-as-blood, no torn tentacles on the man o' war (shown as the octopus holding them, clean outlines), no severed octopus arms.
  The mothers' deaths (item 7) are stated, never drawn.
- **Sexual content:** blanket octopus males detach a mating arm; **do not mention it** (advertiser-friendly, and it is not needed). The size gap alone is the fact.
- **No safety advice:** nothing about handling octopuses or what to do if one grabs you.
- **Strong sources needed:** Sampaio et al. 2020 and 2024; Norman, Finn & Tregenza 2001; Godfrey-Smith et al. 2022; Jones 1963; O'Shea 2004 and Hoving & Haddock
  2017; Robison et al. 2014; Wang et al. 2022; Barry et al. 2023 (and MBARI pages); Jamieson & Vecchione 2020. Neuron counts from a primary or review source
  (e.g. Young 1971 via a recent review), not a listicle.
- **Repetition:** 008 already did the giant Pacific octopus. It may be mentioned once as a callback; it is not an item.
