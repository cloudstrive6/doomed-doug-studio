## New lines verified
**The Snow (Anchorage).** The new line is "a state wildlife biologist estimated that two or three hundred moose lived around Anchorage year-round, and the Alaska Science Forum reported another six or seven hundred arriving in winter." Rozell (21 July 1999) has "Sinnott estimates 200-300 moose live year-round in the Anchorage bowl area", and in the next sentence, with no name, "The population grows by another 600-700 moose in winter." The attribution is now exact, the year is correct, and the Chugach "funnel" sentence supports the line before it.

**The Train.** I checked every line against the text of Didrickson and Kramer, "When Moose and Train Meet" (Alaska Fish & Game magazine, September-October 1986):
- "an article in Fish and Game's own magazine" and "nineteen eighty-six": confirmed by the page footer and the ADF&G masthead.
- "in winters when the snow lies three to five feet deep, moose use the cleared railway line for walking and resting": confirmed. The text has "years of deep snow depth (three to five feet)" and "When snows exceed three feet, the moose find these man-made 'trails' a convenient place to walk or rest". The photo caption has "for walking and resting". The "one and one and a half meters" conversion is a fair rounding of 0.91-1.52 m.
- "Closing the railroad for the winter was no solution ... trains have to keep running on schedule": confirmed. The text has "The trains must run and they must run on or near schedule" and "Closing down the railroad in winter is no solution."
- "keep to its timetable through the whole Alaskan winter" in the frame: supported by the same passage.
- "A train cannot swerve" is common knowledge and not in the source. This is acceptable.
- "one experiment equipped the locomotives with sonic whistles ... didn't whistle ... froze silent": confirmed. The text has "mount sonic whistles on the locomotive", "The experiment failed" and "they froze into silence".
- "once a moose is on the track, after struggling through deep snow, it won't leave, even with a train bearing down on it" and "never learned to fear trains": confirmed nearly word for word. The script's "for the most part" hedge is kept.
- "last day of February nineteen eighty-five ... Anchorage to Fairbanks ... nineteen moose in one trip": confirmed (Locomotive 3006N).
- "By the end of that year ... three hundred and eighty-five": confirmed. The text has "the annual total for the entire length of the railroad came to 385".
- The repeated line "A moose will not get off the track for a train" has been cut, as I asked in round 2.
