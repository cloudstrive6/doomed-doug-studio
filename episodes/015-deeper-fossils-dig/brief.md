## 2. Escalation axis and items
**Axis: depth in the rock = age.** Doug digs one vertical shaft (a single continuous dig site, drawn as a cross-section), and each item is a
find at a deeper layer. The deeper look happens twice per item: **down** (the shovel reaches an older layer) and **in** (scientists look
inside the find). Then the time machine (003 lore) takes Doug to the find's last day. The fossil is the spoiler (005 rule, reused).

On-screen, episode-specific meter beside the death counter: **"DEPTH: N MILLION YEARS"** with a shovel icon, monotonic (only grows), like
009's NIGHT CLOCK and 013's DISTANCE TO HIPPO. The art director owns it. Spoken section headers (one word each, never used before):
**"Shallow."** (opener), **"Deeper."** (before item 4, about 30%), **"Deepest."** (before item 8, about 65%). Background ramp: sandy topsoil
tan, ochre, red-brown, slate grey, ending in near-black basement rock with a faint green (the Ediacaran sea). Art director defines the ramp.

Every number needs a source; cut what can't be sourced. Ages must be verified per item; if the verified ages break the order, swap items
rather than fudge the meter. **Never describe injuries.** Deaths are cartoon only: dust cloud, X eyes, ghost Doug, floating cap.

### Shallow.

**1. Ashfall** (about 12 Ma, Nebraska; the hook item; death 122)
- Facts: Ashfall Fossil Beds State Historical Park; a supereruption from the Yellowstone hotspot in what is now Idaho (verify: Bruneau-Jarbidge,
  about 1,600 km away) dropped ash over a waterhole; hundreds of animals died there (verify the count), mostly the barrel-bodied rhino
  *Teleoceras*; they died over days to weeks, not instantly; bone growths on the skeletons point to lung failure from breathing ash (Voorhies,
  University of Nebraska State Museum; verify the condition's name); smaller animals died first and the big rhinos last (verify the order:
  birds/turtles, then horses/camels, then rhinos).
- Analogy idea: 1,600 km is about the drive from a sourced pair of US cities (writer checks); the ash depth at the site as a sourced object height.
- Twist (by 0:45): **rhinos, in Nebraska. And the volcano that killed them was two states away.** Second beat: the biggest animals lasted longest,
  which is not the good news it sounds like.
- Doug beat: Doug at a sunny waterhole with a picnic; a grey "snow" starts. He builds a tiny ash-man. Death 122 (cough cloud, X eyes; one shot,
  no medical detail). "Doug did not agree to this." lands in the opener (see 3).
- No baby rhinos in drawings or narration (the discovery story features a young rhino skull; skip it).

**2. Wasps Inside Fossil Flies** (about 34-40 Ma, Quercy, France; the costume item; death 123)
- Facts: van de Kamp et al. 2018, *Nature Communications*: synchrotron X-ray scans of 1,510 mineralised fly pupae found 55 parasitoid wasps
  inside (verify both numbers); four new species described; one is named ***Xenomorphia resurrecta*** after the *Alien* xenomorph (verify);
  the wasps grew inside the developing fly and ate it from inside (state once, flat). Verify the age range of the Quercy fissure fillings.
- Analogy idea: a fly pupa is about the size of a sourced object (a grain of rice).
- Twist: **the fossils looked like flies. Inside, they were wasps.** The deeper look reveals a different animal.
- Doug beat: costume gag, Doug zipped into a fly-pupa costume, red cap on top. Narration states once that the wasps only target flies
  ("which is exactly why Doug is dressed as one"). Death 123 (costume goes flat, X eyes). Do not show the emergence; cut on the X-ray glow.

**3. The Snake That Ate the Lizard That Ate the Bug** (about 48 Ma, Geiseltal, Germany; Doug survives)
- Facts: Smith & Scanferla 2016, *Palaeobiodiversity and Palaeoenvironments*: a fossil snake (*Palaeopython fischeri*, verify) with a lizard
  (*Geiseltaliellus maarius*, verify) in its gut, and an insect inside the lizard (verify: likely a beetle); a "nested" fossil, only the
  second known three-level food chain (verify the claim); CT scanning revealed the insect.
- Analogy idea: three levels nested like a sourced everyday object (Russian dolls, the obvious one; the writer may keep it if no better).
- Twist: **the deeper they looked, the more lunch there was.**
- Doug beat: Doug arrives with a sandwich. The snake is about a metre long (verify) and has no interest in him. **"Doug is fine."** Counter
  stays 123. The narrator notes, flatly, that Doug was briefly the fourth level and nobody wanted him.

### Deeper.
(Before item 4, one beat while digging: the shovel passes the 66-million-year boundary clay and turns up **Doug's cap fossil from 003** in the
iridium layer. One line, the only 003 callback. He puts the old cap in his pocket.)

**4. Sue's Jaw** (about 67 Ma, South Dakota; death 124)
- Facts: Sue, the *T. rex* at the Field Museum, Chicago; smooth-edged holes in the lower jaw long read as bite wounds; Wolff et al. 2009,
  *PLoS ONE*: the holes match lesions from a *Trichomonas*-like infection (the parasite behind "frounce" in modern pigeons and raptors);
  similar lesions in a sizeable share of tyrannosaur specimens (verify the percentage); the hypothesis that it made eating hard (attribute; the
  cause of Sue's death is **not** known; say so).
- Analogy idea: Sue's skull length (verify, about 1.5 m) against a sourced object (a bathtub, a front door's width).
- Twist: **the most famous predator on Earth may have had a pigeon disease.**
- Doug beat: Doug, as a would-be vet, offers the T. rex a breath mint / looks in its mouth. Death 124 (chomp off-screen, dust cloud, cap
  floats down). No teeth touch Doug on screen.

**5. The Snake in the Dinosaur Nest** (*Sanajeh*, about 67-70 Ma, Gujarat, India; death 125)
- Facts: Wilson et al. 2010, *PLoS Biology*: *Sanajeh indicus*, a snake about 3.5 m long (verify), fossilised coiled around a crushed sauropod
  egg in a nest; found by Dhananjay Mohabey (Geological Survey of India) in the 1980s and recognised as a snake decades later (verify dates);
  the whole scene was buried suddenly, likely by a storm-driven sediment flow (verify). Verify Lameta Formation age vs Sue's; if older than
  Sue, keep this order; if younger, swap items 4 and 5.
- Analogy idea: 3.5 m is about the length of a sourced object (a small car).
- Twist: **it was waiting at the nest. Then the hill came down on both of them.**
- Doug beat: Doug "guards" the nest. Death 125 (mudflow, cartoon wave). **Kid-appeal rule:** eggs only. One flat mention of a hatchling is
  allowed if the source needs it; never draw a baby dinosaur.

**6. The Mammal That Bit a Dinosaur** (about 125 Ma, Lujiatun, China; death 126)
- Facts: Han et al. 2023, *Scientific Reports*: a badger-sized mammal (*Repenomamus robustus*) locked onto a beaked dinosaur
  (*Psittacosaurus lujiatunensis*) about three times its weight (verify the masses); both buried by a volcanic debris flow (the Lujiatun beds,
  sometimes called "China's Pompeii", verify the nickname's use); Hu et al. 2005, *Nature*: another *Repenomamus* with dinosaur bones in its
  stomach.
- Analogy idea: the mammal's mass as a sourced object (a house cat, a bowling ball).
- Twist: **we were told mammals hid from dinosaurs. This one was eating them.**
- Doug beat: Doug referees, whistle in mouth. The debris flow ends the match. Death 126. One callback line to 005's Fighting Dinosaurs is
  allowed ("Doug has seen a fight like this before."); no more.

**7. The Fish That Caught a Pterosaur** (about 150 Ma, Solnhofen, Germany; death 127)
- Facts: Frey & Tischlinger 2012, *PLoS ONE*: a large fish (*Aspidorhynchus*) with its teeth caught in the wing of a pterosaur
  (*Rhamphorhynchus*); the fish could not let go, and both sank into the oxygen-poor lagoon bottom (verify the interpretation); a small fish in
  the pterosaur's throat (verify). Solnhofen also gave us *Archaeopteryx* (one line, optional).
- Analogy idea: the fish's length (verify) as a sourced object.
- Twist: **the fish won. It just couldn't stop winning.** Three animals, one meal chain, nobody survived it.
- Doug beat: Doug fishing from a rock. He catches the fish; the fish catches the pterosaur; everybody goes down. Death 127. Cap floats on the lagoon.

### Deepest.

**8. Tanystropheus** (about 242 Ma, Monte San Giorgio, Switzerland/Italy; death 128)
- Facts: a reptile whose neck (verify: about 3 m, 13 elongated vertebrae) was longer than its body and tail combined (verify); Spiekman &
  Scheyer 2019, *Swiss Journal of Palaeontology*: two specimens preserved as **head and neck only**, with bite marks, interpreted as a predator
  targeting the neck (verify the wording). One flat line about what was found. **Never draw a severed neck**: show the fossil slab as bones in rock,
  and Doug's death off-screen.
- Analogy idea: the neck vs a sourced object (a giraffe's neck, about 2 m; verify).
- Twist: **the neck was the best thing about it. It was also the only thing left.**
- Doug beat: Doug compliments the neck ("nice"). Something large swims past. Death 128.

**9. Tapeworm Eggs in Shark Dung** (about 270 Ma, Brazil; death 129)
- Facts: Dentzien-Dias et al. 2013, *PLoS ONE*: a spiral coprolite (fossil dung) from a Permian shark held 93 tapeworm eggs (verify), one with a
  developing larva; the oldest evidence of tapeworms in vertebrates (verify the claim); the shark lived in a freshwater or brackish setting
  (verify).
- Analogy idea: 93 eggs in a space smaller than a sourced object (a coin).
- Twist: **the scariest thing in this rock isn't the shark. It's what was living inside the shark.**
- Doug beat: Doug meets the shark ("hi friend?"). Death 129. Wording rule: say "fossil dung" or "coprolite" flatly, at most twice; no toilet
  humour (kid-appeal).

**10. Tongue Worms** (about 425-430 Ma, Herefordshire, England; Doug survives)
- Facts: Siveter et al. 2015, *Current Biology*: *Invavita piratica*, a pentastomid ("tongue worm") parasite fossilised still attached to its
  host, a tiny ostracod crustacean (verify species and size); the fossil was studied by grinding it away 20 micrometres at a time and
  photographing every slice (verify the interval), which destroyed it; modern tongue worms live in the airways of vertebrates (one flat line, no
  human-health detail).
- Analogy idea: the parasite's length vs a sourced object (a grain of sand).
- Twist: **to see it, they had to destroy it.** And it's still hanging on.
- Doug beat: Doug checks the host with a magnifying glass. Too big to be a host. **"Doug is fine."** Counter stays 129. The narrator is
  quietly disappointed in one flat line (don't use "the narrator had the death drawn already").

**11. The First Hole** (about 550 Ma, Ediacaran, China; the boss; death 130)
- Facts: *Cloudina*, one of the first animals with a hard tube shell, built like stacked cones (verify); Bengtson & Zhao 1992, *Science*, and
  Hua et al. 2003, *Palaios*: tiny round bore holes through some shells, the oldest evidence of one animal attacking another (attribute:
  "the oldest known evidence of predation" per these authors; verify hole sizes and the share of bored shells); predation is proposed as
  one driver of the Cambrian explosion (attribute as a hypothesis).
- Analogy idea: a hole about as wide as a sourced object (a human hair is about 0.07 mm; verify the hole diameter first).
- Twist (the thesis line): **the scariest fossil at the bottom of the shaft isn't a monster. It's a hole. It's the first time anything on Earth
  was eaten, and everything in this video started there.**
- Doug beat: Doug arrives in a calm, green, empty sea, "the safest place he has ever been". Costume gag: Doug in a *Cloudina* tube costume, cap on
  top. A tiny dot appears. Death 130 (X eyes, costume intact apart from one hole). Played completely flat.
- **Closing image:** back at the surface, the shaft goes down out of frame; the shovel stands upright with **both caps** on its handle (today's,
  and the 003 fossil cap). Kicker about the caps (e.g. the only two things that came back up). The DEPTH meter reads 550.

Deaths: 9 (122-130). Survivals: The Snake That Ate the Lizard (3), Tongue Worms (10). 11 items. **Option for the writer:** if under 2,900
words, add a survival item, **The Bitten Trilobite**, between 10 and 11 (Cambrian healed bite marks, about 510 Ma;
must not use *Anomalocaris* as the biter on screen, since 009 did) or extend items 1 and 11. Do not add a death; the boss stays 130.

## 7. Risks
- **Kid-appeal (MEDIUM-HIGH):** dinosaurs pull kids' content. Countermeasures: the dry adult narrator; no baby animals drawn (eggs only at 5,
  no young rhino at 1); no "dino" nicknames, no roar sound effects, no cute proportions (the T. rex and pterosaur at the detailed cartoon tier,
  looking dangerous); "dinosaur" not in the title; parasites and predation carry the video. Fossil dung said flatly, no toilet humour.
- **Gore (MEDIUM):** Tanystropheus (head and neck only), parasitoid wasps, being eaten. One clinical line per item at most. Never draw severed
  parts, wounds or emergence; real fossil photos only with a verified licence, and only of bones/rock.
- **Medical advice (MEDIUM):** Sue's infection, tapeworms and tongue worms exist today. No human-health detail, no symptoms, no prevention tips.
- **Overstated claims (HIGH):** "Sue died of the infection" (unknown; say so); "the first predator ever" (say "oldest known evidence of
  predation"); "mammals hunted dinosaurs" (one specimen plus one stomach; say "at least sometimes"); the Ashfall death sequence; the "only
  second three-level food chain" claim; Tanystropheus neck posture/use (debated; skip).
- **Facts that need strong sources (cut if unsourced):** Ashfall eruption, distance, count, lung-failure evidence, death order; van de Kamp
  2018 counts, species names and age; Smith & Scanferla 2016 taxa and CT; Wolff 2009 and the lesion share; Wilson 2010 length, burial, discovery
  dates, Lameta age; Han 2023 masses and burial, Hu 2005; Frey & Tischlinger 2012 interpretation; Spiekman & Scheyer 2019 neck length and bite
  marks; Dentzien-Dias 2013 egg count, larva and "oldest" claim; Siveter 2015 species, size and grinding interval; Bengtson & Zhao 1992 /
  Hua 2003 hole sizes and share, age of *Cloudina*. Prefer primary papers, museums (Field Museum, University of Nebraska State Museum),
  Geological Survey of India.
- **Repetition check (done):** none of these items appear in 003, 005, 009 or 012 (005 used Messel, Solnhofen's horseshoe-crab death march,
  the Fighting Dinosaurs, the Burgess Shale; 012 used La Brea, Altamura and amber). Solnhofen returns with a different specimen; one callback
  only. No amber items at all (012 boss; Burmese amber is banned, 005).
- **Assets:** check `assets/library/` before requesting. Likely new: dig-shaft cross-section background, shovel, DEPTH meter, fly-pupa
  costume, *Cloudina* tube costume, Teleoceras rhino, nested snake/lizard X-ray, T. rex (detailed tier), Sanajeh + egg nest, Repenomamus +
  Psittacosaurus, Aspidorhynchus + Rhamphorhynchus, Tanystropheus, Permian shark, ostracod with parasite, fossil cap. The time machine exists.
- **Duplication:** don't reuse Spinosnack's or Mr. Science's wording or item order. Our twists (rhinos in Nebraska, the flies were wasps,
