## 2. Escalation axis and items
**Axis: up the ocean food chain (trophic rank and size).** Each item is a bigger predator that sits higher than the one before it.
The final item is the animal with no natural predator that hunts the top predators. Where a real, sourced predation link exists
("the next item eats this one"), say it; the script must not invent links that can't be sourced.

**The episode mechanic (the costume gag, from 002):** these predators hunt animals, not people, so at each item Doug is zipped into a
costume of that predator's real prey and plays it completely straight, red cap on top. The narration states this once in item 1
("which is exactly why Doug is dressed as a snail"). The costume escalates with the chain. Two deliberate breaks: at the crocodile
(no costume; it doesn't need one) and at the orca (Doug gets a boat instead).

Spoken section headers (two words or fewer): **"Reef."**, **"Open water."**, **"Apex."**, **"No predators."** (at about 70%).

### Reef.

**1. Mantis Shrimp** (hook item; peacock mantis shrimp) — costume: **snail** (`snail_costume.json` exists)
- Facts: the club strike speed and acceleration (Patek, Korff & Caldwell 2004, *Nature*); the strike creates a cavitation bubble that
  collapses with heat and light (Patek & Caldwell 2005, *J. Exp. Biol.*); it smashes snail and crab shells; claims about breaking
  aquarium glass only if a solid source is found (otherwise cut).
- Analogy idea: a fist the size of a fingertip (`fingertip.json`) that accelerates faster than a bullet leaving a gun barrel, *only* if
  the paper supports the comparison; otherwise a sourced everyday comparison.
- Twist (must land by 0:45, about 145 words): **it hits twice.** The club misses, and the collapsing bubble still lands a second blow.
- Doug beat: Doug dodges the club, gives a hopeful smile, then the bubble pops. Shell cracks (cartoon). Death 66.

**2. Giant Moray** — costume: **small reef fish**
- Facts: the second set of jaws in the throat that shoots forward to drag prey in (Mehta & Wainwright 2007, *Nature*); cooperative
  hunting with roving coral groupers, which recruit morays with a head-shake signal (Bshary et al. 2006, *PLoS Biology*).
- Analogy idea: a second jaw that shoots forward like a tape measure snapping back.
- Doug beat: Doug befriends the grouper (it's so friendly), the grouper headstands over Doug's hiding spot and shakes its head. The moray
  arrives. Death 67. "Doug trying to befriend the thing that will kill him" with a twist: the friend is the informant.

**3. Giant Pacific Octopus** — costume: **crab**
- Facts: largest octopus species (sourced weight range); can squeeze through gaps limited only by its beak; much of its nervous system
  sits in the arms; it has been recorded eating spiny dogfish sharks (Seattle Aquarium tank case and wild footage, source both or cut).
- Analogy idea: an animal as wide as a car (arm span, sourced) that can pass through a gap the size of a coin/lemon (sourced).
- Doug beat: Doug hides inside a jar with the lid screwed on. Octopuses open jars. Death 68. Kicker: it eats sharks, which is why the
  next stop is not a safe place either.

### Open water.

**4. Giant Trevally** — costume: **tern** (new)
- Facts: giant trevally leaping out of the water to catch fledgling sooty terns in flight at Farquhar Atoll, Seychelles. The writer must find and cite the peer-reviewed
  paper (reported around 2018; verify authors and journal, don't trust this note). Any claim about tracking birds' shadows needs the
  same paper or gets cut.
- Analogy idea: a fish the size and weight of a sourced everyday object launching itself out of the water.
- Doug beat: **Doug survives** ("Doug is fine."): he flies too high because the costume is ridiculous; the trevally hits the bird next to
  him. No death.

**5. Goliath Grouper** — costume: **shark** (new, also reused at the boss as a joke prop)
- Facts: size and weight (FWC / Florida Museum); swallows prey whole by suction; documented swallowing a blacktip shark off Florida
  (source the 2016 footage via a reliable outlet or cut); booming warning sound (sourced).
- Analogy idea: a fish as heavy as a sourced everyday object (a grand piano, if the numbers fit).
- Doug beat: Doug in the shark costume feels powerful for the first time. One gulp. Death 69.

**6. Tiger Shark** — costume: **sea turtle** (new)
- Facts: very broad diet; sea turtles are a known prey; non-food items found in stomachs (Florida Museum, sourced list only); arrives at
  French Frigate Shoals, Hawaii, when albatross chicks fledge (Meyer et al. 2010, *Marine Biology*, or the correct citation).
- Analogy idea: list three sourced stomach items that sound like a garage sale.
- Doug beat: one line ends the 006 streak: "In our last ocean episode, sharks never hurt Doug. That streak is over." Swallowed off-screen,
  cap floats. Death 70. No bite description, no human shark-attack facts.

### Apex.

**7. Saltwater Crocodile** — **no costume** ("Saltwater crocodiles don't need one.")
- Facts: largest living reptile; measured bite force (Erickson et al. 2012, *PLoS ONE*); rides ocean currents between coasts (Campbell
  et al. 2010, *J. Animal Ecology*, and/or Read et al. 2007, *PLoS ONE*); documented eating sharks (sourced, Australia).
- Analogy idea: crossing open sea a sourced distance, compared with an everyday trip (a city-to-city drive).
- Doug beat: Doug stands on the beach "safely out of the water". The crocodile surfs in on a current. Death 71 (cartoon: splash, cap).
  No death-roll detail on a person.

**8. Polar Bear** — costume: **seal** (new)
- Facts: classified as a marine mammal (US Marine Mammal Protection Act); a tracked female swam 687 km over 9 days (Pagano et al. 2012,
  *Polar Biology*); still-hunting at seal breathing holes for hours (sourced); keep any smell-distance claim only with a strong source.
- Analogy idea: 687 km compared with a sourced everyday distance.
- Doug beat: Doug in the seal costume pops up at a breathing hole, says "hi friend?". Death 72. This item rides the OctoLab polar bear
  wave; make it one of the strongest segments.

**9. Great White Shark** — costume: **the same seal costume, patched**
- Facts: breaching attacks on seals at Seal Island, False Bay (Martin et al. 2005); researchers use seal-shaped decoys (sourced);
  **the twist:** when orcas showed up at the Farallon Islands, white sharks left the area for the rest of the season (Jorgensen et al.
  2019, *Scientific Reports*).
- Analogy idea: the breach height compared with a sourced everyday height (a basketball hoop or a double-decker bus,
  `double_decker_bus.json`).
- Doug beat: the patched costume on a rubber ring (`rubber_ring.json`). Breach, cartoon launch into the sky, cap lands. Death 73.
  Kicker: the shark is afraid of what's next.

### No predators.

**10. Sperm Whale** — costume: **squid** (`squid_costume.json` exists)
- Facts: largest toothed predator; clicks measured around 230 dB re 1 µPa, among the loudest animal sounds (Møhl et al. 2003, *JASA*;
  explain that underwater dB isn't comparable to air dB, in one line, no more); dives beyond 2 km (sourced); eats giant squid (one
  callback to 001); adults circle young in a "marguerite" defence against orcas (sourced).
- Analogy idea: a dive deeper than a sourced number of stacked Eiffel Towers (`eiffel_tower.json`).
- Doug beat: Doug in the squid costume, echolocated in total darkness. The click arrives first (cartoon shockwave, cap blown off).
  Death 74. Kicker: the defence circle exists because of the next item.

**11. Orca** (the boss; no costume, Doug gets a small boat)
- Facts: hunt great white sharks off South Africa and remove the liver (Engelbrecht et al. 2019; Towner et al. 2022, *Ecology and
  Evolution* / *African J. Marine Science*, state it in one flat clinical sentence, no description); first documented killing of blue
  whales (Totterdell et al. 2022, *Marine Mammal Science*); Iberian orcas have been interacting with sailing-boat rudders since 2020
  (sourced, CSIC / Atlantic Orca Working Group); **no documented fatal attack on a human by a wild orca** (needs a strong source,
  phrased exactly as narrowly as the source; captive incidents are excluded and not mentioned).
- Analogy idea: the blue whale kill, compared by size with a sourced everyday object (a school bus line-up, `school_bus.json`).
- Twist: the most dangerous animal in the ocean, the one the sharks flee and the sperm whales circle against, has never killed a person
  in the wild. Doug survives the boss. The narrator is quietly disappointed (precedent: 001).
- Doug beat: the orcas take the rudder of Doug's boat and leave him drifting. He is alive. The counter stays at 74.
- **Closing image (the LIVE feed, standard for `ocean`):** the monitor with a red "LIVE" tag. An orca surfaces wearing Doug's red cap,
  exactly like the southern resident orcas' 1987 "salmon hat" fad, which reappeared in 2024 (source: Center for Whale Research / NOAA,
  or cut the comparison). Kicker: "Doug is fine. The cap has a new owner." The cap survives; it just isn't Doug's anymore. One-off use.

Deaths: 9 (66 to 74). Survivals: Giant Trevally and Orca.

## 7. Risks
- **Gore (high-risk topic, so the strictest line):** predators killing prey is the subject. Every death is cartoon: swallowed off-screen,
  a splash, X eyes, a flat costume, the cap floating. No blood beyond one bright cartoon-red splash at most, no bite or injury
  description, no death-roll detail, no real footage or photos of kills. The orca liver fact is one flat clinical sentence with no image
  of a carcass (a red "X" over a shark icon at most).
- **Real human victims:** none. No shark-attack, crocodile-attack or polar-bear-attack statistics, names or incidents. No captive-orca
  incidents. This is the advertiser-friendliness line for this episode.
- **Kid-appeal risk (medium-high):** animals plus costumes can read as a kids' show. Keep the narrator dry and adult, the jokes about
  bureaucracy, bad decisions and the narrator's indifference, never about cuteness. No bright "cute" redraws of the predators; creatures
  stay at the detailed cartoon tier. The thumbnail must look like nature horror, not a picture book.
- **Facts that need strong sources (cut the claim if it can't be sourced):** mantis shrimp speed/acceleration and the bullet comparison;
  aquarium-glass breaking; GPO eating sharks (two cases); giant trevally catching terns (the exact paper); goliath grouper swallowing
  a shark; tiger shark stomach items; croc ocean travel distances and croc-eats-shark records; polar bear swim record and any smell
  claim; Farallones white-shark departure; Gansbaai orca predation; blue whale kill; Iberian rudder interactions; the "no fatal wild orca
  attack on humans" statement; the salmon-hat fad dates; sperm whale dB figure with its underwater-dB caveat.
- **Medical/safety advice:** none. Don't tell viewers what to do if they meet any of these animals.
