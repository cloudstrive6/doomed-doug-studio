# Facts: 014 What Dying From Every Brain Parasite Would Feel Like (working)

Every factual claim in script.md is listed here with its source. Fiction and framing are not claims: Doug, his deaths and
survivals, the costumes, the cap, speech bubbles, the salad, the slug wave, the tiny suitcase, the counter "sigh", and the
narrator's asides ("the fish is dancing", "night shift", "thermostat", "layover", "a traveller", "somehow isn't very
reassuring", "the cap never had a brain to lose"). Everyday comparisons are marked "arithmetic" and checked.

Checked 2026-10-10 (draft 1); updated for draft 2 per script_review.md (F1-F3, rule 8, fix 8). "Verified" = I read the claim on the page (WebFetch). "Search snippet" = confirmed from
search-result text quoting the page (page blocked WebFetch or was not opened); the screener should re-open these.

**Medical-advice check:** no prevention or treatment advice, no drug names, no "see a doctor". Transmission routes are stated
as biology only. Not used on purpose: CDC cooking temperatures, washing produce, deworming dogs, gloves, the drug-resistance
finding in the Spirometra paper, the surgical/biopsy details.
**Gore check:** no tissue, surgery or skull-interior description. Not used: necrosis, haemorrhage, hydrocephalus, "brain stem
invaded", Balamuthia skin lesions on the face.
**Real patients:** the Spirometra case is given as year, country, symptoms (one line) and outcome only. Not used: his age,
ethnicity, years in the UK. Halicephalobus and Balamuthia cases are counts only; the paediatric skew of US Balamuthia cases,
the Sydney rat-lungworm case and the Halicephalobus kidney-transplant cluster are left out.
**Myths avoided:** the script corrects "pork puts tapeworms in your brain"; no Toxoplasma; no water-source detail for
Naegleria; Halicephalobus is described as rare, not spreading.
**Cut for lack of a source:** the gid sheep circling "towards the side of the cyst" (only head tilt is sourced as toward the
cyst; circling is reframed as a clue vets use, 62 percent accurate); a neurocysticercosis annual death toll (WHO page gives
none; script says only "can be fatal"); a killifish body-length comparison.
