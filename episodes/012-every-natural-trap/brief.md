## 2. Escalation axis and items
**Axis: how long the trap keeps you (duration of entrapment), from minutes to hundreds of millions of years.** The axis is set by the sourced time
each trap holds a body: until the snow is dug away, the water drains, the glacier melts out, the asphalt is excavated, the cave is surveyed, the
amber is cracked. Within "Short stay." the order follows severity (how fast it kills). The boss is the longest stay nature offers.

Spoken section headers (2 words; these must not repeat 004's "Hours./Minutes./Seconds." or 011's): **"Short stay."** (folded into the opener,
before item 1), **"Long stay."** (before item 6, about 55%, the midpoint zone shift), **"Permanent stay."** (before item 8, about 78%). The hotel
wording fits Doug's suitcase (the `places` prop). The narrator never explains it.
Background ramp (the art director defines it): white snow and ice blue, red-orange canyon, Antarctic navy, then grey avalanche, salt white, to
glacier blue-grey, asphalt black-brown, cave limestone ochre, and a deep amber gold for the boss. Each background is darker or warmer than the last.

Each item lists 2-4 facts to research. **Every number needs a source; cut what you can't source.** Deaths are cartoon only: X eyes, ghost Doug,
the floating cap, a sticker on the suitcase. **Never describe what the trap does to a body** (no suffocation, crushing or drowning detail).

### Short stay.

**1. Tree Well** (the hook item; death 101)
- Facts: a tree well is the hidden pocket of loose snow around the trunk of a conifer whose branches keep the snow from packing (sourced:
  a ski-industry or avalanche-centre source such as NSAA / deepsnowsafety.org or Northwest Avalanche Center); people who fall in **head-first** are
  the usual fatalities (sourced); in an experiment by the Northwest Avalanche Institute, **about 90% of volunteers placed in a tree well could not get
  out on their own** (verify the exact figure and source; if unverifiable, cut); deep-snow and tree-well immersion deaths happen every season in
  North America (sourced count with a year range, or cut).
- Analogy idea: the well can be deeper than a sourced object is tall (a grown man, a standard door, `front_door.json`).
- Twist (must land by 0:45, about 145 words; aim for 25-35 s): **no avalanche, no cliff, no storm. The tree built the trap, and it looks like the
  safest spot on the mountain.**
- Doug beat: Doug skis toward the "shady spot" for a rest. All that shows afterwards is two ski tips sticking out of the snow. Death 101. First sticker
  "TREE WELL" lands on the suitcase. This is item 1's death.

**2. Slot Canyon** (death 102)
- Facts: slot canyons are much deeper than they are wide and can be just a few metres wide (sourced, NPS or USGS); flash floods can arrive from
  storms that are **miles upstream, under a clear sky** where you stand (NWS / NPS); on 12 August 1997, a flash flood in Lower Antelope Canyon,
  Arizona, killed 11 people (sourced; **no names, no details of the deaths**); the water rose within minutes (sourced figure only).
- Analogy idea: the walls are taller than a sourced building (a 10-storey building) and the gap is narrower than a sourced object (a car, a sofa).
- Twist: **the storm that kills you is one you can't see.** The sky above Doug is perfectly blue.
- Doug beat: Doug photographs the beautiful light beams. A distant rumble. The cap washes out of the canyon mouth onto the sand. Death 102.
  **No "climb to high ground" line, and no weather-check advice.**

**3. Brinicle** (the costume item; Doug survives; the narrator is disappointed)
- Facts: under Antarctic sea ice, very salty, very cold brine sinks and freezes the seawater around it into a hollow tube of ice (sourced, BBC
  *Frozen Planet* 2011, filmed at Little Razorback Island, Ross Sea; plus a science source on brine rejection); when it reaches the seabed it spreads
  as a web of ice that **freezes slow animals such as sea stars and sea urchins where they sit** (sourced); filmed in time-lapse, the growth took
  hours (sourced figure only).
- Analogy idea: the tube grows longer than a sourced object (a lamp post, a person) in a few hours.
- Twist: **an icicle that grows down, through the sea, and freezes the floor when it lands.** It only catches what can't move fast enough.
- Costume gag: the brinicle can't hurt a person ("which is exactly why Doug is dressed as a sea star"). The cap sits on top of the costume.
- Doug beat: Doug the sea star crawls at sea-star speed. The ice web spreads. It stops one arm short. "Doug is fine." The narrator had already
  ordered the sticker. **No sticker** (survivals get none).

**4. Avalanche** (death 103)
- Facts: slab avalanches can reach about 130 km/h or more (sourced, an avalanche centre or a reviewed paper); when the debris stops, it **sets
  hard, closer to concrete than to powder** (sourced, an avalanche centre explaining densification or sintering); survival chances drop steeply after
  about the first 15-18 minutes of burial (Brugger et al. 2001, *Nature*, or Haegeli et al. 2011, *CMAJ*; verify and quote one figure only).
- Analogy idea: the speed is faster than a sourced object (motorway traffic); a cubic metre of avalanche debris weighs about the same as a sourced
  object (verify, e.g., a small car or a grand piano).
- Twist: **the snow is soft when it falls on you and hard when it stops.** The trap closes on its own.
- Doug beat: Doug makes a snow angel on a fresh slope. Cut to a smooth white slope with the cap sitting on it. Death 103. Sticker "AVALANCHE".
  **No rescue advice:** no beacons, no shovels, no "swim to the surface", no air pocket tips.

**5. Dead Sea Sinkholes** (Doug survives, barely; "Doug is fine.")
- Facts: thousands of sinkholes have opened along the Dead Sea shore since the 1980s (verify the count and year, e.g., the Geological Survey of
  Israel or a reviewed paper); they form because **the sea is shrinking** (more than about 1 m a year, sourced), and fresh groundwater moves in and
  dissolves a buried layer of salt (sourced); they can open with little warning, swallowing roads, fields and buildings (sourced; **no named victims**).
- Analogy idea: the drop in sea level each year is about the height of a sourced object (a kitchen counter, a desk); a large sinkhole is wider than
  a sourced object (a tennis court, a house).
- Twist: **the ground is disappearing because the sea is.** The water left, and it took the floor with it.
- Doug beat: Doug floats happily in the Dead Sea (the floating is real; one line only), then walks up the beach. The ground opens. Doug lands on his
  suitcase at the bottom, looks up, and gives a thumbs up. "Doug is fine." No sticker.

### Long stay.

**6. Crevasse** (zone shift at about 55%; death 104)
- Facts: crevasses can be hidden under **snow bridges** that look like solid ground (sourced, a glaciology or mountaineering-body source); glaciers
  move, so whatever falls in moves with the ice, often for decades (sourced); in 2017, the bodies of a Swiss couple who vanished in 1942 were found on
  the retreating Tsanfleuron Glacier, **75 years later** (BBC or Swiss press; **no names, no condition of the bodies, no photos**); Swiss and other
  alpine glaciers are now returning many missing climbers as the ice melts (sourced, e.g., Swiss police figures).
- Analogy idea: a deep crevasse is deeper than a sourced building is tall; a glacier moves about the length of a sourced object (a football pitch)
  each year (verify per glacier).
- Twist: **the glacier gives you back. It just takes about a lifetime to do it.**
- Doug beat (must not copy 007's cap-on-the-rim): Doug walks onto the smooth snow and is gone. Caption card "75 YEARS LATER": a melting glacier tongue
  and the red cap slides out, perfectly fine. Death 104. Sticker "CREVASSE". One flat line: Doug was back in the next segment, about 75 years early.

**7. Tar Pit** (La Brea, Los Angeles; death 105)
- Facts: Rancho La Brea is an asphalt seep that has trapped animals for at least about 50,000 years (sourced, the La Brea Tar Pits Museum / NHM
  Los Angeles); millions of fossils have been dug out of it (verify the figure); **most of the large mammals found are carnivores, especially dire
  wolves** (sourced; give the museum's ratio only if it prints one); a thin layer of asphalt, only a few centimetres deep, can hold a large animal
  (verify; if no source gives a depth, cut); it is still active today in the middle of the city (sourced).
- Analogy idea: the thin layer is about as deep as a sourced object (a smartphone's length, a mug); the seep is in a city park next to a sourced
  landmark (a museum).
- Twist: **one stuck animal is the bait.** The predators that came to eat it got stuck too, so the trap kept restocking itself for 50,000 years.
- Doug beat: Doug sees a stuck bison and goes to help, the "hi friend?" bubble. A dire wolf arrives. Then another. Everyone is stuck now. Death 105.
  Sticker "LA BREA". **No animal suffering detail**: the bison looks annoyed, not hurt.

### Permanent stay.

**8. Cave That Grew Over a Body** (Altamura, Italy; death 106)
- Facts: in 1993, cavers in Lamalunga Cave, near Altamura, found a Neanderthal skeleton **encrusted in calcite "cave popcorn" and fused to the
  cave** (sourced); it is dated to roughly 130,000-170,000 years ago (Lari et al. 2015, *Journal of Human Evolution*, verify the range); he likely
  fell into a sinkhole shaft and could not get out (attribute as "researchers think"); the skeleton has never been removed, because the rock has grown
  over it (sourced).
- Analogy idea: the time he has been there is about a sourced multiple of all recorded history (written history is about 5,000 years old).
- Twist: **the cave didn't just keep him. It started turning him into part of the cave.** The way out was never the problem. It was never coming.
- Doug beat: Doug drops into a dark shaft (no injury shown) and sits on a ledge with a torch, waiting. Time-lapse: little stalagmites grow up around
  him, the torch goes out, and he is a lump of rock with a red cap on top. Death 106. Sticker "ALTAMURA". **No skeleton close-ups and no real photo
  of the remains**; cartoon cave only.

**9. Amber** (the boss, the longest stay; Doug in costume; death 107)
- Facts: amber is fossilised tree resin; trees bleed sticky resin to seal wounds, and small animals that touch it get stuck and are sealed in
  (sourced); amber can preserve fine detail such as hairs, wings and even mid-action behaviour (sourced, a reviewed paper or museum); **the oldest
  arthropods found in amber are about 230 million years old**, from the Triassic of the Italian Dolomites (Schmidt et al. 2012, *PNAS*, verify);
  that is older than most dinosaurs (sourced timing).
- Analogy idea: a resin drop is about the size of a sourced object (a raindrop, a grape); 230 million years against a sourced span (the age of the
  Atlantic Ocean, or the entire time since the first mammals; verify).
- Twist: **every other trap on this list eventually gives something back. Amber gives back everything, in perfect detail, to whoever finds it in
  230 million years.**
- Costume gag: resin can't trap a person ("which is exactly why Doug is dressed as a mite" or "a gnat"; pick the animal from the actual find). The cap
  sits on top of the costume. A callback to 002's ant costume is allowed in one line.
- Doug beat: a slow golden drop rolls down the bark. Doug, in costume, waves at it ("hi friend?"). Cut to a polished gold bead on a museum shelf
  with Doug inside, mid-wave, perfectly preserved. Death 107. The cap sits **on top of the bead, outside it**: it got out.
- **Closing image:** the suitcase, covered in stickers (TREE WELL, SLOT CANYON, AVALANCHE, CREVASSE, LA BREA, ALTAMURA, AMBER), with the cap on top.
  Kicker about the cap: it is the only thing nature has ever let go of on time. "The suitcase still has room." is allowed (the `places` outro line).

Deaths: 7 (101 to 107). Survivals: Brinicle (narrator disappointed) and Dead Sea Sinkholes ("Doug is fine."). 9 items.

## 7. Risks
- **Safety advice (HIGHEST for this topic):** traps invite "how to escape" content. **None.** No tree-well or avalanche rescue steps, no beacons,
  shovels or air pockets, no "climb to high ground", no "check the weather", no "rope up on glaciers", no "spread your weight". Doug's choices are
  always wrong and are never framed as tips. The description gets one line: "Entertainment and education only. Nothing here is outdoor safety
  advice." (the titler owns it).
- **Real victims and grief (HIGH):** Antelope Canyon 1997, the 2017 Tsanfleuron couple and the Altamura Neanderthal are real deaths. Use dates and counts
  only: **no names, no ages, no families, no condition of remains, no photos of bodies or remains.** Antelope Canyon gets zero jokes in its fact
  lines; the Doug beat comes after, about Doug, never about the victims.
- **Gore (medium):** suffocation, crushing, drowning and freezing are all in scope. **Never describe the process of dying or the injury.** One clinical
  line per item at most, about time or force. Deaths are cartoon only: ski tips in snow, the cap on the slope, a sticker landing.
- **Kid-appeal risk (low-medium):** a sea-star costume and a cartoon amber bead could tip into nursery tone. The narrator stays dry and adult. The
  jokes are about the narrator's indifference, Doug's terrible holiday choices, and the hotel framing, never about cuteness. No children in any fact
  (this is why the 2018 Tham Luang cave rescue is not in the episode).
- **Animal welfare:** no suffering shown at the brinicle or the tar pit. The animals look stuck or annoyed, never hurt.
- **Facts that need strong sources (cut if unsourced):** the tree-well head-first pattern and the 90% experiment; Antelope Canyon date and toll; brinicle
  mechanism and filming details; avalanche speed, the "sets like concrete" densification and the survival-curve figure; Dead Sea sinkhole count and the
  shrinking rate; snow bridges, glacier movement, the 2017 Tsanfleuron find and the 75 years; La Brea's age, fossil count, carnivore share and
  thin-layer depth; Altamura's date (Lari 2015), calcite encrustation and "never removed"; amber's 230 Myr (Schmidt 2012) and preservation detail.
  Every duration on the axis needs its own source. Every size is "about" or a range.
- **Myths to avoid:** "quicksand swallows people" (not in this episode, and it was debunked in 004); "the Dead Sea sinkholes are caused by earthquakes"
  (they're caused by dissolution; attribute carefully); "the dinosaur in amber" (Myanmar; banned) and anything *Jurassic Park*-style about DNA in amber
  (cut, or one line saying it doesn't survive, sourced); "La Brea is full of dinosaurs" (it isn't; it's Ice Age mammals); "tar pits are tar" (it's natural
  asphalt; say so once).
- **Duplication:** don't reuse Simple Paint's items, wording or case order. Our twists carry the video: the tree built the trap, the storm you can't see,
  the icicle that grows down, soft falling and hard stopping, the floor left with the sea, the glacier gives you back, the bait restocks itself, the cave
  grows over you, and amber gives back everything.
