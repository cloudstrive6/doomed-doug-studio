## 1. Facts: pass

**Iguanodon horn (L86): verified, so the clause stays.**
- Victorian Web, https://victorianweb.org/sculpture/misc/hawkins1b.html, which I opened. Jacqueline Banerjee's 2009
  photo page of the sculptures as they stand in Crystal Palace Park describes them after the 2001-03 restoration. "One
  of the iguanodons stands on all fours ... the other is half reclining", and "both have been given a horn like a
  rhinoceros's". The page does not say "nose" in words, but a rhinoceros horn is a nose horn. This also confirms that
  there are two sculptures, one standing and one reclining, so the draft 2 wording "Both ... are still there" is correct.
- Corroborated by More Than A Dodo, https://morethanadodo.com/2021/06/03/tales-of-iguanodon-tails/ (opened). It says
  the models "now" stand in the park and that "the thumb spike was placed as a nose horn", and that scientists "ditched
  rhino resemblance too, though not in time for the Crystal Palace reconstruction".
- Residual risk is low. The Wikipedia caption "The dinosaurs during renovation in 2026" shows that the park is being
  restored again. I found no source saying the Iguanodon horns were removed or changed. "Still there today" is true as
  of what the sources show.

**Re-checked new or changed claims in draft 2**
- "Most of the twentieth century" (L30): Carnegie 1932-1979 (verified in draft 1). Smithsonian, which I opened, says
  the Yale Peabody Camarasaurus-like plaster head "was mounted on the museum's mighty sauropod in 1931". The AMNH page
  returns 403 and is snippet-only, but it isn't needed. The claim holds.
- The Yale news page (2019) only covers the 2019 skull removal. It does not support a replacement date, and the script
  doesn't use one.
- The Hallucigenia range of 1-3 cm and "the smallest would fit across a quarter" (10 mm against 24.26 mm) hold.
- Brontosaurus kicker: "more than twenty meters away" follows from the 21-23 m length.
