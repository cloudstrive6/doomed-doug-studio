## 7. Risks
- **Gore (highest risk this episode).** The 1997 attack involved severe wounds, and the paper describes them. **None of it goes in the
  script or the drawings.** No blood clouds, no bite marks, no missing flesh, no "chunks", no whale shown dying. Whales are "pulled out" and
  "hurt", and deaths cut to black before contact. Doug's deaths are X eyes or cut to black. No orca is shown biting anything.
- **Kid appeal (high: babies, babysitters, a costume).** No cute calf, no big eyes, no lullaby or nursery tone, no "mommy whale". The
  costume reads as deadpan bureaucracy, and the babysitting beat is adult-coded (shift rotas, the narrator's paperwork). The made-for-kids
  setting stays No.
- **Repetitive content.** This is the 3rd orca-adjacent episode in 8 slots (048, 051). The story belongs to the whales and THE RING keeps the
  fins small. No 021/051 orca facts are repeated (no Port and Starboard, no liver extraction, no prey menu).
- **Misleading packaging.** No title or thumbnail implies the whales win or that orcas are afraid of them. Pitman 2001 says otherwise, and
  037's brief already banned that framing.
- **Overclaims to avoid:** "always form a circle" (use "often", "have been seen"); exact formation times; "the loudest animal" (037, and
  not needed here); calf dive limits without a source; "the bulls come to help" (no evidence); a defecation smoke screen without a source.
- **Strong sources needed:** Pitman, Ballance, Mesnick & Chivers 2001, *Marine Mammal Science* 17(3) (anchor for items 1, 5, 6, 8 and 9);
  Arnbom, Papastavrou, Weilgart & Whitehead 1987, *J. Mammalogy*; Palacios & Mate 1996, *Marine Mammal Science* (item 7); Whitehead 2003 (book)
  and Whitehead 1996 or Gero et al. for social structure and babysitting; Deecke, Ford & Slater 2005 for silent hunting; Watwood et al. 2006
  (*J. Animal Ecology*) for dive figures; IUCN for status. Never a listicle or a competitor video for a number.
