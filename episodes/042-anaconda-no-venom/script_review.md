## 1. Facts (15 claims checked on the web or in the primary PDF)

**Confirmed:**
- **Rivas 2000 PDF**, which I extracted myself:
  - Table 3-1: 780 snakes over 7 years, female mass max 97.5 kg, male max 17.0 kg, TL max 521 cm.
  - "The average female anaconda is 4.7 times heavier than the average male" (SSD section). "Largest SSD reported in any tetrapod".
  - Breeding balls: 1 to 13 males (median 3), lasting 18 days on average (range 2-46). Pregnant females "do not eat for seven months".
  - "opportunistic predators that will take any prey they can kill and swallow".
  - Capybara incisors "razor sharp". Olivia and the deer antlers, "for several days".
  - Submerged snakes were "located by detecting them with feet or poles". The team "often stepped on spectacled caimans".
  - Item 9's "killed off ... near people" is supported: "Presumably the really large animals might have been killed off earlier and the animals that occur now in the areas where I studied (relatively close to human activities)...".
  - The five-hour caiman wrestle: the snake was about 2.5 m and "much smaller than the caiman". The dead male five days later was 247 cm.
- **Boback 2015 abstract** (PubMed E-utilities): within 6 s, femoral arterial pressure fell to half and CVP rose 6-fold. Heart rate nearly halved within 60 s and was 3.9-fold lower by the end (mean 6.5 min). The rats were anesthetized.
- **Boback 2012 abstract:** warm cadaveric rats with a simulated heart. Snakes constricted "longer and with greater total pressure", let go after the heart stopped, and naive snakes responded too ("at least partly innate").
- **Capano 2022** (EurekAlert): a cuff a third of the way down the body, and the boas "breathed using the ribs further back".
- **NYZS reward** (Wikipedia, citing Murphy & Henderson 1997): $1,000 from Roosevelt in the early 1900s and $50,000 in 1980, "no attempt to claim the reward has ever been made".
- **Rivas 1999** (anacondas.org/strike.htm): predatory strikes on two field assistants.
- **Murphy & Crutchfield 2019** (ASI summary): four attacks, both 1956 attacks fatal. The narration's "very few, and hard to verify" is now correct.
- **Smithsonian 1996:** "wade barefoot ... feeling with their toes".
- **Henry Vilas Zoo:** eyes and nostrils on top of the head, "completely submerged for ten minutes".
- **CDC FastStats:** men 20 and over average 199.0 lb (about 90.3 kg), data from 2021-23.

**Errors:**
- **Contradicted, on-screen text at line 346:** the card reads `"PREDATORY STRIKES ON 2 RESEARCHERS. DOCUMENTED KILLINGS OF PEOPLE: NONE."`
  This is the claim fix 2 removed, and Murphy & Crutchfield report two fatal cases. It also says "researchers" where the source
  and the narration both say field assistants. On-screen text counts as a claim.
- **Unsourced and inaccurate, line 294:** "Anacondas eat caimans, the smaller crocodilian relatives of alligators..." facts.md cites
  Rivas et al. 2024 for this, but that is an anaconda paper. "Smaller" is also wrong as a general statement: the black caiman is the
  largest member of the alligator family and usually outgrows the American alligator. Only the spectacled caiman, the species in
  this item, is small.
- **Slanted, line 48:** "Not every scientist accepts the split yet". The Reptile Database lists *E. akayima* as "preliminarily
  considered a synonym of *E. murinus*", and Wikipedia now says the proposed species is "considered to be synonymous". So it isn't a few
  holdouts: the standard checklist currently rejects the split, and "yet" implies acceptance is on its way. The twist is the item 1 payoff.
  If it overstates the case, the comments will correct it, and that costs trust in the first minute. facts.md marks this row Search only and doesn't
  cite the Reptile Database.
- **Unsourced generalisation, line 28:** "...and so is every giant snake you have ever pictured." facts.md has no row for this.
  It is supported for anacondas (the male maximum is 17 kg), but "every giant snake" also takes in pythons.

## Required fixes
1. **Line 346 (stage direction, on-screen text):** `"PREDATORY STRIKES ON 2 RESEARCHERS. DOCUMENTED KILLINGS OF PEOPLE: NONE."`
   Change the card to match the narration and the sources, for example `"STRIKES ON 2 FIELD ASSISTANTS. KILLINGS OF PEOPLE: VERY FEW, HARD TO VERIFY."`
   Do not show "NONE" anywhere.
2. **Line 294:** "Anacondas eat caimans, the smaller crocodilian relatives of alligators that share their pools."
   Name the species and drop the general "smaller", for example: "Anacondas eat spectacled caimans, small crocodilian relatives of
   alligators that share their pools." In facts.md, replace the Rivas 2024 citation with a real source for "spectacled caiman,
   a member of the alligator family" (for example Britannica "caiman" or the IUCN Crocodile Specialist Group species page, with the URL).
3. **Line 48:** "Not every scientist accepts the split yet, but if it holds, ..."
   Say where the dispute actually stands, for example: "For now, the main reptile checklist still counts them as one species, but if the
   split holds, ...". Keep "scientists are still arguing about her name." In facts.md, add the Reptile Database *E. murinus* page
   (https://reptile-database.reptarium.cz/species?genus=Eunectes&species=murinus), Fetched, quoting "preliminarily considered a synonym".
4. **Line 28:** "...and so is every giant snake you have ever pictured."
   Change "snake" to "anaconda" ("...and so is every giant anaconda you have ever pictured."), and add a facts.md row citing Rivas 2000,
   Table 3-1 (female max 97.5 kg, male max 17.0 kg). Or keep "snake" and add a source that covers pythons too. Otherwise cut it.
5. **Line 12 (sentence 1):** "One wild anaconda weighed ninety-seven and a half kilos, more than the average man, and kills without venom."
   Fix the tense. For example: "One wild anaconda weighed ninety-seven and a half kilos, more than the average man, and it kills without
   venom." Or "...and it had no venom at all." Keep the word count so that "The Female." still lands by word 35.

**Recommended, not blocking:**
- Line 360: consider "He may now be one of the very few that nobody can verify either", to match the new honesty line.
- Line 340 visual: the "9 M" tape runs along two minivans, which is 10.4 m. Have the director end the tape short of the second bumper.
