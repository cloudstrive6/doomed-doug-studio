## 1. Facts (14 claims checked on the web, plus all five flags)

**Confirmed:**
- Boback 2015: within 6 s arterial pressure is down by half and venous pressure is up sixfold; heart rate nearly halves within 60 s; it is 3.9-fold lower by the end (mean 6.5 min); the rats were anesthetized.
- Boback 2012: warm rat cadavers with a simulated heart; the snakes let go soon after the heart stopped; snakes naive to live prey responded too ("partly innate").
- Capano 2022: a blood-pressure cuff a third of the way down the body, and the boa breathed with ribs further back.
- CDC average US man is 199.0 lb (90.3 kg, 2021-23). The 97.5 kg comparison holds.
- Rivas 2000: a 97.5 kg / 5.21 m female out of 780 snakes over 7 years. The SSD is "larger than ... any other terrestrial vertebrate".
- NYZS reward: $1,000 from Roosevelt in the early 1900s, $50,000 by 1980, over 30 ft, "no attempt to claim" (Wikipedia, citing Murphy & Henderson 1997).
- Rivas et al. 2024: genetic data from nine countries, *E. akayima*. It is disputed in *Bionomina* 37 (Vásquez-Restrepo et al.; Dubois et al.). The script's hedge "Not every scientist accepts the split yet" is correct.
- Rivas 1999 (anacondas.org/strike.htm): predatory strikes on two field assistants. He says there were "no documented attacks" before his.
- Rivas et al. 1999 caiman note: all six males and two females were killed by spectacled caimans; the five-hour wrestling match; the dead male five days later. The source counts its total as "twelve events", and the script's "at least eight" is the conservative reading. Accepted.

**Errors:**
- **Contradicted, "nobody noticed for centuries" (item 1).** The northern form was named in 1801/1802 (*Boa gigas* Latreille) and long treated as a subspecies, *E. murinus gigas*. Dirksen & Böhme 1998 reviewed its status. The 2024 critics argue that *akayima* is at best a junior synonym of that older name. So people did notice; they disagreed about whether it was a separate species. facts.md marks this row "Fetched", but its source (Linnaeus 1758 to 2024) does not support "nobody noticed".
- **Contradicted, "there has never been a documented case of a green anaconda killing a person" (item 9, FLAG 2).** Murphy & Crutchfield, *Giant Snakes: A Natural History* (2019), report four green anaconda attacks on people (1863, two in 1956, 2007) and say both 1956 attacks were fatal. My source for this is the African Snakebite Institute's summary of the book. The script's only source is a popular magazine making an absolute negative claim. The brief's rule applies here: "if a reliable source records a case, say 'very few'".
- **Overstated, "the squeeze costs the snake nothing in air" (item 6).** Capano 2022 shows the boa shifts which ribs it breathes with. It does not show the squeeze has zero cost. The coverage also notes that the rear lung has a poor blood supply.
- **Imprecise, "On average, a female was almost five times heavier than a male" (item 1).** The writer's own note says the all-age table means give 3.4x, and the 4.7x figure compares adults. As written, the claim is wrong for the population the sentence describes.

## Required fixes
1. **Item 1:** "...the heaviest snake on Earth was quietly two different snakes, and nobody noticed for centuries."
   Replace "and nobody noticed for centuries" with a claim the record supports, for example "and it took genetics to make the case" or "and
   scientists are still arguing about her name". In facts.md, change that row and cite the *Boa gigas* (Latreille 1801/1802) and
   *E. murinus gigas* history (Dirksen & Böhme 1998, *Salamandra* 34(4)).
2. **Item 9:** "...but there has never been a documented case of a green anaconda killing a person."
   Change to the "very few" wording the brief asks for, for example: "...but reports of one actually killing a person are very few, and
   hard to verify." The Doug line after it ("the exception that nobody can verify either") still works. In facts.md, add Murphy &
   Crutchfield 2019 (two fatal 1956 reports) next to Rivas 1999, and resolve FLAG 2.
3. **Item 6:** "The worst part is that the squeeze costs the snake nothing in air."
   Soften it, for example: "The worst part is that the squeeze barely gets in the way of the snake's own breathing." Keep the
   marker phrase.
4. **Item 1:** "On average, a female was almost five times heavier than a male, ..."
   Change to "A grown female averages almost five times the weight of a grown male, ...", which is what Rivas's 4.7x compares. In
   facts.md, cite the page and table for the adult comparison.
5. **Opening (rule 2 and retention):** "One wild green anaconda weighed ninety-seven and a half kilos, with no venom at all."
   Put the man comparison and the kill stake into sentence 1 and keep "The Female." within word 35. For example: "One wild green
   anaconda weighed ninety-seven and a half kilos, more than the average man, and it kills without a drop of venom." Then trim the
   payoff line (for example "...feeling for the biggest one barefoot.") so the name still lands by word 35.
6. **Item 1, removing the duplicate:** "The heaviest female weighed ninety-seven and a half kilos, heavier than the average American man, while the heaviest male weighed seventeen."
   Don't restate 97.5 kg. Lead with the new fact, for example "The heaviest male in the whole study weighed seventeen."
   The split-screen tags can stay.
7. **Item 9 (TTS):** "was five meters twenty-one, about one minivan".
   Change to "was just over five meters, about one minivan" or "five point two meters".

**Recommended, not blocking:**
- Bring The Jaws Doug beat up to 15 words or more.
- Make the Teeth kicker 8 words or more.
- Vary one "Doug has died. Again."
- Reword "picks on something that bites back" so it doesn't echo item 4.
