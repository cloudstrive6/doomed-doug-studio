## The why-thread (WHY SO BIG? clipboard), all spoken as hypotheses
| Reason (item) | Source | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| COLD (2): cold slows life down; growth takes longer, maturity later, so the body can end up larger | Wikipedia, "Deep-sea gigantism" (summarises Timofeev 2001: colder water may raise cell size and lifespan, which raises maximum size; the Bergmann's rule parallel): https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deep-sea_gigantism ; NOAA Ocean Exploration log, "Journey into Midnight" (delayed sexual maturity frees energy for growth; no set size at which they stop growing): https://oceanexplorer.noaa.gov/explorations/19biolum/logs/jun17/jun17.html | Spoken as "One idea is". |
| FOOD IS RARE (4): food mostly falls from above, rarely; a bigger body burns less energy for its size and stores more, so it can wait longer | Wikipedia, "Deep-sea gigantism" (food scarcity, Gad 2005; Kleiber's law: metabolic cost per unit mass falls as size rises): https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deep-sea_gigantism ; NOAA log above (deep prey is scarce) | "One idea is". |
| LIVE LONGER (6): in cold water some animals grow slowly for a very long time | Nielsen et al. 2016 via William & Mary (slow growth, centuries of life): https://www.wm.edu/news/stories/2016/old-and-cold-extreme-longevity-in-greenland-sharks-.php ; Wikipedia gigantism page (cold, longer lifespan, larger size) | Framed as "the next reason", a proposed explanation. |
| BRING YOUR OWN FOOD (7): at vents food is made on site by bacteria, so the worm can grow very fast | See item 7 rows (Lutz et al. 1994; Guinness) | The connection to size is our framing of the sourced growth record. |
| MORE OXYGEN (10): cold water holds more oxygen; maximum amphipod size tracked oxygen (Chapelle & Peck 1999); not every animal fits and it's still tested | USGS Water Science School, "Dissolved Oxygen and Water" ("Cold water can hold more dissolved oxygen than warm water"): https://www.usgs.gov/special-topics/water-science-school/science/dissolved-oxygen-and-water ; Chapelle & Peck 1999, *Nature* 399: 114-115, "Polar gigantism dictated by oxygen availability" (1,853 benthic amphipod species, 12 sites; "maximum potential size (MPS) is limited by oxygen availability"): https://marineinfo.org/doc/publication/229362 ; counter-evidence, sea spiders (Woods et al. 2009, PMC): https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2679066/ | "nearly two thousand species" = 1,853. |
| PRESSURE? crossed out (top of 11, as the cable passes the fish record): none of the main ideas says pressure makes animals bigger; pressure builds limits | Wikipedia gigantism page (proposed causes are cold, food scarcity, reduced predation, oxygen; reduced size-with-depth trends in polar seas argue against hydrostatic pressure as the driver); Yancey et al. 2014, *PNAS* 111: 4461-4465, doi:10.1073/pnas.1322003111 (pressure sets a depth limit for fish), NIWA release: https://niwa.co.nz/news/scientists-discover-how-low-fish-can-go | The molecule and the limit depth are no longer spoken (001 already explained them). |
