# Facts: 033 Why Hyenas Are Deadlier to Doug Than Lions (draft 2)

Every factual claim in script.md is listed here with its source. Checked 2026-10-31.

How each source was checked:
- **Fetched** means I read the passage on the page with WebFetch.
- **Search** means I confirmed the claim only from search-result text that quotes the page. Either the page couldn't be fetched (hyaenidae.org DNS failure, UNESCO 403, WWF 403, a PDF I couldn't extract) or I didn't open it.
- **Secondary** means the page reports a primary study (usually Kruuk 1972) that I could not read myself. The primary reference is given.

The screener should re-open the **Search** rows and the **FLAG** rows first.

**FLAGGED (screener decision needed):**
1. **"About ten to one" in Ngorongoro** (The Lie). This comes from the IUCN SSC Hyaenidae Specialist Group page (hyaenidae.org), which I saw only as quoted search text because the site would not resolve. It is consistent with the Hyena Project's current figures (up to 550 hyenas, 25 to 40 adult lions). If the screener can't open hyaenidae.org, use the fallback: cut "about ten to one" and say "where hyenas far outnumbered lions".
2. **Bite force 4,500 N** (The Bite). The SDZWA fact sheet attributes it to Binder & Van Valkenburgh 2000 as "c.4500 N, one captive individual; likely higher in the wild". I could not read the paper itself, so the script says it exactly that way: one captive hyena, wild ones probably higher.
3. **Whoop "used to recruit the rest of the clan", "up to about five kilometers"**. The source is Lehmann et al. 2022, *Proc. R. Soc. B*, abstract ("long-range recruitment calls called whoops (up to 5 km range)"), seen as quoted search text. ScienceDaily (Fetched) confirms the paper, the authors and a range of about three miles. SDZWA (Fetched) gives "audible by humans >5 km" (Kruuk 1972).
4. **Resolved in draft 2.** The "September / bush fires" line is cut, because the Begg PDF does not contain it. It is replaced by the Begg finding (7 of 13 attacks on people sleeping in the open, Niassa National Reserve), which I read in the PDF (Fetched).
5. **Harar hyena holes and the porridge ritual** come from one magazine source, Notre Dame Magazine's piece on Marcus Baynes-Rock's fieldwork. BBC Wildlife (Discover Wildlife) independently confirms the "hyena gates" built into the city walls.

**Cut from the brief because no adequate source was found:** the "93%" hunted share, and any Kruuk hunted-share figure (the only exact figures found were on forums). The Masai Mara 95% is used instead. Also cut: hyena heart size; "store food in water" (Kruuk diving under floating carcasses is used instead); "lions leave large bones; hyenas finish them"; Cooper 1991's "four hyenas per lioness" (secondhand only; the Hyena Project's "six times" is used); the IUCN "most abundant large carnivore in Africa" wording (not found in the assessment summaries); a lion pride's average size (forum-quality sources only); the Malawi "hot season" line (the Wikipedia sentence has no citation); and an Africa-wide lion-against-hyena death count (none found, so the honesty line uses Tanzania and the eastern Serengeti).
