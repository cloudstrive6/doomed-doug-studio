### Dusk.

**1. The Lie** (death 261)
- Facts:
  - Spotted hyenas **kill most of what they eat**. Kruuk's Ngorongoro and Serengeti work (*The Spotted Hyena*, 1972) put the hunted share far above the scavenged share;
    use the exact figure only from Kruuk or a source that quotes him precisely. The popular "93%" is unverified (my check found it only on a forum); use it only if the writer finds it in Kruuk or a peer-reviewed review.
  - In the Ngorongoro Crater, where hyenas far outnumbered lions, **lions usually scavenged from hyena kills**, not the other way round
    (IUCN SSC Hyaenidae Specialist Group, "Crocuta association with other species", hyaenidae.org, citing Kruuk 1972; Höner et al. 2002 for the later crater data).
  - Where the numbers were more even (Serengeti, Timbavati), each took about the same from the other (same source). Use this to keep the claim exact: it is the crater, not all of Africa.
- Analogy: the hunted share against something everyday (for example "nine meals in ten they caught themselves", only if the figure supports it).
- **Twist by 0:45 (about 145 words):** the famous scavenger-and-thief picture was the wrong way round. The ones standing over the kill when the film crews arrived at dawn were often lions eating hyena food.
- Doug beat:
  - Doug lays out a plate of leftovers at dusk "for the scavengers" and sits beside it to watch. Eyes appear. The hyenas walk past the plate.
  - **"Doug did not agree to this."** (once, here)
  - Death 261. Cut to black; the cap sits beside the untouched plate.
  - Scoreboard: HYENA 1.

**2. The Bite** (death 262)
- Facts:
  - Spotted hyenas crack and eat large bones; their skull and teeth are built for it (Binder & Van Valkenburgh 2000, *J. Zool.*; Tanner et al. 2010 on skull mechanics). Give bite force only as a measured or estimated figure from the paper, with its units.
  - They digest bone, and their droppings turn **white** from the calcium (Kruuk 1972 or a museum/zoo source).
  - **Lion comparison:** lions leave large bones; hyenas finish them (sourced, or cut).
- Analogy: the bone they crack (a giraffe or buffalo leg bone) against something everyday, size or thickness from a source.
- Twist: the lion eats the meal. The hyena eats the plate it came on.
- Doug beat: Doug offers a dog-chew bone "as a gift". The hyena takes Doug's walking stick instead and bites it in half. Death 262. Kicker: "The cap is the only thing in this video a hyena has not tried to digest."
- Scoreboard: HYENA 2.

**3. The Chase** (death 263)
- Facts:
  - Hyenas run prey down over long distances; Kruuk gives chase lengths in kilometres and top speeds (verify; often quoted ~60 km/h). Lions mostly stalk and rush over a short distance and give up fast (Schaller 1972, *The Serengeti Lion*; verify).
  - Their heart is large for their body size (verify, or cut).
- Analogy: the chase distance against a familiar run (a 5 km park run), both sides sourced.
- Twist: against a lion, the plan is "don't be close". Against a hyena, the plan is "don't get tired", and Doug is always tired.
- Doug beat: Doug outruns a lion's rush in his head, stops to catch his breath at 200 m, looks behind him: a hyena trotting, not even hurrying. "still?" Death 263. The cap keeps rolling a little further.
- Scoreboard: HYENA 3, LION 1 (tie: both fast over the first sprint; the narrator gives the lion one tick for honesty).

### Midnight.

**4. The Laugh** (Doug SURVIVES; "Doug is fine.")
- Facts:
  - The "giggle" is not laughter. It encodes the caller's **age and social status** and is given in frustration or under stress (Mathevon et al. 2010, *BMC Ecology*).
  - The **whoop** is a long-distance call that carries for kilometres and can bring clan members in (Holekamp lab or East et al.; give the distance only as the source states it).
- Analogy: the whoop's range against something everyday (it carries further than a car alarm).
- Twist: the noise everyone thinks is a hyena laughing is a hyena being annoyed.
- Doug beat: Doug laughs back to be friendly. The giggling gets louder and higher. A low-status hyena backs off, frustrated, and leaves. "Doug is fine." He does not know what he said. The narrator is quietly disappointed.
  Field guide: "MAYBE" pencilled in.
- Scoreboard: HYENA 4. Counter stays at 263.
- **Re-hook:** "The one that left went to get the others." (Only if the writer can tie whoops to recruitment with a source; otherwise a non-factual narrator line about what happens next.)

**5. The Clan** (death 264)
- Facts:
  - Spotted hyenas live in clans that can number in the dozens and, in places, over a hundred (Holekamp et al., Masai Mara long-term study; give the source's largest figure).
  - **Females outrank males**, and the clan's rank order is inherited (Holekamp & Smale; verify).
  - They hunt large prey in groups; group size goes up with prey size (Kruuk 1972; Holekamp et al. 1997, *J. Zool.*).
- Analogy: a clan's size against a school class or a bus.
- Twist: a lion pride is a family of a handful of adults. A hyena clan is a town, and it votes on who eats.
- Doug beat: Doug counts eyes in the torch beam: "1… 2…", then gives up at a number the writer picks within the source. Death 264. Field guide: MAYBE erased.
- Scoreboard: HYENA 5, LION 2 (tie: prides are also groups; this is the lion's last tick).

**6. The Stomach** (death 265)
- Facts:
  - One hyena can eat a large share of its own weight in one sitting (Kruuk gives about 14.5 kg; verify), and a group can finish a large carcass very fast (Kruuk's zebra or wildebeest timing; verify).
  - They also store food in water (Kruuk 1972; verify).
- Analogy: 14.5 kg against everyday objects (a big bag of dog food, a microwave), only if the source figure stands.
- Twist: there is no "come back for it later". Nothing is left to come back for.
- Doug beat: Doug hides his packed lunch in a water hole "for later". The hyena retrieves it from the water first. Death 265. The cap floats on the water hole.
- **Gore rule:** nothing is drawn being eaten. The carcass is a timer and an empty patch of grass, never a body.
- Scoreboard: HYENA 6.

### Before dawn.

**7. The Town** (Doug SURVIVES; "Doug is fine.")
- Facts:
  - **Harar, Ethiopia:** spotted hyenas come into the old walled city at night through holes in the wall, and the "hyena men" feed them by hand (UNESCO World Heritage listing of Harar Jugol; Baynes-Rock 2015, *Among the Bone Eaters*).
  - **Mekelle, Ethiopia:** hyenas live off waste around the city (Yirga et al. 2012, *J. Zool.*; verify the paper and wording).
  - **No injury is invented.** No mocking of residents or of the feeding tradition. One respectful, factual tone.
- Analogy: the city wall's hole size against a doorway or a cat flap (only with a source, otherwise cut the analogy).
- Twist: in one city, people solved the hyena problem by inviting them to dinner, and it works, which says more about the hyenas than about the city.
- Doug beat: Doug walks into Harar at night with a torch and finds hyenas calmly taking meat from a man's hand. Doug holds out a piece too. It is taken politely. "Doug is fine."
  Kicker: the narrator suspects this only worked because there was a professional in the room.
- Scoreboard: HYENA 7. Counter stays at 265.

**8. The Sleepers** (death 266)
- Facts:
  - Spotted hyenas have attacked people who sleep outside, especially in the hot season when people sleep in the open (Kruuk 1975 *Hyaena* on Malawi; IUCN Hyaenidae SG "Crocuta and humans" page; Balestra or later reviews only if real).
  - **Statistics and places only.** No named victims, no specific attacks with dates, and no wording about where people are bitten.
- Analogy: none needed; or the tent wall against a raincoat (once; 031 did the tent, so the writer may cut it).
- Twist: lions are what people fear in the dark. Hyenas are what is actually walking past the doorway.
- Doug beat: Doug sleeps outside "because of the heat", with a sign "LIONS: NO". A torch rolls away. Death 266, flat. The cap sits on the sign.
- **Sober card (optional):** white on black, numbers only, 027/029 style.
- Scoreboard: HYENA 8.

**9. The Mob** (the boss; death 267)
- Facts:
  - Hyenas drive lions off kills when they **outnumber them**; a Botswana study found it took roughly four hyenas per lioness (Cooper 1991, *African Journal of Ecology*; verify the ratio and wording). Adult male lions are the exception (the same or Watts & Holekamp 2008).
  - Lions also kill hyenas, and the rivalry is real and runs both ways (Watts & Holekamp 2008/2009; Trinkel & Kastberger 2005). Say so in one line: this is not "hyenas beat lions".
  - The spotted hyena is the **most abundant large carnivore in Africa** (IUCN Red List assessment, Bohm & Höner 2015; verify the wording).
- **Honesty line, required:** across Africa, **lions kill more people than hyenas do** (Packer et al. 2005, *Nature*, Tanzania lion attacks; give their figure). Most hyenas never hurt anyone.
  The scoreboard is about Doug, alone, on foot, all night: a lion might be the worse animal to meet once; the hyena is the worse animal to be near for a whole night.
- Twist (the payoff of the promise): Doug spends the last hour hiding behind a pride of lionesses on a kill, because lions are the scary ones. The clan arrives, whooping, outnumbering them.
  The lions leave. Doug does not.
- Doug beat: Doug waves the lions back: "wait". They go anyway. Death 267, played flat. The field guide's LION page still has no NO; the HYENA page has seven.
  Scoreboard final: **LION 2, HYENA 9**.
- **Closing image:** dawn. A pride of lions eating on the left; on the right, a hyena carrying the red cap off toward the city wall from item 7. Kicker (writer rewords):
  "The cap is going to Harar. It will be fed by hand."
- **Outro:** 30 words or fewer. The final counter (267), one dry line, re-hooked to the end slate. No recap, no subscribe pitch.
