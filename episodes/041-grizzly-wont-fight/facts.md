# Facts: 041 Why Even a Grizzly Bear Won't Try to Kill These Animals (draft 2)

Every factual claim in script.md is listed here with its source. Checked 2026-11-09. Draft 2 applies the screener's fixes in script_review.md; changed rows are marked **(d2)**.

How each source was checked:
- **Fetched** means I read the passage on the page (WebFetch, or the PDF text extracted locally).
- **Search** means I confirmed the claim only from search-result text quoting or summarising the page.
- **Secondary** means a news outlet or an agency page reporting someone else's data.
- **Anecdote** means a single observed event, not a study result. The script labels these ("one biologist", "unverified reports").

The screener should re-open the **Search** and **Secondary** rows and the FLAG rows first.

**FLAGGED (screener decision needed):**
1. **Tiger numbers: RESOLVED (d2).** The screener fetched the full 2018 abstract on OpenAlex (https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1134/S1062359018080149): 763 kills, 1992-2013, bears 2.2% of kills, bear remains in 8.4% of scats, all confirmed. The 1.2% / 1.0% split is not in the abstract, so draft 2 says only "about two in every hundred were bears, brown or black". The 17.6% (62 of 353) and "at least 8 cases" (WCS PUB15720) were accepted by the screener from two word-for-word abstract snippets; the 2018 abstract's 16.7% of 427 kills also supports "about one in six".
2. **"The wolves usually back off and let it eat."** Sourced to Smith et al. 2003 (photo caption in Mech & Boitani, Fetched via UNL) and to the Wyoming Game and Fish large-carnivore specialist (Secondary, Cowboy State Daily). The NPS 2021 "wolves typically yield to incoming bears" line is only in news coverage (Search). Gunther & Smith 2004 does NOT support it for mothers with cubs (wolves won 3 of 5), so the script never cites that paper for this claim.
3. **The Alaska cow-moose somersault** is a single observation by ADF&G biologist Rod Boertje, reported in an ADF&G article. Labelled in the script as "an Alaska biologist watched". Anecdote.
4. **Upright piano weight**: screener accepted (mover and retail guides, tall uprights about 500-1,000 lb; Kawai K-500 525 lb).
5. **"The only one on this list that ever came close to finishing them off"** (The Human) is framing from the USFWS decline record (50,000 to 700-800, by shooting, poisoning and trapping). No other item's animal is on record reducing grizzly numbers. Narrator judgement.

**Brief corrections:**
- Gunther & Smith 2004 studies mothers with cubs; it reports 96 wolf-grizzly interactions 1995-2002 and two cubs probably killed by wolves, but it does not show "the bear usually takes the carcass".
- The human-shield paper is Steyaert et al. **2016** (Proc. R. Soc. B), not 2013. Steyaert 2013 (J. Anim. Ecol.) is the male-female segregation paper.
- Nevin & Gilbert 2005 is coastal British Columbia, not Katmai or McNeil River.
- Boertje et al. 1988 shows grizzlies kill adult moose (males 3.3-3.9 a year), with the highest rates on cows at calving. The script says so, and (d2) adds Dahle et al. 2013 (Sweden) for the cow-heavy, bull-light kill record.
- USFWS current lower-48 number: "at least 1,923" (species page) and 2,314 as of 2023 (January 2025 proposed rule). Range: about 6% (SSA 2024). The brief's "about 2,000" is fine as "a little over two thousand".
