## 2. Escalation axis and items
**Axis: severity, meaning what the fight would cost the grizzly.** Spoken bands: **"Its dinner." / "Its face." / "Its bones." / "Its cubs." / "Its
life."** These are spoken once each, the first time we enter the band.
**Episode device:** a **BEAR'S COST** tag. It's a torn paper tag on a string, pinned beside the death counter, and it shows the current band (DINNER,
FACE, BONES, CUBS, LIFE). It only moves up. LIFE is stamped red at item 7. It's staging only and never read aloud. The field guide gets a **GRIZZLY
BEAR** page: "NO" at the first death, "MAYBE" at The Wolverine, rubbed out at The Big Male, and the closing sticky note "AVOIDS YOU." (see item 8).

Deaths go **315 to 322** (7 deaths; item 2 is a survival). Eight items, the most extreme last.

### Its dinner.
**1. The Wolf Pack** (one of the three strongest: Yellowstone, iconic, and it has a twist)
- Facts to research: Yellowstone wolf-grizzly interactions since the 1995 reintroduction. Most happen at carcasses, and the bear usually takes the
  carcass (Gunther & Smith 2004, *Ursus*; also NPS Yellowstone). How often wolves have killed grizzly cubs, and whether wolves have killed adults (cite
  only what's on record). Grizzlies taking wolf kills (kleptoparasitism) and how that has changed bear feeding.
- **Twist (by 0:45):** the grizzly *wins* at the carcass, and it still never hunts the wolves. A pack is fast, it's many, and it bites from behind. So
  the bear takes the dinner and leaves the wolves alone. Use "won't try to kill" in that exact sense.
- Scale analogy: a bear walking into a wolf kill is like one big man taking a pizza from twelve people who all have knives and none of whom want to go
  first.
- Doug beat: Doug carries the elk leg "back to the wolves, to be fair". Both sides agree on one thing. Death 316. "Doug did not agree to this." is used
  here, once.

**2. The Wolverine** (the "internet says" myth check)
- Facts to research: wolverine body mass (roughly 10-18 kg), its jaw and carcass-caching, and accounts of wolverines defending carcasses against
  bears. Say plainly that these are mostly anecdotes, and that bears and wolves are known to kill wolverines (cite a wolverine mortality study, e.g.
  Glacier NP/Copeland work or Inman et al.). The honest verdict: the bear can win, but a 15 kg animal that does not stop is a bad trade for the dinner.
- Scale analogy: picking a fight with a wolverine is like stealing a sandwich from a running chainsaw.
- Doug beat: Doug stands next to the wolverine "for backup". The bear looks at both of them and leaves. **Survival: "Doug is fine."** The narrator
  suspects the bear didn't leave because of Doug.

### Its face.
**3. The Porcupine**
- Facts to research: North American porcupine quill count (about 30,000, NWF or a university extension source), the backward-facing barbs, and how
  quills work their way deeper. Records of bears, cougars and dogs found with quills in the face or mouth. Grizzlies do eat porcupines sometimes,
  which is why they learn.
- Scale analogy: a porcupine is a pincushion that bites back by holding still.
- Doug beat: costume gag (porcupine suit, "so the bear would leave him alone"). The suit's quills point inward. Death 317, a cut to black, no
  description.

### Its bones.
**4. The Bull Moose** (callback to 024, one line only)
- Facts to research: new facts only. Do not reuse 024's Yukon Flats calf numbers or the 64 km/h line. Adult bull mass (up to about 700 kg; ADF&G),
  front-hoof strikes, and that grizzlies take mainly calves and winter-weakened adults (Denali/Alaska predation studies, e.g. Gasaway et al. 1992 or
  Boertje). Rut-season aggression.
- Scale analogy: a bull moose kick is a vending machine falling on you from shoulder height, four times a second.
- Doug beat: Doug brings the moose a calf costume "for diplomacy". Death 318. Callback line: "We have met this moose before. It has not changed."

**5. The Bull Bison** (callback to 034, one line only)
- Facts to research: grizzly use of bison in Yellowstone, mostly winter-killed carcasses and weak or young animals (NPS or a Yellowstone bear
  diet study, e.g. Mattson). Bull mass (up to about 900 kg, NPS). Herd defence. Don't reuse 034's injury statistics.
- Scale analogy: a healthy bull bison is a small car that has decided you are the road.
- Doug beat: Doug waits beside a bison for it to "weaken a bit" so the bear can help. The bison outlasts Doug. Death 319, the calendar flips.
  Callback line: "Bison still have right of way."

### Its cubs.
**6. The Big Male**
- Facts to research: sexually selected infanticide in brown bears (Swenson et al.; Steyaert et al. 2013 in Scandinavia). Mothers with cubs avoid the
  big males, sometimes by feeding close to people (Katmai/McNeil River or Nevin & Gilbert 2005). Cannibalism records (NPS Katmai). The twist: the
  animal a grizzly mother fears most is another grizzly.
- Scale analogy: a big male is roughly a piano's weight (up to about 360+ kg inland; check the source) that wants your kids gone.
- Doug beat: Doug offers to babysit. The mother bear uses Doug the way she uses people, as a shield. It works on the bear, and less well on Doug.
  Death 320. The field guide "MAYBE" is rubbed out. No cubs harmed on screen; the cubs are simply not there in the next shot.

### Its life.
**7. The Amur Tiger** (the payoff promise)
- Facts to research: Russian Far East tigers kill brown bears (the same species as the grizzly) and Asiatic black bears. Bears are a measurable share
  of tiger diet (Seryodkin, Goodrich, Miquelle et al.; WCS Russia). Brown bears also take tiger kills and sometimes kill tigers, so it runs both ways.
  Give size ranges for both. Use "brown bear, the same species as the grizzly", and never claim tigers hunt North American grizzlies.
- Scale analogy: a tiger hunting a bear is a 200 kg house cat that has decided the fridge is food.
- Doug beat: Doug stands between them in the snow to "keep it a fair fight". The red LIFE stamp lands. Death 321.

**8. The Human** (the boss, the honest twist)
- Facts to research: most grizzlies avoid people (NPS, IGBC bear behaviour). USFWS lower-48 numbers: about 50,000 historically, now about 2,000
  (verify the current USFWS figure), occupying a small percentage of the historic range (verify). Grizzly listed as threatened in 1975.
- The twist: the animal grizzlies avoid most is us, and it's the one that actually took nearly all their range. Doug is finally the scariest animal in
  the forest.
- Scale analogy: grizzlies in the lower 48 today would fit in one large high-school gym's bleachers; the historical number would fill a big stadium
  (check the arithmetic).
- Doug beat: the bear sees Doug and walks away. Doug, offended, follows it to "make friends". "hi friend?" once. Death 322, played flat with a cut to
  black. Honesty line: attacks are rare, and the people in danger here are mostly the bears.
- Closing: the cap lands on a "BEAR COUNTRY" sign. The bear sniffs it and walks off. "The cap has been avoided. It is taking it personally."

## 7. Risks
- **Gore:** every predator contact is a cut to black. No wounds, no quills in a face drawn in detail, no cub deaths shown, no blood in the snow at
  the tiger item. Cannibalism and infanticide are stated in one plain clause each, with no description.
- **Safety advice (never approve):** no "what to do if you meet a bear", no bear-spray or play-dead guidance, and nothing that reads as
  "grizzlies are harmless". The boss honesty line is about rarity and statistics only. Real attacks: numbers only, no names, no re-enactment.
- **Kid appeal (medium: bears, cubs and a tiger are kids' topics):** no cute cubs, no big-eyed bears, no Winnie/Paddington/Kung Fu Panda-adjacent
  tone, no baby animals in the thumbnail. The jokes stay dry (diplomacy, fair-fight logic, Doug offended at being avoided).
- **Title honesty:** see sec 1. The wolves, wolverine and tiger items each carry one "the bear sometimes wins" line.
- **Facts that need strong sources:**
  - Gunther & Smith 2004 (*Ursus*) or NPS for the Yellowstone wolf-grizzly interactions.
  - A peer-reviewed wolverine mortality source; wolverine-defends-carcass accounts labelled as anecdotes.
  - Porcupine quill count and barbs (NWF, university or a state wildlife agency).
  - Moose and bison masses (ADF&G, NPS); grizzly predation on moose calves and adults (peer-reviewed).
  - Brown bear infanticide (Swenson / Steyaert); the mothers-near-people shield (peer-reviewed or NPS).
  - Amur tiger predation on brown bears (Seryodkin et al. or WCS Russia), including the share of the diet.
  - USFWS lower-48 grizzly numbers, range share and listing year.
- **Pronunciation (editor):** Ussuri "oo-SOO-ree", Sikhote-Alin "see-KHO-teh ah-LEEN" (only if used), *Ursus arctos* "UR-sus ARK-tos".
