## Brief corrections and notes (read first)
1. **Where 135 comes from.** Not the 1907 book. The book says "no less than twenty-eight Indian coolies" plus "scores of unfortunate
   African natives of whom no official record was kept" (Patterson ch. 9, Fetched). The 135 figure is from Patterson's 1925 Field Museum
   pamphlet (Zoology Leaflet 7: "135 Indian and African artisans and laborers"), per Wikipedia and BHL (Search). The 135 is a claim of people
   KILLED (UCSC: "claimed the lions had killed 135 people"; Field Museum blog: attacks "took the lives of 135"). The script says "his
   number grew to a hundred and thirty-five" "years later", and the opening attributes it: "said they killed".
2. **35 means eaten, not killed.** UCSC release, Dominy: "Our evidence attests only to the number of people eaten, not the number of
   people killed." The script only ever says "ate"/"eaten" for 35, 11 and 24, and item 1 says out loud that the study counted
   people eaten, not killed, before comparing it as "about a quarter of his number" (35 / 135 = 0.26).
3. **Sale year conflict.** Field Museum blog: 1925. Wikipedia and several news articles: 1924. Script: "in the nineteen-twenties". The
   $5,000 is from Wikipedia, The Standard (Kenya) and CBS Chicago (Search), not a Field Museum page; flagged.
4. **Lord Salisbury.** Primary source: Patterson ch. 9 quotes the Prime Minister's House of Lords statement: "The whole of the works
   were put a stop to for three weeks because a party of man-eating lions..." (Fetched (screener), Wikisource raw text). Secondary:
   The Spectator, 3 March 1900; Wikipedia "Tsavo Man-Eaters".
5. **Item order / dates.** Patterson ch. 6 dates the strike to 1 December; ch. 7 dates the trap night to 3 December (Farquhar and his
   sepoys arrived; "the day after the exodus" was 2 December). Draft 2 puts The Walkout (item 6) before The Trap (item 7), both in the
   "December." band. Ungan Singh was taken "about three weeks after my arrival" (ch. 2), after "one or two coolies mysteriously
   disappeared", hence the "Week three." band and "the first attack he could not explain away".
6. **Lions "following" moving camps** (brief, item 3) could not be sourced; replaced with Dr. Brock being stalked at dusk (ch. 3).
7. **Bridge completion date** not found in a primary or museum source; the script uses Patterson's own wording (ch. 10) without a date.
8. **Which lion** had the broken canine and which ate about 24 is not linked in the script (sources summarise it differently).
9. **Bruce D. Patterson** (Field Museum zoologist, Kays & Patterson 2002; DeSantis & Patterson 2017) is not John Henry Patterson.
10. **Cave of bones** omitted (brief). **Ungan Singh** named once, attributed.
