- "The Man-Eaters of Tsavo." starts at word 35, which is the limit. No greeting, channel name or subscribe ask comes before it. Pass.

## 4. Voice & humour (7/10)
The narrator sounds right and the deadpan is consistent. The thorn fence and "the fence has caught exactly one thing" land well. Item 2 has four joke lines in about 70 seconds: milk, "better nights", the costume line and the zip. That breaks rule 10, and the excess sits next to the real victims (P2).

## 5. TTS: PASS
There are no symbols, digits or parentheses. Years and units are spelled out. The pronunciation notes cover Tsavo, Ungan Singh, machan, Yeakel, de Flamingh and Farquhar.

## 6. Policy: FAIL (real victims)
The script handles the hard parts well: Ungan Singh is named once, plainly, and there is the line "It was still about thirty-five real people". There is no gore, no lion death on screen, no cave of bones and no hero framing. Made-for-kids risk is low, and the dentist bit stays adult-coded. The title's "nine months" matches the "final nine months" source. The narrative is original. Three lines turn real victims into a punchline (brief section 7: "never a punchline"):
- **P1** (item 5): "Call it the toothache theory, where if chewing hurts, you order the softest thing on the menu." This makes the workers the menu item. The brief asked for this analogy to land on Doug.
- **P2** (item 2): "...and by Tsavo standards that counted as one of the better nights." This gets its laugh from the deaths on the other nights.
- **P3** (outro): "Patterson's number only went up on paper, but this one goes up for real." It puts the real death toll next to the cartoon counter and implies the real deaths were made up. At least 28 recorded deaths were real.
