**Fact FAIL items**

- **F1. Hantavirus timing is contradicted by CDC.** CDC says infection comes from **fresh** urine, droppings or nesting material, and the virus lasts only days outside a host (archived CDC technical page: "several days"). Three places in the script say the opposite:
  - Line 248: "the most dangerous part of meeting a deer mouse can happen long after the mouse has left the room."
  - Line 272: "The mice had moved out months ago, and they didn't need to stay."
  - Line 264 stage direction: "Old droppings and nest material".
- **F2. "for a long time it looked like it would stay in birds" (line 182)** has no source, and it is wrong in context. The previous sentence counts about 1,000 human cases, and H5N1 had already infected tigers, cats, mink and sea lions years before 2024. "What nobody expected was the cow." is also unsourced as written.

## Required fixes
1. **F1, line 248.** "the most dangerous part of meeting a deer mouse can happen long after the mouse has left the room." Change it to a version that doesn't claim old contamination, for example "the most dangerous part of meeting a deer mouse doesn't involve touching the mouse at all."
2. **F1, line 272.** "The mice had moved out months ago, and they didn't need to stay." The mice must still be living in the shed (fresh material). For example: "The mice had spent the whole winter in there, and they were still home."
3. **F1, line 264 stage direction.** Change "Old droppings and nest material" to "Fresh droppings and a mouse nest; a mouse peeks out".
4. **F2, line 182.** Cut "and for a long time it looked like it would stay in birds", or replace it with a sourced fact, for example "and before two thousand twenty-four, it had never been found in dairy cows" (source it in facts.md: MMWR 73(21) or USDA first-detection notice). If you keep "What nobody expected was the cow.", source it too, or change it to a neutral twist marker such as "Then, in two thousand twenty-four, came the cow." (it must differ from the Burger marker).
5. **F3, line 324.** Cut ", which is roughly how long you have been watching this video". Or change it to something true at that point (about 9 minutes in), for example "...about one person every twelve minutes, a little longer than this video has been playing."
6. **Rule 14, line 220.** "The insane part is the heat." This is PE's marker verbatim. Write our own, for example "Then there's the heat."
7. **Rule 14, line 330.** "The most disturbing part is the timing." This is PE's marker verbatim. For example: "The cruel part is the timing." (Line 262 "The disturbing part is" can stay because it shares only 3 words. Don't put two "disturbing" markers back to back.)
