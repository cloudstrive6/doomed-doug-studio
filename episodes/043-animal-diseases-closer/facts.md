# Facts: 043 The Most Disturbing Diseases Animals Can Pass to Humans (draft 2)

Every factual claim in script.md is listed here with its source. The script writer checked them on 2026-11-10.

How each source was checked:
- **Fetched**: I read the passage myself (WebFetch).
- **Search**: I only confirmed it from search-result text that quotes or summarises the page.
- **Secondary**: the source is a news, encyclopedia or reference page, not the agency or the study itself.

## FLAGGED (screener, please look at these first)
1. **Rabies deaths per year.** The brief says 59,000. The current WHO fact sheet (Fetched) no longer gives that number. It says "rabies causes 44 203 annual deaths" (2021 Global Health Estimates) and "tens of thousands of deaths annually". The script says "about forty-four thousand deaths a year", and the on-screen label is "TENS OF THOUSANDS". The per-minute line is now 525,600 / 44,203 = 11.9, so "about one every twelve minutes".
2. **"100% vaccine-preventable."** WHO's current page says "vaccine-preventable", not "100%". The script says "calls rabies vaccine-preventable".
3. **Rabies survivors after symptom onset.** I found no CDC or WHO page with a count. StatPearls says "only a few dozen cases" (Search). Other figures (fewer than 20, 29) come from weak blogs. The script says "a few dozen people at most". The Milwaukee protocol isn't mentioned.
4. **Q fever infectious dose.** I couldn't find "a single bacterium" on a CDC page. The archived CDC page says "very few organisms may be required". Texas A&M and the Brooke et al. 2013 dose-response model (ID50 ≈ 1.18 bacteria) support "one to ten". The script says "As few as one to ten ... may be enough".
5. **Q fever "about half have no symptoms"** is a Washington State DOH figure, not CDC. The script uses CDC's wording instead: "most people ... have no symptoms, or only mild ones".
6. **H5N1 total.** I couldn't read the latest WHO cumulative table directly. The WHO WPRO avian influenza updates (Search) give 997 cases and 478 deaths to 31 March 2026, and 1,000 cases with 479 fatal (CFR 47.9%) in later 2026 updates. The script rounds this to "about a thousand ... nearly half of them died".
7. **Hantavirus wording.** CDC currently says "Thirty-eight percent of people who develop respiratory symptoms may die" (Fetched). The Four Corners outbreak death toll isn't spoken.
8. **The prion heat line** comes from a 2001 CDC *Emerging Infectious Diseases* article (Search): "only partial inactivation at temperatures as high as 350°C". I found no current agency page. An archived FDA Bad Bug Book page says the BSE agent can survive cooking (Search). The script gives no cooking temperature as advice.
9. **The 2007 Kitaka mine cases were miners.** The source is the CDC document and the EID articles (Search). The tourist card (2 infected, 1 died) is from CDC/EID: a US tourist in December 2007 and a Dutch tourist in July 2008, one fatal. WHO (Fetched) mentions two 2008 traveller cases. The card uses "2007-2008".
