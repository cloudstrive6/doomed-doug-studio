Script writer, 2026-10-22, draft 1. Every factual line in script.md maps to a row here. Quotes are short excerpts used for
checking only; the script's wording is our own. Every size is "about" or "estimated" in the script, using the source's own number.

3. **Megarachne size:** 54 cm is total body length in the 2005 reconstruction; the 50 cm figure is Hünicken's spider leg span. Both used, each labelled.
4. **Palaeodictyopteran winglets:** hedged ("many", "small extra flaps"); not given to Mazothairos.
5. **Mazothairos wingspan:** sources give 55 or 56 cm; script says "about fifty-five", from a fragment.
6. **Meganeuropsis** not used (no separate item; total is over 2,950 without it).
7. **Pulmonoscorpius age:** 341 ± 3 Ma (Garza et al. 2025), not 335 Ma. The brief's diet line is kept only as "probably ... possibly", from Clack 2002 via Wikipedia, plus Countryfile's pincer point.
8. **Hibbertopterus:** trackmaker 1.6 m; species up to 1.8-2 m. Script uses 1.6 m and says it could grow bigger.
9. **Crassigyrinus:** name meaning conflicts (NHM "shallow wriggler" vs common "thick tadpole"), so it is cut and the chapter renamed "The Tadpole From Hell" from the NHM's nickname. Size "around two to three metres" overlaps Arthropleura's up to 2.6 m (ESRF) or around 2.7 m (Cambridge), so neither can be called longer. Item order is kept because the brief locks Arthropleura as the boss; the boss is billed as the biggest *bug* (largest land arthropod known), and the overlap is said on air. On screen, item 8 is a dashed "2 TO 3 M?" bracket, not a tape reading, so the solid reading never drops. The kicker says "the museum calls it a tadpole", since it was an adult early tetrapod.
10. **Arthropleura** habitat is open coastal woodland, not coal forest; closing image adjusted. The Northumberland fossil predates the oxygen peak, which became the first line of the resolution.
11. **2026 evidence added:** Snelling et al. 2026 (*Nature*) post-dates the brief's sources and strengthens "scientists still argue".
