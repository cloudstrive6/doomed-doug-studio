# 060 Oarfish: facts (draft 2)

Every spoken claim maps to a source below. "Fetched" = page text read on 2026-11-27. "Search" = confirmed through search-result snippets from the named page only; flagged for the screener to re-open.

## Brief corrections and notes (read first)
1. **Title claim "Cut Off Its Own Body" is supported.** FishBase: "self-amputation (autotomy) of the posterior part of the body past the vent"; Roberts 2012 summary: autotomy "may occur repeatedly until the entire postabdominal part of the fish has been lost and only the head and abdomen remain." "Body" is accurate; "half" is staging, not a measured claim.
2. **Catalina 2013 gut "nearly empty" is not sourced to the Catalina fish.** The "almost empty, a few euphausiids, filled with parasites" necropsy is the **Oceanside** oarfish (14 ft, October 2013). The script names Oceanside for it; the Catalina fish is used for the haul-ashore story and the parasite paper.
3. **Catalina size:** 18 ft = about 5.5 m (PBS, UCSB). Not over 8 m anywhere.
4. **California count:** Scripps called La Jolla (Aug 2024) the 20th since 1901; later 2024 reports give 21st or 22nd. Script: "about twenty times since nineteen-oh-one".
5. **Earthquake belief:** NPR quotes folklorist Matthew Meyer (yokai.com) saying he knows of no folklore of oarfish predicting earthquakes; Smithsonian says the name dates to 17th-century Japan and folklore has them as messengers warning of disaster. Script attributes ("many people believe", "the story goes") and gives Meyer's doubt in one line. Brief's first-sentence wording kept ("many people believe"); it describes the modern popular belief, which all sources report.
6. **Tingle:** not used. The only on-record line is the Florida Museum's: a close relative (streamer fish) can give a mild shock; for the oarfish it is "unclear". Script repeats exactly that; never calls the oarfish electric.
7. **Silver rubbing off:** one attributed quote (NZ DOC officer, 2015). Supported in general by NatGeo ("skin is soft and easily damaged", guanine coat).
8. **No sharks on screen.** The mako is named as the tapeworm's adult host (Kuris 2015 inference); death 430 is the torch running out, not a shark.
9. **Orihara 2019** was also used in 026 item 1; the brief assigns it to item 1 here, so it is restated in fresh wording.
