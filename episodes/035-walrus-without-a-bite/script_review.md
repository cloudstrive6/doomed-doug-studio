## Required fixes (all must be done for PASS)

1. **FLAG 1, the vacuum cleaner (The Suction, line 104): rejected as sourced.**
   > "A typical household vacuum cleaner is rated at around twenty, so the walrus manages about four times that, with its tongue."

   The only support is manufacturer and retailer blogs. They disagree with each other (14-25 kPa for corded models, and robot or cordless models vary widely), most uprights are rated in air watts rather than kPa, and one vendor guide itself warns that brands inflate kPa numbers. "Four times a vacuum cleaner" is the item's headline number and an obvious Shorts line, so it needs a non-vendor source.
   **Change:** either cite an independent measured source (a test lab or a standards body giving sealed suction in kPa) for the vacuum side, or use the fallback: cut the sentence and the vacuum drawing (line 102), and keep "almost a vacuum" (Kastelein et al. 1994; WWF Arctic also says "a vacuum").
   Item 2 still needs a rule-8 everyday analogy. The clam count is not one. Use something sourced, or simple arithmetic against normal air pressure.

2. **The population of Point Lay (The Haulout, line 362) is out of date and stated in the present tense.**
   > "Point Lay is a village of just under two hundred and fifty people, which works out at about a hundred and forty walruses for every person in town."

   The 2020 US Census counted 330 residents (189 in 2010). KNOM's "just under 250" was correct for 2015 only.
   **Change:** make it past tense and tied to the year. For example: "At the time, Point Lay was a village of just under two hundred and fifty people, which works out at about a hundred and forty walruses per person." Do the same for the label "UNDER 250 PEOPLE" if needed. Alternatively, use the 2020 census figure and redo the arithmetic.

3. **Honesty line (The Stampede, line 420).** The writer's rewording is accepted, but the new line still makes an unsourced claim.
   > "The danger on these beaches is not to people but to the walruses, and mostly to the youngest ones."

   Dropping the brief's "walruses very rarely hurt people" was right. No agency says it, and in 2019 coverage an ADF&G Arctic Marine Mammal Program biologist said that walrus attacks on people and their vessels are not uncommon. FWS also says walruses "can inflict serious injury with their tusks". The words "not to people" bring the same claim back in, and no source supports them.
   **Change:** "On these beaches, the ones in danger are the walruses, and mostly the youngest ones." This is sourced to the FWS guidelines and USGS Icy Cape. Item 4's "nobody was hurt" still carries the people side honestly. The CD should note that the brief's honesty line has gone (see the note below).

4. **The Boat, line 196: an unsourced detail that contradicts its own visual.**
   > "She was not hunting the boat, because it was simply between her and her calf, and the navy lost."

   The source (Barents Observer, quoting the Northern Fleet) says only that she attacked "when protecting her cub". Nothing says the boat was *between* them. The split-screen directly above this line (line 194) shows the calf on her back, so the line and the picture disagree.
   **Change:** "She was not hunting the boat. She was protecting her calf, the boat was in the way, and the navy lost." This is the brief's wording.

5. **FLAG 3, The Sleep, line 290: over-claim. The tusk-hanging row is accepted; this sentence goes past it.**
   > "Hundreds of tusked adults hanging off the ice asleep are the safest thing in this entire video."

   The only source is the researchers' statement in NBC News: walruses seen "resting in water while using their tusks to hang from ice floes". It says nothing about sleep or about hundreds doing it together. Line 274 attributes it correctly. Line 290 turns it into a sourced-sounding fact.
   **Change:** "Walruses hanging off the ice by their tusks, resting, are the safest thing in this entire video." The ABOUT 300 sign and the drawing of the row are staging and can stay.

6. **Rule 14 (competitor wording), The Stampede, line 424:**
   > "But the most disturbing thing is how little it takes."

   "the most disturbing thing" is four consecutive words from a Paint Explainer line ("we haven't talked about the most disturbing thing yet", quoted in style bible 3.3).
   **Change:** use a different marker, for example "But the part that should bother you is how little it takes." Avoid "the most disturbing ..." in any form.

## Recommended (not blocking)

- **The Tusks, line 146:** the analogy is circular ("weighing as much as a big bull"). The pull-up bar is the everyday object, so it passes rule 8, but an everyday weight would land better. Item 4 already uses the dairy cow again.

- **FLAG 4 (the 1,500 count four days earlier): accepted.** I confirmed it in CNN's text quoting the NOAA ASAMM flight report, and in a NOAA-released item that mentions about 1,500. Upgrade to the NOAA flight report if it can be opened.
- **FLAG 5 (a bear as a stampede trigger): accepted.** Add a stronger row: NBC/AP 2008 ("panicked into the water by a polar bear, a hunter or a low-flying plane", https://nbcnews.com/id/wbna22260892) and the ADF&G 2012 Point Lay/Wainwright traditional knowledge report, which links more bear sightings to the 2009 deaths.

"About one in nine" and "in one set of summer samples" are fair. N=44 means about 5 animals, so keep the hedge.
