### Hours.

**1. Nutty Putty Cave** (hook item, one of the three strongest; about 27-28 hours; death 309)
- Facts:
  - Utah, November 2009. An experienced caver took a wrong turn into an unmapped passage and slid in head-first and downward into a squeeze about
    10 by 18 inches. (Verify the measurements, the depth and the distance from the entrance from Utah County / news-of-record sources.)
  - More than 100 rescuers worked for about 27 hours. A pulley system briefly worked and then failed (verify).
  - Afterwards the landowner and family closed the cave, and the passage was **sealed with concrete, with him still inside**. It is now his memorial
    (verify the date of the sealing and the agency involved).
  - Mechanism, in one plain clause: being held upside down for that long was more than the body could take. No medical detail.
- Analogy: the squeeze was narrower than a carry-on suitcase is wide (only if the measurements support it; otherwise use another everyday object
  the measurements do support).
- **Cold-open fact (first sentence):** "In Utah, there is a cave that nobody is allowed into anymore, because the last person who went too deep is still
  there." The writer can reword, but the cave, "still there" and the fact that it's closed must all be in it. Then "Hours." and "Nutty Putty Cave."
- **Twist by 0:45 (about 145 words or fewer):** the passage was unmapped, and the shape that let him slide in was the same shape that stopped him
  coming out. The rescue was technically possible for hours, and then it wasn't.
- Doug beat (**before** the real case is summed up, never on the same card): Doug was volunteered to "just check one more passage". "Doug did not
  agree to this." Doug goes in head-first, then only his boots and his suitcase are visible. Cut to black **before** anything else. Death 309,
  played flat. The cap is left at the passage mouth. Sticker: NUTTY PUTTY.
- **Kicker: a sober card**, no Doug, no drawing of a person: "NUTTY PUTTY CAVE, UTAH, 2009. SEALED." One plain sentence. No name.

### Months.

**2. The Snowbound Car** (about 2 months; Doug SURVIVES; "Doug is fine.")
- Facts:
  - Northern Sweden, 2012. A man was found alive in his car after about two months under snow (verify the dates and the region from the BBC, the
    Guardian, or Swedish police statements).
  - He said he lived on snow (verify the source of that claim).
  - The snow over the car worked as insulation, like an igloo, and kept the inside much warmer than the outside air (verify a sourced temperature
    contrast, or give no numbers).
  - Doctors suggested his body slowed down into a hibernation-like state. **Say "doctors suggested". It's a hypothesis, not a finding.**
- Analogy: a snow drift as a duvet (only if the source supports "insulation").
- Twist: the snow that trapped him is the reason he lived.
- Doug beat: Doug in a car under a huge drift with the suitcase on the back seat. Calendar pages fall. Doug gives a thumbs up. "Doug is fine." The
  narrator is quietly disappointed (series bible). No sticker (survival). Re-hook: "Two months is the longest anyone on this list was held and
  walked out. Everything after this was held for longer."
- **No safety advice** (no "what to do if you're snowed in"). No name.

### Thousands of years.

**3. Ötzi's Hollow** (about 5,300 years; one of the three strongest; death 310, played flat)
- Facts:
  - Found in September 1991 by two hikers at about 3,210 m in the Ötztal Alps, on the Italian-Austrian border (South Tyrol Museum of Archaeology).
  - At first he was **treated as a recent death**, a missing climber, and the recovery itself damaged him (verify exactly what tools were used).
  - The glacier moved over him for thousands of years **without carrying him away**, because he lay in a rock gully that sheltered him from the moving
    ice (verify the gully explanation in the literature).
  - The cold is **not** what killed him (people were involved). One plain clause only: no weapon, no wound and no cause-of-death detail. If the writer
    can't phrase it without detail, cut it.
- Analogy: a coin in a crack in the floor while a rug is dragged over it again and again.
- Twist: the ice didn't freeze him in place. It flowed straight over him, and missed.
- Doug beat: Doug lies in a hollow at the pass, with the glacier sliding over the top in a crude time-lapse. Cut to black. Death 310, flat. 5,300
  years later, two hikers find the cap first. Sticker: ÖTZTAL ALPS.

**4. Hoyo Negro** (about 12,000-13,000 years; death 311)
- Facts:
  - A huge flooded pit inside the Sac Actun cave system, Yucatán, Mexico. Cave divers found it in 2007 (Chatters et al. 2014, *Science*).
  - When sea levels were lower, the cave was dry and dark. A young woman fell into the pit, and so did large Ice Age animals (a sabre-toothed cat
    and a gomphothere among them; verify the count and the species list).
  - When the ice sheets melted, sea level rose and the pit flooded, sealing everything under water until divers arrived.
  - Her remains were among the oldest and most complete in the Americas (verify the exact claim in Chatters 2014).
  - Say "a young woman" and nothing about her age.
- Analogy: the depth of the drop as a building of N storeys (from the paper's measurement).
- Twist: the trap was dry when it caught them. The sea came in afterwards and locked the door.
- Doug beat: Doug in a dark cave with a torch, stepping forward, then the torch spinning down. Cut to black. Death 311. Later the cap floats up in blue
  water past a diver's light. Sticker: HOYO NEGRO.
- Remains are never drawn. Use a sober caption card with the place and the date only.

**5. The Mammoth Sinkhole** (Hot Springs, South Dakota; about 26,000 years per the classic dating, **verify current dating**; death 312)
- Facts:
  - A sinkhole formed and filled with warm spring water. Its walls were steep and slippery (verify).
  - Mammoths came for the water or the plants and couldn't climb back out: 61 Columbian and 3 woolly mammoths have been identified so far (Mammoth
    Site of Hot Springs; Agenbroad; verify the current counts).
  - **Most of the trapped mammoths were young males** (verify the sourced claim and the share).
- Analogy: the pit's size as a familiar space (only from a sourced measurement).
- Twist: the inexperienced young males were the ones who kept wandering in. The adult females, the herd's experienced members, mostly didn't.
- Doug beat: the costume gag. "The sinkhole only caught mammoths, which is exactly why Doug is dressed as one." Doug in a mammoth costume, cap on top,
  walks confidently to the edge for a drink. Cut to black. Death 312. Sticker: HOT SPRINGS.
- No dead or struggling mammoths are drawn. Show bones in the ground only as a clean museum dig layout.

### Tens of thousands of years.

**6. The Permafrost Worms** (about 46,000 years; one of the three strongest; death 313)
- Facts:
  - Nematodes (*Panagrolaimus kolymaensis*) taken from permafrost about 40 m down near the Kolyma River, Siberia, were revived in the lab (Shatilovich
    et al. 2023, *PLOS Genetics*; radiocarbon about 45,839-47,769 years; verify).
  - After waking up, **they reproduced**. Give the sourced mechanism (they reproduce without males) and say it once.
  - Each one is about the length of a grain of salt / under a millimetre (verify).
- Analogy: the last time these worms moved, Neanderthals were still alive (verify the overlap).
- Twist: they didn't just survive. They woke up, ate and had a next generation.
- Doug beat: Doug frozen in the permafrost wall next to a worm. Both thaw in a lab dish. The worm wriggles off. Doug does not. Death 313, flat. Narrator:
  "One of them woke up." Sticker: KOLYMA.

**7. The Permafrost Virus** (about 48,500 years; death 314. **If the draft runs long, merge this into item 6 as its twist**)
- Facts:
  - *Pandoravirus yedoma*, revived from about 48,500-year-old permafrost under a lake in Yakutia, was **still infectious** (Alempic et al. 2023,
    *Viruses*; verify the age and the site, Yukechi Alas).
  - The researchers deliberately used viruses that **only infect amoebae** (*Acanthamoeba*), so the work posed no risk to people or animals. Say
    this plainly.
  - It's a giant virus: big enough to see with a light microscope (verify).
- Analogy: bigger than some bacteria (verify).
- Twist: the virus is the one thing on this list that was trapped and is still able to infect something. It just can't infect Doug.
- Doug beat: the amoeba costume. "It only infects amoebae, which is exactly why Doug is dressed as one." Cut to black. Death 314. Sticker: YUKECHI.
- **Policy:** no "zombie virus", no pandemic speculation, no "what if it gets out", no medical advice. One factual risk line, from the paper only.

### A hundred million years.

**8. The Seafloor** (BOSS; about 101.5 million years; death 315)
- Facts:
  - Microbes from seafloor sediment under the South Pacific Gyre, laid down about 101.5 million years ago, were fed in the lab and **woke up and
    multiplied** (Morono et al. 2020, *Nature Communications*; verify that up to about 99% revived, and verify the water depth (about 5,700 m) and
    the depth below the seafloor).
  - That sediment is among the most nutrient-poor on Earth, with almost no food and almost no oxygen, and it stayed that way for the whole time (verify).
  - When the sediment was laid down, dinosaurs were alive (the Cretaceous; give the period, not a fake dinosaur name).
- Analogy: if the 101.5 million years were one day, the whole of recorded human history would be the last fraction of a second (compute it in
  `facts.md`).
- Twist: the longest trap on this list held its prisoners for a hundred million years, and they were fine. They were just waiting for a meal.
- Doug beat: Doug drilled out of the seafloor core in a sealed pocket of mud next to a few microbes. In the lab, droplets of food go in. The microbes
  multiply on screen (crude dots doubling). Doug does not. Death 315. The narrator plays it dry ("Doug has died. Again. Possibly in the Cretaceous.")
  and the cap is the only thing that comes out clean. Last sticker: SOUTH PACIFIC GYRE.
- 020 used Desulforudis ("The Loneliest Bacterium"). One callback sentence at most, and no repeated facts.
- Outro: 30 words or fewer. End on the full suitcase and the cap. "The suitcase still has room." is allowed.
