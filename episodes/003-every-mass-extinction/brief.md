## 2. Escalation axis: era (oldest to newest), five extinctions in order
Declared axis for the script header: **"Era: the Big Five mass extinctions, oldest to newest."** The background ramp also
darkens across the episode. The boss is the K-Pg asteroid: newest on the axis, the most famous, and the only one that
happened in a single day. (The End-Permian is the biggest by species lost; it sits at the midpoint as "The Great Dying."
and the script says plainly that it was the worst. That is a fact, not a contradiction of the axis.)

Spoken section headers (two-word style, one per extinction): **"End-Ordovician."**, **"Late Devonian."**,
**"The Great Dying."** (midpoint zone shift, about 40-45% in, the darkest palette so far), **"End-Triassic."**,
**"End-Cretaceous."**. The art director defines the ramp (suggestion: icy pale blue, then murky green, then lava
red/black, then acid yellow-green, then soot grey to black).

**Title honesty:** the title promises Doug dies in every mass extinction. He must die at least once inside each of the
five sections (plan below: 9 deaths over 11 items, with 2 survivals for the "narrator is disappointed" gag).
"Every" means the Big Five. **Do not add a "sixth extinction" item:** the framing is debated, it pulls us toward a
preachy climate tone the series bible bans, and it makes "every" a claim we'd have to defend.

Each item: name, frame, mechanism, twist, kicker, Doug beat (style bible 3.3). Each mechanism that is a **hypothesis**
gets one honest framing word ("one leading idea", "scientists suspect") and nothing more. No hedging stacks.

### End-Ordovician (about 445 million years ago)
**1. Killer Moss** (the hook item)
- Research: the first of the Big Five; the share of marine species lost (commonly about 85%, confirm against a primary
  or review source); almost all complex life was in the sea; the hypothesis that the first land plants (moss-like,
  non-vascular) sped up rock weathering and pulled down CO2, helping trigger the glaciation (Lenton et al. 2012,
  Nature Geoscience; frame as a hypothesis).
- Scale analogy: a plant shorter than a thumbnail helping to freeze a planet, like a doormat that causes a blizzard.
- Doug beat: Doug steps out of the time machine and pats the first moss on land. It is the first thing he has ever been
  nice to that had a world-ending effect. Death 19 (comes at the item end: the cold arrives, Doug freezes in a pose
  of patting).
- Twist (must land by 0:45, about 145 words): "the first mass extinction may have been started by plants." That's the hook.

**2. Gondwana Ice**
- Research: ice sheets on the supercontinent Gondwana over the South Pole; sea level fell (find a sourced range); the
  shallow seas where most life lived drained or cooled; a second pulse came when the ice melted (confirm with a source;
  oxygen loss in the second pulse is fine if sourced).
- Scale analogy: the drop in sea level expressed in everyday height (for example, so many stacked houses or a
  skyscraper; pick it from the sourced figure).
- Doug beat: Doug in snorkel and flippers on what was a reef this morning and is now a beach. Death 20.

### Late Devonian (about 372 and 359 million years ago)
**3. Dead Reefs** (Kellwasser event)
- Research: the Devonian had enormous reef systems; the Kellwasser crisis devastated reef builders and shallow tropical
  seas; oxygen-poor water spread (black shale layers as the evidence); the extinction ran in pulses over a long time
  (find a sourced duration). Species lost for the Late Devonian overall (often about 70-75%, confirm).
- Scale analogy: reef size in a familiar comparison only if a sourced area exists; otherwise compare the black shale
  layer to something everyday.
- Doug beat: Doug holds his breath "to be safe". The water has less oxygen than his breath. Death 21.

**4. Ozone Hole** (Hangenberg event; Doug survives)
- Research: fossil spores from the end of the Devonian show UV-type damage, suggesting the ozone layer was weakened
  (Marshall et al. 2020, Science Advances); one proposed trigger is a nearby supernova (Fields et al. 2020, PNAS).
  **Supernova = hypothesis, one clear framing word, no more than two sentences.** Dunkleosteus (armored fish, head
  plates that worked like a blade) disappears around this time (confirm timing with a source; otherwise just show it).
- Scale analogy: a star exploding far away (sourced distance, for example in light-years) and Earth still getting
  sunburnt, like a campfire in another town burning your eyebrows.
- Doug beat: Doug gets a bad cartoon sunburn and lives. Dunkleosteus does not. The narrator is quietly disappointed.
  No death (counter stays 21).

### The Great Dying (End-Permian, about 252 million years ago)
**5. Siberian Traps** (zone shift; the darkest palette so far)
- Research: the worst extinction in Earth's history (marine species lost: Stanley 2016 PNAS gives about 81%; older
  figures say up to 96%; use one sourced figure and say which); the Siberian Traps lava volume and area (sourced);
  magma heated coal and other rock underground, releasing huge amounts of carbon (Burgess et al. 2017, Science
  Advances); the main extinction took a geologically short time (Burgess et al. 2014, PNAS: tens of thousands of years).
- Scale analogy: the lava area against a familiar region (a sourced comparison, for example a country or US states).
- Doug beat: Doug toasts a marshmallow on the lava. The real problem is the gas behind him. Death 22.

**6. Hot Tub Ocean**
- Research: equatorial sea-surface temperatures around 40 °C (Sun et al. 2012, Science, "Lethally hot temperatures");
  that is about hot-tub temperature; few marine animals can live long at that heat (sourced).
- Scale analogy: the whole tropical ocean at the temperature of a hot tub you are told not to stay in for long
  (only the temperature claim needs a source; keep the joke general).
- Doug beat: Doug in the water with a rubber ring and a little towel on his head. It is not relaxing for long.
  Death 23.

**7. Purple Oceans**
- Research: oxygen-free, sulfide-rich (euxinic) water spread; the hypothesis that hydrogen sulfide gas escaped from the
  sea into the air (Kump, Pavlov and Arthur 2005, Geology); biomarker evidence for green sulfur bacteria in the
  water column (Grice et al. 2005, Science). The "purple sea, green sky" picture is a hypothesis: frame it once.
  Survivor: Lystrosaurus did well afterwards (sourced).
- Scale analogy: the rotten-egg smell detectable at tiny concentrations (sourced), against a whole ocean of it.
  One clinical mention of the smell; no bodily detail.
- Doug beat: Doug befriends a Lystrosaurus (deadpan, it ignores him). The Lystrosaurus survives the Great Dying.
  Doug does not. Death 24.

### End-Triassic (about 201 million years ago)
**8. Pangaea Splits** (Doug survives)
- Research: the Central Atlantic Magmatic Province (CAMP) as Pangaea began to break apart; species lost (about 76% is
  often quoted, confirm); the extinction cleared rivals and opened the way for dinosaurs to dominate (sourced, e.g.
  Olsen et al.).
- Scale analogy: the lava spread across what are now four continents (sourced), like one spilled drink reaching four
  tables.
- Doug beat: Doug stands with one foot on each side of the crack as it widens. He picks a side and lives. Narrator:
  "Doug is fine." (He is, this time.) No death (counter stays 24).

**9. Acid Seas**
- Research: CO2 from CAMP made the oceans more acidic; shell and reef builders were hit hardest (Greene et al. 2012,
  Earth-Science Reviews); the reef gap in the record (sourced).
- Scale analogy: a shell dissolving like a mint in a glass of fizzy water (keep it at "dissolving"; check the chemistry
  wording with the source).
- Doug beat: costume gag callback. Doug is zipped into an ammonite or clam costume "to blend in" (cap on top) and the
  shell does not hold up. Death 25. Played straight, adult-coded.

### End-Cretaceous (66 million years ago)
**10. Deccan Traps**
- Research: the Deccan Traps erupted in India across the K-Pg boundary (Schoene et al. 2019 and Sprain et al. 2019,
  Science); the debate about how much they contributed versus the impact (one sentence, fair to both).
- Scale analogy: the lava stack height or area against something familiar (sourced figure).
- Doug beat: Doug has seen three of these by now and has brought a fire extinguisher. Death 26. Callback to the
  marshmallow.

**11. Chicxulub** (the boss, the longest item)
- Research: an asteroid about 10 km wide (sourced range); crater about 180 km wide off the Yucatan Peninsula;
  sulfur and soot in the atmosphere cut sunlight and stopped photosynthesis, with an impact winter (sourced, e.g.
  Chiarenza et al. 2020, PNAS); about three-quarters of species lost, including all non-bird dinosaurs; the iridium
  layer found worldwide (Alvarez et al. 1980, Science). Optional: the Tanis site (DePalma et al. 2019, PNAS) **only if
  the writer frames the contested parts honestly**; otherwise cut it.
- Scale analogy: the asteroid's width against Mount Everest (library asset exists: `mount_everest.json`), or its
  speed in "coast to coast in N minutes" (sourced speed).
- Twist: the impact itself was not what killed most of life; the darkness that followed did. Birds are the dinosaurs
  that made it.
- Doug beat: Doug sees a bright new star in the sky and makes a wish. Death 27. Final image: a cross-section of rock,
  the thin iridium line, and the red cap pressed flat in it. The cap survives, technically, as a fossil.
- Kicker: one deadpan line about the birds, then the outro.

**Death plan:** counter on screen from the first shot at **18**, ending at **27** (9 deaths; survivals at items 4 and
8). At least one death per section: Ordovician 2, Devonian 1, Permian 3, Triassic 1, Cretaceous 2.

## 8. Risks
- **Gore:** low for this topic. Deaths are cartoon (frozen pose, X eyes, ghost Doug, flat cap). No burned, melted or
  dissolved bodies: lava, heat, acid and the impact are shown on the environment and the costume, never on Doug's body.
- **Preachy or political tone:** the CO2 mechanisms invite modern-climate commentary. **None.** State the past science
  and move on; no "sound familiar?", no lessons, no sixth extinction.
- **Kid-appeal:** dinosaurs, a time machine and a costume are kid magnets. Keep the voice deadpan and adult-coded (the
  doormat blizzard, the hot tub you shouldn't stay in, the extinguisher). No mascot voices, no "dino buddies", no
  nursery words.
- **Facts that need strong sources (cut the claim if it can't be sourced):** species-loss percentages for each event
  (use one consistent source family, e.g. a review, and name which measure: species or genera); dates for each event;
  the moss/weathering hypothesis; Devonian UV spores and the supernova idea (hypotheses); Siberian Traps volume and the
  coal link; the 40 °C sea temperature; the hydrogen sulfide and purple ocean idea (hypothesis); CAMP extent; End-Triassic
  acidification; Deccan timing; Chicxulub size, crater size and impact winter; Tanis (cut unless framed honestly).
  Prefer primary papers, the Smithsonian, NHM, USGS, university press releases; avoid pop listicles.
- **Repetition:** do not take Snowball Earth (#24) or "predator from every period" (#10) material; those are future
  episodes.
- **Description:** at most 4,800 characters filled, at least one institutional or primary source per item.
