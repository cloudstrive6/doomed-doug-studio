# Facts: 037 Why the Sperm Whale Gets Deadlier the Deeper Doug Follows It (draft 2)

Every factual claim in script.md is listed here with its source. Checked 2026-11-04.

How each source was checked:
- **Fetched** means I read the passage on the page with WebFetch.
- **Search** means I confirmed the claim only from search-result text quoting the page (the page was blocked (403/404), a PDF I could not open, or a reCAPTCHA wall).
- **Arithmetic** means a conversion or comparison I worked out from the sourced numbers. The working is shown.

The screener should re-open the **Search** rows and the FLAG rows first.

**Draft 2 (screener review 2026-11-04):** FLAG 1, 3, 4 and 5 accepted. FLAG 2 rejected and replaced (Clarke & Roper beak count). FLAG 6 revised: the forty-meter limit is now Doug's dive course, not the tank. Rows changed in draft 2 are marked **[D2]**.

**FLAGGED in draft 1 (decisions above):**
1. **Boss depth, "more than two kilometers" / "past two thousand two hundred meters".** CSIRO (Australia's national science agency) says sperm whales are "capable of exceeding depths of 2200 metres" (Fetched). Its own heading gives 2,250 m, so the page is slightly inconsistent; the script uses the lower figure. I could not open a primary tag paper with a 2,000 m+ maximum. Watkins et al. 1993 (*Marine Mammal Science*) reports "a dive to 1,185 m and one possibly to 2,000 m" (Search, abstract on an aggregator). If the screener wants a primary paper, the fallback is "more than two kilometers" attributed to CSIRO only, with the "two thousand two hundred" clause cut.
2. **[D2: REJECTED, replaced]** **Ten thousand beaks in one stomach (The Scars).** Malcolm Clarke's report on a whale stranded north of Wellington, New Zealand: "about 10,000 beaks representing 5,000 individual squids and 6 or 7 octopods", of which three beaks were giant squid (Search only; core.ac.uk returned 403). Fallback: "hundreds of beaks from many species" (Discover, Search only), or cut the numbers.
3. **Scar sizes exaggerated to twenty centimeters (The Scars).** Roper & Boss 1982, *Scientific American*, April 1982 ("The giant squid"), Smithsonian repository PDF; the screener found the passage word for word: scar diameters "variously exaggerated, sometimes to as much as 20 centimeters", which led "to a distorted estimate of the maximum length of giant squids" (Search only; the PDF returned 403). The second half (scars grow as the whale grows) is Fetched from the Ozeaneum Stralsund. Fallback: cut the twenty-centimeter sentence and keep the growth line.
4. **Ann Alexander, 1851.** The museum records were Search only (MIT Museum, Australian National Maritime Museum; Wikipedia). They agree that a sperm whale destroyed a whaleboat and then rammed and sank the ship in August 1851, and that the crew got home.
5. **The 1956 bent-jaw whale (The Jaw).** Nasu 1958 (ICR Whales Research Institute note, SC013211-212): a 48-foot male with a "heavily bent" lower jaw, normal in body and organs. Search only (PDF not opened).
6. **[D2: REVISED]** Cylinders have no depth rating, so the line now says Doug's dive course stopped at forty meters. **"Rated to forty meters" (The Click, The Bones), draft 1.** This was a joke about Doug's tank. For the record, the common recreational limit is 40 m / 130 ft (Wikipedia "Deep diving", which cites the RSTC table; dive-industry sources agree). No PADI page could be opened. The brief's fallback ("It is not rated for this.") is available if the screener wants no number.

**Brief checks (corrections and cuts):**
- **"Largest brain of any animal, living or extinct": narrowed.** Guinness says "the largest brain of any animal", about 9 kg, six times heavier than a human brain (Fetched). The "or extinct" part came only from a wiki, so the script says "the biggest brain on Earth".
- **"Rammed twice": confirmed.** Mass Moments (Mass Humanities), quoting Owen Chase: the whale struck the ship with its head, passed under it, came back at about twice its earlier speed and "completely stove in her bow" (Fetched).
- **Heezen 1957: 14 cases, not 16.** "Every whale positively identified was a sperm whale" (Tethys summary of the paper, Fetched).
- **Click depth: not found.** No source gives the depth at which regular clicking starts (Watwood 2006 gives only a mean 392 m descent from the start of regular clicking to the first buzz). No depth is spoken in The Click. The gauge says ABOUT 200 M as staging only.
- **Hydrophone range:** used the 2014 towed-array study (more than 60 km). The more typical 7-8 km (Ocean Station PAPA) was not used.
- **Prey stunning: stronger than the brief.** Fais et al. 2016 (*Scientific Reports*, St Andrews repository) measured tagged whales: in the buzz, source levels drop by one to two orders of magnitude, and received levels at the prey are far below what stunning needs. The script says so in The Buzz.
- **Buzzes per dive: about 18** (Watwood et al. 2006), not 20.
- **Food per day:** NOAA gives about 3-3.5% of body weight a day. No per-day squid count was found, so "hundreds a day" is cut.
- **Bartley:** the script does not use the word "hoax" or say no sperm whale has ever eaten a person. It says the story is made up, based on Davis 1991's findings.
- **Tooth size:** Guinness gives weight (up to 1 kg), not length.
- **Lung collapse:** the source (Scientific American) talks about deep-diving whales and seals in general, not sperm whales measured directly. The script says "deep-diving whales".
- **Bottom shark at 3,000 m: cut.** Not found.
- **Orcas: not mentioned.** No title or line says orcas fear sperm whales.
- **Conservation:** IUCN Vulnerable (Aquarium of the Pacific, Fetched; SeaLifeBase listing of the 2008 assessment, Search). NOAA: ESA endangered and a target of commercial whaling 1800-1987 (Fetched). The script says "listed as vulnerable" and "from eighteen hundred to nineteen eighty-seven".
- **Nobody has watched it hunt at two kilometers: cut.** No source states it. The boss says only that it finds food by sound in the dark.
