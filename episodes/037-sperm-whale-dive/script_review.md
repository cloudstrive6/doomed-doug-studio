## Facts re-verified this pass (web)
1. Clarke & Roper, Paekakariki stranding: more than 3,000 beaks, 24 species, *Architeuthis* 0.12% by number and 23.74% by mass (NISC abstract, fetched). "One beak in eight hundred" is a fair rounding: 0.12% works out to 1 in 833.
2. Watwood 2006: the mean maximum dive depths were 985, 644 and 827 m, so all three regions are under 1 km. The 2,200 m figure is 2.2 times the deepest of those (search, abstract).
3. Watwood 2006: dives averaged 45 minutes, with 9-minute surface intervals (search).
4. Watwood 2006: 37 whales, 198 dives, three regions, about 18 buzzes per dive (draft 1 check, unchanged).
5. Guinness: the teeth "do not appear to be essential", and well-fed whales have been found without them (search; the teeth page now sits at /world-records/449716-largest-toothed-mammal, so the writer can update that URL in facts.md, optional).
6. Moore & Early 2004: 16 museum skeletons from the Pacific and Atlantic, dead bone in the rib and chevron bones, damage worse with size and age, only calves free of it, dysbarism the likely cause (search, WHOI and Science News).
7. Miller et al. 2008: 59 whales tagged with suction cups, vertical drift-dives, non-responsive until the vessel touched them, about 7% of their time (search, St Andrews and Nature News).
8. Heezen 1957: only the 620-fathom case is below 1 km, so the singular wording is correct (facts row 134, Tethys).
9. Burj Khalifa is 828 m tall, and 1,134 minus 828 is 306 m to spare (arithmetic checks out).
10. A tennis court is 23.77 m long, so half is 11.9 m, against a 12.2 m female (NOAA). This checks out.
11. Pressure at 1,500 m: 151 atmospheres absolute, about 156 kgf per square centimeter. "Roughly a hundred and fifty kilograms" and "nearly two Dougs" (160 kg) both hold.
12. Dark for more than half the way down: 1,200 of 2,200 m is 55%. This checks out.
