## 1. Facts: PASS
### Changed claims (The Dive)
| Claim (draft 3) | Result | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| "Scientists tested young saltwater crocodiles in water at twenty-eight degrees Celsius, today's summer temperature, and at up to thirty-five degrees" | **Confirmed (fetched and searched)** | UQ News, 16 Dec 2015: "current summer, 28°C; moderate climate warming, 31.5°C; and high climate warming, up to 35°C." Rodgers, Schwartz and Franklin 2015 (*Conservation Physiology* 3: cov054) used juvenile estuarine crocodiles (n = 11), so "young" is correct. |
| "Cooler water meant longer dives" | **Confirmed** | UQ News: "Acute increases in water temperature resulted in significantly shorter crocodile dives." |
| "their time underwater roughly halved for every three and a half degrees of warming" | **Confirmed** | UQ News: "Their submergence times halved with every 3.5°C increase in water temperature." The abstract says the same of mean submergence times (Q10 0.17). |
| "The crocodile never has to come up to check on Doug, because it can simply wait" | OK | This is narrator framing taken from the sourced dive durations, and facts.md marks it as an inference. |

**Nuance, not an error:** the abstract says *maximal* dive performance did not change between 28 and 35°C. The halving applies to *mean* submergence under acute warming. The script says "their time underwater", which fairly describes the mean, and the on-screen "DIVE TIME HALVES EVERY 3.5°C WARMER" is the press release's own wording. No change needed.
