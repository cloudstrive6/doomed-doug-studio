## 1. The Corpse Flower (Rafflesia arnoldii)
- Largest known individual flower; parasitic on Tetrastigma (grape family); buds emerge through the host's bark. Kew, Plants of the World Online: https://powo.science.kew.org/taxon/urn:lsid:ipni.org:names:316069-1/general-information
- About 1 m across, up to about 11 kg; no observable leaves, stems or roots; holoparasite of Tetrastigma; Sumatra. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rafflesia_arnoldii (Quanta, below, gives "over a meter" and "about 10 kg"; the script says "about a meter" and "up to eleven kilograms").
- Lives inside the host vine as thin strands of cells, hard to tell apart from the vine's tissue under a microscope; only the flowers emerge; putrid rotting-meat smell draws carrion flies as pollinators. Quanta Magazine, 2021: https://quantamagazine.org/dna-of-giant-corpse-flower-parasite-surprises-biologists-20210421
- Flies treat the bloom like a carcass and are the pollinators: https://www.botany.one/rafflesias-stinky-secret-under-the-spotlight/
- Close relative R. lagascae: no substantial chloroplast genome recovered, no intact plastid genes (Molina et al. 2014, Mol. Biol. Evol., doi:10.1093/molbev/msu051): https://pgc.up.edu.ph/possible-loss-of-the-chloroplast-genome-in-the-parasitic-plant-rafflesia-lagascae-rafflesiaceae/ ; script hedges "could not even find a working chloroplast genome".
- Car tyre scale analogy: a car tyre is roughly 0.6-0.7 m across, so a 1 m bloom is "wider than a car tyre".
- "Never in people": Rafflesia is host-specific to Tetrastigma vines (Kew, above).

## 2. The Dodder (Cuscuta)
- Leafless vine that winds around its host for life once attached (Penn State, below); typically orange stems; seedlings are the only stage with roots; must find a host within a few days or die; haustoria; host pruning notes. Bugwood / Colorado State: https://wiki.bugwood.org/HPIPM:Dodder
- Original soil root dies once established on a host; haustoria invade the host's vascular tissue. UW-Madison Extension: https://hort.extension.wisc.edu/articles/dodder-cuscuta-spp/ ; UF/IFAS: https://edis.ifas.ufl.edu/publication/EP556/pdf
- Seedlings grow toward tomato volatiles, prefer tomato over wheat; one wheat compound repels them (Runyon, Mescher & De Moraes 2006, Science 313:1964). Penn State: https://www.psu.edu/news/research/story/parasitic-plants-sniff-out-hosts-according-penn-state-research ; paper PDF: https://www.fs.usda.gov/rm/pubs_other/rmrs_2006_runyon_j001.pdf
- Winds around the host: Penn State page above.
- Genomic-scale mRNA exchange in both directions between Cuscuta and hosts (Arabidopsis, tomato) (Kim et al. 2014, Science 345:808): https://pure.psu.edu/en/publications/genomic-scale-exchange-of-mrna-between-a-parasitic-plant-and-its-/ ; https://news.vt.edu/articles/2014/08/081514-cals-talkingplants.html

## 3. The Tapeworm (Dibothriocephalus latus)
- Current name Dibothriocephalus latus (formerly Diphyllobothrium latum); adults can reach more than 10 m; more than 3,000 proglottids (segments); acquired from raw or undercooked fish; perch and pike are common fish hosts; mature adults 2-15 m with occasional larger specimens; infections can persist up to 25 years; most infections are asymptomatic (script: "feel nothing in particular"); humans are definitive hosts ("the host is supposed to be a person"). CDC DPDx: https://www.cdc.gov/dpdx/diphyllobothriasis/index.html
- City bus about 10-12 m long (standard 40 ft bus is about 12 m): everyday comparison, stated as "about the length".
- Whale tapeworm and Placentonema records: cut (not sourced beyond listicles).

## 4. The Cable-Cutting Tree (Nuytsia floribunda)
- Hemiparasite: green leaves photosynthesise; only the theft of water and sap happens through root haustoria, hence "all of its stealing underground" (ANBG and Australian Geographic below).
- Western Australian Christmas tree; haustorial root connections with any host it contacts, including herbs and grasses; "found guilty of damaging underground telephone cables with its girdling haustoria"; roots spread for tens of metres; golden-orange flowers in summer. Australian National Botanic Gardens: https://anbg.gov.au/mistletoe/tree-mistletoes.html
- Roots reach up to 110 m; a collar forms where its root meets another root and a blade forms inside it; feeds on grasses, sedges, weeds, vines, shrubs, eucalypts ("gum trees"); flowers around Christmas; power failures when it attacks buried cables by mistake; telephone lines cut. Australian Geographic: https://www.australiangeographic.com.au/?p=35974
- Constricting rings around underground cables: Britannica: https://www.britannica.com/plant/Australian-Christmas-tree
- Football pitch about 105 m long (FIFA recommended 105 x 68 m), so 110 m is "longer than a football pitch".

## 5. Pando
- About 43 ha, about 47,000 genetically identical stems from one root system; mule deer browsing blocks regeneration; the 2013 fence produced strong regeneration, the 2014 fence was breached by deer and regeneration there was no better than unfenced; cattle graze briefly. Rogers & McAvoy 2018, PLOS ONE: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6192553
- 106 acres, quaking aspen, Fishlake National Forest, "Trembling Giant", spreads by new shoots from the root system, "believed to be the largest organism ever found at nearly 13 million pounds". USDA Forest Service: https://www.fs.usda.gov/r04/fishlake/recreation/explore-forest/pando
- 13 million lb = about 5,900 t, "about six thousand tonnes" (Wikipedia gives about 6,000 t): https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pando_(tree)
- Deer browse young stems, loss of next-generation trees: Utah State University: https://www.usu.edu/today/story/first-comprehensive-assessment-of-pando-aspen-clone-reveals-critical-threats
- 43 ha / 0.714 ha per pitch = about 60 pitches.
- "Heaviest living thing we know of": follows the USFS weight claim; the boss carries the honesty line on area.

## 6. The Humongous Fungus (Armillaria gallica, Michigan)
- 1992 (Smith, Bruhn & Anderson, Nature): one genetic individual, at least 37 ha, more than 100,000 kg, at least 1,500 years. 2018 (Anderson et al., Proc. R. Soc. B, doi:10.1098/rspb.2018.2233): about 75 ha, about 400,000 kg, minimum age 2,500 years; extremely low mutation rate over the genome; Michigan's Upper Peninsula; rhizomorphs are cord-like, grow through soil like roots and forage for dead wood or weakened trees. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6304041
- 2015-2017 resampling, hundreds of samples (245 per press): https://www.atlasobscura.com/articles/biggest-mushroom-in-the-world ; https://phys.org/news/2018-12-giant-fungus-acres-stable-mutation.html
- "That made headlines": the 1992 Nature report drew wide press coverage; the 2018 follow-up press summarises it: https://www.atlasobscura.com/articles/biggest-mushroom-in-the-world
- Rhizomorphs look like bootlaces / shoestrings: https://scientificamerican.com/article/strange-but-true-largest-organism-is-fungus
- Blue whale up to 330,000 lb (about 150 t), so 400 t is about 2.7 blue whales, "close to three". NOAA Fisheries: https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/species/blue-whale
- "Doug has been mould before": 017 callback (series bible).

## 7. The Glowing Forest (Armillaria ostoyae, Washington)
- Announced 1992 by Terry Shaw (USFS) and Ken Russell (WA DNR); about 1,500 acres / 600 ha, south of Mount Adams; age estimated 400 to 1,000 years. Seattle Times 1992: https://archive.seattletimes.com/archive/19920518/1492535/our-fungus-badder-and-bigger-covering-1500-acres ; Scientific American: https://scientificamerican.com/article/strange-but-true-largest-organism-is-fungus ; Britannica: https://www.britannica.com/science/Armillaria-ostoyae
- 600 ha = 6 square kilometers (unit conversion).
- A. ostoyae is the dominant conifer pathogen in western North America; rhizomorphs spread to adjacent roots; white mycelial fans between wood and bark (like a layer of dried latex paint); resinosis; spread by root-to-root contact; mortality centres or gaps in older forests. USDA Forest Service DecAID: https://apps.fs.usda.gov/decaid/views/armillaria_root_disease.html ; USFS FIDL 188: https://www.fs.usda.gov/foresthealth/docs/fidls/FIDL-188-ArmillariaRootDisease.pdf
- Michigan species feeds on dead wood or weakened trees (PMC6304041 above); A. ostoyae kills conifers (SciAm, DecAID).
- Foxfire: honey mushroom emits a very low light from its mycelium; most common source of foxfire; light is yellowish-green; 15-20 minutes of dark adaptation needed. Northern Woodlands: https://northernwoodlands.org/knots_and_bolts/light-in-forest ; Armillaria mycelium and rhizomorphs luminous, fruiting bodies not: https://www.fungimag.com/v17i1/V17I1-Blanchette.pdf

## 8. The Blue Mountains Fungus (Armillaria ostoyae, Malheur NF, Oregon)
- 965 ha (2,385 acres), Malheur National Forest, about 1,350 soccer fields, at least 2,400 years old and may be as much as 8,650 years. Guinness World Records: https://www.guinnessworldrecords.com/world-records/66481-largest-fungi
- Blue Mountains of Oregon; discovered 1998 (script: "found in nineteen ninety-eight"), made public about 2000, published 2003; mapped by pairing cultures to see which fused, then DNA fingerprinting to find where one individual ended; age from growth rate; causes Armillaria root disease that kills conifers. Scientific American: https://scientificamerican.com/article/strange-but-true-largest-organism-is-fungus ; Ferguson et al. 2003, Can. J. For. Res. (cited in USFS: https://webarchive.library.unt.edu/web/20170225001506/http://www.fs.usda.gov/Internet/FSE_DOCUMENTS/fsbdev3_033146.pdf)
- Took the record from the Washington fungus: Guidebook to Fungi: https://davidmoore.org.uk/21st_Century_Guidebook_to_Fungi_PLATINUM/Ch14_04.htm
- Honey mushrooms appear after the first fall rains at the base of infected or recently killed trees (USFS Malheur publication above); clusters around dead or dying trees in fall: https://dnr.wisconsin.gov/topic/foresthealth/armillaria ; the mushrooms are the fruiting structure of a mostly underground infection.
- Kills conifers, including Douglas fir (costume is staging): https://pnwhandbooks.org/plantdisease/host-disease/fir-douglas-true-armillaria-root-rot
- Honesty line: one Posidonia australis clone in Shark Bay, about 200 km2 of meadow (Edgeloe et al. 2022, Proc. R. Soc. B, doi:10.1098/rspb.2022.0538): https://phys.org/news/2022-06-largest-earth-years-km-seagrass.html ; https://www.ecowatch.com/worlds-largest-plant.html . 200 km2 = 20,000 ha, about 21 times 965 ha ("roughly twenty times").
- "Eating this forest for thousands of years": age at least 2,400 years (Guinness). Deliberately not "since before writing" (writing is about 5,000 years old, older than the minimum age).
