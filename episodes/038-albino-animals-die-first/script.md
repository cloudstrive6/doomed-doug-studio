The White Whale.

The White Lion.

## The White Bird

The White Bird.

[TIME LEFT sign: YEARS. Two flight feathers side by side: one dark and crisp, one white and frayed. A red arrow at the white one.]

An albino bird's feathers wear out faster than a normal bird's, because the pigment it's missing is also what makes feathers tough.

[A feather under a crude microscope. Two blocks of keratin, the pale one drawn thicker. Label: "39% THICKER".]

One study tested feather keratin and found the dark kind was harder, so pale keratin would need to be about forty percent thicker to stand up to the same wear.

[A pair of white sneakers on gravel, scuffed grey.]

Picture white sneakers worn every day on gravel, except the sneakers are how you escape.

[A park pond. A white duck with dark eyes. Label: "LEUCISTIC".]

In a British survey of birds with unusual plumage, over three-quarters were classed as leucistic, with dark eyes that work. Some white birds with reddish eyes have a different condition that keeps a little working pigment, and they see much better than albinos do. The British Trust for Ornithology, which ran the survey, says that seeing an adult albino in the wild is very unlikely, because its bad eyesight means its survival prospects are poor.

[A hawk silhouette with a red glow, high above a small white bird.]

The cruel part is that an albino bird is losing on two fronts at once. It can't see the hawk coming, and when it finally flees, the wings carrying it are wearing out underneath it.
