### Winter.

**1. The Wolves** (hook item, one of the three strongest; death 268)
- Facts:
  - Yellowstone wolves seldom hunt bison. Their success rose with pack size only up to about 9-13 wolves, and bison remained the hardest prey
    (MacNulty et al. 2014, *Behavioral Ecology*, "Influence of group size on the success of wolves hunting bison"; verify the exact numbers and wording).
  - Bison stand their ground and bunch together instead of running, and the wolves have to make one bison run (same paper, or Smith et al. on Yellowstone wolf-bison hunts).
  - Elk are the wolves' main prey (NPS Yellowstone wolf pages).
- Analogy: a full pack against one animal (a pack about the size of a football team's starting line-up).
- **Twist by 0:45 (about 145 words):** the top predator in the park needs about a dozen of itself to try a bison, and still loses most of the time.
- Doug beat:
  - Inverted costume gag, once: Doug is zipped into a wolf costume, cap on top, "to make the numbers up". The pack is twelve. Doug is thirteen.
  - **"Doug did not agree to this."** (here)
  - The bison picks the newest member. Death 268, cut to black. The cap sits in the snow among the wolf tracks.
  - Field guide: NO.
  - *Overlap:* 011 said "wolves bring down even bison" in one line. This is the honest version, so say once that bringing one down is rare.

**2. The Snowplow** (Doug SURVIVES; "Doug is fine.")
- Facts:
  - Bison swing their heads side to side to clear snow down to the grass. The hump is muscle on long spines of the backbone that powers the swing (NPS bison page; a museum or university anatomy source for the spines).
  - Weight: bulls up to about 900 kg (2,000 lb), cows about 450 kg (1,000 lb) (NPS).
  - In winter some bison move to thermal areas where the ground is warm and the snow is thin (NPS; verify).
- Analogy: the bull's weight. **Not "a small car"**: 005 used that for a bison. Use something new, for example about two grand pianos, or a big bale of hay times N, but only if the source weights stand.
- Twist: the most terrifying thing about a bison in winter is that it does not care about you at all. It is busy, and it is moving snow with its face.
- Doug beat: Doug shovels beside it to help (the `snow_shovel` asset). It clears a metre of path in the time Doug clears a step, then plows straight through Doug's pile.
  He is buried to the cap. "Doug is fine." The narrator expected more. Field guide: "MAYBE".

**3. The Hump** (death 269)
- Facts:
  - That same head-and-hump build is what a bison hits with. A charging bison hooks with its horns and has thrown people into the air (NPS news releases; give no names and use no release about a child).
  - **Both sexes have horns** (NPS). Most charges seen by visitors involve cows as well as bulls (only if a source says so; otherwise cut that line).
- Analogy: the head swing that moves snow, aimed at Doug instead.
- Twist: everything it does in winter to survive is the same motion it uses on people.
- Doug beat: Doug stands where the bison was about to swing next. He goes up in a long arc and comes down out of frame. The cap floats down a beat later. Death 269. **Cartoon only: no horn contact on screen, no landing, no injury.**

### Spring.

**4. The Calf** (death 270)
- Facts:
  - Calves are born mostly in April and May. They are orange-red at birth ("red dogs"), and the herd and the mothers defend them (NPS).
  - A calf can stand and follow the herd within hours (NPS or a university extension source; verify the number).
  - **2023 (NPS news release):** a visitor pushed a newborn calf out of a river, the herd rejected it, and the park had to put it down. The visitor was fined. **No name, no mockery**, one sober sentence: the danger in this story was to the calf.
- Analogy: a calf's birth weight against something everyday (for example a big bag of dog food), sourced.
- Twist: the smallest bison in the park is the one that gets people hurt, and sometimes the one that gets hurt.
- Doug beat: Doug sees a calf alone and says "hi friend?". The calf is not alone. Death 270, flat. Field guide: MAYBE erased.
- **Kid-appeal rule:** the calf is small, orange, seen from a distance and drawn plainly. No big eyes, no smile, no close-up. The mother fills the frame.

**5. The Speed** (death 271)
- Facts:
  - Bison can run up to about 56 km/h (35 mph), about three times faster than a person (NPS). They can also jump high fences and turn quickly (NPS; verify the fence height).
- Analogy: 56 km/h is faster than the speed limit on most town streets. Or: a 900 kg animal at that speed covers the length of a tennis court in about a second (the writer checks the arithmetic).
- Twist: it looks like a sofa that eats grass, which is why nobody believes it can do this until it does.
- Doug beat: Doug times a bison with a stopwatch "for science". It finishes the distance before he presses start. Death 271. The stopwatch reads 0.00 next to the cap.
  - Re-hook into Summer: "And then the tourists arrived."

### Summer.

**6. The Record** (the core claim; death 272)
- Facts:
  - **NPS: bison have injured more people in Yellowstone than any other animal.** The park's rule is to stay at least 25 yards (23 m) from bison (NPS). State it once as the park's rule, not as advice.
  - CDC MMWR (Cherry et al. 2016, "Injuries Associated with Bison Encounters, Yellowstone National Park, 2015"):
    the five injuries that year, and how close the people had come. Some were taking photos. **Verify every number; no names.**
  - Older data: Conrad & Balison 1994 (*Journal of Wilderness Medicine*), bison goring injuries in Yellowstone 1978-1992 against bear injuries in the same period. Verify, or cut.
- Analogy: 23 m is about two school buses end to end (verify the bus length).
- Twist: almost every one of those people walked up to the bison. The bison did not come to them.
- Doug beat: Doug with the selfie stick (`selfie_stick_phone`), backing toward the bison to fit it in the frame. The photo is great. Death 272. The phone lands screen-up with the photo on it, the cap beside it.
- **Sober card (required):** white on black, numbers only, no names, no injury words (027/029 style). No Doug and no joke on the card.

**7. The Jam** (death 273)
- Facts:
  - "Bison jams": herds walk on and across the park roads, and traffic stops for them (NPS).
  - Vehicles hit bison in the park every year (NPS or a Yellowstone Science article; give the source's figure or cut it). The danger here runs both ways.
  - A bison has charged a car (only if sourced; otherwise cut it).
- Analogy: the herd that crosses against a line of cars (traffic stopped for N minutes, only if sourced).
- Twist: in Yellowstone the bison have right of way. Nobody gave it to them; they took it.
- Doug beat: Doug gets out of his car (`small_car`) to "move them along" with a wave. Death 273. The cap is on the car's roof, and the bison is still in the road. *(If item 7 can't be sourced, cut it and lengthen item 6.)*

### The rut.

**8. The Wallow** (Doug SURVIVES; "Doug is fine.")
- Facts:
  - Bison roll in bare-dirt hollows called wallows. In the rut, bulls wallow more and it is part of the display (NPS; a study on wallowing and rut behaviour if available).
  - Bull bellows during the rut carry a long way (give a distance only if the source has one).
  - Rut season is roughly late July to August, and the park warns that bulls are more aggressive then (NPS). **This is the axis's peak and must be sourced.**
- Analogy: the bellow's range against something everyday (it carries further than a lawnmower), sourced or cut.
- Twist: the dust cloud on the horizon in August is not weather. It is several hundred tonnes of animal in a bad mood.
- Doug beat: Doug rolls in a wallow too "to fit in". A bull watches, decides Doug is not competition, and leaves. "Doug is fine." The narrator finds this result insulting on Doug's behalf.

**9. The Rut** (the boss; death 274)
- Facts:
  - Bulls fight head to head for mating rights. Thick hair and skull on the forehead take the hits. Bulls can lose a lot of weight during the rut (NPS, or Lott, *American Bison: A Natural History*, 2002; verify).
  - A bull stays with one cow for hours or days ("tending") and drives off anything that comes close (NPS or Lott; verify).
  - Yellowstone is the only place in the US where bison have lived continuously since prehistoric times. They fell to about two dozen wild animals around 1902 and number several thousand today (NPS).
    One line, as the reason they are everywhere now.
- **Honesty line, required:** bison very rarely kill anyone. The park's record is injuries, not deaths, and almost all of them came from people getting close. Give NPS's or the MMWR's wording.
  The title is "most terrifying", not "deadliest", for this reason.
- Twist (the payoff of the promise): two bulls of up to about 900 kg each square up, and Doug steps between them to referee, with a whistle.
- Doug beat:
  - Doug blows the whistle.
  - Both bulls charge at once, and Doug is the only thing either of them hits. Death 274, played flat. Cut to black on the impact.
  - Field guide closes: seven NOs, and the sticky note "PLANT EATER."
- **Closing image:** the herd moves off across the valley at dusk. The cap is hooked on a bull's horn, riding away above the dust. Kicker (the writer rewords):
  "The cap has been adopted by the herd. It has right of way now."
- **Outro:** 30 words or fewer. The final counter (274), one dry line, re-hooked to the end slate. No recap, no subscribe pitch.
