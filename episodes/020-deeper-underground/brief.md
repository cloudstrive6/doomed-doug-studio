## 2. Escalation axis and items
**Axis: depth below the surface, in metres, strictly increasing.** The last item is the centre of the Earth. Spoken section headers
(not used before):
- **"Dark."** (opener, items 1-4: caves)
- **"Hot."** (before item 5, about 33%: mines and the borehole)
- **"Nobody."** (before item 9, about 66%: where no person or machine has been)

**Episode devices:**
- **Cross-section cut (new, episode-only):** between items, a crude cutaway Earth with a red dot for Doug. For items 1-8 the dot does not
  visibly move: everything people have ever reached sits inside the outline's line width. This pays off at Kola (0.2% of the way down)
  and then the dot finally moves in "Nobody.".
- **Episode meter: "DOUG DEPTH"** beside the counter (metres, then km), monotonic. No temperature meter (cave temperatures are not
  monotonic). A thermometer annotation is fine inside the "Hot." items.
- **Vehicle:** the real access at each stop (cave rope, the mine cage, a string down the borehole). In "Nobody." the narrator issues a
  **drill pod**: a crude MS Paint capsule with a nose drill and one porthole. New prop, art director owns the drawing, never explained.
- **Suitcase** (`places` standard prop): a sticker per death site. It goes into the drill pod with him.

**Rules:** every number and date needs a source; where estimates vary (core temperature, ringwoodite water), give the range or say
"scientists estimate". If a fact fails, cut it. Deaths: 9, numbered 162 to 170. Death 170 is just a number, no bit.

### Dark.
**1. Movile Cave** (Romania, about 20 m; the hook item; death 162)
- Facts:
  - Found in 1986 near Mangalia; sealed from the surface for about 5.5 million years (verify the estimate and its source).
  - The air pocket has roughly half normal oxygen and high CO2, plus hydrogen sulphide and methane (verify the figures).
  - A whole food web runs on chemosynthesis, not sunlight; dozens of species found nowhere else (verify the count, often "over 30
    endemic").
  - Entry is only by permit, through a shaft and a flooded sump.
- Scale analogy: sealed before the first human ancestors stood up; or "the cave's last fresh air is older than our species by about
  20 times" (writer checks the maths).
- Doug beat: Doug brings a sandwich for the blind spiders; the twist is the air. Cartoon faint, X eyes, the cap rests on the floating
  bacterial mat. Sticker "MOVILE CAVE".

**2. Lechuguilla Cave** (New Mexico, about 489 m deep; death 163)
- Facts:
  - One of the deepest and longest caves in the US (about 240 km mapped; verify), sealed until cavers dug in, 1986.
  - Bacteria isolated for about 4 million years were resistant to most modern antibiotics (Bhullar et al. 2012, *PLoS ONE*; verify
    wording: resistance older than antibiotics).
  - 1991: caver Emily Davis Mobley broke her leg deep inside; the rescue took about four days (verify). Sober one-line card, no Doug.
- Scale analogy: 240 km of passages is roughly New York to Philadelphia and back (verify), all in the dark.
- Doug beat: Doug takes a wrong turn among the gypsum "chandeliers". Lost, lying flat, cap on a crystal. Sticker "LECHUGUILLA".

**3. The Devil Worm** (*Halicephalobus mephisto*, Beatrix gold mine, South Africa, about 1.3 km; Doug survives)
- Facts:
  - Borgonie et al. 2011, *Nature*: a nematode about 0.5 mm long living in fracture water about 1.3 km down.
  - The water it lives in is thousands of years old (verify the range) and around 37-48 C (verify).
  - It was the first animal (multicellular) found that deep in the crust; named after Mephistopheles.
- Scale analogy: shorter than a grain of rice is long, living deeper than five Eiffel Towers stacked (verify: 1.3 km / 330 m).
- Doug beat: "hi friend?" It ignores him. Doug survives; the narrator, who had named the item after the devil for a reason, is
  quietly disappointed. No sticker.

**4. Veryovkina Cave** (Abkhazia, Georgia, about 2,212 m; the deepest known cave; death 164)
- Facts:
  - Deepest known cave on Earth, 2,212 m, reached in 2018 (verify the current record and date).
  - In 2018 a Russian team near the bottom had to climb out as a flash flood filled the lower cave fast (verify the rise in metres and
    timing; nobody died, say so).
  - Getting to the bottom takes days of rope descents and underground camps (verify).
- Scale analogy: deeper than about 2.5 Burj Khalifas stacked (828 m each; writer checks).
- Doug beat: Doug pitches a tent at the bottom. The water arrives; the cap floats up the shaft alone. Sticker "VERYOVKINA".

### Hot.
**5. The Oldest Water** (Kidd Mine, Ontario, about 2.4-3 km; Doug survives)
- Facts:
  - Water found in 2013 at about 2.4 km was estimated at about 1.5 billion years old; water found in 2016 at about 3 km was about 2
    billion years old (Holland et al. 2013 *Nature*; 2016 AGU/news; verify both).
  - Geochemist Barbara Sherwood Lollar tasted it: very salty, more bitter than seawater (verify the quote).
  - It is many times saltier than seawater and carries hydrogen and sulphate that microbes can live on (verify).
- Scale analogy: older than the first plant, the first animal and the first fish; if Earth's history were a day, this water was
  bottled before lunch (writer checks).
- Doug beat: Doug drinks it. "Doug is fine." The narrator does not recommend this (no health advice; just "it is not drinking water").

**6. The Bacterium That Lives Alone** (*Desulforudis audaxviator*, Mponeng mine, about 2.8 km; death 165)
- Facts:
  - Chivian et al. 2008, *Science*: one species makes up almost the whole ecosystem in that fracture water.
  - Its energy ultimately comes from radioactive decay (uranium in the rock splitting water), not from the Sun.
  - The name comes from a line in Jules Verne's *Journey to the Center of the Earth* ("bold traveller"; verify).
- Scale analogy: the loneliest living thing known; one species, no neighbours, for millions of years (verify the isolation estimate).
- Doug beat: Doug moves in to keep it company. Cartoon green glow, X eyes (no radiation sickness detail). Sticker "MPONENG".

**7. Mponeng** (the deepest mine, about 4 km; death 166)
- Facts:
  - The deepest mine on Earth, mining below about 3.8-4 km (verify the current figure).
  - Rock temperature about 60-66 C; the mine pumps ice slurry down to keep air bearable (verify).
  - The trip from the surface to the bottom takes about an hour by cage (verify).
- Scale analogy: rock hotter than a hot bath you can't keep a hand in; a lift ride longer than most films' first act (writer checks).
- Callback (one clause): "Doug has been this hot before" (004, Cave of Crystals). Doug beat: the ice slurry runs out while he is
  waiting for the cage. Steam lines, flat. Sticker "MPONENG" again, so the narrator stamps "AGAIN" on it.

**8. The Kola Superdeep Borehole** (Russia, 12,262 m; Doug survives, the cap goes instead)
- Facts:
  - Drilled 1970-1992; reached 12,262 m in 1989, still the deepest artificial point (verify, versus longer but shallower oil wells).
  - Rock at the bottom was about 180 C, much hotter than predicted; rock behaved plastically and the hole kept closing (verify).
  - The hole is about 23 cm wide (verify). The famous "screams from hell" recording is a hoax: say so in one line, and say it is a
    hoax, without repeating it in detail.
  - Microscopic plankton fossils were found about 6 km down (one sentence only).
  - 12 km is about 0.2% of the way to the centre (6,371 km). The cross-section dot has not moved.
- Scale analogy: the hole is narrower than a dinner plate, deeper than Everest is tall (8,849 m); if Earth were an apple, it doesn't
  break the skin.
- Doug beat: Doug does not fit. The narrator lowers the cap on a string; it comes back up warm. "The cap has now been deeper than Doug.
  Doug is fine." No sticker.

### Nobody.
**9. The Moho** (about 35 km under continents, about 7 km under the ocean floor; death 167)
- Facts:
  - The boundary between crust and mantle, found by Andrija Mohorovičić in 1909 from earthquake waves (verify).
  - Project Mohole (1961) tried to drill to it through the seafloor and was cancelled (1966) (verify); Japan's *Chikyu* is still trying.
  - Nobody has ever sampled it in place.
- Scale analogy: 35 km is about four Everests stacked; a commuter drive straight down (writer checks).
- Doug beat: the drill pod arrives (new prop, introduced dead-pan, no explanation). It works. Doug does not. Sticker "THE MOHO".

**10. The Hidden Ocean** (transition zone, about 410-660 km; death 168)
- Facts:
  - Pearson et al. 2014, *Nature*: a Brazilian diamond carried ringwoodite with about 1.5% water by weight.
  - This suggests the transition zone may hold as much water as the oceans, or more; estimates vary (Schmandt et al. 2014, *Science*;
    verify and say "may").
  - The water is locked in the crystal, not liquid. Say this clearly.
- Scale analogy: an ocean's worth of water hidden in rock, like a sponge the size of a planet layer (writer checks wording).
- Doug beat: Doug packed a snorkel. There is no water to swim in. Pressure, cartoon flat. Sticker "HIDDEN OCEAN".

**11. The Outer Core** (about 2,890-5,150 km; death 169)
- Facts:
  - Liquid iron and nickel, roughly 4,000-5,000+ C (verify range).
  - Its flow generates Earth's magnetic field, which shields the surface from much of the solar wind (verify the safe phrasing).
  - The field's poles drift, and the field has reversed many times (verify).
- Scale analogy: a liquid metal sea bigger than Mars (outer core radius about 3,480 km vs Mars 3,390 km; verify).
- Doug beat: Doug's compass spins. The drill pod melts into a cartoon blob; only the suitcase and cap float in the iron. Sticker
  "OUTER CORE".

**12. The Inner Core** (about 5,150-6,371 km; the boss; death 170)
- Facts:
  - Solid iron-nickel ball, radius about 1,220 km (about 70% of the Moon's radius; verify).
  - About 5,200-6,000 C, comparable to the Sun's surface (Anzellini et al. 2013, *Science*; verify), yet solid because of pressure of
    about 3.3-3.6 million atmospheres.
  - Its rotation relative to the mantle seems to change; recent studies say it slowed or reversed relative to the surface (Yang & Song
    2023 *Nature Geoscience*; Wang et al. 2024 *Nature*; scientists still argue, say so).
- Scale analogy: as hot as the surface of the Sun, but under the weight of the whole planet; it can't melt even though it should.
- Twist: the hottest place Doug has ever been is the one place nothing melts. Doug beat: Doug plants a flag at the exact centre. Every
  direction is now "up". Flat, X eyes. Sticker "THE CENTRE"; suitcase full.
- **Closing image:** back on the surface, the suitcase and the cap come up together out of a hole that wasn't there. The narrator: the
  suitcase "still has room" (approved bible line). Then the outro.

Deaths: 9 (162-170). Survivals: Devil Worm, Oldest Water, Kola. 12 items. There is no optional 13th item: if the draft runs under 2,900 words,
lengthen items 9-12 (they carry the least familiar science).

## 7. Risks
- **Kid-appeal risk (MEDIUM-HIGH):** "centre of the Earth" and "deepest cave" are heavily searched by kids and Minecraft players
  ("deepest cave in minecraft" is in autocomplete).
  - No game references, no "Journey to the Center" movie jokes (the Verne naming fact is fine, as a fact).
  - Adult-coded humour: the narrator's bureaucracy (stamping "AGAIN"), the Soviet project paperwork, the hoax, the loneliest bacterium.
  - Doug's drill pod is crude and dry, not a cute vehicle. No sound effects.
- **Gore (LOW-MEDIUM):** deaths are bad air, getting lost, flood, heat, pressure and molten metal. **No burns, no crushing detail, no
  drowning description.** Cut to X eyes, steam lines, the cap floating, or a cartoon blob.
- **Real people and deaths (MEDIUM):** the Mobley rescue (she survived) and the Veryovkina flood (all survived) are told straight, on
  sober cards, no Doug in those frames. No mine-accident deaths are used. No real-person jokes.
- **Safety-advice risk:** Doug drinks ancient brine and enters sealed caves. Never present any of it as safe or advisable, and give no
  advice. "It is not drinking water" is enough.
- **Accuracy risk (HIGH):**
  - Kola "well to hell" sounds: hoax, say so in one line, and don't play or describe a "recording".
  - The ringwoodite "ocean": water bound in mineral, not liquid; estimates vary.
  - Core temperatures and inner-core rotation: give ranges, name the debate.
  - Deepest cave and deepest mine records change; use the current verified figure.
  - The Moho depth varies; give "about".
- **Copyright:** no cave or mine photos without a verified licence; NASA/USGS public-domain diagrams are fine for evidence insets. No copied
  cutaway art from competitors.
