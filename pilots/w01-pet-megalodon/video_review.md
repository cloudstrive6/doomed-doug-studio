# w01 video review: What If a Baby MEGALODON Lived in Your Pool for 30 Days?

Independent review, 2026-11-26. Reviewer did not build this episode. Judged from the 8 contact sheets (one frame every 3 s), extra full-size frames pulled from `build/final.mp4` at line timings, `script.md`, `build/lines.json`, `build/shots.json`, `build/qc.json`, `packaging.md`, `thumbs/sheet.jpg`, the DOOMPERK reference sheets and the restyle plan. The script is the strongest part and is close to DOOMPERK level. The picture is not: it is clean and consistent in style, but it is full of framing and layering glitches, and many beats the narration names never appear on screen. Fix the picture before upload.

## 1. Hook: 3

What works:
- Line 1 at 0.6 s is second person with a number: "Last night, a storm threw a two metre shark over your back fence and into your swimming pool."
- Rival inside 20 s with a name card and a joke caption: Tanner's card "TANNER SLOANE / Neighbour. 41,000 followers. Met four." is on screen at 0:09, matching the 8.9 s line.
- Four-beat tease from 22.9 s to 39.8 s (pool shrinks, officer with net, neighbour sells, "on night thirty, Tanner is alone in the open sea"), ending on a cliffhanger: "the shark he tried to sell gets to decide what happens next."
- Title drop at 39.8 s, word 128 (QC: "39.8 s, word 128 PASS"), inside the 35 to 90 s, 72 to 153 word window.

What holds it back:
- The first frame is weak. At 0:00 and 0:03 Doug's head is jammed against the top edge beside the DAY 1 badge, and the shark is already parked in the pool with its eyes shut. The most visual moment in the premise (a shark thrown over the fence by a storm) is told, not shown. DOOMPERK's top video opens on the crash itself.
- At 5.6 s the narrator says "it seems to like you" while Meatball's eyes are closed arcs (0:00 to 0:06): no grin, no eye contact.
- At 0:13 ("Your neighbour, Tanner Sloane...") the frame is Doug's headless body and a green-tinted pool; Tanner is off frame.
- The tease does not show what it says: "A fisheries officer with a very large net" (22.9 s) shows Hobbs with a clipboard and no net (0:27), with a stray shark fin on the driveway beside her. "Tanner is alone in the open sea" shows Tanner standing upright on top of the water (0:36), which reads as calm, not drowning.
- The title card reads "WHAT IF YOU HAD / A PET MEGALODON?" and the narrator says "So, what if you had a pet megalodon?" (39.8 s). That is the retired working title. `packaging.md` retired it for being one word off DOOMPERK's "What If You Had a PET Tornado?" and asked for it to be changed before render. It also does not match the title viewers clicked.

## 4. Pacing and variety: 2

Measured numbers are fine on paper: QC "Cuts a minute (target 20 to 31): 25.0"; `shots.json` has 329 shots (28 a minute); 149 speaker changes in 11.4 min of voice (13.1 a minute, above DOOMPERK's median of about 8). The picture still feels repetitive:

- **One camera angle for 41% of the episode.** 135 of 329 shots are the backyard (backyard_pool 102, yard_night 20, storm 13), all from the same side-on view of the fence line. No top-down pool shot, no underwater cutaway, no low angle from the water, no over-the-shoulder. DOOMPERK's reference sheet uses aerials, close inserts, depth of field and diagram cutaways in the first two minutes alone.
- **Meatball gets 4 close-ups in 11.6 minutes** (`frame: close:meg` 4 times). The title character is mostly a wide side-view sticker.
- **Characters barely act.** 314 of 329 shots have no pose; only 15 shots have a held prop. Most frames are two people standing at a fence with one arm out (sheet 1, 1:06 to 1:27; sheet 4, 5:00 to 5:27).
- **Kinetic words: 11 in 11.6 minutes** (CONTENT, FOUR THOUSAND BUCKETS, KEEP SWIMMING, TAP TAP, SIGNED, GROWTH SPURT, TWO WEEKS, THE PLAN, NOT THIS TIME, SPLASH, FOUND HIM). DOOMPERK has an overlay on almost every shot.
- **Dead stretch, 10:18 to 11:12:** the honesty segment loops three shots (Doug wide, the off-model shark, Doug close) for 54 seconds. The script asks for an icon per fact ("tooth, jaw, rings, map pin, thermometer, snout dots"); none were built.
- **Montages are thin:** "four thousand buckets" is two shots of Doug and Wren holding buckets at the fence (1:30, 1:33), no beach or dunes.
- **Voice timing (from `lines.json`, not heard):** 1,954 words in 684 s, 171 words a minute, against DOOMPERK's measured 100 to 136. The narrator holds 60% of voiced time, so it is less dialogue-led than the model. No gap between lines is longer than 1.0 s anywhere, so punchlines get no beat ("He has met four of them." runs straight into "His name is Meatball."). Several Doug lines look too fast to land: "Hi, buddy!" 0.45 s, "That's my boy!" 0.56 s, "Sixty for me?" 0.64 s, "Is that his mum?" 0.64 s, "His name is Meatball." 0.77 s. Check these by ear.

## 5. Story and comedy: 4

The script is the episode's real strength.
- **Arc:** arrival, problems solved one by one with real science (salt, swim to breathe, electric sense, warm body), rival betrayal on day 15, a deadline ("You have two weeks to show me a plan"), the nursery plan, the rival's midnight heist, a rescue, the rival's turn, a release and an honesty close.
- **Callbacks that pay off:** the tap rule is planted at 153.6 s with "Hold on to that." and returns as the climax at 541.4 s ("Tap, tap."); "He ate a ring light, Doug." (416.2 s); "I read the crayon contract, Tanner." (501.6 s); the running "no form" gag ("I do not have a form for extinct", "because, again, there is no form"); and the triple "No." / "No." / "No." button at 615.8 s.
- **Rival turn** is earned: "Me. I called you. I tried to sell him. Doug came out for me anyway." (577.5 s), then "For the first time, Tanner says something without a camera on."
- **Good jokes:** "It's a type of extinct.", "From what, Doug? Chips?", "Tanner carried no buckets. He filmed all four thousand.", "For the first time in his career, Tanner's advert is completely true."
- **Honesty and safety close** matches the format: what was real, what was invented, three real safety rules, then "Tanner broke all three in one night."

Why not 5:
- "You" (Doug) has little agency until day 29. Wren solves the salt, swimming, sensing and nursery problems; Doug's main contribution is jokes. DOOMPERK's "you" is the one with the plan.
- The comment question is not a real split. "Do you take it? Comment Cash or Meatball." comes after 11 minutes that make the answer obvious, and it rewinds to day 6 instead of asking about the ending. DOOMPERK's best ("Which camp do you sleep in? Comment Riker or Rocks.") splits the audience.
- The tease promises "the shark he tried to sell gets to decide what happens next", but no beat on screen shows Meatball choosing.

## 6. Packaging: 3

- **Title** "What If a Baby MEGALODON Lived in Your Pool for 30 Days?" (56 characters) is strong: clear, searchable ("baby megalodon", "megalodon in pool"), one CAPS word, the subject inside the first 40 characters, and far enough from every DOOMPERK title.
- **Fit with the video:** broken at the title drop. The card and narration say "What if you had a pet megalodon?" (39.8 s), so the promise the viewer clicked is not repeated in the video.
- **Thumbnail A (GOOD BOY), recommended:** the strongest of the three at phone size; the label is legible and the shark reads as a shark. But against its own brief: the shark's head and grin, the point of the joke, are cropped at the top and partly under the label; the label is yellow at top left, not white at top right; the sky is pale blue, not hot yellow or orange; Doug's face is small (about a fifth of the height), whereas DOOMPERK's smug hero face is the biggest thing in the frame; the red arrow points at the house; and the shark is off-model compared with the in-video Meatball.
- **Thumbnail B:** the brief's TOO BIG label is missing; at phone size it is two tiny Dougs and a fin.
- **Thumbnail C (FOR SALE):** nothing in the image is visibly for sale; Tanner is small at the right.
