## 2. Policy and safety

- **Gore and injury:** none. Bites hit a watermelon, a ring light and fossil whale bones; nobody is hurt. Pass.
- **Keeping, feeding or approaching sharks: FAIL as written.** The whole story models keeping a wild shark, hand feeding it (scene 07), training it to come to a tap, kayaking beside it, and in scene 25 wading knee deep to a four metre shark and touching it. The climax also shows a shark nudging a swimmer as a friendly act. The close never counters this. It needs a real line and a card, for example:
  - NARRATOR: "And never feed, touch or chase a wild shark. Feeding sharks is against the law in places like Hawaii, because it brings sharks and people too close."
  - CARD: "NEVER FEED A SHARK | Feeding wild sharks is banned in places like Hawaii | SOURCE: Hawaii DLNR, Florida Museum". Sources: Hawaii Revised Statutes 188-40.6 and the DLNR 2018 release (feeding banned to cut "the risk of conflicts with people"); Florida Museum Shark Attack FAQ ("Never feed wildlife!"). Florida also bans shark feeding in state waters (FWC page returns 403 to fetch; confirm in a browser before adding Florida to the card).
  - Optional: "If you find a stranded shark, do not touch it. Call your local fisheries or wildlife line." (NOAA Fisheries Marine Life in Distress; Rhode Island DEM routes shark strandings to NOAA.)
- **The safety rule at the end:** real and correctly stated (row 38). "Tanner broke all three" holds: alone, at night, and he was the one fishing.
- **Honesty close:** says clearly that a living megalodon is made up. Pass. But the made-up list is incomplete: add "a shark that comes when you call, finds you across a dark sea and pushes you to safety; the real electric sense only works up close", and "every law Officer Hobbs quoted" (scene 24's "no law against releasing one" sounds like real legal advice).
- **Made for kids risk:** moderate, manageable. Kid-appeal signals: a cute baby shark named Meatball, toy spade sandpit, crayon contract, "Good boy", fish fingers. General-audience signals that keep it out of kids content: an adult cast, influencer satire, contract jokes ("pens are for clients", "somehow, your car"), a fisheries officer, real research cards. Keep the satire, set the audience to "not made for kids", and keep the thumbnail away from toddler-style cute.
